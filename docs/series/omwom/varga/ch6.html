Starring Everybody

The replacement narrator waited for the laughter to fade.

The script had reached the concessions counter, looped a table leg, and started back toward the screen. Generic Concessions Worker stepped over it without looking.

The narrator adjusted the TacHat™ visor.

"This story contains—"

The screen began counting.

"One traumatized teenager who receives enough power to crack the setting over one knee and chooses instead to improve municipal infrastructure."

Taylor appeared in hard hat and safety vest, pointing at a harbor plan while her tail held three rolled drawings.

"One ancient demon who has outlived civilizations, crossed realities, and discovered his highest calling is helping a fifteen-year-old commit low-grade psychological warfare against authority figures."

Varga's dragonlet form looked directly into the camera and winked.

"One father accidentally promoted from union negotiator to international criminal mastermind by people who have never asked him a single useful question."

Danny signed a purchase order. In four secure offices, alarms went off.

"Two delivery drivers who survive every encounter with Boss Hebert and become more frightened each time."

The men locked both truck doors while Danny waved politely from across a parking lot.

"Three teenage girls with cameras, claws, and a shared commitment to making every official report sound like the person writing it has suffered a break with reality."

Raptaur, Metis, and Ianthe posed over a restrained group of Merchants. Behind them, the building collapsed ten seconds earlier than expected.

All three turned.

All three cameras turned with them.

"We meant to do that," Raptaur said.

The narrator drew a red line through something on the script.

"No, you didn't."

Taylor looked offended. "We mostly meant to do that."

"Honest Trailer drinking game," the narrator continued.

A row of glasses appeared on-screen.

"Take a drink every time someone says the Family and a government agency assumes organized crime."

The glasses filled themselves.

"Take two every time a character invents a perfectly rational explanation which is less believable than the truth."

More glasses appeared.

"Take a drink whenever Taylor discovers a new application for her power."

The entire table vanished beneath glassware.

"Do not actually play this game."

The table collapsed.

"Your liver does not have a Brute rating."

Fury lifted his cup, inspected the label, and set it back down. "PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE."

Mr. Gladly checked his own. "Mine says RESPONSIBLE HYDRATION."

Carlos read his and frowned. "MOP FUND."

"Appropriate," three rows said together.

The narrator turned another page.

"Try to keep track of Taylor's secret identities: Saurial, Raptaur, Umihebi, Kaiju, Varga, Breksta, and whatever new reptile she invents between the beginning of this sentence and—"

A buzzer sounded.

A new name appeared at the bottom of the list.

"There it is."

On-screen, PRT analysts dragged a larger whiteboard into the room. The original had become a web of names, arrows, speculative relationships, height estimates, and angry notes about camera resolution. One analyst uncapped a marker.

"What if they're all the same person?" she asked.

Everyone else stared at her.

Her supervisor gently took the marker away.

"Go home, Jenkins. You've been here too long."

The Gallery groaned.

"She had it," Ianthe said.

"Nobody respects the correct answer when it arrives early," Metis replied.

"Or Jenkins," Taylor added.

The montage outran the analysts. Kaiju towed the tanker. Umihebi surfaced. Saurial handed over evidence. Raptaur chased Clockblocker. Amy weaponized vegetables. Lisa stared at a problem until it confessed. Danny repaired the docks and received another imaginary Mafia promotion.

Coil's bunker went dark.

The Merchants' base fell inward.

Lung looked up.

The delivery drivers locked their doors.

PHO refreshed.

Again.

Again.

Again.

"Experience more than two million words of plans, meals, engineering, jokes, family conversations, government reactions, forum reactions to government reactions, and reptiles reacting to forum reactions to government reactions."

The sun rose over Brockton Bay again.

The same dockworker returned with the correct key.

"All unfolding with the urgency of everyday life and the event density of an insurance company's worst prophetic dream."

A word counter appeared.

The counter passed a novel, a trilogy, and a shelf. War and Peace vanished behind it. On-screen: breakfast, a commute, three technical discussions, a meeting about one discussion, and a PHO thread getting the meeting wrong.

"Read a fic long enough that describing it as 'bigger than War and Peace' is less criticism than unit conversion."

The counter crossed two million.

Confetti fired.

The paper script still had not reached its end.

The narrator lifted the remaining roll and looked through the hollow center toward the Gallery.

"We checked."

"Twice," Generic Concessions Worker said.

It was the first thing the worker had said all night.

Every head turned.

The worker returned to wiping the counter and declined to elaborate.

On-screen, a claims adjuster read the phrase KAIJU-ASSISTED MARITIME SALVAGE, closed the file, and walked out of the building.

"And yet," the narrator said, "somehow..."

The clips slowed.

Danny smiled at Taylor over breakfast.

Amy saw Ianthe in a reflection and did not look away.

Lisa fell asleep somewhere she trusted enough to sleep deeply.

Taylor and Varga stood together in the private geometry of their shared mind, one life with room for two voices.

"...it stays interesting."

The music rose.

"STARRING—"

The first credit struck the screen with enough force to shake dust from letters which had not previously been dusty.

"TAYLOR HEBERT as—"

The list began scrolling.

"The Brain. The Tail. The Entire Dinosaur Section of the Toy Store."

Saurial waved.

"VARGA as World's biggest gecko."

The dragonlet stared at the screen.

"I have ended civilizations."

"And now you're adorable."

"DANNY HEBERT as The Godfather of Collective Bargaining."

Danny looked up from a contract with the weary suspicion of a man who knew someone, somewhere, had capitalized something unnecessarily.

"LISA WILBOURN as Tattletale 2: Now With Additional Tail."

Metis bared her teeth. "Acceptable."

"AMY DALLON as Doctor Dinosaur, But Legally Distinct."

Ianthe covered her face. "No."

"COIL as Fifty Percent Chance of Making the Wrong Choice, Twice."

Both versions of Coil selected the wrong door.

"LUNG as NOT a dragon."

Lung looked up at Kaiju.

Then farther up.

The word NOT doubled in size.

"THE MERCHANTS as Live-Fire Product Testers."

Raptaur caught the rocket.

"ARMSMASTER as Objective Evidence's Biggest Fan."

His helmet camera activated.

"THE PRT as Please Retype That."

A report returned covered in questions.

"PHO as Five Thousand People Being Wrong at Once, Collaboratively."

The thread gained another page.

"BENNY AND RUDY as Waiting for Boss Hebert to Make Them an Offer They Cannot Identify."

The delivery drivers stared at a fruit basket Danny had sent as thanks.

"THE DOCKWORKERS as The Only People in the City Who See a Dinosaur and Ask Whether She Has Forklift Certification."

Raptaur produced the card.

"THE BLOCKED TANKER as Thirty Thousand Tons Wearing Water Wings."

The floats inflated. The tanker rose, turned with the tide, and followed Kaiju toward the docks.

"Good ship," the narrator said.

"ONE LIVE THERMOBARIC ROCKET as Lunch."

Raptaur swallowed.

"And BROCKTON BAY as An Entire City Trapped in the Reaction Shot."

Every screen filled with faces looking upward.

One final credit squeezed in beneath the others.

"MP3.1415PLAYER as The Author Who Looked at the Phrase 'slice of life' and Asked How Thin the Slices Could Get Before Physics Became Involved."

The TacHat™ pencil placed a check mark beside it.

"Featuring special guest appearances by breakfast, second breakfast, the planning meeting for lunch, lunch, post-lunch analysis, and a late-afternoon government response to what everyone ordered."

Taylor lifted one claw. "The food is sometimes important."

"We know," the narrator said. "That's why second breakfast received billing."

Varga's laughter shook her shoulders.

The final title hit the screen. It grew a tail, three sets of claw marks, and a hard hat.

TAYLOR VARGA

The narrator cleared her throat.

"Also known as—"

The title flickered.

HOW TO TRAIN YOUR ELDRITCH HORROR

It flickered again.

GODZILLA: MUNICIPAL WORKS DEPARTMENT

Again.

WAR, PEACE, AND SECOND BREAKFAST

Again.

THE LAND BEFORE CRIME

One last card appeared in tiny type.

THE EXTREMELY SLOW AND THE FURIOUS

"That one seems rude," Taylor said.

"We checked the word count," the narrator replied.

The narrator leaned toward the microphone.

"The only story where 'and then everyone discussed what happened over breakfast' is both a pacing warning and a promise."

The screen went black.

For a moment, no one moved.

Then the Gallery applauded.

It wasn't the roar that had greeted Kaiju or Coil's Very Bad Day. It started with Mr. Gladly, then Carlos, then row after row standing for three girls whose lives had been turned into jokes without turning the important parts into one.

Taylor stood because sitting through it felt impossible.

Metis rose beside her, pretending she was only stretching.

Ianthe stood on Taylor's other side.

At the front, Fury turned around and accepted the applause with an expression of insufferable modesty. Taylor now knew exactly who was looking back through that one visible eye—and exactly what he claimed to have done.

Someone walked up behind him.

The newcomer had the bearing of a man accustomed to controlling disasters through a headset while everyone with direct access to the disaster ignored him. He nodded to Fury, then to the three visitors.

"We were just watching the results of my current project," the Dulles air traffic control chief said. "Care to join us?"

A second screen lit on the far wall.

Generic Concessions Worker appeared beside Taylor holding three bags labeled PLOT-SPECIFIC SNACK.

"Pull up a seat, grab some popcorn, and make yourself comfortable," the chief continued. "We were just about to get things rolling."

Metis looked at the screen. Then at Fury. Then back through their still-open circular door to the laboratory exactly as they had left it.

"Just to be clear," she said, "the door is still ours."

"Never touched it," Fury replied. "I merely happen to own the carpet at this end."

Metis pulled out her control device and checked. The connection remained stable. The return coordinates were unchanged. Nothing on the Gallery side had inserted itself into the system or claimed control of the route home.

"Good," she said.

"Guests," Fury added. "Not prisoners."

Ianthe accepted a bag of popcorn.

Metis took hers next.

Taylor checked the open door home, then the Gallery. Varga's amusement warmed the back of her mind. This was what random exploration was for.

"One movie," she said.

Fury sat down.

"That's how it starts."

Taylor took the third bag.

The label changed beneath her claws.

ONE MOVIE.

Then, after a beat:

PROBABLY.
