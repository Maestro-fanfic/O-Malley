Best Power Ever

DO NOT TOUCH THE LIZARDS remained on-screen.

A claw added UNLESS INVITED.

Another claw added OR PROVIDING SNACKS.

A third squeezed in ASK FIRST beneath both.

The narrator cleared her throat.

All three claws withdrew.

"MARVEL as a girl gets the BEST POWER EVER... and uses it to troll the entire city."

The trailer presented a classification chart.

"Super-strength."

Saurial lifted a truck far enough for a mechanic to reach the axle.

"Super-speed."

Raptaur crossed a warehouse roof between two frames of video.

"Super-senses."

Taylor identified a material by scent, an electrical fault by sound, and a concealed gun by the person carrying it having cleaned it with the wrong oil.

"Nigh-invulnerability."

Bullets flattened against scales. A rifle round interrupted a conversation with New Wave; Taylor paused, glanced toward the shooter, and continued the sentence.

"Voluntary shapeshifting. Instant armor. Spontaneous weapon creation. Matter analysis. Matter generation. Spatial manipulation. Magic. Multiple forms. Variable scale."

Each item produced a faster clip.

Taylor grew armor across her skin. In another clip, she became Raptaur and formed an EDM warhammer. As Saurial, she tasted an unfamiliar composite, made a thoughtful face, and reproduced it. Space folded around an object which should not have fit through the available opening. A rapid time-lapse showed Taylor cycle through three forms while working around the room, and a physicist quietly reconsidered his education.

The chart ran out of rows.

A second page dropped down.

Then a third.

The replacement narrator looked at her endless script, then at the chart.

"This is becoming a formatting issue."

"You get used to it," Ianthe said.

"No, you don't," Metis corrected.

On-screen, a PRT analyst attempted to assign ratings.

BRUTE appeared first.

Then MOVER.

CHANGER.

STRANGER.

BLASTER received a question mark. SHAKER arrived with three. THINKER appeared, vanished, and returned underlined. TINKER was added by one department and angrily removed by another. MASTER was written in the margin by someone who had misunderstood the entire situation.

At last the analyst circled the page and wrote YES.

"I object to Master," Taylor said.

"Everyone objected to Master," Metis replied. "That's why it stayed on three reports."

"Bureaucracy," Fury said. "Natural predator of accuracy."

The narrator tapped the TacHat™ visor with her pencil.

"And because merely having every useful power would lack ambition, watch Taylor discover applications which sound like typographical errors in reality."

The screen showed a block of material that did not exist in any catalogue. Taylor called it the good stuff because Varga's memory supplied properties but no helpful English trade name. Engineers tested it. One machine failed. A second returned numbers its operator accused of being sarcastic.

Taylor made room-temperature superconducting material for a Tinker who understood what she wanted. She analyzed armor by tasting a fragment. She folded distance until a large object occupied a small inconvenience. She performed mathematics with enough dimensions that Vista stared at the page, reproduced part of the method with pencil and paper, and accidentally gave several classmates a new respect for Lovecraft.

"At which point," the narrator continued, "the local science community adopts a rigorous new research protocol."

A physicist stood before a whiteboard covered in equations.

At the bottom he had written ASK THE LIZARD.

"Peer review has never been faster."

"That's irresponsible," Mr. Gladly said.

The physicist added POLITELY.

"Better."

The screen changed to the blocked harbor.

The tanker had blocked the harbor for years. Too big to ignore. Too expensive to move. Perfectly sized for a committee.

Kaiju walked into the water.

The shoreline filled with spectators, raised phones, and officials trying to decide whether this counted as an emergency.

"Witness the city-changing might of a power capable of moving obstacles governments have spent years explaining cannot be moved."

Underwater, Kaiju sealed the hull in a paper-thin skin of Vargastuff. Then she built two enormous float boxes down its sides and filled them with air.

The wreck rose in a chorus of grinding rock and tortured metal.

Thirty thousand tons of tanker bobbed in the bay wearing water wings.

"And the mature restraint of the teenage girl using it."

Kaiju clipped into a towing harness and let the tide swing the ship toward the docks. Then she surfaced in front of it, spotted the news helicopter, and waved.

The helicopter wobbled.

Kaiju resumed her leisurely swim. Four hundred feet behind her, the tanker followed like a very large, very rusty dog.

The Gallery laughed.

Taylor sank lower in her seat.

"The footage was going to exist anyway."

"Of course," Ianthe said.

"You merely optimized it," Metis added.

"Exactly."

The next montage belonged to Brockton Bay.

Taylor went shopping with a tail under a perception filter. A clerk looked straight at it, decided it was Somebody Else's Problem, and asked whether she wanted a receipt.

Clockblocker made a joke about being chased by an angry dinosaur woman.

Behind him, Saurial emerged from invisibility one careful step at a time.

Kid Win saw her, switched on his helmet camera, and chose the scientific method over friendship.

Clockblocker noticed his expression too late.

The chase crossed three rooftops.

"TROLL," the narrator said, the word filling the screen, "a city already held together by corruption, cape violence, and denial... by introducing a problem so cheerful, useful, and physically indestructible that nobody knows which emergency procedure applies."

A city official asked whether Kaiju required a building permit.

Kaiju asked whether the permit applied to the building or to her.

The official's pen stopped.

On PHO, LIZARD CONSPIRACY: MASTER POST gained forty pages overnight. Theories included aliens, projections, and dockworkers breeding dinosaurs in shipping containers. The one poster who got close was laughed out of the thread.

Other threads tracked the practical consequences.

Property values rose. Insurance forms acquired new exclusions. A restaurant advertised a FAMILY-SIZED special, realized its mistake, and changed it before lunch. Children wore homemade tails. Adults called the reptiles dangerous, then asked Kaiju to help with a charity project.

The city learned the new warning signs. Sirens plus smoke meant trouble. Sirens plus phones meant the Family had arrived first. Every bird fleeing the harbor meant check the water—and possibly the zoning laws.

"Troll an entire city," the narrator said, "not by making life worse..."

Raptaur repaired a wall she had gone through.

"...but by making it better in ways that force everyone to admit the impossible thing is helping."

Kaiju lifted a collapsed crane. Saurial delivered evidence. Umihebi mapped something beneath the bay. Dockworkers clocked in around all of it with the practiced acceptance of people who had learned wonder did not exempt anyone from safety procedures.

"Marvel as Brockton Bay meets the only force more powerful than an ancient extradimensional demon."

The screen filled with forum notifications.

"People online with time."

The Gallery groaned in recognition.

Then Amy appeared.

Not Ianthe. Amy, in ordinary clothes, tired and guarded, carrying a power everyone else had spent years defining for her. Heal this person. Fix that wound. Do not touch brains. Do not change yourself. Do not ask what else living matter could become.

Taylor asked.

The trailer moved carefully through the answer. Onion biomass became tissue. Tissue became structure. Structure became a living suit shaped to protect, strengthen, and expand what its wearer could do without erasing the person inside it.

Ianthe opened her eyes.

The theater was quiet.

"Watch a girl told her whole life that her power is only permitted to repair other people finally build something for herself."

Ianthe looked down at her claws on-screen.

In the Gallery, Amy looked down at the same hands.

Taylor did not say anything. She shifted her arm until their scales touched.

Lisa came next.

Her power supplied answers without mercy and consequences without instruction. Coil had treated her like equipment sophisticated enough to threaten. Taylor and Amy offered her a body Coil had never planned for, a name he did not own, and a place in a Family whose capital letter meant what they decided it meant.

Metis stepped into view, black scales swallowing the light.

"And watch a human lie detector become a reptilian stealth platform, because subtlety is apparently more fun when it can leave claw marks in concrete."

Metis nodded. "Fair."

The three appeared together.

Raptaur in dark armor. Ianthe in violet. Metis nearly invisible except for her eyes. Cameras rode on headbands, armor, and between scales, recording every angle because they had learned that objective evidence was cheaper than arguing with a bureaucracy after the fact.

"Together, they're the Family: three girls, several tons of combat-capable biomatter, enough processing power to frighten a research university, and absolutely no possibility that any of them will ever say something embarrassing while the cameras are running."

The clips began.

Raptaur completed a solemn warning to armed gang members, turned away, and whispered, "Was that too dramatic?"

Her own forehead camera recorded every word.

Ianthe landed hard enough to crack pavement, stood with perfect menace, then asked, "Did I bend that street sign or was it already like that?"

Metis, believing the microphones were off, offered a detailed and unkind analysis of a Protectorate cape's attempt at stealth.

The cape was standing behind her.

"Oh," Metis said on-screen.

In the Gallery, she covered her face.

"You said you deleted that."

"I did," Taylor replied.

Both looked toward Fury.

He ate popcorn.

The trailer accelerated through accidental admissions, unfinished jokes, wrong names, private comments delivered one second before someone noticed a red recording light, and the unique silence of three people simultaneously realizing the evidence package included themselves.

"WATCH as three girls manage to put their foot in it—ON CAMERA."

The screen split three ways.

Raptaur charged.

Metis disappeared into darkness.

Ianthe lowered one armored shoulder.

Three enormous feet struck three separate entrances at the same instant.

Doors, frames, and adjacent sections of wall entered the building ahead of them.

"Sometimes figuratively."

The three reptilian girls emerged inside the Merchant base amid settling dust.

"Sometimes not."

One gangster raised his hands.

"Does the camera get my good side?" he asked.

Raptaur looked at him.

"At the moment, your good side is the one not committing another felony."

The caption RECORDING appeared in the corner.

He raised his hands higher.

The montage ended with the girls on a rooftop. Police lights moved below. Cranes lined the docks. The moon shone on a bay full of impossible things.

They talked over each other, teased, replayed one clip, and cracked up. Three teenagers in bodies capable of terrifying nations, off duty for five minutes.

"And somehow," the narrator said, quieter now, "the Best Power Ever isn't the strength, the armor, the magic, the matter creation, the space-folding, or the ability to become a kaiju before breakfast."

The screen held on Taylor between Lisa and Amy.

"It's getting to build a life large enough that none of them has to face it alone."

Nobody in the Gallery made a joke.

Then Carlos sniffed.

"Who used the consequence remover again?"

The moment shattered.

"Your legacy," Fury told him.

"I hate this theater."
