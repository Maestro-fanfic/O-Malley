Chapter 4 of 6

Every Timeline Ends in Teeth

The word PROBABLY remained on-screen for exactly long enough to become legally concerning.

Then a gloved hand reached in from the edge of the frame and turned the title card over.

On its back was a map of Brockton Bay.

Pins marked gangs, informants, capes, police, politicians, and several people unaware they belonged on a conspiracy board. Beneath it all, Coil sat in a bunker built by a man who trusted concrete more than humanity.

"WATCH as one villain," the narrator said, "and his minions have a VERY BAD DAY."

Coil split the timeline.

In one branch, he sent an operative to watch the docks.

The operative's camera found Saurial.

The image went black.

In the other, Coil kept the operative home.

He frowned, retained the safe branch, and tried again.

One branch tested a listening device near the Hebert house.

Black.

One branch attempted to follow Raptaur.

Black.

One branch instructed a mercenary to collect a discarded scale.

The mercenary reached down.

Something just outside the camera's field said, "Hello."

Black.

Coil abandoned that branch so quickly the coffee in his retained reality spilled down the front of his suit.

"Meet Thomas Calvert," the narrator continued. "A discount Bond villain whose superpower lets him experience two possible timelines and keep the one he prefers."

The screen showed Coil at his desk, dry and composed in one reality, covered in coffee and swearing in the other.

"Meaning every decision is risk-free... right up until both options end in teeth."

The two screens went black together.

Coil screamed.

In the Gallery, Metis smiled with every visible tooth.

Fury noticed. "Personal history?"

"Professional satisfaction."

"That's a yes with paperwork."

Coil answered uncertainty with information, mercenaries, cameras, concrete, fallback positions for his fallback positions, and escape plans for the escape plans.

Each precaution gave him more ways to encounter the same result.

Black.

Black.

Black.

The minions began noticing.

One team approached a warehouse, abandoned its car ninety seconds later, and walked home separately. Another installed surveillance gear all night; Coil had the office stripped before sunrise. Three mercenaries rehearsed a kidnapping, drove six blocks, and were paid to forget the address.

They did not forget the address.

They merely stopped saying it where microphones might exist.

One veteran kept a list of orders Coil canceled mid-sentence. Every entry involved the docks, the Heberts, or reptiles. By page two he requested reassignment. Coil approved it in one timeline and refused in the other.

Both versions of the man quit.

"Witness the rare supervillain achievement of inspiring your own disposable henchmen to develop pattern recognition," the narrator said.

On-screen, Coil addressed a room of armed professionals.

"The target is a teenage girl."

No one reacted.

"She may be accompanied by a lizard."

Six hands went up.

"Question," the team leader said. "Define a lizard."

Coil closed his eyes.

"Singular."

"That's not what I asked."

Metis applauded once, sharply.

Once, the screen did not go black.

It filled with a single enormous eye.

Coil dropped that branch.

"No," he said aloud in the reality he kept.

He split again.

The eye was waiting in both.

"No."

"Marvel as the city's most methodical control freak discovers an opponent his power cannot model, his cameras cannot follow, his mercenaries cannot intimidate, and his captive teenage Thinker keeps finding increasingly creative reasons not to tell him anything useful."

The trailer cut to Lisa in Coil's base, wearing an expression of dutiful concentration.

"The lizards are probably unrelated," she said.

Coil studied her.

"Probably?"

"Statistically."

"What does that mean?"

"It means I'm considering the statistics."

The clip froze on Coil's face.

A small counter appeared in the corner: DAYS SINCE USEFUL ANSWER: 0.

It reset to zero without ever moving.

Metis looked pleased. "Accurate."

Ianthe turned to her. "You actually said that?"

"Not those exact words. Spiritually, constantly."

The narrator moved on.

"Watch Coil respond to every warning by becoming more Coil, until the man who treats entire realities as disposable discovers he is the disposable variable."

The trailer cut to the operation that finally ended it.

A caption identified the footage as the real assault on Coil's base.

Taylor and Varga entered under cloak, using their established dual-aspect trick: Taylor directed one projected form, Varga the other. Labels identified Saurial and Raptaur before the Gallery could accuse the trailer of inventing a spare Taylor.

The base was nearly twice its official size. Forty-six mercenaries. Three medics. Armory. Servers. Power. Demolition charges. Trapped escape tunnel.

Then they found the room which smelled of old blood.

The humor vanished for several seconds.

Metis's expression hardened in the Gallery. Ianthe stopped eating popcorn.

The trailer resumed with Taylor and Varga sealing off the medical bay, armory, power room, and server room. To Coil's men, the doors had not merely locked. The rooms could no longer be found.

Dragon guided them through attaching her bypass to Coil's servers. His security surrendered almost immediately. His external backups were seized at the same time. The self-destruct's main charges had already been rendered inert.

Outside, a tiny quantity of thioacetone emptied the surrounding streets far more effectively than a conventional evacuation order.

Inside, the lights went out.

The security feeds died. The sensors died. Coil's men retained just enough power and internal communications to understand exactly how blind they had become.

One monitor lit up.

LEVEL 1

MERCENARIES 44 — ME 3

Captain Smith stared at it.

The count changed.

MERCENARIES 43 — ME 4

Somewhere in the dark, something snickered.

"MARVEL," the narrator said, "as forty-six professional mercenaries discover they have been drafted into a survival-horror game whose protagonist is having the time of her life."

On-screen, Varga moved across the ceiling above a search team and removed its rearmost man without being noticed. When everyone spun around, he took another.

The score updated.

The mercenaries shot cameras, bunched up, and fired at shadows. Taylor and Varga appeared just long enough to waste their ammunition.

"At least they're trying to keep everyone alive," Ianthe said.

Fury watched another armed man disappear straight upward. "Generous definition of reassuring."

Meanwhile, Coil attempted to leave his private quarters.

Taylor folded his corridor into an endless loop.

He marked his progress with a green glow stick. After walking far farther than the dimensions of the bunker allowed, he found it again.

On the next circuit it was blue.

Then orange.

Coil shot it.

The corridor stretched toward vanishing points in both directions. One distant ceiling light disappeared. Then the next. Then another, each swallowed faster than the last as something approached through the dark.

A wet, inhuman call followed him.

"Tekeli-li."

Coil ran.

The Gallery listened to his footsteps and increasingly ragged breathing.

"Okay," Carlos admitted. "That's horrifying."

Taylor's tail shifted behind her seat. "It was supposed to be."

Elsewhere, Varga continued thinning the mercenary teams. A man looked away from his partner for a moment and looked back to empty concrete. Another saw a shape on the ceiling. A third fired at it, and the bullets struck nothing.

The score kept changing.

Once the mercenaries had been separated, disarmed, and frightened into wasting much of their ammunition, every speaker in the base clicked on.

"Endgame."

Legend blasted through the main entrance. PRT and Protectorate teams poured into the bunker from multiple directions. Metis and Ianthe accompanied the assault groups while Dragon went directly to the compromised servers.

Coil reached a control point, ordered his data purged, and activated the self-destruct.

Dragon stopped the purge.

The detonators would make noise. The main explosives would do nothing.

Coil did not know that when he abandoned every mercenary in the base, along with all the civilians in the buildings above it, and ran for his secret escape tunnel.

The Gallery went very quiet.

A tiny dragon clung unseen to his backpack as he fled.

"Taylor?" Mr. Gladly asked.

"Taylor," Metis confirmed.

Coil overrode the tunnel's traps and started toward the bay. Partway down the passage he encountered a door which had never existed before.

He listened. Heard nothing. Opened it.

An enormous yellow-orange reptilian eye stared back at him.

Coil closed the door with great care.

Then he screamed and ran.

The passage changed around him. It twisted through distances which could not fit beneath Brockton Bay and finally opened onto pale sand beside a perfectly black underground lake.

Behind him came the sound again.

"Tekeli-li."

Coil emptied magazine after magazine into the approaching shadow. When the ammunition was gone, a mass of tentacles, eyes, and teeth crawled into view.

One tentacle touched him.

A cheerful girl's voice said, "Tag. You're it."

The creature was approximately a foot tall.

Coil fainted.

For several seconds, the Gallery was silent.

Then Carlos folded over the arm of his chair.

The trailer jumped to the shoreline, where Director Piggot, Armsmaster, Dragon, and a PRT team arrived to collect their prisoner. Saurial waited beside something enormous which had approached so quietly that none of them had noticed it.

Umihebi opened her mouth.

Her tongue extended, unwrapped, and deposited an unconscious, slime-covered Thomas Calvert on the beach.

Saurial relayed Umihebi's considered opinion that he tasted terrible.

Coil woke, saw Saurial, then looked past her at Umihebi.

He fainted again.

"One villain," the narrator reminded them.

The PRT secured Coil inside its deepest holding area. Saurial left behind a detailed rubber model of the little tentacled creature.

Dragon squeezed it.

"Tekeli-li!"

Coil's eyes snapped open.

He screamed.

"And his minions," the narrator finished, as the score counter froze over footage of the PRT carrying prisoners and evidence out of the bunker, "have a VERY BAD DAY."

The words stamped themselves across the image.

Mr. Gladly shifted in his seat. "I'm beginning to understand why the cameras matter."

"Accountability," Taylor said.

"And highlight reels," Carlos added.

The trailer rewound to the beginning of the villain montage.

Lung grew. Fire climbed. Buildings shook. The projected repair bill developed commas. Taylor decided Raptaur was the wrong tool and changed forms.

Kaiju rose.

Lung looked up.

And up.

And up.

The unconscious narrator strapped into his seat made a distressed sound without waking.

"No," the replacement narrator said. "Not him. Almost too easy."

A red pencil struck through Lung's name.

The clip lasted six more seconds. Five of them were Lung realizing that his entire professional identity had just been reduced to the smaller dragon.

"There's always a bigger lizard," someone in the Gallery observed.

"Technically, I'm not—" Varga began.

Taylor stopped him. "Let them have it."

The screen switched to the Merchants.

Raptaur stood outside their base and offered them time to surrender. They answered with automatic weapons.

Bullets bounced.

She offered them less time.

They fired a thermobaric rocket into her chest.

When the smoke cleared, Raptaur looked down at the impact point, then back at the building. Unmarked.

Another rocket passed beside her.

She caught it.

The image slowed as every cape and trooper behind cover watched her inspect the live round.

Raptaur ate it.

"Now she's just showing off," someone said on the recording.

"Excellent psychological technique," someone else replied.

In the Gallery, Fury turned toward Taylor.

"I'm with the second guy."

"It worked."

"Best kind."

Raptaur, Metis, and Ianthe hit the Merchant base from three sides. Doors failed. Walls followed. Gunfire stopped. Prisoners and evidence came out. Then Raptaur pointed one claw at what remained.

The structure collapsed into its own footprint.

Dust rolled toward the bay.

The narrator let the silence sit.

"Brockton Bay gangs begin the story asking whether the Family can be controlled."

The screen showed representatives from three organizations staring at footage of Kaiju, Umihebi, the Merchant base, and Coil being removed from his own bunker.

"They end it asking a much more useful question."

One man closed the laptop.

"How do we avoid annoying them?"

The title card arrived in two simultaneous versions.

In the first:

COIL'S MASTER PLAN

In the second:

DO NOT TOUCH THE LIZARDS

The second remained.
