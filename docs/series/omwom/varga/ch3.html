Chapter 3 of 6

The Hebert Family Business

The title card tipped back into place.

Before it settled, a steel-toed work boot stepped down on one corner and held it there.

Steel-toed boot. Worn jeans. DWU jacket. Danny Hebert held a clipboard, pinned a phone to his shoulder, and somehow followed three conversations over a reversing forklift.

"LAUGH ALONG WITH US," the narrator announced, "as an ordinary dockworker gets mistaken by every intelligence agency on the planet for some sort of mafioso."

Danny ended the call.

Six thousand miles away, an analyst froze the image on the exact frame where his eyes passed over the camera.

"Subject appears aware of surveillance," a subtitle reported.

In the Gallery, Taylor made a strangled noise.

"Dad was looking at a gull."

The trailer zoomed in on the gull.

It had stolen half a sandwich and was watching Danny with criminal intensity.

"Known courier remains unidentified," the subtitle added.

Metis folded forward against the seat in helpless laughter.

The screen split twelve ways. From Washington to Beijing—and several offices that officially didn't exist—Danny's photograph appeared on secure monitors. Lines connected him to unions, police, politicians, shipping firms, and increasingly large reptiles.

The lines multiplied.

One chart ran out of room and continued onto a second display.

The theories grew according to national temperament.

One agency made Danny the civilian head of a parahuman logistics network. Another made the DWU a weapons-research front. A third capitalized Family and built a Mafia org chart from coffee-break seating.

A British analyst suggested, cautiously, that the organization might simply be a labor union with unusually capable friends.

His supervisor asked whether he had been compromised.

The Russians called his silence professional discipline. The Chinese decided his lack of bodyguards meant the whole city was his bodyguard. An American committee argued jurisdiction for three hours, then classified the minutes to hide that everyone had brought a secret Danny file.

"Every intelligence agency on the planet," the narrator repeated, while the screen added PLEASE HOLD FOR INTERPRETER beneath a video conference with too many windows.

"That's an exaggeration," Mr. Gladly said.

A thirteenth window opened.

An intelligence service belonging to a country with fewer people than the Dockworkers Association had prepared a seventeen-page assessment of Danny's handshake.

Mr. Gladly took off his glasses. "I withdraw the objection."

"Meet Danny Hebert," the narrator said. "Hiring manager. Lead negotiator. Widowed father. Man who knows a guy."

Each phrase appeared beneath a different image.

Hiring manager: Danny shook hands with a new crane operator.

Lead negotiator: Danny sat across from a city official who had arrived confident and left agreeing to six points he had intended to reject.

Widowed father: Danny stood in a kitchen at two in the morning, waiting for Taylor to come home, pretending the coffee in front of him was the reason he was awake.

Man who knows a guy: Danny greeted a police sergeant by name, asked after his knee, and received a favor which was neither illegal nor especially dramatic.

The intelligence chart flashed red.

"CONTACT CONFIRMED," someone had typed.

"Oh, come on," Taylor protested.

"Your father has an excellent poker face," Fury said.

"He was asking about physical therapy."

"Best cover stories are boring."

"It wasn't a cover story."

"Even better."

On-screen, the Dockworkers Association began to recover.

Contracts multiplied. Old equipment woke up. Warehouses stopped collecting pigeons and started collecting paychecks. At the center stood Danny, armed with lists and the frightening ability to get the right machine to a crew before it admitted needing one.

Around him, the Family appeared.

Sometimes the word meant Taylor and Varga.

Sometimes it meant Saurial, Raptaur, and the other public identities the city insisted must belong to separate reptilian relatives because the alternative required rewriting too many forms.

Later it meant Metis and Ianthe too.

To the dockworkers, it meant powerful friends who showed up, did useful work, and never treated them as scenery.

To the analysts, it meant capitalization.

"THE Family," the narrator said, as the words appeared on-screen in letters usually reserved for organized crime documentaries. "A shadowy organization with unknown membership, impossible resources, unexplained technology, deep political connections, control of critical waterfront infrastructure... and a suspicious tendency to repair public property for free."

The trailer showed Saurial welding a damaged support.

A caption appeared beneath her: ENFORCER?

In a later clip, Taylor was Raptaur, carrying a shipping container one-handed.

HEAVY ENFORCER.

Umihebi raised her head beyond the breakwater.

MARITIME ENFORCER.

Kaiju moved behind the skyline.

The caption box appeared, thought better of it, and vanished.

"All reporting to the quiet man with the clipboard."

Danny looked down at his clipboard.

"We're short two pallets of fasteners," he said.

In a secure office, three analysts exchanged grim looks.

"Code phrase," one whispered.

The Gallery dissolved.

Mr. Gladly covered a laugh. Carlos slapped his armrest. Generic Concessions Worker silently upgraded Fury's popcorn.

"Dad is going to hate this," she said.

"Your father would need to know about it first," Metis pointed out.

"He always knows eventually."

"See?" Fury said. "Mafioso."

Taylor threw a piece of popcorn at him.

He caught it without looking.

The screen cut to a delivery truck entering the DWU yard.

Benny and Rudy came up from New York with Antonio's delivery. They found a union full of hard-looking men, overheard talk of sniping people that was much less homicidal in context, and met Danny at the center of it all.

By the time the truck left Brockton Bay, Benny had become convinced that the mild-looking union official was the Boss of the Docks and that everyone around him was a stone-cold killer.

He subsequently got drunk and told people.

That had not improved his peace of mind.

"WATCH," the narrator said, "as two delivery drivers have an increasingly improbable series of encounters with said mafioso."

NEW YORK appeared across the screen.

Danny had traveled there to visit Antonio, the old family friend he had not seen in far too long. Benny and Rudy did not know that. As far as they knew, Boss Hebert had left his territory and appeared in theirs.

The first encounter took place at a bowling alley.

Benny arrived with Rudy, Clara, and Ella—and found Danny already there, alone at a lane. Danny recognized them immediately and greeted them with cheerful surprise.

"It's good timing," he said, gesturing toward the scoring console. "I was just about to start another game."

In Danny's head, this meant: Bowling is more fun with company.

In Benny's head, it meant: I knew when you would arrive and prepared a position for you.

The trailer printed both translations beneath the same friendly smile.

"Oh, no," Taylor said, already beginning to laugh.

The evening somehow became worse for Benny when a bowling ball came flying toward his head and Danny saved him from taking it in the face.

Clara saw a decent man reacting quickly to prevent an accident.

Benny saw an attack which Boss Hebert had allowed to proceed precisely far enough to demonstrate that Benny's life remained in his hands.

"That is an impressively unhealthy interpretation," Mr. Gladly said.

Fury shook his head. "Consistent, though."

The second encounter occurred at Antonio's place.

Benny had spent years learning how people behaved around men like Antonio. He watched Gino treat Danny with unmistakable respect. Then he heard Danny refer to the old man as Uncle Tony.

The words echoed through the theater.

UNCLE TONY.

Benny's remaining hope that the bowling alley had been coincidence quietly packed a bag and left the country.

"For anyone keeping score," the narrator said, "Danny is visiting an elderly family friend to apologize for letting fourteen years pass without speaking to him."

On-screen, Antonio and Serafina welcomed Danny like a long-absent nephew.

"Benny concludes he has just witnessed a meeting between two national crime lords."

"Dad didn't even know about the rumor until Antonio told him," Taylor managed.

"That's what a good cover identity would say," Fury replied.

Taylor threw another piece of popcorn at him. He caught this one too.

The next morning, Benny refused to go anywhere Boss Hebert might plausibly be. When Clara suggested returning to the restaurant, he vetoed it. Absolutely not. They would go somewhere else.

Rudy proposed a small café on the way.

Safe. Ordinary. Unconnected.

They turned onto the side street and slowed while Rudy looked for parking.

Benny glanced through the café window.

Danny Hebert was sitting at the table beside it, having coffee.

Danny looked up, recognized them, smiled, and raised his cup in greeting.

Benny went white.

"Keep going," he croaked.

Rudy looked.

"Oh, hell."

The car accelerated away while Danny peered after them, puzzled by the sudden departure.

"Boss Hebert knows where you're going to be before you do," Benny whispered.

The screen replayed the sequence from above. Danny's route from his hotel to the café was a straight line. Benny and Rudy's route acquired three turns specifically chosen to avoid him, then converged on the same building anyway.

An animated probability meter caught fire.

"He isn't following them," Taylor said helplessly.

"Correct," the narrator agreed. "That would be less frightening."

By the end of the visit, Danny had reconciled with old friends, enjoyed a game of bowling, saved a man from a head injury, and had a pleasant cup of coffee.

Benny and Rudy believed they had received three increasingly elaborate reminders that Boss Hebert could reach them anywhere.

"And the best part?" the narrator asked. "He still has no idea he's doing it."

Taylor was laughing too hard to object.

"Because the only thing more dangerous than a criminal mastermind," the narrator said, "is a decent man everyone has mistakenly agreed must be one."

On-screen, Danny stood at the kitchen stove making dinner.

Taylor entered in human form, dropped her bag, and hugged him from behind without warning.

He covered her hands with one of his.

No agents saw that part.

No intelligence chart included it.

"Behind the international conspiracy, behold its true objective: keep one girl safe, put a dying waterfront back to work, and get spaghetti on the table before seven."

The image cut to a classified briefing room.

An agent pointed at a photograph of the spaghetti.

"Possible meeting of regional captains," she said.

The Gallery groaned as one.

"Oh, that's weak," Carlos called.

"No," the narrator replied. "That's Tuesday."

The next title card arrived stamped in red.

THE HEBERT FAMILY BUSINESS

Beneath it, in smaller letters:

NO, NOT THAT KIND.

The smaller line flickered.

PROBABLY.
