Sleeper

Sleep came in pieces that night, if it came at all. Dana surfaced every hour or so to the same gray ceiling and the same knot of everything from the hotel garage sitting exactly where she'd left it, then went back under for another twenty minutes that felt less like rest than a different kind of waiting.

By the time actual light started working in gray around the edges of the blinds, she gave up pretending and just lay there. Her body still felt wrung out, but her head was clearer than it had been last night, and clearer was going to have to do.

Faith hadn't said a word since the porch. Not sulking. Just quiet, the kind that meant somebody was working something over instead of resting. Dana had felt her sitting there in the dark for hours, turning something around and around, weighing it against every dumb action movie she'd ever half watched with somebody who actually knew what to do with a gun. Not this movie. Faith had never heard of Harry Tasker in her life. Just the shape of it. The kind of story this was turning out to be, and what usually happens next in stories like that.

Okay. Dana said it the way you'd test weight on a bad ankle, a little at a time. I'm home. I'm rested. Not strictly true, but close enough not to argue with. Now what the hell was important enough to hold back but not urgent enough to spit out right there?

A pause. The kind that already knew exactly where it was going to land.

No you're not. Faith, dry, letting the rested claim sit there a second before she knocked it down. But it's close enough for government work, so it'll have to do.

This is where the hard part lives, kid. Faith's voice, when it came, had none of the coaching briskness from the garage left in it, none of the flat grounding facts on repeat. Just something slower, more careful, like she was setting something down instead of handing it over. Take a couple of breaths. And stay right there in the bed. You're gonna need both.

Dana's stomach dropped through the mattress. Faith.

Let me know when you're ready.

Dana lay there a minute, running through her own private version of a pep talk. Nothing eloquent about it. Just suck it up, suck it up, suck it up on repeat, until it started to mean something.

Okay. Small. Tentative. Hit me.

Okay. Faith didn't rush into it. First of all, this is gonna be a real one-two punch. I just need you to bear through it until I lay it all out.

Dana gathered up what nerve she had left and held onto it with both hands.

First. Faith's voice landed hard enough to feel like a grip on her shoulder. YOU HAD NO PART IN THIS. Then, softer, right on top of it, like she needed this half heard just as clearly as the first: This is all on whichever of your parents started this whole train rolling. But, important, it's not THEIR fault either. It's a consequence. Not a blame game.

Dana breathed through it, in and out, holding herself together one exhale at a time.

Then a second voice joined Faith's, warm and sudden, layered in like it belonged there. ROB-Helen, though Dana had no way of knowing that, no name or face to put to it, just warmth, steady as a hand between her shoulder blades. That's my girl. Keep breathing. You can get through this.

Somewhere behind that, Faith went quiet for exactly one beat too long, quiet that would've meant something if Dana had been in any state to notice it.

Here's the heavy part, Faith said, moving past it. I've seen a bunch of these play out in the movies. Lived through more than I can count myself, too. And the one thing the movies always get right: there's a reason behind the madness. This wasn't a random snatch. So one of your folks is mixed up in something dangerous. My guess, counter-intelligence. That's the shape those five in the hall had. Arabic-speaking, professional, and ready for exactly what they were doing.

Just keep breathing, ROB-Helen said again, quieter this time, like she already knew she didn't need to compete for space.

Okay. Dana was still breathing. Still here. That's the one-punch. What's the two?

This is the hard part for you. Faith didn't soften into it. There's nothing you can change about how it starts. But there's a lot you might be able to do about how it ends. There's no way to make this one gentle, so I'm not gonna try.

A beat.

You're going to be Taken.

✳

Somewhere in the anonymous rows, a voice had just gotten as far as grumbling about who owned that line, when Harry came up out of his seat like the row itself had launched him.

"WHAT!? What the fuck, Faith? How can you say that to my daughter?!" He didn't stop there. "What is she even supposed to do with that? How's a fifteen-year-old supposed to change an outcome? Why would you tell her that, why would you say it like THAT, what if she can't, what happens if she can't, what then..."

ROB-Helen's hand found his arm before he could get any further, not gripping, just there. "Sit down." Quiet enough that it cut through the rest of the room better than raising her own voice would have. "There's method to the madness. Remember: you already lived through this. You already know it's happening. Changing the outcome is the whole point." A beat, something almost fond creeping into it. "Besides. This is like someone talking to the screen at the movies." Another beat. "Actually. Exactly like that."

✳

Back with Dana, the reaction hit before she could stop it. WHAT?!

Remember. Faith cut through it before it could build into anything bigger. Hold it together until I lay it all out.

Someone's going to come for you. No hedging in it, none of the usual maybe she'd been getting all week. You know your dad's got that picture of you and your mom in his wallet? It's inevitable.

Dana sat with that a second, breathing through it the way she'd been taught an hour ago in an elevator that already felt like a different lifetime. "Okay," she said out loud, quiet, to the empty room. "So what do I do?"

Nothing. Flat. Final. Literally nothing. That's why this is the hard part. You have to let it happen. You have to keep Trent from putting himself anywhere near the line of fire. You have to stay calm. And most important, you can't give any sign, not one, that you already know what's coming when it does.

There's only one way that actually works. Faith didn't slow down for her to catch up. Your mom's got mood-meds in the medicine cabinet. You know what they're for. You need one, RIGHT NOW. You need to be resting, and actually RELAXED, when this happens, or you're gonna blow the whole thing before it even starts.

Dana breathed for a moment before she trusted her own voice again. "Okay. Okay. I can do this." A beat. "Yeah. Panic time's later. Right now there's a job to do. I can do this." She was still saying some version of it under her breath, the same handful of words on a loop, as she got up, made her way to the bathroom, filled a glass at the tap, and found the bottle in the cabinet exactly where she knew it would be. She swallowed one down, refilled the glass out of habit more than thirst, and carried both of them back to bed with her.

✳

Sleep came fast, the meds doing exactly what Faith had promised, pulling her down into something too heavy to call rest and too dreamless to call anything else. Time stopped meaning much after that. An hour. Maybe three.

The front door went first, wood cracking, a sound too big for the house to actually hold.

Faith was talking before Dana's eyes had even opened, before her mind had finished deciding the sound was real. Keep it together. Stay calm. Let it happen. You're still out of it. You don't know a single thing.

Boots next, up the stairs, more than one pair, timed and unhurried, somehow worse than if they'd been running.

Stay loose, Faith said, right through it, the same handful of words on a loop, patient as a tide. Stay quiet. Let them do the work.

Then hands, gloved, closing around her before she'd finished processing that any of it was real. She let her whole body go slack, let it happen exactly the way she'd promised she would.

Good. Faith, steady, not once letting up. That's it. Just like that.

The van came next, cold metal under her, a door sliding shut on a sound she was starting to know too well.

Then a plane, engines climbing under her in a register the house had never made, the world tilting sideways as it left the ground.

Then, eventually, stillness again, and hands hauling her up, and the echo of somewhere enormous and unfinished, concrete and open air, and far below, the muffled, ordinary hum of a city that had no idea any of this was happening to one of its own kids twenty stories up.

✳

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery didn't just react. It came apart, and this time nobody bothered picking up the pieces first.

"They TOOK her? They just took her, right out of her own house..."

"How does that even happen? How does nobody see five armed men walk up to a house in broad daylight..."

"You want somebody to blame, blame the two idiots who left her alone in that house in the first place..."

"That's not fair and you know it..."

"I didn't say it was fair. I said it was true..."

"I will end every single one of them." Xander, and for once nobody laughed at him for saying it. "I don't care whose universe I have to reach into to do it. I will personally..."

"Get in line."

"If they've so much as touched her..." Hard, unmistakable, the same voice that had already staked its claim on Dana back in the hotel lobby. Nobody needed the name attached to know exactly who meant it.

"This is on me." Gib, quieter than the rest of it, almost lost underneath. "I'm the one who left that file where anybody could..."

"Nobody left anything anywhere, Gib. Sit down."

"Somebody just tell me she's okay. That's all I want. Just tell me that much..."

"She's twenty stories up in a building full of men with automatic weapons, what part of that sounds..."

"Not helping!"

"I wasn't trying to help. I was trying to be accurate..."

Fury tried twice to get the room back and got run over both times. Harry didn't try at all. He just sat there, all the fury from an hour ago burned down to nothing, staring at a point on the floor that wasn't actually there.

Underneath all of it, one voice didn't raise itself at all. Trilby, cigar smoke curling up ahead of the words. "Two men minimum on that door, more scattered through the floor below it. Same discipline as the hotel crew, professionals, not amateurs." He let that settle before he went on. "If the kid plays it exactly the way Faith's coaching her, calm, no sign she knows a thing, she's got a real window." A beat. "If she doesn't..."

He didn't finish it. Didn't need to.

Nobody argued with him either.

"He's right." The same someone whose hand had come out of nowhere ten chapters back and put Gib's whole skull half an inch forward over a stolen remote, one clean shot, no windup, gone before anybody turned around fast enough to catch him at it. Nobody in this Gallery had ever actually confirmed who'd thrown it. Somebody, somewhere else entirely, in a theater running an entirely different story, already had. The name had made the trip over anyway, the GibbsSlap, trademark pending, arriving well ahead of the face or the voice to go with it. Just a cup of coffee that never seemed to run empty and a way of saying more with less than anybody else in the room had managed all night. "I've sat through this. More times than I want to count." Clipped. Economical. Not a wasted word in it. "Hostage upstairs. Professional crew downstairs. Everybody outside losing their minds about it. Looks unsurvivable from here." A beat. "Usually isn't. She's got somebody in there talking her through it, real time. Most people I've watched walk out of buildings like that didn't have that."

It took a while for the rest of the room to catch up to that kind of quiet. The shouting thinned out first, then the crosstalk, then even the anonymous rows ran out of things to say that hadn't already been said twice. By the time it was actually silent, really silent, it had taken most people a good long minute to get there, and even then it didn't feel settled so much as spent, everybody sitting in the wreckage of having said all of it at once and having nowhere left to put any more.
