No Safe Angle

"Quiet, everybody." Fury's voice cut clean through the usual pre-scene noise, flat, no room in it for argument. "Shit's about to get real."

"That serious, huh?" somebody asked, already half-joking, already not quite managing it.

"Yeah." Fury didn't dress it up any further than that.

"How real we talking?"

"Real enough that I already told you once."

Nobody pushed it a third time. The rows went quiet, one after another, faster than they usually did, until there was nothing left in the theater but the sound of everybody settling in for something none of them were going to enjoy watching.

✳

Dana found a stretch of wall with a clean line on the door and made herself small against it, and then there was nothing to do but wait.

It was the worst part so far. Worse than the elevator, worse than watching her mother tear a dress apart in a hotel hallway. There, at least, something had been happening. Here there was only a closed door, and a hallway that stayed exactly as bright and exactly as empty as it had been five minutes ago, and Dana's own head filling in everything the door wouldn't show her.

She built explanations and knocked them down one after another. An affair didn't fit, not with the envelope, not with a stranger's flat, coded voice on the phone. A kidnapping didn't fit either, not yet, not with her mother walking in on her own two feet, dressed for something. Nothing fit. The door didn't care what fit. It just stayed shut.

You don't have to solve it standing here, Faith said, quiet, when the silence had gone on long enough that Dana's own thoughts had started circling instead of moving.

I know.

Doesn't mean it's easy. Just means you don't have to.

That was when the elevator dinged behind her.

Dana bolted for the nearest cover before she'd fully processed the sound, heart going twice as fast as the two seconds it actually took her to get there.

Other way, Faith said, already redirecting her mid-step. Away from the room. Whoever that is, you do not want to be caught standing anywhere near her door when they step out.

Dana changed direction without breaking stride and made it around the far corner just as the doors opened behind her.

Five of them came out. Four of them barely looked like they needed the elevator at all, big, blunt, the kind of men built for standing in doorways and not much else, moving with more muscle than thought behind it.

The fifth one had the eye.

Dana couldn't have explained what she meant by that even to herself, but Faith didn't need it explained. Something in the way he scanned the hallway before he'd even fully cleared the doors, quick, precise, cataloguing exits and angles instead of just walking through them like the other four.

That one, Faith said, already gone flat and focused. That one's not muscle. Watch him, not the rest of them.

Eye Guy, Dana thought back, because she had to call him something, and it fit.

Eye Guy, Faith agreed, like the name had already been waiting for somebody to use it.

"You see that, Trilby?" Fury called back, delighted. "Even the Author's mocking you."

"You do remember what I said at the debrief, right?" Trilby shot back, unbothered. "No slack just 'cause you're on Harry's team. Don't make me come over there."

Eye Guy ran them through a recon that was, even to Dana's untrained eye, nowhere near as thorough as he clearly wanted it to be. He threw hand signals at the other four, quick, sharp, unmistakably meant to send them checking angles and sightlines along the hall, and the other four responded to those signals the way men respond to instructions they've had explained to them more than once already and still haven't quite absorbed.

He's doing this whole thing with a crew he doesn't trust, Faith said, something almost like sympathy creeping into it. Watch. He's already had to repeat that signal twice.

He had. One of the four drifted the wrong way down the hall before catching himself and correcting. Another stood frozen a beat too long, waiting on a cue he'd apparently already missed the first time it was given. Eye Guy's jaw worked, tight, controlled, a man visibly recalculating how much of his own plan he was going to have to walk them through by hand.

"And how many of 'em he's gonna have to cull afterward," somebody added, "for not having enough brain cells between 'em to put two and two together."

It cost them time. Real time, the kind that mattered, and Dana could feel every extra second of it stretching out behind that closed door down the hall, whatever was happening in there getting that much longer to happen before anyone was ready to stop it.

"Ohh, this is bad," somebody in the gallery murmured. "Not the goons. The other thing. The thing happening on the other side of that door while these idiots figure out how a hallway works."

Nobody argued the point.

The crew finally got itself moving, and once they actually started down the hall, all the fumbling dropped away like it had never happened. They weren't running. That was the first wrong thing about them once they got going, the thing that told Faith more than the guns did. Four men in plain jackets moved at a controlled, unhurried walk, weapons already out and already held low and close to the body, carried the way real training taught it, not the movies. No shouting. No wasted motion.

Eye Guy moved through that hallway like he owned the exact amount of space he needed and not one inch more, hand signals instead of words, the other four falling into position around the door without needing to be told twice, one covering the hall behind them, two flanking the frame, the last dropping to a knee to sight the lock.

That's not muscle, Faith said, and for the first time since the elevator, there was something in her voice that wasn't just calm. That's an actual officer. Somebody who's done this enough times he doesn't have to think about the order anymore. Whoever's in there just got a lot more dangerous than a jealous husband's guys with guns.

Get out of the open, she added, before Dana had to be told twice either. Count what you can, stay low, and do not, under any circumstances, be standing anywhere near that door in about four seconds.

Dana was already moving, back around the corner she'd used before, when Eye Guy raised two fingers, held them a beat, and dropped them.

The door went in on the third fist.

No warning shout. No hesitation. Just four men flowing through a doorway in the exact order their leader had already decided on before he'd ever raised his hand, and Eye Guy stepping through last, unhurried, like a man walking into a meeting he already knew the outcome of.

The door didn't swing shut behind them. It hung open on a splintered frame, nobody left outside who needed to bother hiding what was happening anymore.

Dana stayed exactly where she was, back to the wall, breathing as quietly as she'd ever breathed in her life, and started counting seconds instead of people, because Faith had already told her the number that mattered.

Five. Just five. And not one of them had looked like this was the hard part of their night.

✳

Dana couldn't see any of it. The door hung open on its splintered frame down the hall, but the angle gave her nothing, no line into the room at all.

She heard every word of it.

Hotel hallways carried sound better than they had any right to, and nobody inside that room was bothering to keep their voice down anymore. It came to her whole, whisper and shout both, close enough that she'd have sworn afterward she'd been standing in the doorway herself.

Inside, it was already over before it started.

Harry stood by the curtained windows with both hands raised, a striped shirt open at the collar, his eyes on the two men closing the distance toward him.

"Don't move!"

He didn't. One gunman kept a pistol trained square on his chest. Another swept a flashlight straight into the room, throwing a hard white flare across everything it touched.

Harry's attention slid past both of them, toward Helen.

"Helen, just don't do anything."

Calm. Deliberate. A man managing the room instead of fighting it.

Behind him, a man in a leather jacket seized his arms and forced them back. Harry let it happen, gave nothing, exposed nothing.

Across the room, a burgundy jacket caught Helen from behind, an arm hooked hard across her chest and shoulders, hauling her backward. She fought it immediately, twisting, throwing her head back against the hold.

"No! He has nothing to do with it!"

The gray-suited gunman crossed in front of her, pistol never leaving Harry.

Helen strained forward against the arm locked around her. "I'm the one you want!"

"Shut up!"

Her captor wrenched her back. The gray garment she'd worn over the black dress hung displaced and useless around her now, catching at her arms.

The leather-jacketed man folded Harry forward and drove him face-first onto the bed, beside the black telephone resting on the spread. His arms went behind his back, held there.

"Quiet, Helen. Do what they say."

Even pinned, even with the phone inches from his mouth, his voice didn't lose the same deliberate calm.

Near the door, the bald one, Eye Guy, moved out ahead of the rest, pistol and flashlight both up, checking the hall beyond the suite while the others finished securing the room behind him. He didn't wait to be told the way out was clear. He went and found out himself.

They hauled Harry back up off the bed, one man pulling his arms behind him while the gray-suited gunman pressed a pistol to the side of his head, and turned him toward the door.

"Come on, guys. Let the hooker go. She's not important."

"Who's a hooker?" Even now, even like this, the offense in Helen's voice was real.

"You don't need her."

The burgundy-jacketed man kept his arm cinched hard across her chest, pistol close, dragging her along behind Harry.

"Harry, please, just let me handle this."

She still believed, even now, that this was somehow still about the thing she thought she'd been sent here to do.

They forced both of them out through the suite's entrance hall together, Harry pushed ahead with his arms pinned, Helen pulled stumbling after him, her displaced coat tangling around her sides with every step.

"Shut up!"

Then they were out of the room and into the hallway, and Dana could see them again.

Harry first, arms wrenched back, a gunman's pistol never leaving the side of his skull. Helen a beat behind him, held upright by the arm locked across her chest, stumbling on heels that had betrayed her all night for entirely different reasons than this one.

Harry's eyes swept the hallway as they came, quick, professional, the same kind of scan Eye Guy had used going in.

They found her.

For less than a second, Dana was certain of it, dead certain, the way you're certain of a hand closing around your wrist even before you feel it tighten. Her father's eyes landed on her, recognized her, and kept moving, sliding on past her hiding spot like she was just another shadow in a hallway full of them, like he'd never seen anything there worth a second look at all.

He didn't react to her at all.

She understood, later, that not reacting had been the reaction.

The Harry standing in that hallway was only ever doing it once, the same as everything else happening to him that night. It was the Harry watching from the theater who'd been through this part before, the same eyes, the same fraction of a second spent checking every doorway and shadow on the way past, a scan he remembered making himself, once, for real, a long time before any of this got put on a screen. He remembered making it clean. He didn't remember it ever finding anything.

"SHIT!" Harry's own voice, from somewhere back in the theater, sharp, real alarm cutting straight through it. "She wasn't there the first time! What the fuck is she doing there?"

Why did Mom go to the hotel room, why was Dad even there, why did he call her a hooker, why are there armed guys with guns, what the fuck is going on, what is even happening, I don't, I can't...

Not the time. Faith cut through it flat, no room for anything else in it. You can panic later. Promise. Right now you gotta get to the garage. Pronto.

Why the garage?

'Cause we need the license plate on whatever they're loading your parents into, and it is leaving without you if you stand here having a breakdown about it.
