Exit Interview

The ballroom didn't go away. That was the strange part, if anything about that theater could still surprise anyone this many chapters in. It just stopped being the only thing on the screen.

A second screen came up beside it, unhurried, like it had always been there and simply hadn't bothered turning on until now. No projector beam, no visible seam where one ended and the other began, just suddenly two of them, side by side, the ballroom still running on the left, Harry and Helen still cutting hard through the crowd toward their daughter, Dana still standing frozen at her own table with one hand pressed flat against her own chest.

On the right, the picture resolved into someplace far plainer. White walls. Fluorescent light. A door that led nowhere real.

The waiting room.

Some of the anonymous rows noticed before others did. A ripple of quiet worked its way backward through the theater as more and more of them clocked what they were actually looking at.

"That's..." somebody started, and didn't finish it, because everybody who'd been here since Chapter Two already knew exactly what they were looking at.

Fade into the waiting room. No screen inside a screen this time, no Gallery framing at all. Wherever this actually was, it was real, in whatever way any of this had ever counted as real.

The first thing Faith noticed was the face.

Same one from the very start of this whole book. Same borrowed mother's face, familiar down to the last detail after a year spent watching it move through somebody else's life, still dressed for the embassy party, black gown, hair up, that same cigarette holder dangling loose from two fingers like she'd walked straight out of the ballroom and hadn't bothered changing for the occasion. Faith didn't wait for an explanation, didn't wait for a word, didn't wait for anything at all.

She hit it dead in the mouth before it had even finished turning toward her, one clean shot, no windup, the same punch Helen herself had thrown at Fury back before any of this had a name yet. This time it was Faith doing the throwing. This time it landed on the one face in the whole story that actually owed her an answer.

Somewhere else entirely, Fury's hand flew to his own jaw. "I felt that." He worked it, genuinely bewildered. "How did I feel that?"

"YOU UTTER BITCH!" Faith was already shouting before ROB-Helen hit the ground, no interest left in pretending otherwise. "How could you do that to your own daughter?!"

ROB-Helen went down hard, one heel catching a chair that hadn't been anywhere in the room a second earlier, and landed flat on her back for it.

She took her time getting up. Straightened her dress. Checked her own lip with two fingers, entirely unbothered by whatever she found there.

"I told you when this all started." Calm. Almost bored. "'Reasons that will become apparent.'" A beat, brushing something off her sleeve that wasn't actually there either. "Just because they're apparent doesn't mean you're seeing them yet."

"Well, clear it the hell up for me!"

"In a minute." She smoothed the last wrinkle out of her dress and looked past Faith, toward the door. "We have more guests coming."

Three shapes resolved out of nothing behind her, a rising hum and a shimmer of gold-white particles sparkling top to bottom, an effect anybody who'd owned a television at any point in the nineties would have recognized on sight, whether or not they could have named where they'd seen it first. The sound faded. The light held its shape. Where a second ago there had been nothing at all, three actual people now stood.

It took Faith exactly one second to recognize all three of them.

They're here. She said it like saying it out loud might make it stop being true. They're not supposed to be here. How are they here?

"You're not the only one owed an explanation," ROB-Helen said, still not remotely worried about any of this.

Real Helen's eyes found her.

Real Helen hit her.

"BITCH!"

ROB-Helen hit the floor.

She lay there a moment, staring up at the ceiling, and let out a long breath.

"Really? Twice in less than a minute?"

Somewhere else entirely, in a theater running an entirely different story, Gladly hit the floor in the exact same pose, blood already welling at his lip, and had absolutely no idea why.

ROB-Helen picked herself up off the floor a second time, entirely unhurried, and took a moment to dust off both palms against her own dress, one leisurely pass smoothing out the wrinkles a punch and a half had put in it. A sequence anybody alive at the time would recognize on sight followed it, whether or not they could have named the first place they'd ever seen it.

She raised one hand to shoulder height.

And snapped her fingers.

The black gown was gone. In its place: a familiar uniform, red across the shoulders and chest, fading down into solid black below, the kind of silhouette that didn't need a single word of explanation for anyone who'd ever owned a television at all.

The room went with it. White walls and fluorescent light gave way to something warmer, wood paneling, low lamps, a fireplace crackling away in a wall that hadn't had one a second earlier. Comfortable armchairs stood arranged in a loose half circle around the hearth, and everyone was already sitting in one, Faith, Dana, Harry, Helen, all of them settled in like they'd been there the whole time and the room had simply gotten around to admitting it.

"And now, to business." ROB-Helen settled back into her own chair like she owned the room, the fireplace, and everyone currently sitting in it. "I owe you all an explanation."

"You owe us more than that." Helen, arms crossed, nowhere near finished being furious. "You owe us a fucking apology."

ROB-Helen brushed right over her, not even a flicker of acknowledgment. "Do none of you see why I did this?" Something almost wounded crept into it, buried again just as fast. "I swear, sometimes I wonder why I even try..." The rest dissolved into muttering, words too quiet and too fast to actually follow.

"What the hell do you mean?" Harry leaned forward, out of patience for anything cryptic.

ROB-Helen turned serious, all the muttering gone out of her at once.

"Look at your daughter, Harry." Dead straight, not a flicker of a joke on her face. "Now look at Faith." A beat. "Now back at your daughter."

Her body changed under that same borrowed face, shoulders squaring, stance widening into something built like it had never once worried about anything in its entire life, unmistakably lifted from one very specific decade-old commercial nobody in that room had actually managed to forget. Same face. Everything else, completely different.

Dana stared. "...What is happening right now."

The only answer she got was a whistle, short, cheerful, unmistakable, the exact same handful of notes that had closed out every single commercial that build had ever starred in, coming from everywhere in the room and nowhere in it at the same time.

ROB-Helen let the pose hold a moment longer than it needed to, entirely pleased with herself, before dropping straight back into serious.

"Do you remember what she used to look like? Before all of this?" She wasn't looking at Harry anymore. She was looking at Dana. "Dana. Have you looked in a mirror lately?"

A mirror simply appeared, the way everything in this room seemed to, propped against nothing in particular, angled just right to catch both of them at once.

Dana looked. Kept looking, because the first look didn't seem to have actually landed yet.

Two faces stared back out of the glass. Hers. And, impossibly, right alongside it, a second one that had no business being anywhere near a mirror at all. Faith didn't have a face. Not one anybody could actually see, not once, not in an entire year of this. And yet there it was, and the longer Dana looked, the less she could find the exact line where one face stopped and the other one started.

"...fuuuuuuuu!!"

Faith didn't wait for the mirror to fade. "Okay, but what about me?" Something raw crept into it, none of her usual armor bothering to show up for this part. "It can't just be because I looked like her!"

ROB-Helen turned to face her fully, all the theater gone out of her voice at once.

"You're right. There was madness to my method." A beat, and for once it didn't sound like a line. "Yours was a lesson just as important. You've been defining yourself as the Slayer for far too many years. You needed to find out how strong you were ready to become, even without that backing you up."

Nobody said anything for a beat that stopped being a beat almost immediately and turned into something closer to eternity, the fire the only thing in the whole room still willing to make a sound.

ROB-Helen dusted off her upper legs and stood, brisk about it, like the whole conversation had already wrapped itself up as far as she was concerned. "Well. It's time for everyone to go back home and..."

"Wait." Faith was on her feet before the sentence finished dying. "You can't just screw us over like that, even if you had a reason! I want something!"

ROB-Helen didn't ask what she meant. Didn't need to.

"I don't know..." Drawn out, almost playful, and then she turned toward Dana instead, and something happened to her voice that had nothing to do with the face it was still coming out of, dropping and changing in the same breath into something the whole room recognized on sound alone, the exact cadence of somebody none of them had actually heard speak for herself in this entry before tonight.

"ARE YOU READY TO BE STRONG?!"

Dana blinked. "What? Huh?" A beat, still catching up. "Well... yeah, I guess."

Something shifted through her the instant the words left her mouth. Her spine straightened without her telling it to. For one brief second she looked almost feral, shoulders dropped, weight settling low and ready into her own feet like her body had just remembered something her mind hadn't caught up to yet. Then it passed, and what was left behind wasn't feral at all. Just poised. Certain. Steady in a way she'd never once been before tonight.

"There." ROB-Helen studied her own handiwork, entirely pleased with herself. "I even gave her a little oomph."

Something moved behind Dana's eyes for just a fraction of a second, old and vast and nothing at all like a fifteen-year-old girl from Virginia, gone again before anyone could be fully sure they'd actually seen it.

"Now." ROB-Helen straightened, brisk again, like the whole conversation really was finished this time. "It's time for everyone to go home."

She raised her hand and snapped her fingers a second time.

The screen went bright white, blinding, total, for one whole second.

Then it faded to black.
