Chapter 2 of 16

Loose Lips

"Faith Lehane" (POV):

Dark, and then not dark, and then a room that hadn't existed a half a second ago and now looked like it had always been there, the particular kind of wrong that didn't announce itself so much as sit there daring you to notice.

Six doors. A coffee machine offering something labeled, in cheerful block letters, PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE. Diplomas on the wall from universities that didn't exist anywhere real. Faith took in exactly none of it consciously, the part of her brain that catalogued rooms for exits and threats running well ahead of the part that might have found any of it strange, and she was already moving before she'd finished arriving, some instinct three steps ahead of thought the way it always was when the floor changed underneath her without asking first.

Another person, standing there like they'd been waiting.

She had them by the front of whatever they were wearing and up against the nearest wall before either of them said a word, feet planted, weight set, every year of Slayer training doing exactly what it was built to do the second the world stopped making sense.

Somewhere, somebody hit a pause button that had no business existing inside a movie theater, and the whole scene froze dead: a woman in a little black dress, six inches off the ground, skirt caught mid-swing, an expression of pure professional affront on a face that, in real life, had never once been handled like a full-grown Marine.

"...I'm keeping this," a voice said, appreciative, from somewhere in the anonymous rows. "This is going on a wall somewhere."

"Put it back," Fury said, unimpressed.

"Somebody's framing that."

"Who's the hottie?" a new voice asked, apparently unbothered by having just watched Faith Lehane put a full-grown person through a wall with one hand.

"Focus, people. We're in the middle of something."

"I am focused. I'm focused on the hottie."

"There's always one," Gladly muttered, not quite under his breath. "Every entry, there's always one guy who just tunes out the actual plot the second somebody shows up in a dress."

"I can multitask."

A ripple of agreement went through the anonymous rows, entirely too enthusiastic about it, and for a moment the whole gallery sounded less like an audience and more like last call at a bar nobody remembered choosing to go to.

"Little black dress, though. Somebody's got taste."

"And who taught her how to walk in those heels?"

"Hey, I recognize that dress." A different voice, tilting its head at the frozen image. "I've seen her wear it out. Dinner, maybe? Something like that." A pause. "It's supposed to have way more going on, though. Big poufy sleeves, frills all down the front. This is like the stripped-down version."

"And the hair's doing something specific. That's not just 'got ready in a hurry,' that's an actual look. Somebody styled that on purpose."

"Somebody styled it in about ninety seconds, tops." A different voice, closer to the screen, squinting. "Look at the hair, it's still wet. And the dress has got little strings hanging off it, like the tags just got ripped off and nobody had time to check for stragglers."

"Thought you said you'd seen her in this before."

"I have. Never with tags still on it."

"...somebody changed mid-scene."

"That's not a ROB thing." A voice near the back, matter-of-fact about it. "That's not even a costume glitch. She had on this whole fancy number when she left the room, poufy sleeves, the works, and ditched it about halfway down the hall. Had the stripped-down version on underneath it the entire time."

"That's not a costume change. That's the exact look right after she made herself hot in about six feet of space outside a hotel room door: four minutes' warning, one hand fighting a stocking off, the other hand finding a rhinestone garter that had no business surviving a dead sprint, and whatever was on the hall table under the mirror."

"Hey. I recognize that look." The same voice from the back, closer to the screen than a second ago. "There's tape of that look."

"...you watched it?"

"Course I watched it. Wasn't gonna not watch it." No shame in the delivery at all, just a plain statement of fact. "Doesn't mean I should have. Doesn't mean I didn't."

"That's ROB, genius."

"...ROB's a snack?"

"ROB is not a..." Fury caught himself, visibly regrouping. "It's complicated. And frankly none of your business."

The same voice again, slower this time, like the pieces were only now finishing their landing.

"...wait. That's Helen."

The bar-at-last-call energy died all at once.

"Gib?"

"That's Helen. That's actually Helen. I've had dinner at that woman's house." A pause, long enough to be its own punchline. "I once brought the wrong wine to that woman's house."

"Gibson."

"I'm not doing anything! I'm just saying, this is information I did not need to have about someone I have personally watched carve a turkey." A beat. "Also apparently watched on tape. Unrelated. Bad timing on my part."

Nobody argued the point. Nobody really could.

The pause held a beat longer, just long enough for somebody in the front row to lean in close and squint at the frozen image. "Is that... is that a flower petal? In the hair?"

"That's not on purpose. There's a flower vase back down that hall." A beat. "That's where the water for the hair came from. Dunked her hand in, ran it through, didn't check what came with it. Petal just went along for the ride."

"...and you know that because."

"I watched the tape."

"Nobody asked you to keep watching it."

"Nobody asked me to stop, either."

The scene resumed, unbothered by any of it, the way it always did.

"What the hell! I was about to get my groove on!"

"No you weren't. You're not ready. And Xander knows that."

Faith went still. It had nothing to do with letting go.

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?"

Nothing. No answer, no flicker of acknowledgment that she'd said anything at all. The figure just moved, unbothered by six inches of Slayer strength pinning them to drywall, brushing past the question like it had never been asked, already crossing the room, already gesturing at the six doors with the easy, practiced rhythm of someone who'd given this particular speech more than once before.

"I wanted to leave you completely unfettered for this one. Reasons of my own that will become very clear shortly." A pause, brisk, almost bureaucratic, the tonal opposite of what had just come out of the same mouth thirty seconds earlier. "But I overruled me. I can be such a dick sometimes. I said that we were gonna lock down everything but that smart mouth of yours this time. Wouldn't take no for an answer. Bastard!"

Faith stared. Whatever grip she'd had loosened without her quite deciding to let go of it.

"...the FUCK!?"

"That mouth of yours is gonna get you in trouble some day."

The edges of the room had already started going soft, color bleeding out of the corners first, the whole impossible space folding itself quietly toward dark the way a held breath finally let go. Faith opened her mouth, a dozen better questions lined up behind the one that hadn't landed anywhere, and got exactly none of them out before the last of the light went with everything else.

"...might get you out of trouble too."

The voice followed her down into the dark, unhurried, already sounding like it knew something she didn't, and then there was nothing at all.
