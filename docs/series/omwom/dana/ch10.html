Codename Doris

Harry found ROB-Helen already waiting when he came back through, arms crossed, watching something only she could actually see.

"She's good," he said. Not a question.

"Damned good." ROB-Helen didn't even look over, like she'd been waiting for somebody else to say it first so she wouldn't have to sound like she was bragging. "You know how much actual coaching that kid's had? Real, hands-on, somebody-in-her-head-telling-her-where-to-put-her-feet coaching?"

Harry considered it. "I'd have guessed hours. Days, maybe."

"Ten minutes. Total. Maybe less if you don't count the parts where the passenger's mostly just narrating." She finally turned to look at him, something almost proud in it. "Rest of it, that's just her. Nobody walked her through hiding on a staircase. Nobody taught her which floorboards creak. She's just... good at this. Always was, probably, and never had a reason before now to find out."

"That's not nothing." Harry's voice stayed even, professional, the same register he'd have used evaluating an actual recruit. "Most people freeze the first time somebody explains their own house has blind spots. She didn't freeze once."

"No." ROB-Helen's smile had an edge to it now, fond and a little sharp at the same time. "She really didn't."

Neither of them said anything else about it for a moment. Then ROB-Helen tipped her head, like she'd just remembered whose story was up next.

"Speaking of good at this." She nodded toward the theater at large. "You're about to watch your own wife hold a phone conversation together in front of you without so much as a hitch. Might wanna sit down for that one too."

Harry didn't sit down. He watched anyway.

✳

Dinner was most of the way over, plates picked at and mostly abandoned, when Harry set his fork down and made his voice deliberately, carefully casual.

"So." He watched Helen instead of his food. "Last night must have been pretty exciting for you."

He didn't say what part of the night he meant. He didn't have to. Helen's head came up sharp, spoon still half-buried in the serving dish.

"What?"

Dana kept her eyes on her plate, chewing slow, saying nothing.

Helen recovered fast, faster than the question deserved. "Um, my flat tire was actually a little scary, because the tow-truck driver took such a long time." The words gathered speed and confidence the further she got from the moment she'd been caught flat-footed, and she went right back to serving as she talked, using the motion to paper over everything the first half-second had shown on her face.

Nice recovery, Faith said, quiet, almost admiring in spite of herself. Real nice. Didn't even blink twice.

Harry didn't challenge the story. He lowered his eyes to his own plate, took a bite, chewed. Nothing on his face said he believed her. Nothing said he didn't, either. He'd thrown the question out flat and taken back exactly the rehearsed answer he'd been braced for.

Dana's eyes flicked sideways, quick, then back down. She'd heard the question. She'd watched her mother jump. She'd heard the flat tire show up out of nowhere to explain a jump that hadn't needed explaining, not for anybody who hadn't already been listening for something to go wrong.

Helen gave Harry a small, careful smile, like she could will the subject closed just by looking pleasant enough about it. Harry kept eating. Kept watching. Said nothing either way.

The silence held a beat too long before the phone rang.

Helen's head turned toward the kitchen wall so fast it looked almost like relief. "I'll get it." She was up and moving before either of them could offer.

Harry's head turned to follow her. His body didn't leave the table. Dana stayed where she was, still watching, still saying nothing.

Helen lifted the receiver. "Hello?"

The voice on the other end came through flattened, mechanical, scrubbed of whatever it had actually sounded like underneath.

"Doris."

Helen went very still. For a second her whole face changed, routine dinner-table attention sliding into something wary and exact, and her eyes cut toward Harry without her head turning to follow them, checking whether he was watching.

He was watching. He kept chewing anyway, performing indifference with the same care Helen was performing normal.

"Um... um, yes?"

That's it, Faith said, quiet, sharp. That's the word from the waiting room. Right there.

Dana didn't know what that meant. She filed it away anyway, next to everything else she didn't have room to unpack yet.

Helen waited through another silence, phone pressed close. "Yes?"

Nobody at the table remarked on the strange name or the hesitation in her voice. Dana kept eating. Harry kept chewing. The flattened voice came back.

"Listen carefully."

Helen's whole attention narrowed onto the receiver. The camera of the moment, if there'd been one, would have found nothing else in the room worth looking at.

"In exactly one hour, go to the Hotel Marquis." A pause, Helen's lips parting slightly, brow tightening. "Pick up an envelope marked Doris at the front desk." Another glance toward Harry, quick, controlled. "And dress sexy."

Whatever composure Helen had been holding together came apart on that last line, plain surprise and disbelief breaking straight through the mask.

"What?" She pressed the receiver closer, like proximity might undo the dead air on the other end. "Hello?"

Nothing came back.

Harry looked down at his plate, and the beginning of a smile started somewhere Dana couldn't quite name.

"...if that's not the strangest thing this family's heard all week," somebody in the gallery murmured, and got no argument from anyone.

"Yeah?" another voice cut in. "You didn't see the other side of that phone call."

The big screen flickered, the movie itself shoved aside for a few seconds, and there was Gib, headphones clamped over one ear, a little handheld voice modulator pressed close to his mouth, hunched over a microphone in some dim Omega Sector recording booth.

"...and dress sexy," Gib's own voice said, flattened and metallic, nothing like itself.

He clicked the mic off, yanked the headphones down around his neck, and dropped his face straight into both hands.

"Oh, I'm going to hell."

The screen flicked back to the dinner table like nothing had happened.

✳

Dana caught her mother in the bedroom mirror through the crack of a door left just barely open, and stopped dead in the hallway.

Helen wasn't throwing on whatever was closest for a sick-friend, middle-of-the-night pharmacy run. She had the good dress out, the one that only came off its hanger for actual occasions, and she was studying herself in it with the kind of attention nobody spent on a quick errand, turning slightly one way and then the other, checking the line of it, deciding something about her own hair.

That's not "Allison needs her prescription called in" energy, Faith said, flat.

No, Dana agreed silently. It really isn't.

Helen finished with her hair, gave herself one more look, and reached for her purse.

Don't go in there. Don't say anything. You want to actually know what this is, you gotta let her go do it first.

I wasn't gonna say anything.

Sure you weren't. Just checking.

Dana eased back from the door before Helen could catch movement in the gap, and ghosted down the hall to her own room, listening to the ordinary sounds of her mother getting ready to leave a house she thought nobody was paying attention to.

Okay. Dana kept her voice down to nothing, more thought than whisper. So what do I do.

Okay. Faith's voice, quieter than usual, none of the usual push behind it. You ready to be strong?

"Hey," a strong female voice said from somewhere in the back rows, "that's my line!"

Ready for what?

Finding out what's got your mom acting like a cat on a hot skillet.

Yeah. Dana didn't have to think about it. I... I gotta know. And Mom might need my help.

"Now THAT," a voice boomed out from somewhere in the anonymous rows, delighted, "sounds like the daughter of my best agent!"

"Spence?" Fury, half turning in his seat. "Is that you back there?"

All he got back was a low growl and a fresh cloud of cigar smoke drifting up over the rows.

You follow her. Quiet, careful, and you do not let her clock you, not for one second. She sees you, this whole night's over before it starts.

How am I supposed to follow her without a car?

Quick. Faith again, already thinking two steps past where Dana was standing. Call your boytoy. We're gonna need a lift.

Dana snatched the cordless off its cradle on the nightstand and dialed Trent's number by feel, keeping her voice down to almost nothing once he picked up.

Downstairs, the front door opened and shut, quiet, deliberate, the sound of somebody trying very hard not to sound like they were sneaking out of their own home. A moment later, an engine turned over in the driveway.

Now, Faith said. Move. Stay off the porch light, come out through the side, and don't you dare slam anything.

Dana didn't need telling twice. She set the handset back in its cradle, grabbed her jacket off the back of her door, and went.

✳

Trent picked up on the second ring, groggy, confused, and awake in under ten seconds once Dana got the actual words out. He didn't ask the questions that would have slowed either of them down. He just told her where, and how fast.

He was already idling down the block when she got there, headlight off, engine barely turned over, holding a spare helmet out to her before she'd even finished crossing the yard.

"You gonna tell me what this is?"

"Not right now." Dana pulled the helmet on. "Just don't lose that car up ahead."

Trent didn't lose it. He held back a full block for most of the ride, lights off whenever they could get away with it, closing the gap only when a turn threatened to put too much distance between them, and Dana spent the whole ride with her arms locked around his waist and her eyes fixed on one set of taillights the entire way across town.

He's good at this, Faith said, almost approving.

He's had practice not getting caught. Different reasons, but yeah.

"Maybe this one's a keeper after all," Harry said, from somewhere back in the theater, grudging and a little surprised at himself for meaning it.

The Hotel Marquis rose up out of the dark ahead of them, all glass and valet lights and a doorman who looked entirely too dressed up for eleven o'clock at night. Trent peeled off before the entrance, killing the engine half a block short, and Dana was off the bike and moving before it had even fully stopped rolling.

"Wait here," she said, already walking. "I don't know how long."

She caught the door just as it swung shut behind another guest, slipped through on his heels, and made it into the lobby in time to see her mother crossing toward the front desk, purse held close, moving like a woman who'd rehearsed the walk in her head the whole way over.

Dana held back near a cluster of oversized chairs, doing her best impression of a kid waiting on somebody, and watched Helen lean in at the desk. Words passed she couldn't hear. A clerk produced an envelope from somewhere behind the counter, checked something written on the front of it, and slid it across.

"That's gotta be the worst impression of a kid waiting on somebody I've seen in all my years of spycraft," a voice said, riding out on a fresh cloud of smoke.

"Ahh, whatta you know anyway?" Fury shot back. "You're missing half your visual apparatus!"

"Yeah," somebody else put in, delighted. "Look who's talkin', Fury!"

Marked for her, Faith said. Somebody knew she was coming before she did.

Helen didn't open it there. She tucked it into her purse, crossed to a bank of house phones near the wall, and spoke into one of them for a few seconds too short for Dana to catch anything worth catching.

Then she was moving again, toward the elevators.

Dana angled herself so she wouldn't be standing in a straight line from the doors when they opened, and watched her mother step into a waiting car alone. The elevator's back wall was mirrored floor to ceiling, and for one useful second before the doors slid shut, Dana caught the reflection of the panel Helen's hand had already found, one lit button glowing back at her at an angle nobody inside that car would have thought to hide.

Got it?

Got it.

The doors closed. Dana didn't wait for them to open again. She crossed to the next car over, stepped in alone, and pressed the button for the same floor before she'd even had time to be nervous about doing it.

Helen's doors opened first. Dana caught it through the gap of her own car's closing ones, just a flash: her mother striding out into a bright, pale-colored hallway like a woman with somewhere very specific to be.

Her own doors dinged open two seconds later. Dana eased out slow, hugging the corner of the little vestibule, and looked around it in time to see the whole thing happen.

Helen had stopped at an alcove down the hall, a big mirror mounted over a console table, flowers in a vase off to one side. She dropped her purse onto the console and shrugged the bulky outer coat off her shoulders in one hard motion, not bothering to catch it before it hit the floor, and started taking herself apart.

The sheer sleeves went next, and they didn't come off so much as come apart, Helen's fingers finding the seam and tearing it loose, fabric giving with a small, ugly ripping sound that had nothing gentle in it at all. A pouf of fabric at the neckline went the same way, torn free in one motion, and then she bent down and did the same to the matching pouf at the hem, ripping it loose and leaving a fringe of stray threads and loose strings hanging off the bottom of the dress where it had been stitched on. Underneath all of it was the same black dress Dana had seen her mother wear a dozen times before, except it wasn't, not really, stripped down to something close-fitting and sleeveless that the version hanging in the front hall closet had never once looked like. Helen bent down right there in the hallway and tore her hose down and off, one leg and then the other, straightening back up bare-legged under the short hem like she'd done it a hundred times before, even though every motion said she hadn't.

She's not glamming up, Faith said, quiet, something almost like respect in it. She's field-stripping. Different thing entirely.

Helen checked the fit in the mirror, tugged the dress at the shoulder, the waist, the hem, adjusted the fit at her own chest until it sat the way she needed it to, testing it the way you'd test gear, not an outfit. She found lipstick from somewhere and put it on fast, watching her own reflection change under it. She tore the tags off what was left of the clothes, one after another, and dropped them with the discarded jacket.

Then she reached for the flower vase, wet her fingers, and ran them back through her hair, reshaping it with nothing but water and her own two hands.

Dana didn't move the entire time. Faith timed every shift in her weight for the exact half-second Helen was bent over or turned toward the glass, and Dana never once had to freeze mid-motion.

Helen checked herself a last time, shoulders, posture, the way the dress sat when she turned, made two small corrections nobody but somebody who'd been standing right there would ever have caught, and gathered her things.

Then she started for the door.

It was, without question, the single worst attempt at a sexy walk Dana had ever personally witnessed. Helen's heels caught twice in the first ten feet, her hips overcorrecting hard enough to look like she was recovering from a stumble rather than selling one, one hand going briefly out toward the wall before she caught herself and forced her spine back into something resembling confidence.

"And who," a voice said, striding into the Gallery from somewhere that hadn't existed a second earlier, arms crossed, watching the whole thing with open, delighted disbelief, "taught her how to walk in those heels?"

ROB-Helen didn't wait for an answer. She didn't need one.

"There you go repeating yourself," somebody said. "You said that way back in Chapter Two."

Helen made it to the assigned door, knocked the wobble out of her last two steps through sheer will, and found it already off the latch, unlocked, waiting. She pushed it open into a room gone dark on the other side and stepped through.

...And the door closes.

...And the door closes.

...And the door closes.

Dana stayed exactly where she was until it had been shut a full ten seconds, and the hallway had gone all the way quiet again, before she let herself breathe.
