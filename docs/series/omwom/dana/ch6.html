Cover Story

Mystery Guest walked into the Gallery like he'd done it before, unhurried, easy, the specific comfort of a man who already knew exactly how this room worked.

"Do we know this guy?" somebody in the anonymous rows asked.

"Give it a second," Fury said, not looking up. "We're about to."

"Also, anybody else catch how specific the narration's being about the way this guy walks?" somebody in the anonymous rows asked, not unreasonably.

Mystery Guest didn't so much as pause at the top of the aisle. His eyes found the one face he'd actually come looking for, and he was already moving before the anonymous rows had finished deciding whether to be nervous about him.

"Carlos."

The anonymous rows went very still, all at once, a crowd collectively deciding not to be the next name called.

Carlos, three rows back, went a color that didn't have a name yet.

"Hello again." Mystery Guest's voice stayed pleasant, almost warm, which was somehow so much worse than if it hadn't been. He crossed the last few feet unhurried, hands loose at his sides, every line of him saying he already knew exactly how this was going to go and was, if anything, looking forward to it. "It's been a while."

Carlos opened his mouth. Nothing came out of it, not even air, nothing that was ever going to make it all the way to being a word.

"No, I don't think you remember me." He smiled, and it was a genuinely pleasant smile, and that was the worst part of all. "You will."

Nobody in the anonymous rows breathed.

Carlos's chair creaked, a small, telling sound, and then something gave.

A dark patch spread fast across the front of his pants, and Carlos looked down at it with the flat, resigned horror of a man who had, on some level, been waiting three chapters for exactly this to happen and had simply run out of ways to stop it.

"There it is," somebody said, not unkindly, the way you'd narrate a foregone conclusion.

"Called it back in Chapter Two," another voice added.

"We ALL called it back in Chapter Two."

Mystery Guest looked down at the spreading patch with mild, professional interest, the same look he might've given a printout. "Piss boy," he said, not a question, the nickname landing like he'd already known it the whole time and was only now confirming it out loud.

"OUT OF ORDER," someone read aloud from somewhere, dry as the sign itself, and got a scattered, ugly laugh for it.

Carlos didn't say anything. Carlos, wisely, did not say anything at all.

"We'll talk again," Mystery Guest told him, pleasant as ever, and turned away, already done with him, moving on to the rest of the room.

Mystery Guest crossed to where a woman stood waiting, already halfway into digging something out of a little black clutch that hadn't been there a second ago.

"Hello, Doris."

"Hello, Dutch." She didn't look up from what she was doing. "Feels like old times, huh?"

Mystery Guest stopped walking.

It was a full, honest double-take, the kind that belonged to a man who'd walked in expecting an easy, familiar face and gotten one, just not for any reason he could have wanted. ROB-Helen. His wife's own face, down to the last detail, producing a very familiar tube of lipstick like she had all the time in the world.

She took her time putting it on, clearly enjoying every second of his stalled-out silence, checking the result in a compact mirror that also hadn't been there a moment ago.

"What?" ROB-Helen capped the lipstick and finally deigned to look at him properly. "You didn't think I knew? I'M OMNIPOTENT!"

"Did he seriously just get out-quipped by God herself?" someone in the anonymous rows said, delighted.

"Welcome to the club," Fury said.

"Wait." Gib, sitting up straighter, the same slow, two-step recognition he'd had back in Chapter Two, dawning a beat behind everyone else's laughter. "I know that guy too. That's Harry Tasker."

"'Course you do," somebody muttered. "Man's had dinner at everybody's house, apparently."

Harry didn't so much as glance their way, recovering fast, the professional kind of fast, and let the whole exchange go without acknowledging it out loud, the same discipline that probably kept him alive doing whatever he actually did for a living. Point made, mystery solved, he turned and walked off toward the rest of the Gallery without waiting for a response from anyone.

✳

The curtain moved an inch, then stopped.

Dana had gotten good at this part, the not-all-at-once part, easing the fabric back just far enough to see the street without giving the street anything to see back. She'd left the lights off, too, on purpose, the way she always did for this. Her room behind her was doing its usual impression of a disaster nobody had asked it to clean up: clothes on the floor where they'd landed, a binder open to homework she hadn't started, a mug from three days ago holding down a stack of nothing important, all of it reduced to shapes and shadows in the dark. Not exactly a lookout post. Just her room, same as always, with a girl standing at the window in it a little longer than usual tonight.

You've done this before, Faith said, not really a question.

Couple times. Dana didn't look away from the street. Not, like, a hobby. Just... sometimes I want to know when he's actually home before he wants me to know.

A car eased to the curb below, dark, unremarkable, a car that could have belonged to anybody. It didn't belong to anybody. Dana watched the passenger door open and her father climb out of it, and watched the car pull away again before he'd even made it up the front walk, no goodbye, no wave, gone the second its job was done.

Huh, Faith said. That's not a carpool.

That's Dad.

Didn't say it wasn't. Said it's not a carpool. Nobody drops off a guy that fast unless dropping him off was the whole point of the trip.

Harry moved quick across the lawn, quicker than a tired salesman coming home from a trade show had any business moving, head down, a glance back over one shoulder that lasted half a second and covered the whole street. Then the front door, and something in his shoulders let go all at once, the fast version of him folding itself away somewhere before he was even all the way inside.

That, Faith said. Right there. Watch that.

Watch what? He just went in the house.

Watch how fast he stopped being the guy who just got dropped off by a car with no plates I could read, and started being the guy with the tired computer-salesman walk. That's not tired catching up to him. That's a costume going on.

Dana let the curtain fall the rest of the way, careful, no snap to it, and crossed her room in sock feet to ease her door open the same way. The hall outside was dark and quiet, the house settled into whatever late-night version of itself it became once nobody was awake to perform for.

Stairs creak second and sixth step, Faith said. You know that already, probably. Keep your weight toward the wall, banister side. Breaks up your shape if anybody looks up.

I know how to sneak around my own house.

Didn't say you didn't. Just don't get lazy 'cause it's your own house. That's when people get caught.

Dana went down the way she'd gone down a hundred times before, quieter this time, stopping two steps from the bottom where the wall and the staircase gave her enough cover that she'd have to actually be looked for to be found. From there she could see most of the dining room without stepping into the light of it.

Her mother was asleep at the table.

Dana hadn't expected that. Some part of her had braced for a fight, the kind that happened in low voices after she was supposed to be in bed, the kind she usually only caught the tail end of. What she got instead was quieter and somehow worse: the whole birthday party still sitting there gone stale around her mom, plates nobody had cleared, a half-collapsed stack of napkins, one deflated balloon drooping off the back of a chair like it had given up hours ago same as everyone else had. Helen's head was tipped against her own shoulder, arms crossed on the table in front of her, out cold in the middle of the wreckage of a party her husband hadn't shown up for.

Faith?

Yeah. I see it.

Neither of them said anything else for a second. There wasn't really anything else to say.

Harry came in from the front hall a moment later, and Dana watched every last trace of the fast, sharp-eyed man from the driveway disappear off him the second he saw his wife asleep at the table. He didn't call her name across the room. He crossed it instead, slow, careful, the way you'd approach something you were afraid of waking wrong.

"Hey." Quiet, right at her shoulder now, one hand resting light against her arm. "Hey, Helen."

Helen came awake the way people did when they'd been fighting sleep for hours and finally lost, disoriented first, then embarrassed, then something harder underneath both of those that she didn't quite let all the way onto her face.

"You're home." Not a question either.

"I'm sorry." Harry crouched down next to her chair instead of standing over her. "I got here as fast as I could."

"I know." Helen didn't ask him where here had been coming from. Dana noticed that, filed it away next to everything else she'd been filing away lately.

"I'm sorry I missed it." He looked around at the party's remains like he was only now taking in the size of what he'd missed, streamers, wrapping paper, a cake with one slice cut out of it and the rest gone soft and untouched. "Thank you for doing all this."

"It was good." Helen said it like she meant it, and also like the meaning it cost her something. "She had a good time. Before she noticed you weren't here, anyway."

That's the whole marriage right there, Faith said, quiet, no joke waiting to land underneath it. Two people being real gentle with each other and still not telling each other one true thing.

He's not, like, a bad dad. Dana didn't know why she felt like she had to say it, even silently, even to a voice that hadn't accused him of anything.

Didn't say he was. Said he's gentle and he's lying, both, same guy, same minute. Seen it before. Doesn't automatically make him a bad guy. Just means there's a version of him you're not getting to see, and it's a big one.

Harry reached up and brushed a piece of hair back from Helen's face, and for a second neither of them said anything at all, just sat there in the ruin of a party neither of them had actually wanted to be the reason it went badly. Dana watched from the stairs, close enough to hear every word of it and far enough that neither of them so much as glanced her way.

She thought about going the rest of the way down. Making some noise, letting them know she was awake, turning the whole thing into an ordinary late-night kitchen scene instead of whatever this actually was. She didn't do it. She wasn't sure yet why she didn't do it, only that some instinct she hadn't had a week ago told her the truest version of what she was watching only stayed true as long as nobody knew she was watching it.

Good call, Faith said, like she'd heard the thought form. For what it's worth.

You don't know that it's a good call. You don't know anything about this family.

Don't know the specifics, no. A beat. Know the shape of it, though. Two people hiding something from each other and both of 'em still showing up anyway. That shape, I know real well.

Dana didn't ask what she meant by that. She was starting to learn that Faith answered more when nobody made her.

Downstairs, Harry got Helen to her feet, one arm around her, and steered her gently toward the stairs, talking low about getting her to bed, about cleaning the rest of this up in the morning, about how tomorrow could just be tomorrow. Dana slipped back up ahead of them, fast and quiet, and was behind her own door with it clicked shut a full ten seconds before her parents' footsteps started on the stairs below.

She stood there a while in the dark, back against the door, listening to them pass her room and go on down the hall to their own.

So, she thought, once the house had gone properly quiet again. What was that. All of it. The car, the running, the whole switching-costumes thing.

Don't know yet. No hedge in it, same flat honesty as everything else Faith had given her so far. Know it's not insurance sales. Past that, I got nothing solid. Just a lot of pieces that don't fit the story he tells at breakfast.

Great. Dana sat down on the edge of her bed instead of getting into it, not ready yet to just lie there in the dark with this. So he's just... always like that? The whole switch, car to couch?

Every night, probably not. First time doing it, definitely not. Faith didn't have to think hard about that one. Nobody moves like that the first time they try it. That's practiced. Guy's done that switch enough times he doesn't have to think about doing it anymore.

Dana pulled her knees up, arms wrapped around them, staring at nothing in the dark. "Is Mom in on it?"

No. No hedge in it at all this time, nothing like the last answer. Didn't need a second look for that one. You want it straight, those two aren't covering for each other. They're covering for themselves. Separately. From each other. Different secrets, same table.

"How do you even know that."

Same way I know when somebody's telling me the truth in a fight and somebody's just trying to get through the next thirty seconds alive. Practice. A beat, dry. Could've called that one blindfolded, kid. Your mom wasn't performing for him tonight. She was just tired and hurt and doing her best not to make it his problem. That's not somebody keeping a secret with him. That's somebody keeping one from him.

"Called it," a voice in the gallery said, quiet, not even smug about it. "Back in the bedroom scene. 'How was your day.' Two people saying a whole lot of nothing to each other and neither one of 'em noticing. It was right there the whole time."

"Kind of thing you only really see once you already know how it ends," Fury said, not looking up. "Nobody wanted to be the one to say it out loud back then."

"So he's hiding something from her, and she's hiding something from him, and neither one of them knows the other one's doing it."

That's my read. A beat. Not gonna pretend I've got the rest of it figured. But that part, yeah. I'd bet on that part.

"So I just watched my dad lie to my mom's face and be, like, genuinely sweet about it at the same time."

Yeah. No softening it. That's about the size of it.

Dana pressed the heels of her hands against her eyes, hard enough to see color behind them. "I don't know what to do with that."

Don't gotta do anything with it tonight. Just keep your eyes open tomorrow. Something in Faith's voice shifted, not gentler exactly, just steadier, like she'd found her footing on ground she actually recognized. Seen my share of people running two versions of themselves for people who loved them. Not gonna pretend I know exactly what your dad's version is. But I know it eats at you, carrying two of you around at once. Whatever he's doing, it's not free. Not for him, not for her.

"You say that like you know from somewhere."

Everybody I've ever really mattered to has gotten the short version of me at some point. A beat, and Dana could feel the door easing shut on it before it had even finished opening. Not talking about that tonight either, though. One family's worth of secrets is plenty for one night.

Dana let it go, too tired to chase it, and lay back against her pillow instead, staring up at the same ceiling she'd stared up at every night of her life, in a house that had gotten a little less like the one she thought she'd grown up in somewhere in the last hour.

"I have to go back down there tomorrow and act normal. Sit at the table. Eat dinner. Pretend I didn't see any of this."

Yeah. Faith didn't dress that up either. That part doesn't get easier just 'cause you know more. Sometimes it gets harder.

"Awesome."

Get some sleep. Whatever tomorrow night's gonna be, you'll do better at it rested than you will running tonight all over again in your head.

Dana didn't answer that, just let her eyes close, too full up on everything she'd seen to sort one more piece of it into place tonight.

Somewhere below her, a party that had never quite happened waited to be cleaned up in the morning, and two parents who loved each other lay down next to each other without saying one true thing about where either of them had actually spent the day.
