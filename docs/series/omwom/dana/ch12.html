Home First

Dana made it to the elevator on legs that didn't feel entirely like her own, jabbed the button, and stood there shaking while it took its time arriving.

By the time the doors opened, her breath had started coming in short, ragged pulls that weren't getting enough air down no matter how many of them she took.

Hey. Hey. Look at me, well, listen to me, same thing. Faith's voice didn't rise to meet the panic. If anything it went quieter, steadier, deliberately unhurried, nothing about the pace admitting how little time they actually had. You're hyperventilating. It's not gonna kill you, but it will slow you down, and we don't have the minutes to spare for that right now. In through the nose. Four count. Hold it. Out slow.

I can't...

You can. Do it with me. In.

Dana did it, badly, the first time, air catching halfway down like her own body didn't believe her.

Again. In.

The elevator doors closed. The car started down.

Hold it. There you go. Out slow, don't dump it all at once.

By the second floor her hands had stopped shaking quite so hard. By the fourth, the ragged edge was mostly gone from her breathing, replaced by something still too fast but at least even, in and out, in and out, Faith counting her through every single cycle of it like it was the only thing either of them had to do all night.

Good. Faith didn't make a big deal of it once it actually worked. That's enough to work with. Rest of the panicking, like I said, you get to have later. Full breakdown, your choice, soon as your parents aren't in a van somewhere.

Okay.

Okay, Faith agreed. Now let's go get that plate.

The doors opened on the garage level, and by every measure that mattered, less than two minutes had passed since the door had gone in on the third fist.

She was already too late.

The garage hit her all at once, exposed pipes and fluorescent fixtures casting everything in a cold blue-green, a wall sign pointing one way for FIRST AID and another for PERSONNEL SECURITY like this was any ordinary night in any ordinary building. A white van sat waiting with its side door already open.

Harry went in first, bent hard at the waist, shoved through the opening by the leather-jacketed man before he'd even finished folding down for it. Helen came right behind him, forced in beside him, the burgundy-jacketed man keeping one hand locked on her the whole way and never once lowering the pistol in the other.

Dana's eyes went looking for Eye Guy on instinct, the one face in this whole night she genuinely did not want finding hers.

He was already inside the van. Already through the door, already swallowed up by the dark of it, never so much as glancing back at the garage he was leaving behind. He hadn't needed to. His whole crew was already loaded, already his problem to manage from here, and the hallway behind him had stopped being worth a second look the moment he'd gotten what he came for.

The last man in slammed the side door shut.

Dana was moving before it had even finished closing, but the van was already ahead of her, engine climbing hard, tires shrieking once against the concrete as it wheeled toward the ramp and the flat gray daylight beyond it.

She got less than a step. Didn't even clear the elevator.

It was gone before she was close enough to read a single number off the plate.

Dana stood right where she'd stopped, one foot still holding the elevator door from closing, chest still not quite done heaving, hands empty, nothing to show for the two most important minutes of her entire life. She hadn't even made it out into the garage. She hadn't made it anywhere.

Faith?

Yeah.

I didn't get it.

I know, kid. No blame in it. Just the plain fact of it, laid out flat, same as everything else Faith had ever told her. I know.

Her foot slipped back. The doors slid shut around her, and something in the sound of it, small, final, ordinary, was the thing that actually broke her.

Dana went down where she stood, knees first, then the rest of her, back sliding down the elevator's own cold metal wall until she hit the floor of the car that had never actually let her out.

What do I do. What do I do, what do I do, how could I not, why didn't I just, I should have, I could have, where are they taking them, why do they even want Mom, what does any of this, why did Dad, why didn't he, what do I DO...

Breathe. Faith, steady, right on top of it, not waiting for a gap to fit the word into. Just breathe.

There was nothing you could do. Again, right through the next wave of it. There was never anything you could do.

...why is this happening, why my family, why now, what if they, what if I never, what do I even...

You saw your dad clock your presence. Faith didn't raise her voice. She just kept saying the true things, over and over, patient as a tide. He turned away for a reason. That was him keeping you safe, on purpose, in the middle of all of it.

...I don't know what to do I don't know what to do I don't...

There are still options. Steady. Unbothered by the fact that Dana clearly wasn't hearing a word of it yet. You're not out of moves. You're not out of anything. Breathe. There are still options.

It took a long time. Faith never once stopped saying it.

Let's get you home, Faith said, eventually, once some of it had actually started to land. Nothing else you can do here. You need to let this settle, or you're gonna be a wreck right when they need you most.

I can't just leave. I have to do something.

Do what. Chase the van on foot? Stand around a hotel garage asking strangers if they caught a plate number? There's nothing left here, kid. Not anymore.

You're not telling me something.

A pause. Longer than it should have been.

Get home first.

Faith.

Home. First.

She didn't get anything else out of her, no matter how many times she asked on the way back up.

Trent was still exactly where she'd left him, idling half a block down, and one look at her face told him everything he needed to know and nothing he could actually name.

He was off the bike before she'd made it halfway to him, both hands already reaching. "What happened, are you..."

He didn't finish it. Something about her face stopped him halfway through the question, and instead of asking again he just pulled her in and held on.

"Take me home," Dana said, into his shoulder, and nothing else.

He didn't say a word back. He got her onto the bike and rode, and she held on the entire way, arms locked around him, and couldn't have said afterward whether she'd been holding onto him or onto the last thread of herself. Probably both.

He walked her as far as the porch. Said something quiet she didn't really hear. Let her go up alone when she didn't ask him to come any further.

The stairs took longer than they should have. So did the bathroom, water she barely felt running over hands that wouldn't quite stop shaking, like washing her face could do anything at all about what she'd actually seen tonight. So did the walk from there to her own bed, where she finally lay down and stopped moving altogether.

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery came apart all at once.

"They TOOK them?"

"Both of them? At the same time?"

"Somebody explain to me how a fifteen-year-old ends up alone in a parking garage after THAT and we're all just sitting here..."

"She didn't get the plate!"

"I know she didn't get the plate, I was THERE, I'm not asking for a recap..."

"Somebody get Fury. Somebody get Trilby. Somebody get ANYBODY who's actually done this before, because I don't know what happens next and I don't think anybody else in this room does either..."

Voices piled on top of voices, nobody actually listening to the one before it, the whole theater dissolving into noise that wasn't riffing anymore, wasn't commentary, wasn't anything the Gallery usually let itself become.

Harry came up out of his seat in the middle of it, loud enough to cut straight through everything else in the room.

"What the HELL, Doris?" He wasn't looking for anyone in particular, or he was looking for everyone at once, it came to the same thing. "How could you put your own daughter through that? What fucking right does Faith have to put her in that situation? Why is she even in that hallway?"

"Because she chose to be." ROB-Helen, not raising her voice at all, not needing to. "That's the whole point of all of this."

It didn't calm him down. Nothing about him settled, nothing about the fury went anywhere at all, it just had nowhere left to go once she'd said it, because she was right and he knew it and being right didn't make him feel one degree less furious about any of it. He stood there a second longer with his mouth still half open around the next thing he'd been about to say, and didn't say it, because there wasn't a next thing that actually answered that.

Somewhere underneath the rest of the noise, one voice didn't shout at all, even though it wasn't remotely calm underneath.

"Get her some sleep first," Harry said, low, the words coming out flat and controlled, every last bit of him spent holding it there instead of letting it go anywhere else. "She's gonna need it."

Nobody argued with him either.
