Speak Into the Purse

Dana stayed folded behind the stairs while the rest of dinner wound down around her, nothing in it worth the risk of moving. Helen came back through with the dessert plates. Harry pushed his chair back. Ordinary sounds, cabinet doors and running water, the whole unremarkable business of a kitchen closing up for the night.

By the time Helen went upstairs, she'd traded her dinner clothes for sweats, and Dana trailed her at a careful distance, close enough to hear the exercise bike start up in the room at the end of the hall, that steady mechanical whir that meant her mother would be up there a while working off whatever the evening had left in her. Dana found her own patch of dark on the landing and waited it out.

Minutes went by. The bike kept going, faster in places, slower in others, and somewhere under it Dana caught the sound of her mother breathing hard, working up a real sweat over nothing anybody in this house had said out loud yet.

Harry came up the stairs while she was still on it.

There was no doorway here worth risking, no gap to ease an eye around, just a door standing most of the way open onto a room with nowhere in it for a person to hide. Dana stayed back in the hall instead, pressed to the wall short of the frame, and let her ears do what her eyes couldn't.

"I thought that we might have lunch together tomorrow."

The bike's rhythm faltered, just slightly. "Um, you know, darn it, I'm gonna meet Allison." Helen's voice, a little breathless. "We're gonna go shopping. Sorry."

A pause, a beat longer than the answer really needed. "No problem."

He already knew what she was gonna say, Faith said, quiet. Didn't need to see his face for that one. Guy who actually wanted lunch doesn't let a shopping trip go that easy.

The bike kept turning behind the door, slower now, winding down. Dana couldn't see him crossing the room, but she heard it, the particular quiet of somebody who already knew exactly where he was going and didn't need to look for it. Then nothing, a stillness that had a shape to it even through a wall, and Faith was already moving before Dana had worked out why.

Move. Now. He's got it, and he's headed for the bathroom, and there's a window in there that's gonna matter a lot more than anything happening in this hallway.

Got what?

Purse was sitting right there on the dresser. Straight line from that door to the bathroom one. Man didn't even have to slow down for it.

Dana didn't waste time asking how Faith knew that without seeing it either. She backed off down the hall, quiet, fast, hunting for anything that looked out over that side of the house, and found it two doors down, a window in a room she'd never once had a reason to go into, angled just right to catch that whole stretch of wall.

She eased the sash up an inch, just enough, and found the angle in time.

Below, the bathroom window opened. Harry leaned out into the rain with the purse held by its strap, lowering it down into the dark.

A raincoat was already waiting for it.

Gib. Hood up, shoulders hunched against the weather, one hand coming up to take the bag the second it was in reach. Harry didn't let go until he had it.

Nobody said a single word the whole exchange. Harry let the strap go once Gib had it secure, tapped his watch, and held up a single finger. Gib gave nothing back, just turned and walked off along the side of the house with the purse tucked in against his coat, unhurried, just a guy out in bad weather for reasons of his own. Harry pulled the window shut behind him.

"That's a whole silent-handoff language and nobody's ever gonna teach it to the rest of us," somebody muttered from the anonymous rows.

"You don't need it," Fury said. "You've got a mouth. Use it like everybody else has to."

Where's he going?

Guessing there's a van somewhere close by with his name on it. Guys like that don't walk far in the rain unless they've got a ride waiting.

Dana lost him past the hedge line and pulled back from the window, crossing back toward the stairs the way she'd come. She'd barely made it down to the front hall when the side door opened again below her, and this time it was Harry, Gizmo's leash wound twice around one fist, hauling the dog out into the wet dark with a determined stride that had nothing to do with a bathroom break.

Gizmo didn't want to keep up. Harry didn't particularly care. Every time the dog's pace fell behind his own, a short jerk on the leash brought him forward again, Harry's eyes already fixed somewhere past the yard, somewhere the dog wasn't the actual point of the walk at all.

Dana slipped out after them at a distance, keeping to the dark side of the house, all the way around to the front, to the one window closest to the street. She eased the sash up an inch and pressed close.

Voices, low, indistinct, mostly lost under the rain on the glass. She caught the shape of a laugh that was probably Gib's, a longer stretch of something that might have been an inventory of something, numbers or names she couldn't make out.

Then, clear enough to survive the distance, clear enough that she'd remember it long after she'd forgotten most of the rest of that night:

"...if you need to talk, speak into the purse."

Dana eased the window shut, slow, careful not to let it catch.

Faith?

Yeah, I heard it.

What does that even mean?

Means your mom's purse isn't just a purse anymore, kid. A beat. Means somebody's about to be listening to a lot more than shopping trips with Allison.

Dana didn't remember most of the walk back to her own room. She was in bed, lights off, staring at the same ceiling as every other night of her life, before any of it caught up with her enough to actually say something.

But why?

Don't whine like that. You're not two. A beat, softer than the words themselves. Anyway. Like an old mentor of mine used to say, methinks the game is afoot.

"My word," a voice in the gallery said, delighted, taking off his glasses to polish them on nothing in particular. "She remembered something."

What does that mean?

Means one of your parents is far from who you think they are. And damned good at hiding it. Faith didn't dress it up any further than that. Now get some sleep. Got a feeling you're gonna need it tomorrow. No matter what happens.

Dana closed her eyes and, somehow, eventually, slept.

"Harry Tasker" (POV):

Gib's voice came in before the picture did, transcripts already crossing the space between them.

"Hey. Because it's you, I got today's transcripts."

Harry took the stack one-handed, kept his other hand on the wheel, and started reading while the streetlights slid past the windshield.

"Not much going on." He turned a page. "Nothing from Simon?"

"Um, no."

Gib faced forward and let the silence sit there while Harry kept working through the stack, pages bunching against the top of the steering wheel.

The GMC crossed a rain-slick intersection and pulled to the curb. Harry got out with the transcripts still in hand. Gib came around from the other side.

"My turn to drive?"

Harry held up the stack instead of answering that. "Give me the page."

"What are you talking about?"

"It jumps from page nine to page eleven." Harry's voice stayed level, which was somehow worse than if it hadn't. "Where's page ten?"

"It's gotta be a typo."

Harry crowded him back against the side of the vehicle.

"Give me the goddamn page!"

His right arm came up as the last word left his mouth and drove backward through the GMC's side window without so much as a glance to aim it. The safety glass went white all at once, a sharp, ugly crash, fragments spraying out through the opening behind him.

He lowered his arm. His eyes never left Gib's face.

"That's a security deposit gone," somebody said, into a silence nobody else in the anonymous rows was in a hurry to fill.

"Man doesn't know his own strength," Fury said, "or he knows it exactly and just doesn't care. Never did work out which one's scarier."

Gib looked down at the papers in his hands.

"Okay."

He dug out the sheet he'd been sitting on, crumpled from however long he'd been holding it back, and handed it over. Harry unfolded it, forcing the creases flat with both thumbs.

"Seek help, Harry."

Harry didn't answer that either. His wedding band caught the light as his thumbs spread the page open.

"Hello?"

Helen's voice, thin and tinny, off the page itself, the transcript becoming the call it had recorded.

Simon sat behind the desk in his cramped little used-car office, wood paneling on every wall, a corded phone pinned to his ear with one shoulder while his free hand drifted toward the small air pistol sitting within reach.

"Helen?" A pause, timed for effect. "I need your help."

He lifted the Daisy and sighted down it one-handed, playing at something he'd clearly rehearsed alone more than once.

"Can you meet me tonight?"

"What's happened?" Helen's voice, from her own office now, receiver pressed close.

"It's serious."

Simon kept the receiver wedged against his ear and extended his gun arm across the room, aiming at a paper silhouette taped straight to the wall beside the blinds, some old car calendar hanging underneath it like nobody had ever bothered giving him a real target.

"Meet me on K Street, under the Key Bridge. Eight o'clock sharp."

He let the receiver drop toward the desk without lowering the pistol, and squeezed the trigger. The little gun answered with a dry, unimpressive pop. The BB found the paper.

Simon held the pose a second longer, pleased with himself in a way the moment didn't remotely earn.

"Gotcha."

"This man thinks he's James Bond," somebody said, delighted. "He is a man. With a pellet gun. In a used car dealership."

"Let him have it," Fury said. "It's the only backup he's got."

Back on the page in Harry's hands, Simon's voice kept going.

"Shit. It's almost eight o'clock."

Harry's fingers tightened on the paper. Gib watched his face and didn't say anything else.

They didn't discuss it. Harry turned for the driver's side, Gib went around for the other, and they were both back inside before either of them had said a single word about what came next. The shattered window stayed where it was, the only record of the conversation they'd already had without speaking.

The tracker glowed on its little screen between them. The marker hadn't moved.

"She's still at the house."

Cut, in Harry's own head if nowhere else, to the dark dining room he wasn't standing in: Helen's purse, sitting alone on the table, nobody home to notice it.

"The purse is still at the house," Gib said.

Harry stared through the windshield at nothing.

"Shit."

He put the GMC in gear and pulled out into traffic, the broken window letting in a thin, cold whistle of night air the whole way.
