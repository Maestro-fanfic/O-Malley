Chapter 1: Freedom



"Faith Lehane" (POV):

The branch didn't get any lighter for having two people on it, but it went faster, and by the time they'd wrestled it clear of the fence line the worst of the storm had eased off into a steady, ordinary rain, the kind that soaked through a poncho eventually anyway if you stood in it long enough being sincere at each other.

"So," Xander said, wiping mud off his hands and onto jeans that were already a lost cause. "Home-cooked meal. You weren't kidding."

"Told you." Faith led the way up the porch steps, shaking rain out of her hair. "Don't get used to it. I only cook when somebody's earned it hauling a tree around with me."

"I'll take it. High bar, but I'll take it."

Inside, the house smelled like woodsmoke and somebody's abandoned homework, and Deb caught them both in the front hall, a dish towel over one shoulder, openly delighted by the sight of Faith Lehane, still dripping in that same olive-drab poncho, standing on her own welcome mat next to an equally soaked stranger.

"You're doing a face," Deb said. "I've literally never seen that face. What is that face."

"I don't have a face."

*"Enough with 'the face'!"* someone in the anonymous rows said, indignant. *"That's Donna's bit! West Wing did this joke first!"*

*"Nobody's checking, Sorkin. Sit down."*

*"I'm just saying, there's a version of this where somebody owes somebody a residual..."* The voice gave up, muttering something about walk-and-talks, and didn't get another word in for the rest of the scene.

"You've got a face," Deb said, entirely too pleased with herself. "It's new. I'm framing it."

"Go help with dinner or go to your room, those are the options."

Deb went, laughing, dragging a curious sideways look at Xander on her way past that Faith chose not to acknowledge.

Dinner was simple, whatever Faith had on hand made to stretch for two more mouths than she'd planned on, and it went easier than it had any right to, six girls, Faith still in her poncho because she hadn't gotten around to taking it off, and one very soaked stranger, all crowded around one table, Xander somehow already on a first-name basis with half of them by dessert. Faith watched him do it without trying, the same easy, unforced way he did everything, and didn't examine too closely why it made her chest feel tight in a good way instead of a warning one.

Later, dishes done, girls scattered to their rooms, Xander was the one who spotted it, an ad clipped to Faith's own fridge with a magnet, half-buried under grocery lists: the Bijou downtown was running a Sergio Leone triple bill all week, and tonight, of all nights, happened to be the one with the poncho in it.

"Okay," he said, tapping the clipping. "That's not a coincidence. That's the universe handing me material."

"You are not making me go watch a western. In this weather."

"Rain's basically stopped. I'm not making you do anything. I'm just saying, there's a poncho on record, there's a squint on record, and the actual movie is playing forty minutes from now, and it feels weird not to finish the bit."

She was already grabbing her jacket, real this time, before he finished the sentence.

The Bijou sat on the corner of Main and nothing much, a single screen, sticky floors, a marquee that had clearly been running the same revival series for weeks. The lot was nearly empty, most of the town smart enough to stay in on a night like this, and the kid at the concession stand looked personally thrilled to have someone, anyone, to sell popcorn to.

They were still in line for tickets when a woman near the box office window did a double take and lit up like she'd won something. "Xander? Xander Harris?"

"Carrie. Hey." He said it easy enough, but Faith caught the half-second of scrambling underneath it, a man doing quick math on how a conversation was about to go. "Small world."

"Small town, mostly." Carrie's eyes flicked to Faith, curious, cataloging, and back to Xander, waiting.

"Right, yeah." Xander cleared his throat. "Carrie, this is Faith. Carrie's, uh. Old girlfriend. Long story, back before Sunnydale went into the ground. We should probably grab seats before the good ones are gone."

"Good seeing you," Carrie said, already moving off, easy about it, whatever awkwardness there might once have been apparently settled a long time ago on her end.

Faith didn't say anything. She bought her own ticket, exact change, and didn't look at him while she did it, something small and sharp sitting in her chest that she didn't have a name for yet and didn't especially want to go looking for one.

*"Oh, that's not gonna go unpacked,"* Gladly's voice said, delighted, from somewhere in the rows. *"That is a face. Somebody's jealous and doesn't know what to do about it."*

*"Leave it alone,"* Fury said. *"She'll get there on her own time. Everybody does, eventually."*

*"There she is,"* a voice said, somewhere that wasn't anywhere, the way it never was. *"Wondered when we'd get the other side of this one."*

*"Told you,"* another voice said, quieter, satisfied in a way that didn't need to raise itself to be heard. *"That's the one she's gonna keep. Said so myself, a while back now. Nice to see the receipts."*

Neither Faith nor Xander heard a word of it. The house lights were already going down.

The film opened on a dusty nothing town and a man riding into it who didn't say much and didn't need to, and about four minutes in, right on cue, the poncho showed up on screen and Xander made a sound like he'd been personally vindicated by the universe.

"There it is," he said. "That's you. That's the actual guy right there."

"I hate you."

"You wore that exact poncho. Tonight. In front of witnesses."

*"And still wearing it, no less,"* someone in the anonymous rows noted, delighted. *"Didn't even take it off for the movie. This narrator respects the commitment."*

"I will throw you out into that storm."

*"He won't leave,"* Fury's voice noted, dry. *"She won't throw him. Watch."*

*"This narrator has seen approximately four hundred gunfights across eleven prior entries,"* another voice put in, warming to it, *"and this remains, somehow, still the most anatomically confident quick-draw in the genre. Man's clearing leather in under a second in a coat that heavy. Someone buy this stuntman a drink, wherever he is."*

Faith didn't hear any of that either, but she didn't need to, because she and Xander had started doing the exact same bit on their own, low-voiced, heads bent together in the dark.

"Nobody reloads that fast," she said, twenty minutes in, watching somebody empty a six-shooter and immediately have six more shots. "Nobody. I've watched vamps get dusted faster than that gun's supposedly working."

"In fairness, vamps don't usually stop to monologue first."

"Some of 'em do. You'd be amazed."

"Okay, but the real crime," Xander said, "is nobody in this whole town knows how a ricochet works. That bullet should not have hit three separate barrels on its way to that guy's hat."

"You're not wrong."

"I'm never wrong about ricochets. It's a gift. Vaguely useless gift, but a gift."

She caught herself smiling, not the performed one, the other one, the one she didn't usually let out where anybody could clock it, and didn't bother putting it away, not tonight.

Somewhere past the halfway mark, the film slowed down for a stretch that wasn't shootouts, just a man watching a town eat itself from the outside, choosing, for reasons the movie didn't rush to explain, to actually do something about a family getting torn apart by people with more guns than sense. Nothing loud happened. Just a woman on screen, scared and trying not to show it, and a man who noticed anyway.

Faith went quiet.

It wasn't a big thing. Nobody watching from outside would've caught it, probably. Just her jaw doing something it did sometimes, a half a second of her face forgetting to hold its own shape, thinking about six girls asleep down the hall from an empty room that used to have Robin's stuff in it, thinking about whether she was actually any good at this or just too stubborn to admit she might be failing it quietly, one Tuesday at a time, without anybody telling her.

Xander didn't say anything.

He didn't ask what was wrong, didn't reach for her hand, didn't do the thing some people did where they made your one bad second into a whole conversation you hadn't agreed to have. He just shifted an inch closer in the ratty theater seat, close enough that his shoulder was there if she wanted it, and said, low, eyes still mostly on the screen:

"You're doing good with them. The girls. Just so you know I noticed."

That was it. No follow-up. No fishing for what had actually crossed her face. He'd seen something, she was sure of that much, the same way he always seemed to see things nobody else bothered looking for, and instead of chasing it down he'd just handed her the one true thing he actually knew and let her decide what to do with it.

She didn't say anything back. Didn't need to. She let her shoulder find his, and stayed there for the rest of the movie.

*"Huh,"* Gladly's voice said, softer than his usual register. *"That was almost subtle. Since when does he do subtle?"*

*"Since the alternative was making it worse,"* Fury said. *"Some things you don't stand on with a spotlight. You just make sure you're standing there at all."*

By the time the credits rolled, the rain had finally quit, and the two of them walked out past the Bijou's flickering marquee into a night gone properly clear, washed clean, small-town dark with actual stars in it if you bothered to look.

They didn't bother. They talked instead, the whole walk back, about nothing in particular, whether the movie's math on bullets actually held up, whether Deb was going to burn the kitchen down eventually on general principle, whether Cleveland was going to survive one night without him. He told her about a supply run gone sideways, some new Slayer's first real scrap ending in a dumpster and a lot of laughing about it afterward. She told him about the loan officer who still couldn't quite believe a woman running a house full of teenage girls didn't want to talk about a man cosigning anything. Neither of them was in any particular hurry to get where they were going, and neither of them said so out loud.

Faith's house came up on the left eventually, porch light still on, the branch they'd hauled off the fence line that same afternoon still lying where they'd left it in the wet grass. They ended up on the couch anyway once they were inside, damp shoes by the door, an easy quiet settling over the whole house.

"So," Xander said, stretching his legs out, entirely too pleased with himself. "Vindicated. Officially. On the record."

"You are never gonna let the poncho thing go, are you."

"Nope. It's my whole beat now, chronicling you. Working title's 'Clint Eastwood's Rowdy Ohio Years.' It's gonna be huge."

"There is no audience for that."

"There's an audience of one, and she's sitting right here pretending she's not into it."

"God help us all." But she was laughing when she said it, easy, unguarded, nothing underneath it working overtime to keep its shape. He was laughing too, and for a second neither of them said anything else, just sat there in the quiet, the TV off, the house settled and asleep around them, everything about the night still new enough that nothing about it felt safe to assume yet.

She looked at him. He wasn't looking anywhere in particular, just at her, actually, now that she thought about it, and hadn't stopped for a minute.

Faith opened her mouth, working up to asking him the thing she hadn't let herself think all the way through yet, whether he'd want to stick around tonight instead of driving back through whatever the storm had left of the roads, whether that was a thing she was actually ready to ask out loud or just a thing she'd gotten brave enough to almost say.

She never got the words out.

The room went dark around the edges, quiet, gentle, nothing like a fight and nothing like falling asleep, and the last thing Faith Lehane felt, before everything else went with it, was the couch cushion under her and his shoulder still warm against hers and absolutely no idea, yet, that she wasn't going to get to finish that sentence for a very long time.

*"MAN,"* a voice said, somewhere in the anonymous rows, aggrieved on a level that suggested personal investment. *"Cockblocked by an omnipotent being. That's gotta be some kind of record."*

*"Watch it,"* Fury said, but there wasn't much heat behind it, and nobody in the gallery believed for a second he disagreed.



Chapter 2: Loose Lips



"Faith Lehane" (POV):

Dark, and then not dark, and then a room that hadn't existed a half a second ago and now looked like it had always been there, the particular kind of wrong that didn't announce itself so much as sit there daring you to notice.

Six doors. A coffee machine offering something labeled, in cheerful block letters, PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE. Diplomas on the wall from universities that didn't exist anywhere real. Faith took in exactly none of it consciously, the part of her brain that catalogued rooms for exits and threats running well ahead of the part that might have found any of it strange, and she was already moving before she'd finished arriving, some instinct three steps ahead of thought the way it always was when the floor changed underneath her without asking first.

Another person, standing there like they'd been waiting.

She had them by the front of whatever they were wearing and up against the nearest wall before either of them said a word, feet planted, weight set, every year of Slayer training doing exactly what it was built to do the second the world stopped making sense.

Somewhere, somebody hit a pause button that had no business existing inside a movie theater, and the whole scene froze dead: a woman in a little black dress, six inches off the ground, skirt caught mid-swing, an expression of pure professional affront on a face that, in real life, had never once been handled like a full-grown Marine.

*"...I'm keeping this,"* a voice said, appreciative, from somewhere in the anonymous rows. *"This is going on a wall somewhere."*

*"Put it back,"* Fury said, unimpressed.

*"Somebody's framing that."*

*"Who's the hottie?"* a new voice asked, apparently unbothered by having just watched Faith Lehane put a full-grown person through a wall with one hand.

*"Focus, people. We're in the middle of something."*

*"I am focused. I'm focused on the hottie."*

*"There's always one,"* Gladly muttered, not quite under his breath. *"Every entry, there's always one guy who just tunes out the actual plot the second somebody shows up in a dress."*

*"I can multitask."*

A ripple of agreement went through the anonymous rows, entirely too enthusiastic about it, and for a moment the whole gallery sounded less like an audience and more like last call at a bar nobody remembered choosing to go to.

*"Little black dress, though. Somebody's got taste."*

*"And who taught her how to walk in those heels?"*

*"Hey, I recognize that dress."* A different voice, tilting its head at the frozen image. *"I've seen her wear it out. Dinner, maybe? Something like that."* A pause. *"It's supposed to have way more going on, though. Big poufy sleeves, frills all down the front. This is like the stripped-down version."*

*"And the hair's doing something specific. That's not just 'got ready in a hurry,' that's an actual look. Somebody styled that on purpose."*

*"Somebody styled it in about ninety seconds, tops."* A different voice, closer to the screen, squinting. *"Look at the hair, it's still wet. And the dress has got little strings hanging off it, like the tags just got ripped off and nobody had time to check for stragglers."*

*"Thought you said you'd seen her in this before."*

*"I have. Never with tags still on it."*

*"...somebody changed mid-scene."*

*"That's not a ROB thing."* A voice near the back, matter-of-fact about it. *"That's not even a costume glitch. She had on this whole fancy number when she left the room, poufy sleeves, the works, and ditched it about halfway down the hall. Had the stripped-down version on underneath it the entire time."*

*"That's not a costume change. That's the exact look right after she made herself hot in about six feet of space outside a hotel room door: four minutes' warning, one hand fighting a stocking off, the other hand finding a rhinestone garter that had no business surviving a dead sprint, and whatever was on the hall table under the mirror."*

*"Hey. I recognize that look."* The same voice from the back, closer to the screen than a second ago. *"There's tape of that look."*

*"...you watched it?"*

*"Course I watched it. Wasn't gonna not watch it."* No shame in the delivery at all, just a plain statement of fact. *"Doesn't mean I should have. Doesn't mean I didn't."*

*"That's ROB, genius."*

*"...ROB's a snack?"*

*"ROB is not a..."* Fury caught himself, visibly regrouping. *"It's complicated. And frankly none of your business."*

The same voice again, slower this time, like the pieces were only now finishing their landing.

*"...wait. That's Helen."*

The bar-at-last-call energy died all at once.

*"Gib?"*

*"That's Helen. That's actually Helen. I've had dinner at that woman's house."* A pause, long enough to be its own punchline. *"I once brought the wrong wine to that woman's house."*

*"Gibson."*

*"I'm not doing anything! I'm just saying, this is information I did not need to have about someone I have personally watched carve a turkey."* A beat. *"Also apparently watched on tape. Unrelated. Bad timing on my part."*

Nobody argued the point. Nobody really could.

The pause held a beat longer, just long enough for somebody in the front row to lean in close and squint at the frozen image. "Is that... is that a flower petal? In the hair?"

*"That's not on purpose. There's a flower vase back down that hall."* A beat. *"That's where the water for the hair came from. Dunked her hand in, ran it through, didn't check what came with it. Petal just went along for the ride."*

*"...and you know that because."*

*"I watched the tape."*

*"Nobody asked you to keep watching it."*

*"Nobody asked me to stop, either."*

The scene resumed, unbothered by any of it, the way it always did.

"What the hell! I was about to get my groove on!"

"No you weren't. You're not ready. And Xander knows that."

Faith went still. It had nothing to do with letting go.

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?"

Nothing. No answer, no flicker of acknowledgment that she'd said anything at all. The figure just moved, unbothered by six inches of Slayer strength pinning them to drywall, brushing past the question like it had never been asked, already crossing the room, already gesturing at the six doors with the easy, practiced rhythm of someone who'd given this particular speech more than once before.

"I wanted to leave you completely unfettered for this one. Reasons of my own that will become very clear shortly." A pause, brisk, almost bureaucratic, the tonal opposite of what had just come out of the same mouth thirty seconds earlier. "But I overruled me. I can be such a dick sometimes. I said that we were gonna lock down everything but that smart mouth of yours this time. Wouldn't take no for an answer. Bastard!"

Faith stared. Whatever grip she'd had loosened without her quite deciding to let go of it.

"...the FUCK!?"

"That mouth of yours is gonna get you in trouble some day."

The edges of the room had already started going soft, color bleeding out of the corners first, the whole impossible space folding itself quietly toward dark the way a held breath finally let go. Faith opened her mouth, a dozen better questions lined up behind the one that hadn't landed anywhere, and got exactly none of them out before the last of the light went with everything else.

"...might get you out of trouble too."

The voice followed her down into the dark, unhurried, already sounding like it knew something she didn't, and then there was nothing at all.



Chapter 3: Blowback



"Helen Tasker" (POV):

The gallery had no doors that led anywhere real, but it had an entrance all the same, and Helen Tasker came through it stage left, heels loud on a floor that wasn't actually there, scanning the rows like a woman who had already decided exactly who she was looking for.

She found him fast.

Fury didn't even get all the way to his feet before she'd crossed the distance, and the anonymous rows went dead silent, the way a crowd goes quiet right before something worth telling people about later.

At least one voice clocked exactly where this was headed a full two seconds before it happened, and said nothing. Warning Fury wasn't worth becoming the second target in the room. Wiser to just watch.

She hit him dead in the mouth before he'd gotten a single word out, one clean shot, no windup, thrown by a woman who'd clearly had time to think about it.

Fury worked his jaw, tasting blood, and didn't look remotely surprised by any of it. "Hello, Helen."

"Fuck you!"

"Feel better?"

"Don't you dare." Helen rounded on him, voice shaking with something that hadn't decided yet whether it was rage or something closer to grief. "I asked for one thing. One. Keep her hands off my daughter, whatever else happens, whatever it costs. That's all I asked you for, right here, right in front of everybody."

"And we kept it."

"Then explain to me," she said, sweeping an arm to take in the whole gallery, every row of it, "how every single person in this room got a front-row seat to me getting dressed in a hallway thirty years ago."

"You know how we work, Helen. There are always more reasons than you see."

Nobody in the anonymous rows made a sound. Nobody wanted to be first.

"And don't think I don't know what you just said, Gib."

She didn't wait for an answer. She turned on her heel and sat, arms crossed, radiating the specific stillness of a woman who had said everything she'd come to say and was now simply waiting to see who was foolish enough to make her say more.

*"...'the specific stillness of.' Again."* A voice, flat, from the back. *"That's twice this chapter. We're doing this again?"*

*"We are always doing this again,"* Fury said, still working blood out of his teeth. *"Ask Taylor's gallery. Ask literally anyone who's sat through more than one of these."*

*"Somebody send the author a thesaurus."*

*"Somebody send the author a word count that isn't 'specific.'"*

Several rows back, the voice who had spent a significant portion of the previous scene Very Focused On The Hottie folded himself down low enough to nearly vanish beneath the seat in front of him, sweating through what this narrator can only describe as a genuine crisis of bladder control, and did not breathe again for a good long while.

Fury spat, checked his hand for blood, found some, and didn't bother wiping it off. "Somebody take that remote away from Gib before I smack him."

"Sorry, boss," Gib said, to no one Fury could account for, already rubbing the back of his head.

Nobody asked which remote. Nobody needed to.

The scene rolled.



Chapter 4: Petty Cash



It was still snowing inside the little glass dome when Harry set it in her hands, some souvenir village he'd picked up wherever he'd actually been instead of wherever he'd told them he'd been. Dana turned it over once, unimpressed, and said something that passed for thanks. Harry kissed the top of her head anyway, told her to get moving or she'd be late for school, and was gone before the glass had even finished settling.

She looked at it a second longer once he was out of the room, decided it was exactly as lame as it looked, and dropped it straight into the wastebasket on her way past.

Down the hall, Harry sidestepped the dog on his way to the bedroom. "Feed Gizmo before you leave," he called back, already thinking about something else.

Helen was picking out an outfit, hair still wrapped in a towel, and neither of them looked at the other while they talked, the same easy rhythm of two people who'd been doing this every morning for fifteen years.

"I'm late," Harry said.

"Me too."

"How'd it go at the trade show?" Helen asked, pulling a blouse off its hanger. "Bet you made all the other salesmen jealous."

Harry lit up, launching into some elaborate account of a new ordering system, how the second a customer's name went into the computer it pulled their whole credit history, checked what they'd bought before, calculated a discount on the spot, on and on, enthusiasm doing most of the actual lying for him.

Helen had stopped tracking the specifics somewhere around the second sentence. Fifteen years married to the man, and she genuinely had no idea what he actually did all day. It had simply never once occurred to her to wonder harder about it.

"That's fabulous, Harry," she said, not really listening.

"Yeah. It was wild." He watched her brush her teeth, oblivious to him in exactly the same way, and smiled to himself. The best lies were always the ones told with real enthusiasm behind them.

"So how was your day?" Harry asked, once he'd run out of steam.

"Well, we had a leak by the water heater." Helen didn't look up from the closet. "I talked to the plumber. He said it's gonna cost six hundred dollars."

"That's okay."

"That's not okay. That's extortion."

"So what'd you tell him?"

"I slept with him. He knocked off a hundred."

"That's good thinking." Harry, already moving, pecked her on the cheek and was gone before she'd fully turned to kiss him back.

Helen kissed the empty air where he'd been a second ago, and went back to picking out an outfit like it hadn't happened.

By the time Gib let himself in the front door, sunglasses already on indoors for no reason anybody had ever gotten a straight answer out of him about, Gizmo had worked up the nerve to come say hello and immediately regretted it. Gib pointed a finger at him. "Don't."

The dog reconsidered his whole life plan and backed off.

*"That's gotta be the smartest member of the cast right there."*

*"Whole rest of the episode, and he's already tapped out. Smartest decision anybody's gonna make all day."*

*"Somebody get that dog an agent."*

Gib slung his jacket over the back of the couch, then, for reasons that made sense only to him, produced a pack of cigarettes and set it on the mantelpiece across the room, nudging it a careful half inch left, then right, then left again, until it satisfied whatever standard he was holding it to.

In the kitchen, Dana was already working on a pop-tart she hadn't bothered toasting, orange juice straight from the bottle, gone again before Gib had even finished pouring two cups of coffee.

"Hey, kid."

"Hey, Gib."

Harry came in a beat later and Gib handed him the second cup without being asked.

"Thanks, dear."

"Check these out." Gib held out the sunglasses, entirely too pleased with himself.

Harry turned them over, found nothing unusual about them at a glance, and put them on anyway. Somewhere in the corner of the left lens, a small, grainy image of his own living room flickered to life.

"Where's the picture coming from?"

"CCD camera and a transmitter, tucked right inside a pack of smokes." Gib nodded toward the mantel, delighted with himself. "Pretty slick, huh?"

"Mhm." Harry meant it the way a man means anything he's only half listening to.

*"Man, we're starting off early with these. 'The way X means Y.' Chapter's not even half done."*

*"Give the guy a break,"* Fury said, not looking up. *"He's planning, outlining, and re-reading a few thousand words of this stuff every single day. Something's gonna slip through."*

*"Yeah, he couldn't do it at all without a 'certain assistant' tho."*

Through the lens, Dana wandered into frame, glancing back toward the kitchen before she moved.

Her fingers found the edge of the wallet before the rest of her had fully committed to the idea, muscle memory outrunning conscience the way it always did lately. Gib's jacket. She knew it by the beat-up leather and the faint smell of aftershave that had never once in her memory smelled like anything else.

Her fingers closed around the wallet.

The room tilted.

In the half second before it all settled, before the world finished deciding what shape it was going to be, Faith clocked the little lens on the mantle, aimed square at the couch, the kind of thing nobody sets up for no reason.

Professional habit didn't care that she didn't have a body yet. It was the only thing she still had, the last sliver of a window before whatever was coming shut the rest of it down, and she used it exactly the way she'd have used it in her own skin.

She winked at it.

*"Wait."* A voice, indignant. *"That's a breach of the rules right there. We just discussed the rules not five minutes ago!"*

*"Door's still closing,"* Fury said, unbothered. *"Give it a second."*

Through the glasses, Harry watched his own daughter's hand disappear into Gib's jacket and come out with a fistful of twenties.

"Did I just see her wink at the camera?"

"What the hell."

"DANA!"

Harry was already on his feet, glasses off, coffee abandoned, moving for the door with Gib right behind him.

Outside, a motorcycle was already idling in the driveway, Dana halfway across the yard toward it and the gangly, too-cool boy waiting astride it, earbuds in, music cranked loud enough that Harry could hear the tinny buzz of it from the porch, a helmet already going on over the top of them.

"Dana!" Harry hit the porch at a dead run. "Dana, wait!"

Hot coffee he didn't even remember still holding sloshed clean over his hand on the second step, and he didn't so much as slow down for it.

"She's ripping you off." He sounded almost impressed, in spite of himself, in spite of the fact that his own daughter was practically swinging her leg over the bike by the time the words got all the way out.

"I knew it." Gib, catching up a step behind, rueful, already resigned to it, a man for whom getting quietly taken advantage of had apparently become a personal theme. "Same as the beer. Never a goddamn beer in my own fridge, and I've been blaming Carol's boyfriend for a year."

"Can't stop, I'm late!" Dana was already swinging on behind the boy, one arm looping around his waist. "Bye, Dad!"

Piss boy deflated on the spot, turned around, and shuffled back to his seat empty-handed and considerably more uncomfortable than when he'd left it, and did not, this narrator notes, ask the obvious question out loud.



Chapter 5: Need to Know



Dana skipped the bus. Told herself it was because she needed the air, which wasn't exactly a lie, and started walking the long way home instead, backpack slung over one shoulder, streets emptying out around her the further she got from school.

*Okay,* she thought, once the last knot of kids from her bus stop had peeled off toward their own houses and there was nobody left close enough to make talking to herself look insane. *We're doing this properly. Who are you.*

*Told you already. Faith.*

*That's a name. Not an answer.*

A pause, the kind that felt like someone deciding how much rope to give a question.

*Yeah.*

Dana chewed on that for most of a block, long enough that her own house came into view before she'd worked out what to say next.

"Okay." She slowed her pace without meaning to, and this time there was something almost sly in it, turnabout occurring to her all at once. "Your turn. Who were you. Before all this."

Nothing.

"Faith."

*Don't really do that part.*

"That's not fair, I just told you my whole..."

She reached her own driveway a minute later, alone with a voice that wasn't going anywhere, in a house that had absolutely no idea what had actually walked in the front door in her body that afternoon.

The house itself was empty when she let herself in, quiet the way it always was this time of day, her mom's car still gone from the driveway. Helen didn't get home from work until closer to six, some office job Dana had never bothered asking many questions about, the same way she'd never bothered asking many questions about anything, apparently.

*"And THERE's the tic that's out of place."*

*"The assistant can't land 'em all, after all."*

*Yeah. Doesn't mean he's wrong about anything.*

*Depends what you're asking.*

Faith was quiet a second, not a door shutting this time, just someone deciding how to actually answer instead of deflecting it.

Dana turned that over, scratching Gizmo's ear on autopilot. "That's kind of what today's felt like. For me."

*Yeah.* No argument in it. *Guess we've got that much in common already.*

They stayed like that a while, girl and dog and the voice of a dead-tired nineteen-year-old occupying the same quiet room, neither of them in any hurry to be the one who spoke next.

*Huh. Not one piece of leather in that whole closet.*

Nobody in the gallery said a word.

Chapter 6: Cover Story



Mystery Guest walked into the Gallery like he'd done it before, unhurried, easy, the specific comfort of a man who already knew exactly how this room worked.

*"Do we know this guy?"* somebody in the anonymous rows asked.

The curtain moved an inch, then stopped.

Helen waited through another silence, phone pressed close. "Yes?"

Nobody at the table remarked on the strange name or the hesitation in her voice. Dana kept eating. Harry kept chewing. The flattened voice came back.

"Listen carefully."

Helen's whole attention narrowed onto the receiver. The camera of the moment, if there'd been one, would have found nothing else in the room worth looking at.

"In exactly one hour, go to the Hotel Marquis." A pause, Helen's lips parting slightly, brow tightening. "Pick up an envelope marked Doris at the front desk." Another glance toward Harry, quick, controlled. "And dress sexy."

Whatever composure Helen had been holding together came apart on that last line, plain surprise and disbelief breaking straight through the mask.

Nothing came back.

Harry looked down at his plate, and the beginning of a smile started somewhere Dana couldn't quite name.

*"...if that's not the strangest thing this family's heard all week,"* somebody in the gallery murmured, and got no argument from anyone.

*"Yeah?"* another voice cut in. *"You didn't see the other side of that phone call."*

The big screen flickered, the movie itself shoved aside for a few seconds, and there was Gib, headphones clamped over one ear, a little handheld voice modulator pressed close to his mouth, hunched over a microphone in some dim Omega Sector recording booth.

*"...and dress sexy,"* Gib's own voice said, flattened and metallic, nothing like itself.

He clicked the mic off, yanked the headphones down around his neck, and dropped his face straight into both hands.

*"Oh, I'm going to hell."*

The screen flicked back to the dinner table like nothing had happened.

* * *

Dana caught her mother in the bedroom mirror through the crack of a door left just barely open, and stopped dead in the hallway.

Helen wasn't throwing on whatever was closest for a sick-friend, middle-of-the-night pharmacy run. She had the good dress out, the one that only came off its hanger for actual occasions, and she was studying herself in it with the kind of attention nobody spent on a quick errand, turning slightly one way and then the other, checking the line of it, deciding something about her own hair.

*That's not "Allison needs her prescription called in" energy,* Faith said, flat.

*No,* Dana agreed silently. *It really isn't.*

Helen finished with her hair, gave herself one more look, and reached for her purse.

*Don't go in there. Don't say anything. You want to actually know what this is, you gotta let her go do it first.*

*I wasn't gonna say anything.*

*Sure you weren't. Just checking.*

Dana eased back from the door before Helen could catch movement in the gap, and ghosted down the hall to her own room, listening to the ordinary sounds of her mother getting ready to leave a house she thought nobody was paying attention to.

*Okay.* Dana kept her voice down to nothing, more thought than whisper. *So what do I do.*

*Okay.* Faith's voice, quieter than usual, none of the usual push behind it. *You ready to be strong?*

*"Hey,"* a strong female voice said from somewhere in the back rows, *"that's my line!"*

*Ready for what?*

*Finding out what's got your mom acting like a cat on a hot skillet.*

*Yeah.* Dana didn't have to think about it. *I... I gotta know. And Mom might need my help.*

*"Now THAT,"* a voice boomed out from somewhere in the anonymous rows, delighted, *"sounds like the daughter of my best agent!"*

*"Spence?"* Fury, half turning in his seat. *"Is that you back there?"*

All he got back was a low growl and a fresh cloud of cigar smoke drifting up over the rows.

*You follow her. Quiet, careful, and you do not let her clock you, not for one second. She sees you, this whole night's over before it starts.*

*How am I supposed to follow her without a car?*

*Quick.* Faith again, already thinking two steps past where Dana was standing. *Call your boytoy. We're gonna need a lift.*

Dana snatched the cordless off its cradle on the nightstand and dialed Trent's number by feel, keeping her voice down to almost nothing once he picked up.

Downstairs, the front door opened and shut, quiet, deliberate, the sound of somebody trying very hard not to sound like they were sneaking out of their own home. A moment later, an engine turned over in the driveway.

*Now,* Faith said. *Move. Stay off the porch light, come out through the side, and don't you dare slam anything.*

Dana didn't need telling twice. She set the handset back in its cradle, grabbed her jacket off the back of her door, and went.

* * *

Trent picked up on the second ring, groggy, confused, and awake in under ten seconds once Dana got the actual words out. He didn't ask the questions that would have slowed either of them down. He just told her where, and how fast.

He was already idling down the block when she got there, headlight off, engine barely turned over, holding a spare helmet out to her before she'd even finished crossing the yard.

"You gonna tell me what this is?"

"Not right now." Dana pulled the helmet on. "Just don't lose that car up ahead."

Trent didn't lose it. He held back a full block for most of the ride, lights off whenever they could get away with it, closing the gap only when a turn threatened to put too much distance between them, and Dana spent the whole ride with her arms locked around his waist and her eyes fixed on one set of taillights the entire way across town.

*He's good at this,* Faith said, almost approving.

*He's had practice not getting caught. Different reasons, but yeah.*

*"Maybe this one's a keeper after all,"* Harry said, from somewhere back in the theater, grudging and a little surprised at himself for meaning it.

The Hotel Marquis rose up out of the dark ahead of them, all glass and valet lights and a doorman who looked entirely too dressed up for eleven o'clock at night. Trent peeled off before the entrance, killing the engine half a block short, and Dana was off the bike and moving before it had even fully stopped rolling.

"Wait here," she said, already walking. "I don't know how long."

She caught the door just as it swung shut behind another guest, slipped through on his heels, and made it into the lobby in time to see her mother crossing toward the front desk, purse held close, moving like a woman who'd rehearsed the walk in her head the whole way over.

Dana held back near a cluster of oversized chairs, doing her best impression of a kid waiting on somebody, and watched Helen lean in at the desk. Words passed she couldn't hear. A clerk produced an envelope from somewhere behind the counter, checked something written on the front of it, and slid it across.

*"That's gotta be the* worst *impression of a kid waiting on somebody I've seen in all my years of spycraft,"* a voice said, riding out on a fresh cloud of smoke.

*"Ahh, whatta you know anyway?"* Fury shot back. *"You're missing half your visual apparatus!"*

*"Yeah,"* somebody else put in, delighted. *"Look who's talkin', Fury!"*

*Marked for her,* Faith said. *Somebody knew she was coming before she did.*

Helen didn't open it there. She tucked it into her purse, crossed to a bank of house phones near the wall, and spoke into one of them for a few seconds too short for Dana to catch anything worth catching.

Then she was moving again, toward the elevators.

Dana angled herself so she wouldn't be standing in a straight line from the doors when they opened, and watched her mother step into a waiting car alone. The elevator's back wall was mirrored floor to ceiling, and for one useful second before the doors slid shut, Dana caught the reflection of the panel Helen's hand had already found, one lit button glowing back at her at an angle nobody inside that car would have thought to hide.

*Got it?*

*Got it.*

The doors closed. Dana didn't wait for them to open again. She crossed to the next car over, stepped in alone, and pressed the button for the same floor before she'd even had time to be nervous about doing it.

Helen's doors opened first. Dana caught it through the gap of her own car's closing ones, just a flash: her mother striding out into a bright, pale-colored hallway like a woman with somewhere very specific to be.

Her own doors dinged open two seconds later. Dana eased out slow, hugging the corner of the little vestibule, and looked around it in time to see the whole thing happen.

Helen had stopped at an alcove down the hall, a big mirror mounted over a console table, flowers in a vase off to one side. She dropped her purse onto the console and shrugged the bulky outer coat off her shoulders in one hard motion, not bothering to catch it before it hit the floor, and started taking herself apart.

The sheer sleeves went next, and they didn't come off so much as come apart, Helen's fingers finding the seam and tearing it loose, fabric giving with a small, ugly ripping sound that had nothing gentle in it at all. A pouf of fabric at the neckline went the same way, torn free in one motion, and then she bent down and did the same to the matching pouf at the hem, ripping it loose and leaving a fringe of stray threads and loose strings hanging off the bottom of the dress where it had been stitched on. Underneath all of it was the same black dress Dana had seen her mother wear a dozen times before, except it wasn't, not really, stripped down to something close-fitting and sleeveless that the version hanging in the front hall closet had never once looked like. Helen bent down right there in the hallway and tore her hose down and off, one leg and then the other, straightening back up bare-legged under the short hem like she'd done it a hundred times before, even though every motion said she hadn't.

*She's not glamming up,* Faith said, quiet, something almost like respect in it. *She's field-stripping. Different thing entirely.*

Helen checked the fit in the mirror, tugged the dress at the shoulder, the waist, the hem, adjusted the fit at her own chest until it sat the way she needed it to, testing it the way you'd test gear, not an outfit. She found lipstick from somewhere and put it on fast, watching her own reflection change under it. She tore the tags off what was left of the clothes, one after another, and dropped them with the discarded jacket.

Then she reached for the flower vase, wet her fingers, and ran them back through her hair, reshaping it with nothing but water and her own two hands.

Dana didn't move the entire time. Faith timed every shift in her weight for the exact half-second Helen was bent over or turned toward the glass, and Dana never once had to freeze mid-motion.

Helen checked herself a last time, shoulders, posture, the way the dress sat when she turned, made two small corrections nobody but somebody who'd been standing right there would ever have caught, and gathered her things.

Then she started for the door.

It was, without question, the single worst attempt at a sexy walk Dana had ever personally witnessed. Helen's heels caught twice in the first ten feet, her hips overcorrecting hard enough to look like she was recovering from a stumble rather than selling one, one hand going briefly out toward the wall before she caught herself and forced her spine back into something resembling confidence.

*"And who,"* a voice said, striding into the Gallery from somewhere that hadn't existed a second earlier, arms crossed, watching the whole thing with open, delighted disbelief, *"taught her how to walk in those heels?"*

ROB-Helen didn't wait for an answer. She didn't need one.

*"There you go repeating yourself,"* somebody said. *"You said that way back in Chapter Two."*

Helen made it to the assigned door, knocked the wobble out of her last two steps through sheer will, and found it already off the latch, unlocked, waiting. She pushed it open into a room gone dark on the other side and stepped through.

...And the door closes.

...And the door closes.

...And the door closes.

Dana stayed exactly where she was until it had been shut a full ten seconds, and the hallway had gone all the way quiet again, before she let herself breathe.



Chapter 11: No Safe Angle



"Quiet, everybody." Fury's voice cut clean through the usual pre-scene noise, flat, no room in it for argument. "Shit's about to get real."

*"That serious, huh?"* somebody asked, already half-joking, already not quite managing it.

*"Yeah."* Fury didn't dress it up any further than that.

*"How real we talking?"*

*"Real enough that I already told you once."*

Nobody pushed it a third time. The rows went quiet, one after another, faster than they usually did, until there was nothing left in the theater but the sound of everybody settling in for something none of them were going to enjoy watching.

* * *

Dana found a stretch of wall with a clean line on the door and made herself small against it, and then there was nothing to do but wait.

It was the worst part so far. Worse than the elevator, worse than watching her mother tear a dress apart in a hotel hallway. There, at least, something had been happening. Here there was only a closed door, and a hallway that stayed exactly as bright and exactly as empty as it had been five minutes ago, and Dana's own head filling in everything the door wouldn't show her.

She built explanations and knocked them down one after another. An affair didn't fit, not with the envelope, not with a stranger's flat, coded voice on the phone. A kidnapping didn't fit either, not yet, not with her mother walking in on her own two feet, dressed for something. Nothing fit. The door didn't care what fit. It just stayed shut.

*You don't have to solve it standing here,* Faith said, quiet, when the silence had gone on long enough that Dana's own thoughts had started circling instead of moving.

*I know.*

*Doesn't mean it's easy. Just means you don't have to.*

That was when the elevator dinged behind her.

Dana bolted for the nearest cover before she'd fully processed the sound, heart going twice as fast as the two seconds it actually took her to get there.

*Other way,* Faith said, already redirecting her mid-step. *Away from the room. Whoever that is, you do not want to be caught standing anywhere near her door when they step out.*

Dana changed direction without breaking stride and made it around the far corner just as the doors opened behind her.

Five of them came out. Four of them barely looked like they needed the elevator at all, big, blunt, the kind of men built for standing in doorways and not much else, moving with more muscle than thought behind it.

The fifth one had the eye.

Dana couldn't have explained what she meant by that even to herself, but Faith didn't need it explained. Something in the way he scanned the hallway before he'd even fully cleared the doors, quick, precise, cataloguing exits and angles instead of just walking through them like the other four.

*That one,* Faith said, already gone flat and focused. *That one's not muscle. Watch him, not the rest of them.*

*Eye Guy,* Dana thought back, because she had to call him something, and it fit.

*Eye Guy,* Faith agreed, like the name had already been waiting for somebody to use it.

*"You see that, Trilby?"* Fury called back, delighted. *"Even the Author's mocking you."*

*"You* do *remember what I said at the debrief, right?"* Trilby shot back, unbothered. *"No slack just 'cause you're on Harry's team. Don't make me come over there."*

Eye Guy ran them through a recon that was, even to Dana's untrained eye, nowhere near as thorough as he clearly wanted it to be. He threw hand signals at the other four, quick, sharp, unmistakably meant to send them checking angles and sightlines along the hall, and the other four responded to those signals the way men respond to instructions they've had explained to them more than once already and still haven't quite absorbed.

*He's doing this whole thing with a crew he doesn't trust,* Faith said, something almost like sympathy creeping into it. *Watch. He's already had to repeat that signal twice.*

He had. One of the four drifted the wrong way down the hall before catching himself and correcting. Another stood frozen a beat too long, waiting on a cue he'd apparently already missed the first time it was given. Eye Guy's jaw worked, tight, controlled, a man visibly recalculating how much of his own plan he was going to have to walk them through by hand.

*"And how many of 'em he's gonna have to cull afterward,"* somebody added, *"for not having enough brain cells between 'em to put two and two together."*

It cost them time. Real time, the kind that mattered, and Dana could feel every extra second of it stretching out behind that closed door down the hall, whatever was happening in there getting that much longer to happen before anyone was ready to stop it.

*"Ohh, this is bad,"* somebody in the gallery murmured. *"Not the goons. The other thing. The thing happening on the other side of that door while these idiots figure out how a hallway works."*

Nobody argued the point.

The crew finally got itself moving, and once they actually started down the hall, all the fumbling dropped away like it had never happened. They weren't running. That was the first wrong thing about them once they got going, the thing that told Faith more than the guns did. Four men in plain jackets moved at a controlled, unhurried walk, weapons already out and already held low and close to the body, carried the way real training taught it, not the movies. No shouting. No wasted motion.

Eye Guy moved through that hallway like he owned the exact amount of space he needed and not one inch more, hand signals instead of words, the other four falling into position around the door without needing to be told twice, one covering the hall behind them, two flanking the frame, the last dropping to a knee to sight the lock.

*That's not muscle,* Faith said, and for the first time since the elevator, there was something in her voice that wasn't just calm. *That's an actual officer. Somebody who's done this enough times he doesn't have to think about the order anymore. Whoever's in there just got a lot more dangerous than a jealous husband's guys with guns.*

*Get out of the open,* she added, before Dana had to be told twice either. *Count what you can, stay low, and do not, under any circumstances, be standing anywhere near that door in about four seconds.*

Dana was already moving, back around the corner she'd used before, when Eye Guy raised two fingers, held them a beat, and dropped them.

The door went in on the third fist.

No warning shout. No hesitation. Just four men flowing through a doorway in the exact order their leader had already decided on before he'd ever raised his hand, and Eye Guy stepping through last, unhurried, like a man walking into a meeting he already knew the outcome of.

The door didn't swing shut behind them. It hung open on a splintered frame, nobody left outside who needed to bother hiding what was happening anymore.

Dana stayed exactly where she was, back to the wall, breathing as quietly as she'd ever breathed in her life, and started counting seconds instead of people, because Faith had already told her the number that mattered.

Five. Just five. And not one of them had looked like this was the hard part of their night.

* * *

Dana couldn't see any of it. The door hung open on its splintered frame down the hall, but the angle gave her nothing, no line into the room at all.

She heard every word of it.

Hotel hallways carried sound better than they had any right to, and nobody inside that room was bothering to keep their voice down anymore. It came to her whole, whisper and shout both, close enough that she'd have sworn afterward she'd been standing in the doorway herself.

Inside, it was already over before it started.

Harry stood by the curtained windows with both hands raised, a striped shirt open at the collar, his eyes on the two men closing the distance toward him.

"Don't move!"

He didn't. One gunman kept a pistol trained square on his chest. Another swept a flashlight straight into the room, throwing a hard white flare across everything it touched.

Harry's attention slid past both of them, toward Helen.

"Helen, just don't do anything."

Calm. Deliberate. A man managing the room instead of fighting it.

Behind him, a man in a leather jacket seized his arms and forced them back. Harry let it happen, gave nothing, exposed nothing.

Across the room, a burgundy jacket caught Helen from behind, an arm hooked hard across her chest and shoulders, hauling her backward. She fought it immediately, twisting, throwing her head back against the hold.

"No! He has nothing to do with it!"

The gray-suited gunman crossed in front of her, pistol never leaving Harry.

Helen strained forward against the arm locked around her. "I'm the one you want!"

"Shut up!"

Her captor wrenched her back. The gray garment she'd worn over the black dress hung displaced and useless around her now, catching at her arms.

The leather-jacketed man folded Harry forward and drove him face-first onto the bed, beside the black telephone resting on the spread. His arms went behind his back, held there.

"Quiet, Helen. Do what they say."

Even pinned, even with the phone inches from his mouth, his voice didn't lose the same deliberate calm.

Near the door, the bald one, Eye Guy, moved out ahead of the rest, pistol and flashlight both up, checking the hall beyond the suite while the others finished securing the room behind him. He didn't wait to be told the way out was clear. He went and found out himself.

They hauled Harry back up off the bed, one man pulling his arms behind him while the gray-suited gunman pressed a pistol to the side of his head, and turned him toward the door.

"Come on, guys. Let the hooker go. She's not important."

"Who's a hooker?" Even now, even like this, the offense in Helen's voice was real.

"You don't need her."

The burgundy-jacketed man kept his arm cinched hard across her chest, pistol close, dragging her along behind Harry.

"Harry, please, just let me handle this."

She still believed, even now, that this was somehow still about the thing she thought she'd been sent here to do.

They forced both of them out through the suite's entrance hall together, Harry pushed ahead with his arms pinned, Helen pulled stumbling after him, her displaced coat tangling around her sides with every step.

"Shut up!"

Then they were out of the room and into the hallway, and Dana could see them again.

Harry first, arms wrenched back, a gunman's pistol never leaving the side of his skull. Helen a beat behind him, held upright by the arm locked across her chest, stumbling on heels that had betrayed her all night for entirely different reasons than this one.

Harry's eyes swept the hallway as they came, quick, professional, the same kind of scan Eye Guy had used going in.

They found her.

For less than a second, Dana was certain of it, dead certain, the way you're certain of a hand closing around your wrist even before you feel it tighten. Her father's eyes landed on her, recognized her, and kept moving, sliding on past her hiding spot like she was just another shadow in a hallway full of them, like he'd never seen anything there worth a second look at all.

He didn't react to her at all.

She understood, later, that not reacting had been the reaction.

The Harry standing in that hallway was only ever doing it once, the same as everything else happening to him that night. It was the Harry watching from the theater who'd been through this part before, the same eyes, the same fraction of a second spent checking every doorway and shadow on the way past, a scan he remembered making himself, once, for real, a long time before any of this got put on a screen. *"SHIT!"* Harry's own voice, from somewhere back in the theater, sharp, real alarm cutting straight through it. *"She wasn't there the first time! What the fuck is she doing there?"*

*Why did Mom go to the hotel room, why was Dad even there, why did he call her a hooker, why are there armed guys with guns, what the fuck is going on, what is even happening, I don't, I can't...*

*Not the time.* Faith cut through it flat, no room for anything else in it. *You can panic later. Promise. Right now you gotta get to the garage. Pronto.*

*'Cause we need the license plate on whatever they're loading your parents into, and it is leaving without you if you stand here having a breakdown about it.*



Chapter 12: Home First



Dana made it to the elevator on legs that didn't feel entirely like her own, jabbed the button, and stood there shaking while it took its time arriving.

By the time the doors opened, her breath had started coming in short, ragged pulls that weren't getting enough air down no matter how many of them she took.

*Hey. Hey. Look at me, well, listen to me, same thing.* Faith's voice didn't rise to meet the panic. If anything it went quieter, steadier, deliberately unhurried, nothing about the pace admitting how little time they actually had. *You're hyperventilating. It's not gonna kill you, but it will slow you down, and we don't have the minutes to spare for that right now. In through the nose. Four count. Hold it. Out slow.*

*I can't...*

*You can. Do it with me. In.*

Dana did it, badly, the first time, air catching halfway down like her own body didn't believe her.

*Again. In.*

The elevator doors closed. The car started down.

*Hold it. There you go. Out slow, don't dump it all at once.*

By the second floor her hands had stopped shaking quite so hard. By the fourth, the ragged edge was mostly gone from her breathing, replaced by something still too fast but at least even, in and out, in and out, Faith counting her through every single cycle of it like it was the only thing either of them had to do all night.

*Good.* Faith didn't make a big deal of it once it actually worked. *That's enough to work with. Rest of the panicking, like I said, you get to have later. Full breakdown, your choice, soon as your parents aren't in a van somewhere.*

*Okay.*

*Okay,* Faith agreed. *Now let's go get that plate.*

The doors opened on the garage level, and by every measure that mattered, less than two minutes had passed since the door had gone in on the third fist.

She was already too late.

The garage hit her all at once, exposed pipes and fluorescent fixtures casting everything in a cold blue-green, a wall sign pointing one way for FIRST AID and another for PERSONNEL SECURITY like this was any ordinary night in any ordinary building. A white van sat waiting with its side door already open.

Harry went in first, bent hard at the waist, shoved through the opening by the leather-jacketed man before he'd even finished folding down for it. Helen came right behind him, forced in beside him, the burgundy-jacketed man keeping one hand locked on her the whole way and never once lowering the pistol in the other.

Dana's eyes went looking for Eye Guy on instinct, the one face in this whole night she genuinely did not want finding hers.

He was already inside the van. Already through the door, already swallowed up by the dark of it, never so much as glancing back at the garage he was leaving behind. He hadn't needed to. His whole crew was already loaded, already his problem to manage from here, and the hallway behind him had stopped being worth a second look the moment he'd gotten what he came for.

The last man in slammed the side door shut.

Dana was moving before it had even finished closing, but the van was already ahead of her, engine climbing hard, tires shrieking once against the concrete as it wheeled toward the ramp and the flat gray daylight beyond it.

She got less than a step. Didn't even clear the elevator.

It was gone before she was close enough to read a single number off the plate.

*Faith?*

*Yeah.*

*I didn't get it.*

*I know, kid.* No blame in it. Just the plain fact of it, laid out flat, same as everything else Faith had ever told her. *I know.*

Her foot slipped back. The doors slid shut around her, and something in the sound of it, small, final, ordinary, was the thing that actually broke her.

*What do I do. What do I do, what do I do, how could I not, why didn't I just, I should have, I could have, where are they taking them, why do they even want Mom, what does any of this, why did Dad, why didn't he, what do I DO...*

*You're not telling me something.*

A pause. Longer than it should have been.

*Get home first.*

*Faith.*

*Home. First.*

She didn't get anything else out of her, no matter how many times she asked on the way back up.

Trent was still exactly where she'd left him, idling half a block down, and one look at her face told him everything he needed to know and nothing he could actually name.

"Take me home," Dana said, into his shoulder, and nothing else.

He didn't say a word back. He got her onto the bike and rode, and she held on the entire way, arms locked around him, and couldn't have said afterward whether she'd been holding onto him or onto the last thread of herself. Probably both.

He walked her as far as the porch. Said something quiet she didn't really hear. Let her go up alone when she didn't ask him to come any further.

The stairs took longer than they should have. So did the bathroom, water she barely felt running over hands that wouldn't quite stop shaking, like washing her face could do anything at all about what she'd actually seen tonight. So did the walk from there to her own bed, where she finally lay down and stopped moving altogether.

* * *

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery came apart all at once.

*"They TOOK them?"*

*"Both of them? At the same time?"*

*"Somebody explain to me how a fifteen-year-old ends up alone in a parking garage after THAT and we're all just sitting here..."*

*"She didn't get the plate!"*

*"I know she didn't get the plate, I was THERE, I'm not asking for a recap..."*

*"What the HELL, Doris?"* He wasn't looking for anyone in particular, or he was looking for everyone at once, it came to the same thing. *"How could you put your own daughter through that? What fucking right does Faith have to put her in that situation? Why is she even in that hallway?"*

*"Because she chose to be."* ROB-Helen, not raising her voice at all, not needing to. *"That's the whole point of all of this."*

It didn't calm him down. Nothing about him settled, nothing about the fury went anywhere at all, it just had nowhere left to go once she'd said it, because she was right and he knew it and being right didn't make him feel one degree less furious about any of it. He stood there a second longer with his mouth still half open around the next thing he'd been about to say, and didn't say it, because there wasn't a next thing that actually answered that.

Somewhere underneath the rest of the noise, one voice didn't shout at all, even though it wasn't remotely calm underneath.

*"Get her some sleep first,"* Harry said, low, the words coming out flat and controlled, every last bit of him spent holding it there instead of letting it go anywhere else. *"She's gonna need it."*

Nobody argued with him either.



Chapter 13: Sleeper



Sleep came in pieces that night, if it came at all. Dana surfaced every hour or so to the same gray ceiling and the same knot of everything from the hotel garage sitting exactly where she'd left it, then went back under for another twenty minutes that felt less like rest than a different kind of waiting.

By the time actual light started working in gray around the edges of the blinds, she gave up pretending and just lay there. Her body still felt wrung out, but her head was clearer than it had been last night, and clearer was going to have to do.

Faith hadn't said a word since the porch. Not sulking. Just quiet, the kind that meant somebody was working something over instead of resting. Dana had felt her sitting there in the dark for hours, turning something around and around, weighing it against every dumb action movie she'd ever half watched with somebody who actually knew what to do with a gun. Not this movie. Faith had never heard of Harry Tasker in her life. Just the shape of it. The kind of story this was turning out to be, and what usually happens next in stories like that.

*Okay.* Dana said it the way you'd test weight on a bad ankle, a little at a time. *I'm home. I'm rested.* Not strictly true, but close enough not to argue with. *Now what the hell was important enough to hold back but not urgent enough to spit out right there?*

A pause. The kind that already knew exactly where it was going to land.

*No you're not.* Faith, dry, letting the rested claim sit there a second before she knocked it down. *But it's close enough for government work, so it'll have to do.*

*This is where the hard part lives, kid.* Faith's voice, when it came, had none of the coaching briskness from the garage left in it, none of the flat grounding facts on repeat. Just something slower, more careful, like she was setting something down instead of handing it over. *Take a couple of breaths. And stay right there in the bed. You're gonna need both.*

Dana's stomach dropped through the mattress. *Faith.*

*Let me know when you're ready.*

Dana lay there a minute, running through her own private version of a pep talk. Nothing eloquent about it. Just *suck it up, suck it up, suck it up* on repeat, until it started to mean something.

*Okay.* Small. Tentative. *Hit me.*

*Okay.* Faith didn't rush into it. *First of all, this is gonna be a real one-two punch. I just need you to bear through it until I lay it all out.*

Dana gathered up what nerve she had left and held onto it with both hands.

*First.* Faith's voice landed hard enough to feel like a grip on her shoulder. *YOU HAD NO PART IN THIS.* Then, softer, right on top of it, like she needed this half heard just as clearly as the first: *This is all on whichever of your parents started this whole train rolling. But, important, it's not THEIR fault either. It's a consequence. Not a blame game.*

Dana breathed through it, in and out, holding herself together one exhale at a time.

Then a second voice joined Faith's, warm and sudden, layered in like it belonged there. ROB-Helen, though Dana had no way of knowing that, no name or face to put to it, just warmth, steady as a hand between her shoulder blades. *That's my girl. Keep breathing. You can get through this.*

Somewhere behind that, Faith went quiet for exactly one beat too long, quiet that would've meant something if Dana had been in any state to notice it.

*Here's the heavy part,* Faith said, moving past it. *I've seen a bunch of these play out in the movies. Lived through more than I can count myself, too. And the one thing the movies always get right: there's a reason behind the madness. This wasn't a random snatch. So one of your folks is mixed up in something dangerous. My guess, counter-intelligence. That's the shape those five in the hall had. Arabic-speaking, professional, and ready for exactly what they were doing.*

*Just keep breathing,* ROB-Helen said again, quieter this time, like she already knew she didn't need to compete for space.

*Okay.* Dana was still breathing. Still here. *That's the one-punch. What's the two?*

*This is the hard part for you.* Faith didn't soften into it. *There's nothing you can change about how it starts. But there's a lot you might be able to do about how it ends. There's no way to make this one gentle, so I'm not gonna try.*

A beat.

*You're going to be Taken.*

* * *

Somewhere in the anonymous rows, a voice had just gotten as far as grumbling about who owned that line, when Harry came up out of his seat like the row itself had launched him.

*"WHAT!? What the fuck, Faith? How can you say that to my daughter?!"* He didn't stop there. *"What is she even supposed to do with that? How's a fifteen-year-old supposed to change an outcome? Why would you tell her that, why would you say it like THAT, what if she can't, what happens if she can't, what then..."*

ROB-Helen's hand found his arm before he could get any further, not gripping, just there. *"Sit down."* Quiet enough that it cut through the rest of the room better than raising her own voice would have. *"There's method to the madness. Remember: you already lived through this. You already know it's happening. Changing the outcome is the whole point."* A beat, something almost fond creeping into it. *"Besides. This is like someone talking to the screen at the movies."* Another beat. *"Actually. Exactly like that."*

* * *

Back with Dana, the reaction hit before she could stop it. *WHAT?!*

*Remember.* Faith cut through it before it could build into anything bigger. *Hold it together until I lay it all out.*

*Someone's going to come for you.* No hedging in it, none of the usual maybe she'd been getting all week. *You know your dad's got that picture of you and your mom in his wallet? It's inevitable.*

Dana sat with that a second, breathing through it the way she'd been taught an hour ago in an elevator that already felt like a different lifetime. "Okay," she said out loud, quiet, to the empty room. "So what do I do?"

*Nothing.* Flat. Final. *Literally nothing. That's why this is the hard part. You have to let it happen. You have to keep Trent from putting himself anywhere near the line of fire. You have to stay calm. And most important, you can't give any sign, not one, that you already know what's coming when it does.*

*There's only one way that actually works.* Faith didn't slow down for her to catch up. *Your mom's got mood-meds in the medicine cabinet. You know what they're for. You need one, RIGHT NOW. You need to be resting, and actually RELAXED, when this happens, or you're gonna blow the whole thing before it even starts.*

Dana breathed for a moment before she trusted her own voice again. "Okay. Okay. I can do this." A beat. "Yeah. Panic time's later. Right now there's a job to do. I can do this." She was still saying some version of it under her breath, the same handful of words on a loop, as she got up, made her way to the bathroom, filled a glass at the tap, and found the bottle in the cabinet exactly where she knew it would be. She swallowed one down, refilled the glass out of habit more than thirst, and carried both of them back to bed with her.

* * *

Sleep came fast, the meds doing exactly what Faith had promised, pulling her down into something too heavy to call rest and too dreamless to call anything else. Time stopped meaning much after that. An hour. Maybe three.

The front door went first, wood cracking, a sound too big for the house to actually hold.

Faith was talking before Dana's eyes had even opened, before her mind had finished deciding the sound was real. *Keep it together. Stay calm. Let it happen. You're still out of it. You don't know a single thing.*

Boots next, up the stairs, more than one pair, timed and unhurried, somehow worse than if they'd been running.

*Stay loose,* Faith said, right through it, the same handful of words on a loop, patient as a tide. *Stay quiet. Let them do the work.*

Then hands, gloved, closing around her before she'd finished processing that any of it was real. She let her whole body go slack, let it happen exactly the way she'd promised she would.

*Good.* Faith, steady, not once letting up. *That's it. Just like that.*

The van came next, cold metal under her, a door sliding shut on a sound she was starting to know too well.

Then a plane, engines climbing under her in a register the house had never made, the world tilting sideways as it left the ground.

Then, eventually, stillness again, and hands hauling her up, and the echo of somewhere enormous and unfinished, concrete and open air, and far below, the muffled, ordinary hum of a city that had no idea any of this was happening to one of its own kids twenty stories up.

* * *

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery didn't just react. It came apart, and this time nobody bothered picking up the pieces first.

*"They TOOK her? They just took her, right out of her own house..."*

*"How does that even happen? How does nobody see five armed men walk up to a house in broad daylight..."*

*"You want somebody to blame, blame the two idiots who left her alone in that house in the first place..."*

*"That's not fair and you know it..."*

*"I didn't say it was fair. I said it was true..."*

*"I will end every single one of them."* Xander, and for once nobody laughed at him for saying it. *"I don't care whose universe I have to reach into to do it. I will personally..."*

*"Get in line."*

*"If they've so much as touched her..."* Hard, unmistakable, the same voice that had already staked its claim on Dana back in the hotel lobby. Nobody needed the name attached to know exactly who meant it.

*"This is on me."* Gib, quieter than the rest of it, almost lost underneath. *"I'm the one who left that file where anybody could..."*

*"Nobody left anything anywhere, Gib. Sit down."*

*"Somebody just tell me she's okay. That's all I want. Just tell me that much..."*

*"She's twenty stories up in a building full of men with automatic weapons, what part of that sounds..."*

*"Not helping!"*

*"I wasn't trying to help. I was trying to be accurate..."*

Fury tried twice to get the room back and got run over both times. Harry didn't try at all. He just sat there, all the fury from an hour ago burned down to nothing, staring at a point on the floor that wasn't actually there.

Underneath all of it, one voice didn't raise itself at all. Trilby, cigar smoke curling up ahead of the words. *"Two men minimum on that door, more scattered through the floor below it. Same discipline as the hotel crew, professionals, not amateurs."* He let that settle before he went on. *"If the kid plays it exactly the way Faith's coaching her, calm, no sign she knows a thing, she's got a real window."* A beat. *"If she doesn't..."*

He didn't finish it. Didn't need to.

Nobody argued with him either.

*"He's right."* The same someone whose hand had come out of nowhere ten chapters back and put Gib's whole skull half an inch forward over a stolen remote, one clean shot, no windup, gone before anybody turned around fast enough to catch him at it. Nobody in this Gallery had ever actually confirmed who'd thrown it. Somebody, somewhere else entirely, in a theater running an entirely different story, already had. The name had made the trip over anyway, the GibbsSlap, trademark pending, arriving well ahead of the face or the voice to go with it. Just a cup of coffee that never seemed to run empty and a way of saying more with less than anybody else in the room had managed all night. *"I've sat through this. More times than I want to count."* Clipped. Economical. Not a wasted word in it. *"Hostage upstairs. Professional crew downstairs. Everybody outside losing their minds about it. Looks unsurvivable from here."* A beat. *"Usually isn't. She's got somebody in there talking her through it, real time. Most people I've watched walk out of buildings like that didn't have that."*

It took a while for the rest of the room to catch up to that kind of quiet. The shouting thinned out first, then the crosstalk, then even the anonymous rows ran out of things to say that hadn't already been said twice. By the time it was actually silent, really silent, it had taken most people a good long minute to get there, and even then it didn't feel settled so much as spent, everybody sitting in the wreckage of having said all of it at once and having nowhere left to put any more.



Chapter 14: Signal Lost



Gibbs' last line hung in the room longer than words usually got to, nobody in any hurry to be the one who spoke first and broke it.

Most of the Gallery held its breath right along with it, waiting for something that didn't have a name yet to either land or not.

Two faces in the crowd didn't look anything like the rest of the room. Not dread. Not fear holding still and calling itself patience. Something closer to anticipation, quiet and sure of itself, almost out of place next to everybody else's white-knuckled waiting.

One was ROB-Helen. Not the real one, not down to a single cell of her, and tonight, of all nights, that didn't matter. She'd sat with Dana's fear the same as if she'd carried her. Worried over her the same as if she'd raised her. Real in every way that actually mattered. Not real in every way that mattered too, and never once pretending otherwise, not to herself and not to anybody watching.

The other wasn't anybody's mother in that building. Buffy didn't have a daughter riding on this. She had a fellow Slayer, on the clock, doing the job the way the job was actually supposed to get done. She'd watched Faith handle worse than this with steadier hands than most of this room would manage on their best day, and she saw no reason tonight would be any different.

* * *

Somewhere past the edge of the room, a television kept looping the same handful of images: a reporter standing too close to a police line, a graphic with a mushroom cloud icon nobody had bothered making tasteful, and underneath it all, her father's own face, blurred past all recognition, some agency name stamped along the bottom that had nothing to do with computers or trade shows.

The broadcast held a beat too long, long enough to almost forget there was a room around it at all. Then it pulled back, and pulled back further, off the screen and past the crates it sat on, until it wasn't a broadcast anymore, just a room again, familiar and airless and much too high up, Dana's own shoulder filling the near edge of the frame again, same as always once things turned serious.

Dana had been crying earlier. She wasn't now, not exactly. Just staring, quiet in the flat, used-up way a person goes quiet once they've spent everything they had on being scared and haven't found anything new to replace it with yet.

Malik crossed to the crate, drew a key out from beneath his shirt on a chain, and fit it into the lock.

*I got this.* Faith, low, certain, no room left in it for anything else.

*Ok.* Dana didn't trust herself with more than the one word.

"Why don't you shove that key somewhere more useful?" The words were out of her mouth before she'd decided to say a single one of them, sharp and mocking and far too loud for the room. "And shove the case in after it?"

Malik's head came up.

The backhand caught her before she even saw it start, hard enough to snap her head sideways and drop her against the crate behind her.

*I thought you had this,* Dana managed, once the ringing let her think in words again.

*I do.* Faith, unbothered, almost fond about it. *People lose focus when they lose their temper.*

Malik was already turning back to the camera, already reaching for the next line of his own speech, temper spent and forgotten as fast as it had come. He never saw her hand move the second time, quiet and small and nothing at all like the first, closing around the key and drawing it free of the lock while his whole attention went back to the reporter, the lens, and the five million people he was about to threaten all over again.

*Ok. Now quick.* Faith's voice snapped from settled to urgent in the space of one breath. *To the stairwell.*

Dana was already moving, key fisted tight, sneakers near silent on the concrete. She hit the stairwell door and looked down, and stopped dead.

Two floors below, the rest of the crew was already spread out across the landing, guns up, watching the door like they'd known exactly which way she'd run before she did.

*No.* Faith, flat, redirecting without missing a beat. *Up. Not down.*

She went up instead, two steps at a time, lungs burning inside a minute. Malik's shout chased her up the stairwell, closer with every landing. Faith called every turn a half second before Dana's own eyes caught up to it, *left, not right, right's a dead end,* *he's slower than he looks, don't waste breath looking back,* *door at the top, go.*

The roof door gave onto open air and a wash of city noise she hadn't heard in what felt like a lifetime, wind and traffic and, underneath both of them, something bigger, a low mechanical roar climbing steadily out of the distance that hadn't been there a minute ago.

*Faith?*

*Not now. Keep moving.*

She backed toward the edge instead of away from it, the whole roof suddenly too small and too open at once. The chain went up and over her own head before Faith could get out more than half a word of objection, key settling cold against her collarbone, hers now, attached, not something anybody was going to talk her out of just handing back.

Malik came through the door a breath later, two of his men right behind him, and stopped short at what he found waiting for him.

It ran the way these things always seemed to, in the movies and, apparently, in real life too: him advancing, her backing along the edge to match him step for step, the key held out over open air the whole time so he'd know exactly what happened if he pushed it.

Her heel caught something solid, cold lattice-metal instead of rooftop concrete, and there wasn't anywhere left to go but up.

*Climb.* Faith, steady, relentless. *Don't look down, don't look at him, just climb. Hands first. Feet find their own way.*

She climbed. It wasn't graceful, hands finding gaps in the lattice more by feel than sight, the chain catching once on a strut and yanking hard enough at her neck to punch a gasp out of her before she worked it loose. Every foot of height put more air under her and less rooftop, until she was standing on the boom itself, the crane's long arm stretching out over open space toward nothing at all.

*Out.* Faith didn't slow down for her to catch up. *Onto the arm. Keep the key clear. Don't stop till I tell you to.*

She went, one hand sliding along the cable strung the length of it, sneakers finding the narrow lattice rail underneath, the ground reduced to a smear of color she didn't let herself actually look at. Faith walked her through every foot of it after that, where to plant her weight, when to breathe, the cable swinging gently under her the second she trusted it with any of hers.

Malik didn't hesitate at the base of the crane the way she had. He came up after her fast and sure-footed, like heights had never once been a problem for a man like him, and followed her out onto the arm anyway. He always was going to.

The roar behind them had stopped being distant.

*I think the cavalry's arrived,* Faith said, something almost like relief creeping in around the edges of it.

*Huh?*

*You hear that?* Faith. *That's a gun putting out more rounds a second than anything handheld ever could. Big ones, too.*

She looked down in time to watch the jet all but erase an entire floor of the building, glass and light and something that used to be a wall gone in the same half second, a sound like the sky itself tearing along one clean seam.

*Yep.* Faith, almost admiring. *Exactly what it sounded like.*

Malik didn't stop moving. If anything, whatever had just happened four floors down put more urgency into his own steps out onto the boom, not less. Dana kept backing away from him along the lattice, key still cold against her collarbone, the ground so far down it had stopped registering as a real place with real consequences and started reading more like a very bad photograph instead.

They stopped moving eventually, the boom running out of usable length in front of her, Malik still ten feet back and closing an inch at a time, like he had all day for it.

"Come on, child." His voice carried easy across the gap, gentle, and somehow worse for it than shouting would have been. "Give me the key. Don't you want to live? I give you my word."

"Fuck off, pencil dick."

It came out flat and instant, no hesitation in it anywhere, nothing like the panic that had leaked through the last time her own mouth had stopped being entirely her own.

*That one was mine,* Faith said, easy, almost pleased with herself. *Figured it was my turn.*

Her own hands moved next, without her deciding a single part of it, working the chain back up over her head and out into open air, key swinging loose at the end of it now, dangling over a drop that didn't have a bottom worth thinking about anymore.

"See this?" Still not entirely her own voice, same borrowed confidence riding her mouth like it had always belonged there. "You so much as breathe wrong and this takes a dive. Then what? Then you're just a two-bit psycho with a soft dick and an empty box, twenty stories up, and jack shit to show for it."

Malik's jaw went tight.

*Keep going,* Faith said, quieter now, just for her. *He's rattled. Rattled's good.*

"Or," Faith went on, Dana's own mouth carrying every word of it, "you could jump. Save everybody the trouble. Or better yet, use that gun for something more useful than waving it around."

Then the plane itself came up out of nothing, rising into the space in front of her like it had torn a hole straight through the air to get there, filling the whole gap between the building and the sky until there wasn't room left for anything else to exist. Behind the glass of it, hands on the stick, was her father.

*Yep.* Faith again, quieter this time, something settling into place behind it. *That nails it.*

*What the actual fuck?!* It came out of her before she'd decided whether it was meant for Faith or Malik or the entire universe at large.

*I had my suspicions on this one.*

*You'll have to explain that one.* She didn't take her eyes off the plane, off him. *When we're not dangling a thousand feet in the air.*

*Fair.* A beat, Faith's attention clearly split between the conversation and something else entirely. *He's not as good behind that stick as he thinks he is. Watch, he's gonna bump the gantry. Be ready. When he's steady and he's right under you, let go.*

*WHAT?!* Dana's hands closed on the cable hard enough to feel it bite. *Just let go? A million miles up?*

*Parachutists do it all the time.*

*I don't HAVE a parachute!*

*You've got something better.* Faith, steady, sure, not even a flicker of doubt in it, smug enough to slice like pie. *Your dad. Behind the stick of a jet.*

One of Malik's men had a rifle. Malik took it off him without asking, without even looking, eyes already locked on the jet, and opened up on the windshield the second Harry brought the nose around.

Harry peeled off hard, tailfin catching the gantry on the way out with a shriek of metal Dana felt through her own back teeth, the boom lurching hard behind her at the impact. Malik's rifle, still raised, still firing, went with the jolt, torn clean out of his hands, gone, it didn't fall clear, it snagged, somewhere back along the plane's own fuselage, hanging off an after-fairing near the tail like it had decided to switch sides.

Below her, the jet finally held rock steady for one full second, Harry's voice carrying up even over the engine noise. "Jump! I've got you! Trust me!"

*He's got you,* Faith said, right on top of it, immediate, unhesitating. *Let go. Now.*

She let go.

The nose of the plane came up to meet her before she'd finished falling, glass and metal instead of open air, hard enough to knock the breath clean out of her. Her hands found nothing at first, sliding, and then her father's own hand closed around her wrist, wrenching her the rest of the way onto the fuselage.

"Hang on!" Harry, shouting over the engine, stick still gripped in his other hand. "Just hang on!"

*Just hang on.* Faith, right behind it, like it needed saying twice to actually count. *You heard the man.*

Behind them, on the crane, Malik watched his own rifle ride away on the back of the one plane in the whole sky worth chasing, and didn't waste a second deciding what to do about it. He went after it, off the boom and onto the tail of the plane, empty-handed and furious and nowhere near finished.

Dana screamed, the sound tearing out of her before she could stop it. Harry's eyes cut back for half a second, catching the shape of Malik landing on the fuselage behind them. Then his eyes cut sideways instead, fast, to something Dana couldn't see yet from where she was pinned flat against the nose.

*Hang on,* Faith said, quiet and certain underneath everything else happening at once. *For the ride of a lifetime.*

The helicopter came around the building's edge a half second later, door open, a gunner already swinging his weapon into line. Harry banked hard, cutting fast around one whole side of the building and then the other, the gunship falling in right behind like the fight had just opened a second front. Cannon fire chewed long furrows into open ocean out past the building, missing. Tracer fire raked a ragged stripe up the face of a neighboring tower, missing that too. He couldn't shake Malik loose without shaking her loose too, and he clearly knew it, every maneuver pulled up just short of what it could have been, less a clean battle than two very fast, very lethal things trying to occupy the same airspace and mostly failing to hit anything at all.

Just her own two hands locked white-knuckled on whatever she could reach, flattened against the nose of a fighter jet while a war happened all around her, close enough to feel the heat of it, like she was cargo instead of a person, like the only thing that actually mattered up here was the fight itself.

One full second, staring at something so ordinary it didn't belong anywhere near tonight. One second too many.

The janitor never even got the vacuum shut off. Headphones still on, he didn't notice a thing until the tail assembly was sitting six inches off the end of his own nose, and by then there was nothing left to do but stand there, frozen in the wreckage of somebody's cubicle wall, with absolutely no framework whatsoever for processing it. The whole office lit up around them for one long, ridiculous second before Harry wrenched it back out the way it had come.

Malik didn't have anything to hold onto for that part. He went backward too, flung clean off the fuselage, and came down hard, nuts-first, straddling the tail stabilizer with a sound that made Dana wince even over the engine noise, and an expression that hit every male member of the Gallery at once, a single involuntary full-body wince that needed no further explanation whatsoever.

"Daddy, behind you!"

Harry looked back. Looked at her, just for a second, checking. Then his eyes cut sideways again, the same look from before.

*Get ready for the ride of a lifetime.* Faith again, the same line she'd already used once tonight, apparently not above repeating herself when it still fit.

He wasn't holding onto anything at all, in fact. He said it quiet. He said it like the whole rest of the night had just been waiting on those two words to be ready.

She stared at him. At all of him, the stranger who'd apparently been living inside her own father this whole time, wearing his face like it had never once been a disguise.

*"...and the crowd goes wild!"* Gib, sports-announcer voice, already halfway out of his seat, both arms going up like he'd personally kicked the winning field goal.

*"HE FLEW A HARRIER JET"* Somebody, several rows back, not even bothering to finish one thought before starting the next. *"HE JUST COMMANDEERED A MILITARY AIRCRAFT WITH HIS BARE HANDS AND I'M SUPPOSED TO ACT LIKE THAT'S A NORMAL TUESDAY FOR THIS FAMILY..."*

*"That's not a job. That's a superpower with a pension plan..."*

*"Gibbs called it too!"*

*"Gibbs always calls it. That's not news, that's a Tuesday."*

Gibbs himself got in on it too, for once, coffee cup lowered just long enough to say his piece. *"Twenty years in the Corps. I know exactly how a Sidewinder's supposed to fly."* A beat, dry as the rest of him. *"That wasn't it."*

Xander was on his feet, both fists in the air, shouting something that stopped resolving into actual words by the third syllable, pure noise, matching the anonymous rows for once instead of standing apart from them the way he usually did.

Somewhere in the middle of it all, Harry laughed. Real laughter, unguarded, the first sound he'd made all chapter that wasn't rage or grief wearing rage's clothes, like something in his chest had finally been given permission to let go along with the rest of the room.

Through all of it, two people still didn't move, and both of them had settled into the exact same pose. Buffy had her boots up on the row in front of her, slouched back with the complete, unbothered ease of someone who already knew how tonight ended. A few rows up, ROB-Helen had her own feet propped just the same way, up on a row that wasn't actually there to prop them on, gravity apparently as unconvinced by the arrangement as everyone else in the theater, and just as unable to do anything about it.

*"Would somebody explain to me,"* Fury said, raising his voice over the noise, *"how you two are the only calm people in a five-hundred-seat theater right now?"*

*"Because we already knew,"* ROB-Helen said, not even looking away from the screen.

*"I've known Faith longer than any of you have."* Buffy, deadpan, arms crossed the exact same way. *"This was never actually in question."*

*"Show-offs,"* somebody muttered, and got laughed at for it, and didn't seem to mind one bit.



Chapter 15: New Assignment



One year later, dinner at the Tasker house had wound down into something sillier than usual: three sets of hands locked together over a table still cluttered with plates, mid thumb war, all three of them talking over each other about the rules.

"One, two, three..." Harry started.

"No, wait, stop." Dana and Helen, almost in unison. "Just begin."

"Okay, fine. But do it at the same time."

*He's been running this exact same early-attack strategy for a year now,* Faith observed, somewhere behind Dana's own eyes, warm as anything else at this table. *Still hasn't figured out we've clocked it.*

*Don't tell him. It's the only advantage I've got left.*

"Okay, ready?" Dana said.

"Do it properly," Helen said, amused.

All three together, not quite synced: "One, two, three, four, I declare a thumb war. Five, six, seven, eight, try to keep your thumbs straight."

Harry went for both of them at once, thumbs working independently, watching for an opening on either side.

"Dad, wait! Stop! You have to wait!" Dana yanked for her own thumb, too late. He'd already caught it.

"I got you!" His grin was enormous, entirely too pleased with himself for a man in his forties who'd just won a thumb war against his own teenage daughter.

The phone started ringing in the kitchen.

Helen tried to reset their hands, patient about it. "Right. Just listen. Listen to the rules." She glanced toward the sound, already halfway to letting go.

She pushed back from the table and crossed into the kitchen while Harry and Dana went right back to resetting their own grip.

"Okay, here. Okay, again. One, two, three..." Dana stopped herself. "No, start over."

Helen lifted the receiver. "Hello?"

Whatever came through it changed her face fast, ordinary domestic attention sharpening into something else entirely.

*There it is,* Faith said, quiet.

Dana felt the shift the same instant Faith did, the two of them arriving at the same read half a beat apart from two different directions, same as it had gone for a year now.

"We're on," Helen said into the phone, and turned to find Harry already looking at her, already understanding, already rising out of his chair.

He crossed to stand behind her shoulder while she listened another moment, composed now, professional in a way that had nothing left in it of the woman who'd been losing a thumb war two minutes ago.

"Yes? Go ahead."

Dana didn't wait to be told. She was already off the stairs and moving, homework forgotten, a year's worth of practice carrying her the rest of the way there without needing a single instruction.

*Ready?* Faith asked anyway. She always asked anyway.

*Ready.*

* * *

The chandelier came first, all faceted glass and gilded molding, before the rest of the room ever widened out to take it in. Live orchestral music filled a ballroom dressed in flags and curtained arches and uniformed officers, white-jacketed waiters threading between tables loaded with more silver than Dana had ever seen in one place in her life.

Harry moved through it in a white dinner jacket and black bow tie. Helen wore black, gown and upswept hair and a long black cigarette holder she used less for smoking than for punctuation, raising it whenever she wanted an excuse to look somewhere without looking like she was looking.

An officer passed close enough to greet.

"Colonel, how nice to see you again," Helen said, warm, effortless.

"Good evening," he said, and kept moving.

Harry watched him go. "Who was that?"

Helen didn't stop long enough to answer. They kept moving, kept scanning, two more well-dressed strangers in a room full of them.

Dana sat at her own table near the edge of the floor, one earbud tucked in against the noise, doing her best impression of a bored teenager she'd rehearsed for a solid week in her bedroom mirror. Gib's voice came through the same earbud a moment later, transmitting from somewhere blue-lit and cramped, a technician working equipment behind him she couldn't see.

"What's the scoop, team? You see your contact yet?"

"Haven't seen him yet," Helen murmured, not looking anywhere near conspicuous about it.

Then Harry's whole expression changed, recognition sliding in under the professional blank he'd been wearing all night. "But I see somebody I'd like to say hello to."

*Oh, this is gonna be good,* Faith said, and wouldn't explain why no matter how many times Dana asked.

Across the room, a waiter was busy being someone else's idea of a secret agent. He leaned in close to an unimpressed brunette, champagne bottle balanced on his forearm like a prop, working his own cover story a little too hard.

"Let me pour you some more champagne." He lowered his voice like the room might be listening. "I gotta keep up the waiter bit. These stakeouts can be tricky." A theatrical scan of the room. "You never know when things might explode into a life-or-death situation."

Harry closed the last of the distance without him noticing.

"If it gets rough, stay low till it's over, I'll contact you later." One more line, leaning in. "Maybe you should give me your..."

Harry's arm came around him from behind before he got the rest of it out.

"So. We meet again, Carlos."

*"CARLOS?!"* Half the anonymous rows lost their minds at once. *"That's CARLOS? That's PISS BOY? That's the actual mapping, right there, on the actual page, verbatim..."*

*"Called it,"* somebody said, unbothered.

*"Nobody called it, we didn't even know there WAS a Carlos to call back in Chapter Two..."*

*"I'm retroactively calling it. That's allowed."*

Simon's eyes went wide, the whole confident secret-agent posture gone in an instant. The brunette watched, abandoned mid-conversation, as the man she'd been half-listening to got claimed by two strangers who clearly already knew him.

*Oh, I recognize him now.* Dana, half to herself. *Mom told me about that guy. Even showed me a surveillance shot once.*

*And your dad's still got his Stingray in the garage,* Faith said, apropos of absolutely nothing, dry as anything.

Helen stepped in without hesitation. "Honey, I'm just gonna do him right here, okay?"

Her hand snapped onto his face before he could react, palm clamping his cheek and jaw, forcing his head sideways. Something small and cylindrical pressed up under his chin at the same instant, familiar to anyone who'd been paying attention all book long, close enough to his own throat that he had every reason to assume it was something considerably sharper than lipstick.

"Go for it," Harry said.

"Oh, God," Simon managed, somewhere around the pressure, and lived up to every syllable of his own nickname right there on the ballroom floor, a dark spreading stain nobody standing close enough to notice would ever be polite enough to mention out loud.

She held him there a beat longer than strictly necessary, made her point, and let go. He staggered upright, grasping for whatever dignity he had left, which by that point amounted to considerably less than he'd started the evening with.

Helen leaned in close before he could get any further, voice dropping to a whisper meant for him and nobody else in the room.

"Fear is not an option."

Whatever he'd been trying to rebuild in the last five seconds went with it. He turned and vanished into the crowd without another word.

Helen watched him go, entirely unbothered, uncapped the same tube, and touched up her own lipstick without missing a beat.

Harry watched him go too, all the edge draining back out of his face into something closer to amusement. He turned back to Helen.

She held the cigarette holder near her face, composed again, pleased with herself and not bothering to hide it.

Then something on a nearby table caught his eye: a single red rose in a short vase. He took it without asking anyone's permission and turned back to her with it already between two fingers.

"Dance?"

The orchestra shifted underneath the question, accordion pushing forward over the strings, turning the whole room's rhythm from polite reception into something with actual teeth in it.

Harry raised the rose between himself and Helen, close enough to her face that it was somewhere between an invitation and a dare.

"Tango," Helen said, already answering the challenge before he'd finished making it.

"Here we go," Harry said, and they moved together into a real hold, formal, close, exact.

They were good at it. Genuinely good, not movie-good, the two of them clearly having done this exact dance more than once in whatever life happened between missions. Harry worked the rose between his fingers and his teeth and back again, sharp turns, sudden stops, a dip that left Helen arched back over his arm with her whole trust riding on it and not one flicker of doubt on her face.

*Okay,* Faith admitted. *That's actually kind of impressive.*

*That's my DAD.*

*I know. Still impressive.*

Gib's patience gave out somewhere in the middle of it. "Harry? Helen? Guys?"

Nothing.

"Come on, now. Let's not get distracted. We got some work to do."

They danced through that too, turn after turn, the crowd around them thinning into background as the two of them kept finding each other on every beat.

"All right, guys. Come on." A pause. "You guys better get serious in there. I'd be serious if I was in there." Another pause, longer. "Don't ignore me, Harry. That's rude."

*Somebody's having a rough night,* Faith said, not unkindly.

Dana watched her parents dance until they were just two more shapes among a whole floor of couples, indistinguishable except for the way they moved, and let herself feel, for the length of one whole song, like she was allowed to just watch instead of watch the door.

"Helen. I was always on your side." Gib, still trying, something fraying at the edge of his voice now. "I was always there for you."

Nobody answered him.

"You know what?" Something had finally broken loose, however many years he'd been holding it in. "I'm sick of being in the van. You guys are gonna be in the van next time." A beat. "I been in the van for fifteen years."

Back in the ballroom, tight on Dana, still watching the door out of habit more than actual duty at this point.

Something left her.

There wasn't a word for it at first, just a sudden, enormous silence where a voice had lived for exactly one year, a whole shape of presence gone out of her all at once, the way a room changes the instant somebody who'd been standing right behind you steps away. Her hand flew to her own chest without her telling it to. The earbud suddenly sounded very loud and very alone, Gib's voice the only thing left in it.

*Faith?*

Nothing answered.

*Faith!*

Still nothing. Not a flicker, not a beat, not the smallest scrap of the constant, familiar weight that had been living behind her own eyes for three hundred and sixty-five straight days.

Whatever her face did, it did it fast. Across the room, Harry's head came up first, some old instinct of his own catching the change in her a beat before Helen's did, and then they were both moving, weaving hard through the dancers, abandoning the tango and the cover and the whole operation without a second's hesitation, because whatever else either of them was tonight, they were her parents first.

* * *

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery had gone quiet in the good way, the kind that didn't need filling, right up until it very much didn't stay that way.

*"CARLOS,"* somebody finally said, the word landing like it had been sitting on the tip of their tongue for ten chapters. *"That was really him. Piss boy. Bladder guy. The sign on the bathroom door. All of it, this whole time, and it was ALWAYS gonna pay off on the actual page, verbatim, no invention required."*

*"Somebody update the bible,"* Fury said. *"Before Gladly wanders over from some other entry and takes credit for calling it."*

*"She's still got the earbud in,"* Gib pointed out, not quite believing it himself. *"That's not ending it. That's just... continuing it. Differently."*

Trilby's cigar smoke curled up from somewhere in the front rows, unhurried as ever. *"Girl's got real instincts. Good posture, good cover, eyes never left the door once. Somebody trained her right."*

*"Somebody did,"* Faith said, and for once there wasn't a joke riding underneath it.

Xander's voice came from a few rows back, quieter than his usual register. *"So that's it? She just... gets to keep it? All of it?"*

*"That's the whole deal."* ROB-Helen. *"Always was. She chose to be there tonight, same as every other night this whole book's been about. Nobody's taking that away from her now just because the credits are close."*

*"Yeah,"* Faith agreed, from wherever it was Faith actually said things from these days. *"That's kind of the whole point."*

Fury let out a long breath, the closest thing to sentimental anybody in that room had ever caught him being. *"Well. Good for the kid."*

*"Good for both of 'em,"* somebody added, unnamed, and nobody in the anonymous rows argued with that either.

*"You know what, boys."* ROB-Helen, already rising, already somewhere else in her attention before she'd finished the sentence. *"This is where I bow out. Places to go, people to do."* A beat, unbothered, already halfway gone. *"You know how it is."*



Chapter 16: Exit Interview



The ballroom didn't go away. That was the strange part, if anything about that theater could still surprise anyone this many chapters in. It just stopped being the only thing on the screen.

A second screen came up beside it, unhurried, like it had always been there and simply hadn't bothered turning on until now. No projector beam, no visible seam where one ended and the other began, just suddenly two of them, side by side, the ballroom still running on the left, Harry and Helen still cutting hard through the crowd toward their daughter, Dana still standing frozen at her own table with one hand pressed flat against her own chest.

On the right, the picture resolved into someplace far plainer. White walls. Fluorescent light. A door that led nowhere real.

The waiting room.

Some of the anonymous rows noticed before others did. A ripple of quiet worked its way backward through the theater as more and more of them clocked what they were actually looking at.

*"That's..."* somebody started, and didn't finish it, because everybody who'd been here since Chapter Two already knew exactly what they were looking at.

Fade into the waiting room. No screen inside a screen this time, no Gallery framing at all. Wherever this actually was, it was real, in whatever way any of this had ever counted as real.

The first thing Faith noticed was the face.

Same one from the very start of this whole book. Same borrowed mother's face, familiar down to the last detail after a year spent watching it move through somebody else's life, still dressed for the embassy party, black gown, hair up, that same cigarette holder dangling loose from two fingers like she'd walked straight out of the ballroom and hadn't bothered changing for the occasion. Faith didn't wait for an explanation, didn't wait for a word, didn't wait for anything at all.

She hit it dead in the mouth before it had even finished turning toward her, one clean shot, no windup, the same punch Helen herself had thrown at Fury back before any of this had a name yet. This time it was Faith doing the throwing. This time it landed on the one face in the whole story that actually owed her an answer.

Somewhere else entirely, Fury's hand flew to his own jaw. *"I felt that."* He worked it, genuinely bewildered. *"How did I feel that?"*

*"YOU UTTER BITCH!"* Faith was already shouting before ROB-Helen hit the ground, no interest left in pretending otherwise. *"How could you do that to your own daughter?!"*

ROB-Helen went down hard, one heel catching a chair that hadn't been anywhere in the room a second earlier, and landed flat on her back for it.

She took her time getting up. Straightened her dress. Checked her own lip with two fingers, entirely unbothered by whatever she found there.

"I told you when this all started." Calm. Almost bored. "'Reasons that will become apparent.'" A beat, brushing something off her sleeve that wasn't actually there either. "Just because they're apparent doesn't mean you're seeing them yet."

*"Well, clear it the hell up for me!"*

"In a minute." She smoothed the last wrinkle out of her dress and looked past Faith, toward the door. "We have more guests coming."

Three shapes resolved out of nothing behind her, a rising hum and a shimmer of gold-white particles sparkling top to bottom, an effect anybody who'd owned a television at any point in the nineties would have recognized on sight, whether or not they could have named where they'd seen it first. The sound faded. The light held its shape. Where a second ago there had been nothing at all, three actual people now stood.

It took Faith exactly one second to recognize all three of them.

*They're here.* She said it like saying it out loud might make it stop being true. *They're not supposed to be here. How are they here?*

"You're not the only one owed an explanation," ROB-Helen said, still not remotely worried about any of this.

Real Helen's eyes found her.

Real Helen hit her.

"BITCH!"

ROB-Helen hit the floor.

She lay there a moment, staring up at the ceiling, and let out a long breath.

"Really? Twice in less than a minute?"

Somewhere else entirely, in a theater running an entirely different story, Gladly hit the floor in the exact same pose, blood already welling at his lip, and had absolutely no idea why.

ROB-Helen picked herself up off the floor a second time, entirely unhurried, and took a moment to dust off both palms against her own dress, one leisurely pass smoothing out the wrinkles a punch and a half had put in it. A sequence anybody alive at the time would recognize on sight followed it, whether or not they could have named the first place they'd ever seen it.

She raised one hand to shoulder height.

And snapped her fingers.

The black gown was gone. In its place: a familiar uniform, red across the shoulders and chest, fading down into solid black below, the kind of silhouette that didn't need a single word of explanation for anyone who'd ever owned a television at all.

The room went with it. White walls and fluorescent light gave way to something warmer, wood paneling, low lamps, a fireplace crackling away in a wall that hadn't had one a second earlier. Comfortable armchairs stood arranged in a loose half circle around the hearth, and everyone was already sitting in one, Faith, Dana, Harry, Helen, all of them settled in like they'd been there the whole time and the room had simply gotten around to admitting it.

"And now, to business." ROB-Helen settled back into her own chair like she owned the room, the fireplace, and everyone currently sitting in it. "I owe you all an explanation."

"You owe us more than that." Helen, arms crossed, nowhere near finished being furious. "You owe us a fucking apology."

ROB-Helen brushed right over her, not even a flicker of acknowledgment. "Do none of you see why I did this?" Something almost wounded crept into it, buried again just as fast. "I swear, sometimes I wonder why I even try..." The rest dissolved into muttering, words too quiet and too fast to actually follow.

"What the hell do you mean?" Harry leaned forward, out of patience for anything cryptic.

ROB-Helen turned serious, all the muttering gone out of her at once.

"Look at your daughter, Harry." Dead straight, not a flicker of a joke on her face. "Now look at Faith." A beat. "Now back at your daughter."

Her body changed under that same borrowed face, shoulders squaring, stance widening into something built like it had never once worried about anything in its entire life, unmistakably lifted from one very specific decade-old commercial nobody in that room had actually managed to forget. Same face. Everything else, completely different.

Dana stared. "...What is happening right now."

The only answer she got was a whistle, short, cheerful, unmistakable, the exact same handful of notes that had closed out every single commercial that build had ever starred in, coming from everywhere in the room and nowhere in it at the same time.

ROB-Helen let the pose hold a moment longer than it needed to, entirely pleased with herself, before dropping straight back into serious.

"Do you remember what she used to look like? Before all of this?" She wasn't looking at Harry anymore. She was looking at Dana. "Dana. Have you looked in a mirror lately?"

A mirror simply appeared, the way everything in this room seemed to, propped against nothing in particular, angled just right to catch both of them at once.

Dana looked. Kept looking, because the first look didn't seem to have actually landed yet.

Two faces stared back out of the glass. Hers. And, impossibly, right alongside it, a second one that had no business being anywhere near a mirror at all. Faith didn't have a face. Not one anybody could actually see, not once, not in an entire year of this. And yet there it was, and the longer Dana looked, the less she could find the exact line where one face stopped and the other one started.

"...fuuuuuuuu!!"

Faith didn't wait for the mirror to fade. "Okay, but what about me?" Something raw crept into it, none of her usual armor bothering to show up for this part. "It can't just be because I looked like her!"

ROB-Helen turned to face her fully, all the theater gone out of her voice at once.

"You're right. There was madness to my method." A beat, and for once it didn't sound like a line. "Yours was a lesson just as important. You've been defining yourself as the Slayer for far too many years. You needed to find out how strong you were ready to become, even without that backing you up."

Nobody said anything for a beat that stopped being a beat almost immediately and turned into something closer to eternity, the fire the only thing in the whole room still willing to make a sound.

ROB-Helen dusted off her upper legs and stood, brisk about it, like the whole conversation had already wrapped itself up as far as she was concerned. "Well. It's time for everyone to go back home and..."

"Wait." Faith was on her feet before the sentence finished dying. "You can't just screw us over like that, even if you had a reason! I want something!"

ROB-Helen didn't ask what she meant. Didn't need to.

"I don't know..." Drawn out, almost playful, and then she turned toward Dana instead, and something happened to her voice that had nothing to do with the face it was still coming out of, dropping and changing in the same breath into something the whole room recognized on sound alone, the exact cadence of somebody none of them had actually heard speak for herself in this entry before tonight.

"ARE YOU READY TO BE STRONG?!"

Dana blinked. "What? Huh?" A beat, still catching up. "Well... yeah, I guess."

Something shifted through her the instant the words left her mouth. Her spine straightened without her telling it to. For one brief second she looked almost feral, shoulders dropped, weight settling low and ready into her own feet like her body had just remembered something her mind hadn't caught up to yet. Then it passed, and what was left behind wasn't feral at all. Just poised. Certain. Steady in a way she'd never once been before tonight.

"There." ROB-Helen studied her own handiwork, entirely pleased with herself. "I even gave her a little oomph."

Something moved behind Dana's eyes for just a fraction of a second, old and vast and nothing at all like a fifteen-year-old girl from Virginia, gone again before anyone could be fully sure they'd actually seen it.

"Now." ROB-Helen straightened, brisk again, like the whole conversation really was finished this time. "It's time for everyone to go home."

She raised her hand and snapped her fingers a second time.

The screen went bright white, blinding, total, for one whole second.

Then it faded to black.
