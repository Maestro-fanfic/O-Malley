Signal Lost

Gibbs' last line hung in the room longer than words usually got to, nobody in any hurry to be the one who spoke first and broke it.

Most of the Gallery held its breath right along with it, waiting for something that didn't have a name yet to either land or not.

Two faces in the crowd didn't look anything like the rest of the room. Not dread. Not fear holding still and calling itself patience. Something closer to anticipation, quiet and sure of itself, almost out of place next to everybody else's white-knuckled waiting.

One was ROB-Helen. Not the real one, not down to a single cell of her, and tonight, of all nights, that didn't matter. She'd sat with Dana's fear the same as if she'd carried her. Worried over her the same as if she'd raised her. Real in every way that actually mattered. Not real in every way that mattered too, and never once pretending otherwise, not to herself and not to anybody watching.

The other wasn't anybody's mother in that building. Buffy didn't have a daughter riding on this. She had a fellow Slayer, on the clock, doing the job the way the job was actually supposed to get done. She'd watched Faith handle worse than this with steadier hands than most of this room would manage on their best day, and she saw no reason tonight would be any different.

✳

Somewhere past the edge of the room, a television kept looping the same handful of images: a reporter standing too close to a police line, a graphic with a mushroom cloud icon nobody had bothered making tasteful, and underneath it all, her father's own face, blurred past all recognition, some agency name stamped along the bottom that had nothing to do with computers or trade shows.

The broadcast held a beat too long, long enough to almost forget there was a room around it at all. Then it pulled back, and pulled back further, off the screen and past the crates it sat on, until it wasn't a broadcast anymore, just a room again, familiar and airless and much too high up, Dana's own shoulder filling the near edge of the frame again, same as always once things turned serious.

Dana had been crying earlier. She wasn't now, not exactly. Just staring, quiet in the flat, used-up way a person goes quiet once they've spent everything they had on being scared and haven't found anything new to replace it with yet.

Malik crossed to the crate, drew a key out from beneath his shirt on a chain, and fit it into the lock.

I got this. Faith, low, certain, no room left in it for anything else.

Ok. Dana didn't trust herself with more than the one word.

"Why don't you shove that key somewhere more useful?" The words were out of her mouth before she'd decided to say a single one of them, sharp and mocking and far too loud for the room. "And shove the case in after it?"

Malik's head came up.

The backhand caught her before she even saw it start, hard enough to snap her head sideways and drop her against the crate behind her.

I thought you had this, Dana managed, once the ringing let her think in words again.

I do. Faith, unbothered, almost fond about it. People lose focus when they lose their temper.

Malik was already turning back to the camera, already reaching for the next line of his own speech, temper spent and forgotten as fast as it had come. He never saw her hand move the second time, quiet and small and nothing at all like the first, closing around the key and drawing it free of the lock while his whole attention went back to the reporter, the lens, and the five million people he was about to threaten all over again.

Ok. Now quick. Faith's voice snapped from settled to urgent in the space of one breath. To the stairwell.

Dana was already moving, key fisted tight, sneakers near silent on the concrete. She hit the stairwell door and looked down, and stopped dead.

Two floors below, the rest of the crew was already spread out across the landing, guns up, watching the door like they'd known exactly which way she'd run before she did.

No. Faith, flat, redirecting without missing a beat. Up. Not down.

She went up instead, two steps at a time, lungs burning inside a minute. Malik's shout chased her up the stairwell, closer with every landing. Faith called every turn a half second before Dana's own eyes caught up to it, left, not right, right's a dead end, he's slower than he looks, don't waste breath looking back, door at the top, go.

The roof door gave onto open air and a wash of city noise she hadn't heard in what felt like a lifetime, wind and traffic and, underneath both of them, something bigger, a low mechanical roar climbing steadily out of the distance that hadn't been there a minute ago.

Faith?

Not now. Keep moving.

She backed toward the edge instead of away from it, the whole roof suddenly too small and too open at once. The chain went up and over her own head before Faith could get out more than half a word of objection, key settling cold against her collarbone, hers now, attached, not something anybody was going to talk her out of just handing back.

Malik came through the door a breath later, two of his men right behind him, and stopped short at what he found waiting for him.

It ran the way these things always seemed to, in the movies and, apparently, in real life too: him advancing, her backing along the edge to match him step for step, the key held out over open air the whole time so he'd know exactly what happened if he pushed it.

Her heel caught something solid, cold lattice-metal instead of rooftop concrete, and there wasn't anywhere left to go but up.

Climb. Faith, steady, relentless. Don't look down, don't look at him, just climb. Hands first. Feet find their own way.

She climbed. It wasn't graceful, hands finding gaps in the lattice more by feel than sight, the chain catching once on a strut and yanking hard enough at her neck to punch a gasp out of her before she worked it loose. Every foot of height put more air under her and less rooftop, until she was standing on the boom itself, the crane's long arm stretching out over open space toward nothing at all.

Out. Faith didn't slow down for her to catch up. Onto the arm. Keep the key clear. Don't stop till I tell you to.

She went, one hand sliding along the cable strung the length of it, sneakers finding the narrow lattice rail underneath, the ground reduced to a smear of color she didn't let herself actually look at. Faith walked her through every foot of it after that, where to plant her weight, when to breathe, the cable swinging gently under her the second she trusted it with any of hers.

Malik didn't hesitate at the base of the crane the way she had. He came up after her fast and sure-footed, like heights had never once been a problem for a man like him, and followed her out onto the arm anyway. He always was going to.

The roar behind them had stopped being distant.

I think the cavalry's arrived, Faith said, something almost like relief creeping in around the edges of it.

Huh?

You hear that? Faith. That's a gun putting out more rounds a second than anything handheld ever could. Big ones, too.

She looked down in time to watch the jet all but erase an entire floor of the building, glass and light and something that used to be a wall gone in the same half second, a sound like the sky itself tearing along one clean seam.

Yep. Faith, almost admiring. Exactly what it sounded like.

Malik didn't stop moving. If anything, whatever had just happened four floors down put more urgency into his own steps out onto the boom, not less. Dana kept backing away from him along the lattice, key still cold against her collarbone, the ground so far down it had stopped registering as a real place with real consequences and started reading more like a very bad photograph instead.

They stopped moving eventually, the boom running out of usable length in front of her, Malik still ten feet back and closing an inch at a time, like he had all day for it.

"Come on, child." His voice carried easy across the gap, gentle, and somehow worse for it than shouting would have been. "Give me the key. Don't you want to live? I give you my word."

"Fuck off, pencil dick."

It came out flat and instant, no hesitation in it anywhere, nothing like the panic that had leaked through the last time her own mouth had stopped being entirely her own.

That one was mine, Faith said, easy, almost pleased with herself. Figured it was my turn.

Her own hands moved next, without her deciding a single part of it, working the chain back up over her head and out into open air, key swinging loose at the end of it now, dangling over a drop that didn't have a bottom worth thinking about anymore.

"See this?" Still not entirely her own voice, same borrowed confidence riding her mouth like it had always belonged there. "You so much as breathe wrong and this takes a dive. Then what? Then you're just a two-bit psycho with a soft dick and an empty box, twenty stories up, and jack shit to show for it."

Malik's jaw went tight.

Keep going, Faith said, quieter now, just for her. He's rattled. Rattled's good.

"Or," Faith went on, Dana's own mouth carrying every word of it, "you could jump. Save everybody the trouble. Or better yet, use that gun for something more useful than waving it around."

Then the plane itself came up out of nothing, rising into the space in front of her like it had torn a hole straight through the air to get there, filling the whole gap between the building and the sky until there wasn't room left for anything else to exist. Behind the glass of it, hands on the stick, was her father.

Yep. Faith again, quieter this time, something settling into place behind it. That nails it.

What the actual fuck?! It came out of her before she'd decided whether it was meant for Faith or Malik or the entire universe at large.

I had my suspicions on this one.

You'll have to explain that one. She didn't take her eyes off the plane, off him. When we're not dangling a thousand feet in the air.

Fair. A beat, Faith's attention clearly split between the conversation and something else entirely. He's not as good behind that stick as he thinks he is. Watch, he's gonna bump the gantry. Be ready. When he's steady and he's right under you, let go.

WHAT?! Dana's hands closed on the cable hard enough to feel it bite. Just let go? A million miles up?

Parachutists do it all the time.

I don't HAVE a parachute!

You've got something better. Faith, steady, sure, not even a flicker of doubt in it, smug enough to slice like pie. Your dad. Behind the stick of a jet.

One of Malik's men had a rifle. Malik took it off him without asking, without even looking, eyes already locked on the jet, and opened up on the windshield the second Harry brought the nose around.

Harry peeled off hard, tailfin catching the gantry on the way out with a shriek of metal Dana felt through her own back teeth, the boom lurching hard behind her at the impact. Malik's rifle, still raised, still firing, went with the jolt, torn clean out of his hands, gone, it didn't fall clear, it snagged, somewhere back along the plane's own fuselage, hanging off an after-fairing near the tail like it had decided to switch sides.

Below her, the jet finally held rock steady for one full second, Harry's voice carrying up even over the engine noise. "Jump! I've got you! Trust me!"

He's got you, Faith said, right on top of it, immediate, unhesitating. Let go. Now.

She let go.

The nose of the plane came up to meet her before she'd finished falling, glass and metal instead of open air, hard enough to knock the breath clean out of her. Her hands found nothing at first, sliding, and then her father's own hand closed around her wrist, wrenching her the rest of the way onto the fuselage.

"Hang on!" Harry, shouting over the engine, stick still gripped in his other hand. "Just hang on!"

Just hang on. Faith, right behind it, like it needed saying twice to actually count. You heard the man.

Behind them, on the crane, Malik watched his own rifle ride away on the back of the one plane in the whole sky worth chasing, and didn't waste a second deciding what to do about it. He went after it, off the boom and onto the tail of the plane, empty-handed and furious and nowhere near finished.

Dana screamed, the sound tearing out of her before she could stop it. Harry's eyes cut back for half a second, catching the shape of Malik landing on the fuselage behind them. Then his eyes cut sideways instead, fast, to something Dana couldn't see yet from where she was pinned flat against the nose.

Hang on, Faith said, quiet and certain underneath everything else happening at once. For the ride of a lifetime.

The helicopter came around the building's edge a half second later, door open, a gunner already swinging his weapon into line. Harry banked hard, cutting fast around one whole side of the building and then the other, the gunship falling in right behind like the fight had just opened a second front. Cannon fire chewed long furrows into open ocean out past the building, missing. Tracer fire raked a ragged stripe up the face of a neighboring tower, missing that too. He couldn't shake Malik loose without shaking her loose too, and he clearly knew it, every maneuver pulled up just short of what it could have been, less a clean battle than two very fast, very lethal things trying to occupy the same airspace and mostly failing to hit anything at all.

There was nothing for her to do in any of it. No boom to climb, no key to dangle, not even a mouth worth borrowing for this part. Just her own two hands locked white-knuckled on whatever she could reach, flattened against the nose of a fighter jet while a war happened all around her, close enough to feel the heat of it, like she was cargo instead of a person, like the only thing that actually mattered up here was the fight itself.

Steady. Steady. Faith, low and even, right through all of it. Just hold on. He's got to survive before he can help you.

Harry glanced back to check on Malik, on the threat riding his own tail, and caught something else in the same look: a janitor in the office tower dead astern of them, pushing a vacuum in slow, even lines, headphones clamped over his ears, entirely unaware that a dogfight was about to end up in his cubicle. One full second, staring at something so ordinary it didn't belong anywhere near tonight. One second too many.

The jet went in tail first, punching through glass and drywall and a cubicle wall in a spray of sparks and falling ceiling tile before Harry ever got it back under him. The janitor never even got the vacuum shut off. Headphones still on, he didn't notice a thing until the tail assembly was sitting six inches off the end of his own nose, and by then there was nothing left to do but stand there, frozen in the wreckage of somebody's cubicle wall, with absolutely no framework whatsoever for processing it. The whole office lit up around them for one long, ridiculous second before Harry wrenched it back out the way it had come.

Malik didn't have anything to hold onto for that part. He went backward too, flung clean off the fuselage, and came down hard, nuts-first, straddling the tail stabilizer with a sound that made Dana wince even over the engine noise, and an expression that hit every male member of the Gallery at once, a single involuntary full-body wince that needed no further explanation whatsoever.

He didn't stay down. He looked up, furious, and found the Kalashnikov hanging right there below him, snagged on the fairing exactly where the gantry had left it.

He grabbed it and opened fire, wild, indiscriminate, missing everything in the entire recorded history of ever, rounds sparking off metal and vanishing into open sky in every direction except anywhere useful.

"Daddy, behind you!"

Harry looked back. Looked at her, just for a second, checking. Then his eyes cut sideways again, the same look from before.

Get ready for the ride of a lifetime. Faith again, the same line she'd already used once tonight, apparently not above repeating herself when it still fit.

Harry rolled the plane hard, some maneuver too fast for Dana to actually follow, and when the world stopped spinning long enough to make sense again, Malik wasn't on the stabilizer anymore. He wasn't holding onto anything at all, in fact. He was hanging from something instead, the Kalashnikov's own sling snagged somehow around the nose of the last remaining missile, his entire weight swinging off a loop of canvas webbing while he dangled below it, boots kicking empty air, still firing the gun one-handed like letting go of the trigger had simply never occurred to him as an option.

The helicopter kept circling, patient, waiting for an opening that hadn't come yet, and Malik kept not hitting it, and Harry, very slowly, started lining up a shot that made no sense from any angle Dana could actually see, aiming not at the helicopter itself but at the ragged, smoking hole they'd punched through the office tower a minute ago.

"You're fired."

He said it quiet. He said it like the whole rest of the night had just been waiting on those two words to be ready.

The missile dropped away and lit and went exactly where he'd aimed it, straight through the wreckage of somebody's ninth-floor conference room and out the other side, two hundred extra pounds swinging off its own nose cone the entire way, every actual law of physics filing a formal complaint and getting overruled anyway, and found the helicopter waiting right where he'd said it would be.

"Man," somebody in the anonymous rows said, into the aftermath. "That was more satisfying to watch than it probably was to actually line up. Not sure how any of that worked." A beat. "But whatever."

Harry reached back and hauled her the rest of the way into the cockpit, settling her onto his lap like she weighed nothing at all.

"Don't touch the stick, baby."

She stared at him. At all of him, the stranger who'd apparently been living inside her own father this whole time, wearing his face like it had never once been a disguise.

He grinned. Raised one eyebrow. Waggled the plane's wings, just slightly, just enough to feel it in her stomach.

"Hi, pumpkin."

Down below, on a stretch of street the police had cleared without ever quite knowing what for, real Helen was already running before the landing gear had finished settling into the pavement, hair a wreck, dress torn at one shoulder, looking like she'd fought her own entire war tonight and hadn't stopped moving since. She didn't wait for a ladder. She just reached up, and Harry handed their daughter down into her arms like it was the most natural motion either of them had ever made, and for a long moment neither of them said a single word, just held on.

"...and the crowd goes wild!" Gib, sports-announcer voice, already halfway out of his seat, both arms going up like he'd personally kicked the winning field goal.

The whole Gallery went off at once, noise breaking over the room like something that had been dammed up the entire chapter and had just found the exit all at the same time.

"HE FLEW A HARRIER JET" Somebody, several rows back, not even bothering to finish one thought before starting the next. "HE JUST COMMANDEERED A MILITARY AIRCRAFT WITH HIS BARE HANDS AND I'M SUPPOSED TO ACT LIKE THAT'S A NORMAL TUESDAY FOR THIS FAMILY..."

"That's the job," Fury said, not even trying to hide how pleased he sounded about it.

"That's not a job. That's a superpower with a pension plan..."

"Somebody give Trilby his 'I told you so.' He called the tactical read a whole chapter ago..."

Trilby didn't say anything. Just took a long pull on the cigar and let the smoke answer for him.

"Gibbs called it too!"

"Gibbs always calls it. That's not news, that's a Tuesday."

Gibbs himself got in on it too, for once, coffee cup lowered just long enough to say his piece. "Twenty years in the Corps. I know exactly how a Sidewinder's supposed to fly." A beat, dry as the rest of him. "That wasn't it."

Xander was on his feet, both fists in the air, shouting something that stopped resolving into actual words by the third syllable, pure noise, matching the anonymous rows for once instead of standing apart from them the way he usually did.

Somewhere in the middle of it all, Harry laughed. Real laughter, unguarded, the first sound he'd made all chapter that wasn't rage or grief wearing rage's clothes, like something in his chest had finally been given permission to let go along with the rest of the room.

Through all of it, two people still didn't move, and both of them had settled into the exact same pose. Buffy had her boots up on the row in front of her, slouched back with the complete, unbothered ease of someone who already knew how tonight ended. A few rows up, ROB-Helen had her own feet propped just the same way, up on a row that wasn't actually there to prop them on, gravity apparently as unconvinced by the arrangement as everyone else in the theater, and just as unable to do anything about it.

"Would somebody explain to me," Fury said, raising his voice over the noise, "how you two are the only calm people in a five-hundred-seat theater right now?"

"Because we already knew," ROB-Helen said, not even looking away from the screen.

"I've known Faith longer than any of you have." Buffy, deadpan, arms crossed the exact same way. "This was never actually in question."

"Show-offs," somebody muttered, and got laughed at for it, and didn't seem to mind one bit.
