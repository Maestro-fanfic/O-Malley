Chapter 1: Freedom

"Faith Lehane" (POV):

The branch didn't get any lighter for having two people on it, but it went faster, and by the time they'd wrestled it clear of the fence line the worst of the storm had eased off into a steady, ordinary rain, the kind that soaked through a poncho eventually anyway if you stood in it long enough being sincere at each other.

"So," Xander said, wiping mud off his hands and onto jeans that were already a lost cause. "Home-cooked meal. You weren't kidding."

"Told you." Faith led the way up the porch steps, shaking rain out of her hair. "Don't get used to it. I only cook when somebody's earned it hauling a tree around with me."

"I'll take it. High bar, but I'll take it."

Inside, the house smelled like woodsmoke and somebody's abandoned homework, and Deb caught them both in the front hall, a dish towel over one shoulder, openly delighted by the sight of Faith Lehane, still dripping in that same olive-drab poncho, standing on her own welcome mat next to an equally soaked stranger.

"You're doing a face," Deb said. "I've literally never seen that face. What is that face."

"I don't have a face."

"Enough with 'the face'!" someone in the anonymous rows said, indignant. "That's Donna's bit! West Wing did this joke first!"

"Nobody's checking, Sorkin. Sit down."

"I'm just saying, there's a version of this where somebody owes somebody a residual..." The voice gave up, muttering something about walk-and-talks, and didn't get another word in for the rest of the scene.

"You've got a face," Deb said, entirely too pleased with herself. "It's new. I'm framing it."

"Go help with dinner or go to your room, those are the options."

Deb went, laughing, dragging a curious sideways look at Xander on her way past that Faith chose not to acknowledge.

Dinner was simple, whatever Faith had on hand made to stretch for two more mouths than she'd planned on, and it went easier than it had any right to, six girls, Faith still in her poncho because she hadn't gotten around to taking it off, and one very soaked stranger, all crowded around one table, Xander somehow already on a first-name basis with half of them by dessert. Faith watched him do it without trying, the same easy, unforced way he did everything, and didn't examine too closely why it made her chest feel tight in a good way instead of a warning one.

Later, dishes done, girls scattered to their rooms, Xander was the one who spotted it, an ad clipped to Faith's own fridge with a magnet, half-buried under grocery lists: the Bijou downtown was running a Sergio Leone triple bill all week, and tonight, of all nights, happened to be the one with the poncho in it.

"Okay," he said, tapping the clipping. "That's not a coincidence. That's the universe handing me material."

"You are not making me go watch a western. In this weather."

"Rain's basically stopped. I'm not making you do anything. I'm just saying, there's a poncho on record, there's a squint on record, and the actual movie is playing forty minutes from now, and it feels weird not to finish the bit."

She was already grabbing her jacket, real this time, before he finished the sentence.

The Bijou sat on the corner of Main and nothing much, a single screen, sticky floors, a marquee that had clearly been running the same revival series for weeks. The lot was nearly empty, most of the town smart enough to stay in on a night like this, and the kid at the concession stand looked personally thrilled to have someone, anyone, to sell popcorn to.

They were still in line for tickets when a woman near the box office window did a double take and lit up like she'd won something. "Xander? Xander Harris?"

"Carrie. Hey." He said it easy enough, but Faith caught the half-second of scrambling underneath it, a man doing quick math on how a conversation was about to go. "Small world."

"Small town, mostly." Carrie's eyes flicked to Faith, curious, cataloging, and back to Xander, waiting.

"Right, yeah." Xander cleared his throat. "Carrie, this is Faith. Carrie's, uh. Old girlfriend. Long story, back before Sunnydale went into the ground. We should probably grab seats before the good ones are gone."

"Good seeing you," Carrie said, already moving off, easy about it, whatever awkwardness there might once have been apparently settled a long time ago on her end.

Faith didn't say anything. She bought her own ticket, exact change, and didn't look at him while she did it, something small and sharp sitting in her chest that she didn't have a name for yet and didn't especially want to go looking for one.

"Oh, that's not gonna go unpacked," Gladly's voice said, delighted, from somewhere in the rows. "That is a face. Somebody's jealous and doesn't know what to do about it."

"Leave it alone," Fury said. "She'll get there on her own time. Everybody does, eventually."

"There she is," a voice said, somewhere that wasn't anywhere, the way it never was. "Wondered when we'd get the other side of this one."

"Told you," another voice said, quieter, satisfied in a way that didn't need to raise itself to be heard. "That's the one she's gonna keep. Said so myself, a while back now. Nice to see the receipts."

Neither Faith nor Xander heard a word of it. The house lights were already going down.

The film opened on a dusty nothing town and a man riding into it who didn't say much and didn't need to, and about four minutes in, right on cue, the poncho showed up on screen and Xander made a sound like he'd been personally vindicated by the universe.

"There it is," he said. "That's you. That's the actual guy right there."

"I hate you."

"You wore that exact poncho. Tonight. In front of witnesses."

"And still wearing it, no less," someone in the anonymous rows noted, delighted. "Didn't even take it off for the movie. This narrator respects the commitment."

"I will throw you out into that storm."

"He won't leave," Fury's voice noted, dry. "She won't throw him. Watch."

"This narrator has seen approximately four hundred gunfights across eleven prior entries," another voice put in, warming to it, "and this remains, somehow, still the most anatomically confident quick-draw in the genre. Man's clearing leather in under a second in a coat that heavy. Someone buy this stuntman a drink, wherever he is."

Faith didn't hear any of that either, but she didn't need to, because she and Xander had started doing the exact same bit on their own, low-voiced, heads bent together in the dark.

"Nobody reloads that fast," she said, twenty minutes in, watching somebody empty a six-shooter and immediately have six more shots. "Nobody. I've watched vamps get dusted faster than that gun's supposedly working."

"In fairness, vamps don't usually stop to monologue first."

"Some of 'em do. You'd be amazed."

"Okay, but the real crime," Xander said, "is nobody in this whole town knows how a ricochet works. That bullet should not have hit three separate barrels on its way to that guy's hat."

"You're not wrong."

"I'm never wrong about ricochets. It's a gift. Vaguely useless gift, but a gift."

She caught herself smiling, not the performed one, the other one, the one she didn't usually let out where anybody could clock it, and didn't bother putting it away, not tonight.

Somewhere past the halfway mark, the film slowed down for a stretch that wasn't shootouts, just a man watching a town eat itself from the outside, choosing, for reasons the movie didn't rush to explain, to actually do something about a family getting torn apart by people with more guns than sense. Nothing loud happened. Just a woman on screen, scared and trying not to show it, and a man who noticed anyway.

Faith went quiet.

It wasn't a big thing. Nobody watching from outside would've caught it, probably. Just her jaw doing something it did sometimes, a half a second of her face forgetting to hold its own shape, thinking about six girls asleep down the hall from an empty room that used to have Robin's stuff in it, thinking about whether she was actually any good at this or just too stubborn to admit she might be failing it quietly, one Tuesday at a time, without anybody telling her.

Xander didn't say anything.

He didn't ask what was wrong, didn't reach for her hand, didn't do the thing some people did where they made your one bad second into a whole conversation you hadn't agreed to have. He just shifted an inch closer in the ratty theater seat, close enough that his shoulder was there if she wanted it, and said, low, eyes still mostly on the screen:

"You're doing good with them. The girls. Just so you know I noticed."

That was it. No follow-up. No fishing for what had actually crossed her face. He'd seen something, she was sure of that much, the same way he always seemed to see things nobody else bothered looking for, and instead of chasing it down he'd just handed her the one true thing he actually knew and let her decide what to do with it.

She didn't say anything back. Didn't need to. She let her shoulder find his, and stayed there for the rest of the movie.

"Huh," Gladly's voice said, softer than his usual register. "That was almost subtle. Since when does he do subtle?"

"Since the alternative was making it worse," Fury said. "Some things you don't stand on with a spotlight. You just make sure you're standing there at all."

By the time the credits rolled, the rain had finally quit, and the two of them walked out past the Bijou's flickering marquee into a night gone properly clear, washed clean, small-town dark with actual stars in it if you bothered to look.

They didn't bother. They talked instead, the whole walk back, about nothing in particular, whether the movie's math on bullets actually held up, whether Deb was going to burn the kitchen down eventually on general principle, whether Cleveland was going to survive one night without him. He told her about a supply run gone sideways, some new Slayer's first real scrap ending in a dumpster and a lot of laughing about it afterward. She told him about the loan officer who still couldn't quite believe a woman running a house full of teenage girls didn't want to talk about a man cosigning anything. Neither of them was in any particular hurry to get where they were going, and neither of them said so out loud.

Faith's house came up on the left eventually, porch light still on, the branch they'd hauled off the fence line that same afternoon still lying where they'd left it in the wet grass. They ended up on the couch anyway once they were inside, damp shoes by the door, an easy quiet settling over the whole house.

"So," Xander said, stretching his legs out, entirely too pleased with himself. "Vindicated. Officially. On the record."

"You are never gonna let the poncho thing go, are you."

"Nope. It's my whole beat now, chronicling you. Working title's 'Clint Eastwood's Rowdy Ohio Years.' It's gonna be huge."

"There is no audience for that."

"There's an audience of one, and she's sitting right here pretending she's not into it."

"God help us all." But she was laughing when she said it, easy, unguarded, nothing underneath it working overtime to keep its shape. He was laughing too, and for a second neither of them said anything else, just sat there in the quiet, the TV off, the house settled and asleep around them, everything about the night still new enough that nothing about it felt safe to assume yet.

She looked at him. He wasn't looking anywhere in particular, just at her, actually, now that she thought about it, and hadn't stopped for a minute.

Faith opened her mouth, working up to asking him the thing she hadn't let herself think all the way through yet, whether he'd want to stick around tonight instead of driving back through whatever the storm had left of the roads, whether that was a thing she was actually ready to ask out loud or just a thing she'd gotten brave enough to almost say.

She never got the words out.

The room went dark around the edges, quiet, gentle, nothing like a fight and nothing like falling asleep, and the last thing Faith Lehane felt, before everything else went with it, was the couch cushion under her and his shoulder still warm against hers and absolutely no idea, yet, that she wasn't going to get to finish that sentence for a very long time.

"MAN," a voice said, somewhere in the anonymous rows, aggrieved on a level that suggested personal investment. "Cockblocked by an omnipotent being. That's gotta be some kind of record."

"Watch it," Fury said, but there wasn't much heat behind it, and nobody in the gallery believed for a second he disagreed.

Chapter 2: Loose Lips

"Faith Lehane" (POV):

Dark, and then not dark, and then a room that hadn't existed a half a second ago and now looked like it had always been there, the particular kind of wrong that didn't announce itself so much as sit there daring you to notice.

Six doors. A coffee machine offering something labeled, in cheerful block letters, PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE. Diplomas on the wall from universities that didn't exist anywhere real. Faith took in exactly none of it consciously, the part of her brain that catalogued rooms for exits and threats running well ahead of the part that might have found any of it strange, and she was already moving before she'd finished arriving, some instinct three steps ahead of thought the way it always was when the floor changed underneath her without asking first.

Another person, standing there like they'd been waiting.

She had them by the front of whatever they were wearing and up against the nearest wall before either of them said a word, feet planted, weight set, every year of Slayer training doing exactly what it was built to do the second the world stopped making sense.

Somewhere, somebody hit a pause button that had no business existing inside a movie theater, and the whole scene froze dead: a woman in a little black dress, six inches off the ground, skirt caught mid-swing, an expression of pure professional affront on a face that, in real life, had never once been handled like a full-grown Marine.

"...I'm keeping this," a voice said, appreciative, from somewhere in the anonymous rows. "This is going on a wall somewhere."

"Put it back," Fury said, unimpressed.

"Somebody's framing that."

"Who's the hottie?" a new voice asked, apparently unbothered by having just watched Faith Lehane put a full-grown person through a wall with one hand.

"Focus, people. We're in the middle of something."

"I am focused. I'm focused on the hottie."

"There's always one," Gladly muttered, not quite under his breath. "Every entry, there's always one guy who just tunes out the actual plot the second somebody shows up in a dress."

"I can multitask."

A ripple of agreement went through the anonymous rows, entirely too enthusiastic about it, and for a moment the whole gallery sounded less like an audience and more like last call at a bar nobody remembered choosing to go to.

"Little black dress, though. Somebody's got taste."

"And who taught her how to walk in those heels?"

"Hey, I recognize that dress." A different voice, tilting its head at the frozen image. "I've seen her wear it out. Dinner, maybe? Something like that." A pause. "It's supposed to have way more going on, though. Big poufy sleeves, frills all down the front. This is like the stripped-down version."

"And the hair's doing something specific. That's not just 'got ready in a hurry,' that's an actual look. Somebody styled that on purpose."

"Somebody styled it in about ninety seconds, tops." A different voice, closer to the screen, squinting. "Look at the hair, it's still wet. And the dress has got little strings hanging off it, like the tags just got ripped off and nobody had time to check for stragglers."

"Thought you said you'd seen her in this before."

"I have. Never with tags still on it."

"...somebody changed mid-scene."

"That's not a ROB thing." A voice near the back, matter-of-fact about it. "That's not even a costume glitch. She had on this whole fancy number when she left the room, poufy sleeves, the works, and ditched it about halfway down the hall. Had the stripped-down version on underneath it the entire time."

"That's not a costume change. That's the exact look right after she made herself hot in about six feet of space outside a hotel room door: four minutes' warning, one hand fighting a stocking off, the other hand finding a rhinestone garter that had no business surviving a dead sprint, and whatever was on the hall table under the mirror."

"Hey. I recognize that look." The same voice from the back, closer to the screen than a second ago. "There's tape of that look."

"...you watched it?"

"Course I watched it. Wasn't gonna not watch it." No shame in the delivery at all, just a plain statement of fact. "Doesn't mean I should have. Doesn't mean I didn't."

"That's ROB, genius."

"...ROB's a snack?"

"ROB is not a..." Fury caught himself, visibly regrouping. "It's complicated. And frankly none of your business."

The same voice again, slower this time, like the pieces were only now finishing their landing.

"...wait. That's Helen."

The bar-at-last-call energy died all at once.

"Gib?"

"That's Helen. That's actually Helen. I've had dinner at that woman's house." A pause, long enough to be its own punchline. "I once brought the wrong wine to that woman's house."

"Gibson."

"I'm not doing anything! I'm just saying, this is information I did not need to have about someone I have personally watched carve a turkey." A beat. "Also apparently watched on tape. Unrelated. Bad timing on my part."

Nobody argued the point. Nobody really could.

The pause held a beat longer, just long enough for somebody in the front row to lean in close and squint at the frozen image. "Is that... is that a flower petal? In the hair?"

"That's not on purpose. There's a flower vase back down that hall." A beat. "That's where the water for the hair came from. Dunked her hand in, ran it through, didn't check what came with it. Petal just went along for the ride."

"...and you know that because."

"I watched the tape."

"Nobody asked you to keep watching it."

"Nobody asked me to stop, either."

The scene resumed, unbothered by any of it, the way it always did.

"What the hell! I was about to get my groove on!"

"No you weren't. You're not ready. And Xander knows that."

Faith went still. It had nothing to do with letting go.

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?"

Nothing. No answer, no flicker of acknowledgment that she'd said anything at all. The figure just moved, unbothered by six inches of Slayer strength pinning them to drywall, brushing past the question like it had never been asked, already crossing the room, already gesturing at the six doors with the easy, practiced rhythm of someone who'd given this particular speech more than once before.

"I wanted to leave you completely unfettered for this one. Reasons of my own that will become very clear shortly." A pause, brisk, almost bureaucratic, the tonal opposite of what had just come out of the same mouth thirty seconds earlier. "But I overruled me. I can be such a dick sometimes. I said that we were gonna lock down everything but that smart mouth of yours this time. Wouldn't take no for an answer. Bastard!"

Faith stared. Whatever grip she'd had loosened without her quite deciding to let go of it.

"...the FUCK!?"

"That mouth of yours is gonna get you in trouble some day."

The edges of the room had already started going soft, color bleeding out of the corners first, the whole impossible space folding itself quietly toward dark the way a held breath finally let go. Faith opened her mouth, a dozen better questions lined up behind the one that hadn't landed anywhere, and got exactly none of them out before the last of the light went with everything else.

"...might get you out of trouble too."

The voice followed her down into the dark, unhurried, already sounding like it knew something she didn't, and then there was nothing at all.

Chapter 3: Blowback

"Helen Tasker" (POV):

The gallery had no doors that led anywhere real, but it had an entrance all the same, and Helen Tasker came through it stage left, heels loud on a floor that wasn't actually there, scanning the rows like a woman who had already decided exactly who she was looking for.

She found him fast.

Fury didn't even get all the way to his feet before she'd crossed the distance, and the anonymous rows went dead silent, the way a crowd goes quiet right before something worth telling people about later.

At least one voice clocked exactly where this was headed a full two seconds before it happened, and said nothing. Warning Fury wasn't worth becoming the second target in the room. Wiser to just watch.

She hit him dead in the mouth before he'd gotten a single word out, one clean shot, no windup, thrown by a woman who'd clearly had time to think about it.

Fury worked his jaw, tasting blood, and didn't look remotely surprised by any of it. "Hello, Helen."

"Fuck you!"

"Feel better?"

"Don't you dare." Helen rounded on him, voice shaking with something that hadn't decided yet whether it was rage or something closer to grief. "I asked for one thing. One. Keep her hands off my daughter, whatever else happens, whatever it costs. That's all I asked you for, right here, right in front of everybody."

"And we kept it."

"Then explain to me," she said, sweeping an arm to take in the whole gallery, every row of it, "how every single person in this room got a front-row seat to me getting dressed in a hallway thirty years ago."

"You know how we work, Helen. There are always more reasons than you see."

Nobody in the anonymous rows made a sound. Nobody wanted to be first.

"And don't think I don't know what you just said, Gib."

She didn't wait for an answer. She turned on her heel and sat, arms crossed, radiating the specific stillness of a woman who had said everything she'd come to say and was now simply waiting to see who was foolish enough to make her say more.

"...'the specific stillness of.' Again." A voice, flat, from the back. "That's twice this chapter. We're doing this again?"

"We are always doing this again," Fury said, still working blood out of his teeth. "Ask Taylor's gallery. Ask literally anyone who's sat through more than one of these."

"Somebody send the author a thesaurus."

"Somebody send the author a word count that isn't 'specific.'"

Several rows back, the voice who had spent a significant portion of the previous scene Very Focused On The Hottie folded himself down low enough to nearly vanish beneath the seat in front of him, sweating through what this narrator can only describe as a genuine crisis of bladder control, and did not breathe again for a good long while.

Fury spat, checked his hand for blood, found some, and didn't bother wiping it off. "Somebody take that remote away from Gib before I smack him."

"Sorry, boss," Gib said, to no one Fury could account for, already rubbing the back of his head.

Nobody asked which remote. Nobody needed to.

The scene rolled.

Chapter 4: Petty Cash

It was still snowing inside the little glass dome when Harry set it in her hands, some souvenir village he'd picked up wherever he'd actually been instead of wherever he'd told them he'd been. Dana turned it over once, unimpressed, and said something that passed for thanks. Harry kissed the top of her head anyway, told her to get moving or she'd be late for school, and was gone before the glass had even finished settling.

She looked at it a second longer once he was out of the room, decided it was exactly as lame as it looked, and dropped it straight into the wastebasket on her way past.

Down the hall, Harry sidestepped the dog on his way to the bedroom. "Feed Gizmo before you leave," he called back, already thinking about something else.

Helen was picking out an outfit, hair still wrapped in a towel, and neither of them looked at the other while they talked, the same easy rhythm of two people who'd been doing this every morning for fifteen years.

"I'm late," Harry said.

"Me too."

"How'd it go at the trade show?" Helen asked, pulling a blouse off its hanger. "Bet you made all the other salesmen jealous."

Harry lit up, launching into some elaborate account of a new ordering system, how the second a customer's name went into the computer it pulled their whole credit history, checked what they'd bought before, calculated a discount on the spot, on and on, enthusiasm doing most of the actual lying for him.

Helen had stopped tracking the specifics somewhere around the second sentence. Fifteen years married to the man, and she genuinely had no idea what he actually did all day. It had simply never once occurred to her to wonder harder about it.

"That's fabulous, Harry," she said, not really listening.

"Yeah. It was wild." He watched her brush her teeth, oblivious to him in exactly the same way, and smiled to himself. The best lies were always the ones told with real enthusiasm behind them.

"So how was your day?" Harry asked, once he'd run out of steam.

"Well, we had a leak by the water heater." Helen didn't look up from the closet. "I talked to the plumber. He said it's gonna cost six hundred dollars."

"That's okay."

"That's not okay. That's extortion."

"So what'd you tell him?"

"I slept with him. He knocked off a hundred."

"That's good thinking." Harry, already moving, pecked her on the cheek and was gone before she'd fully turned to kiss him back.

Helen kissed the empty air where he'd been a second ago, and went back to picking out an outfit like it hadn't happened.

By the time Gib let himself in the front door, sunglasses already on indoors for no reason anybody had ever gotten a straight answer out of him about, Gizmo had worked up the nerve to come say hello and immediately regretted it. Gib pointed a finger at him. "Don't."

The dog reconsidered his whole life plan and backed off.

"That's gotta be the smartest member of the cast right there."

"Whole rest of the episode, and he's already tapped out. Smartest decision anybody's gonna make all day."

"Somebody get that dog an agent."

Gib slung his jacket over the back of the couch, then, for reasons that made sense only to him, produced a pack of cigarettes and set it on the mantelpiece across the room, nudging it a careful half inch left, then right, then left again, until it satisfied whatever standard he was holding it to.

In the kitchen, Dana was already working on a pop-tart she hadn't bothered toasting, orange juice straight from the bottle, gone again before Gib had even finished pouring two cups of coffee.

"Hey, kid."

"Hey, Gib."

Harry came in a beat later and Gib handed him the second cup without being asked.

"Thanks, dear."

"Check these out." Gib held out the sunglasses, entirely too pleased with himself.

Harry turned them over, found nothing unusual about them at a glance, and put them on anyway. Somewhere in the corner of the left lens, a small, grainy image of his own living room flickered to life.

"Where's the picture coming from?"

"CCD camera and a transmitter, tucked right inside a pack of smokes." Gib nodded toward the mantel, delighted with himself. "Pretty slick, huh?"

"Mhm." Harry meant it the way a man means anything he's only half listening to.

"Man, we're starting off early with these. 'The way X means Y.' Chapter's not even half done."

"Give the guy a break," Fury said, not looking up. "He's planning, outlining, and re-reading a few thousand words of this stuff every single day. Something's gonna slip through."

"Yeah, he couldn't do it at all without a 'certain assistant' tho."

Through the lens, Dana wandered into frame, glancing back toward the kitchen before she moved.

Her fingers found the edge of the wallet before the rest of her had fully committed to the idea, muscle memory outrunning conscience the way it always did lately. Gib's jacket. She knew it by the beat-up leather and the faint smell of aftershave that had never once in her memory smelled like anything else.

Her fingers closed around the wallet.

The room tilted.

In the half second before it all settled, before the world finished deciding what shape it was going to be, Faith clocked the little lens on the mantle, aimed square at the couch, the kind of thing nobody sets up for no reason.

Professional habit didn't care that she didn't have a body yet. It was the only thing she still had, the last sliver of a window before whatever was coming shut the rest of it down, and she used it exactly the way she'd have used it in her own skin.

She winked at it.

"Wait." A voice, indignant. "That's a breach of the rules right there. We just discussed the rules not five minutes ago!"

"Door's still closing," Fury said, unbothered. "Give it a second."

Through the glasses, Harry watched his own daughter's hand disappear into Gib's jacket and come out with a fistful of twenties.

"Did I just see her wink at the camera?"

"What the hell."

"DANA!"

Harry was already on his feet, glasses off, coffee abandoned, moving for the door with Gib right behind him.

Outside, a motorcycle was already idling in the driveway, Dana halfway across the yard toward it and the gangly, too-cool boy waiting astride it, earbuds in, music cranked loud enough that Harry could hear the tinny buzz of it from the porch, a helmet already going on over the top of them.

"Dana!" Harry hit the porch at a dead run. "Dana, wait!"

Hot coffee he didn't even remember still holding sloshed clean over his hand on the second step, and he didn't so much as slow down for it.

"She's ripping you off." He sounded almost impressed, in spite of himself, in spite of the fact that his own daughter was practically swinging her leg over the bike by the time the words got all the way out.

"Okay, fine. But do it at the same time."

He's been running this exact same early-attack strategy for a year now, Faith observed, somewhere behind Dana's own eyes, warm as anything else at this table. Still hasn't figured out we've clocked it.

Don't tell him. It's the only advantage I've got left.

"Okay, ready?" Dana said.

"Do it properly," Helen said, amused.

All three together, not quite synced: "One, two, three, four, I declare a thumb war. Five, six, seven, eight, try to keep your thumbs straight."

Harry went for both of them at once, thumbs working independently, watching for an opening on either side.

"Dad, wait! Stop! You have to wait!" Dana yanked for her own thumb, too late. He'd already caught it.

"I got you!" His grin was enormous, entirely too pleased with himself for a man in his forties who'd just won a thumb war against his own teenage daughter.

The phone started ringing in the kitchen.

Helen tried to reset their hands, patient about it. "Right. Just listen. Listen to the rules." She glanced toward the sound, already halfway to letting go.

She pushed back from the table and crossed into the kitchen while Harry and Dana went right back to resetting their own grip.

"Okay, here. Okay, again. One, two, three..." Dana stopped herself. "No, start over."

Helen lifted the receiver. "Hello?"

Whatever came through it changed her face fast, ordinary domestic attention sharpening into something else entirely.

There it is, Faith said, quiet.

Dana felt the shift the same instant Faith did, the two of them arriving at the same read half a beat apart from two different directions, same as it had gone for a year now.

"We're on," Helen said into the phone, and turned to find Harry already looking at her, already understanding, already rising out of his chair.

He crossed to stand behind her shoulder while she listened another moment, composed now, professional in a way that had nothing left in it of the woman who'd been losing a thumb war two minutes ago.

"Yes? Go ahead."

Dana didn't wait to be told. She was already off the stairs and moving, homework forgotten, a year's worth of practice carrying her the rest of the way there without needing a single instruction.

Ready? Faith asked anyway. She always asked anyway.

Ready.

✳

The chandelier came first, all faceted glass and gilded molding, before the rest of the room ever widened out to take it in. Live orchestral music filled a ballroom dressed in flags and curtained arches and uniformed officers, white-jacketed waiters threading between tables loaded with more silver than Dana had ever seen in one place in her life.

Harry moved through it in a white dinner jacket and black bow tie. Helen wore black, gown and upswept hair and a long black cigarette holder she used less for smoking than for punctuation, raising it whenever she wanted an excuse to look somewhere without looking like she was looking.

An officer passed close enough to greet.

"Colonel, how nice to see you again," Helen said, warm, effortless.

"Good evening," he said, and kept moving.

Harry watched him go. "Who was that?"

Helen didn't stop long enough to answer. They kept moving, kept scanning, two more well-dressed strangers in a room full of them.

Dana sat at her own table near the edge of the floor, one earbud tucked in against the noise, doing her best impression of a bored teenager she'd rehearsed for a solid week in her bedroom mirror. Gib's voice came through the same earbud a moment later, transmitting from somewhere blue-lit and cramped, a technician working equipment behind him she couldn't see.

"What's the scoop, team? You see your contact yet?"

"Haven't seen him yet," Helen murmured, not looking anywhere near conspicuous about it.

Then Harry's whole expression changed, recognition sliding in under the professional blank he'd been wearing all night. "But I see somebody I'd like to say hello to."

Oh, this is gonna be good, Faith said, and wouldn't explain why no matter how many times Dana asked.

Across the room, a waiter was busy being someone else's idea of a secret agent. He leaned in close to an unimpressed brunette, champagne bottle balanced on his forearm like a prop, working his own cover story a little too hard.

"Let me pour you some more champagne." He lowered his voice like the room might be listening. "I gotta keep up the waiter bit. These stakeouts can be tricky." A theatrical scan of the room. "You never know when things might explode into a life-or-death situation."

Harry closed the last of the distance without him noticing.

"If it gets rough, stay low till it's over, I'll contact you later." One more line, leaning in. "Maybe you should give me your..."

Harry's arm came around him from behind before he got the rest of it out.

"So. We meet again, Carlos."

"CARLOS?!" Half the anonymous rows lost their minds at once. "That's CARLOS? That's PISS BOY? That's the actual mapping, right there, on the actual page, verbatim..."

"Called it," somebody said, unbothered.

"Nobody called it, we didn't even know there WAS a Carlos to call back in Chapter Two..."

"I'm retroactively calling it. That's allowed."

Simon's eyes went wide, the whole confident secret-agent posture gone in an instant. The brunette watched, abandoned mid-conversation, as the man she'd been half-listening to got claimed by two strangers who clearly already knew him.

Oh, I recognize him now. Dana, half to herself. Mom told me about that guy. Even showed me a surveillance shot once.

And your dad's still got his Stingray in the garage, Faith said, apropos of absolutely nothing, dry as anything.

Helen stepped in without hesitation. "Honey, I'm just gonna do him right here, okay?"

Her hand snapped onto his face before he could react, palm clamping his cheek and jaw, forcing his head sideways. Something small and cylindrical pressed up under his chin at the same instant, familiar to anyone who'd been paying attention all book long, close enough to his own throat that he had every reason to assume it was something considerably sharper than lipstick.

"Go for it," Harry said.

"Oh, God," Simon managed, somewhere around the pressure, and lived up to every syllable of his own nickname right there on the ballroom floor, a dark spreading stain nobody standing close enough to notice would ever be polite enough to mention out loud.

She held him there a beat longer than strictly necessary, made her point, and let go. He staggered upright, grasping for whatever dignity he had left, which by that point amounted to considerably less than he'd started the evening with.

Helen leaned in close before he could get any further, voice dropping to a whisper meant for him and nobody else in the room.

"Fear is not an option."

Whatever he'd been trying to rebuild in the last five seconds went with it. He turned and vanished into the crowd without another word.

Helen watched him go, entirely unbothered, uncapped the same tube, and touched up her own lipstick without missing a beat.

Harry watched him go too, all the edge draining back out of his face into something closer to amusement. He turned back to Helen.

She held the cigarette holder near her face, composed again, pleased with herself and not bothering to hide it.

Then something on a nearby table caught his eye: a single red rose in a short vase. He took it without asking anyone's permission and turned back to her with it already between two fingers.

"Dance?"

The orchestra shifted underneath the question, accordion pushing forward over the strings, turning the whole room's rhythm from polite reception into something with actual teeth in it.

Harry raised the rose between himself and Helen, close enough to her face that it was somewhere between an invitation and a dare.

"Tango," Helen said, already answering the challenge before he'd finished making it.

"Here we go," Harry said, and they moved together into a real hold, formal, close, exact.

They were good at it. Genuinely good, not movie-good, the two of them clearly having done this exact dance more than once in whatever life happened between missions. Harry worked the rose between his fingers and his teeth and back again, sharp turns, sudden stops, a dip that left Helen arched back over his arm with her whole trust riding on it and not one flicker of doubt on her face.

Okay, Faith admitted. That's actually kind of impressive.

That's my DAD.

I know. Still impressive.

Gib's patience gave out somewhere in the middle of it. "Harry? Helen? Guys?"

Nothing.

"Come on, now. Let's not get distracted. We got some work to do."

They danced through that too, turn after turn, the crowd around them thinning into background as the two of them kept finding each other on every beat.

"All right, guys. Come on." A pause. "You guys better get serious in there. I'd be serious if I was in there." Another pause, longer. "Don't ignore me, Harry. That's rude."

Somebody's having a rough night, Faith said, not unkindly.

Dana watched her parents dance until they were just two more shapes among a whole floor of couples, indistinguishable except for the way they moved, and let herself feel, for the length of one whole song, like she was allowed to just watch instead of watch the door.

"Helen. I was always on your side." Gib, still trying, something fraying at the edge of his voice now. "I was always there for you."

Nobody answered him.

"You know what?" Something had finally broken loose, however many years he'd been holding it in. "I'm sick of being in the van. You guys are gonna be in the van next time." A beat. "I been in the van for fifteen years."

Back in the ballroom, tight on Dana, still watching the door out of habit more than actual duty at this point.

Something left her.

There wasn't a word for it at first, just a sudden, enormous silence where a voice had lived for exactly one year, a whole shape of presence gone out of her all at once, the way a room changes the instant somebody who'd been standing right behind you steps away. Her hand flew to her own chest without her telling it to. The earbud suddenly sounded very loud and very alone, Gib's voice the only thing left in it.

Faith?

Nothing answered.

Faith!

Still nothing. Not a flicker, not a beat, not the smallest scrap of the constant, familiar weight that had been living behind her own eyes for three hundred and sixty-five straight days.

Whatever her face did, it did it fast. Across the room, Harry's head came up first, some old instinct of his own catching the change in her a beat before Helen's did, and then they were both moving, weaving hard through the dancers, abandoning the tango and the cover and the whole operation without a second's hesitation, because whatever else either of them was tonight, they were her parents first.

✳

Somewhere else entirely, the Gallery had gone quiet in the good way, the kind that didn't need filling, right up until it very much didn't stay that way.

"CARLOS," somebody finally said, the word landing like it had been sitting on the tip of their tongue for ten chapters. "That was really him. Piss boy. Bladder guy. The sign on the bathroom door. All of it, this whole time, and it was ALWAYS gonna pay off on the actual page, verbatim, no invention required."

"Somebody update the bible," Fury said. "Before Gladly wanders over from some other entry and takes credit for calling it."

"She's still got the earbud in," Gib pointed out, not quite believing it himself. "That's not ending it. That's just... continuing it. Differently."

Trilby's cigar smoke curled up from somewhere in the front rows, unhurried as ever. "Girl's got real instincts. Good posture, good cover, eyes never left the door once. Somebody trained her right."

"Somebody did," Faith said, and for once there wasn't a joke riding underneath it.

Xander's voice came from a few rows back, quieter than his usual register. "So that's it? She just... gets to keep it? All of it?"

"That's the whole deal." ROB-Helen. "Always was. She chose to be there tonight, same as every other night this whole book's been about. Nobody's taking that away from her now just because the credits are close."

"Yeah," Faith agreed, from wherever it was Faith actually said things from these days. "That's kind of the whole point."

Fury let out a long breath, the closest thing to sentimental anybody in that room had ever caught him being. "Well. Good for the kid."

"Good for both of 'em," somebody added, unnamed, and nobody in the anonymous rows argued with that either.

"You know what, boys." ROB-Helen, already rising, already somewhere else in her attention before she'd finished the sentence. "This is where I bow out. Places to go, people to do." A beat, unbothered, already halfway gone. "You know how it is."

Chapter 16: Exit Interview

The ballroom didn't go away. That was the strange part, if anything about that theater could still surprise anyone this many chapters in. It just stopped being the only thing on the screen.

A second screen came up beside it, unhurried, like it had always been there and simply hadn't bothered turning on until now. No projector beam, no visible seam where one ended and the other began, just suddenly two of them, side by side, the ballroom still running on the left, Harry and Helen still cutting hard through the crowd toward their daughter, Dana still standing frozen at her own table with one hand pressed flat against her own chest.

On the right, the picture resolved into someplace far plainer. White walls. Fluorescent light. A door that led nowhere real.

The waiting room.

Some of the anonymous rows noticed before others did. A ripple of quiet worked its way backward through the theater as more and more of them clocked what they were actually looking at.

"That's..." somebody started, and didn't finish it, because everybody who'd been here since Chapter Two already knew exactly what they were looking at.

Fade into the waiting room. No screen inside a screen this time, no Gallery framing at all. Wherever this actually was, it was real, in whatever way any of this had ever counted as real.

The first thing Faith noticed was the face.

Same one from the very start of this whole book. Same borrowed mother's face, familiar down to the last detail after a year spent watching it move through somebody else's life, still dressed for the embassy party, black gown, hair up, that same cigarette holder dangling loose from two fingers like she'd walked straight out of the ballroom and hadn't bothered changing for the occasion. Faith didn't wait for an explanation, didn't wait for a word, didn't wait for anything at all.

She hit it dead in the mouth before it had even finished turning toward her, one clean shot, no windup, the same punch Helen herself had thrown at Fury back before any of this had a name yet. This time it was Faith doing the throwing. This time it landed on the one face in the whole story that actually owed her an answer.

Somewhere else entirely, Fury's hand flew to his own jaw. "I felt that." He worked it, genuinely bewildered. "How did I feel that?"

"YOU UTTER BITCH!" Faith was already shouting before ROB-Helen hit the ground, no interest left in pretending otherwise. "How could you do that to your own daughter?!"

ROB-Helen went down hard, one heel catching a chair that hadn't been anywhere in the room a second earlier, and landed flat on her back for it.

She took her time getting up. Straightened her dress. Checked her own lip with two fingers, entirely unbothered by whatever she found there.

"I told you when this all started." Calm. Almost bored. "'Reasons that will become apparent.'" A beat, brushing something off her sleeve that wasn't actually there either. "Just because they're apparent doesn't mean you're seeing them yet."

"Well, clear it the hell up for me!"

"In a minute." She smoothed the last wrinkle out of her dress and looked past Faith, toward the door. "We have more guests coming."

Three shapes resolved out of nothing behind her, a rising hum and a shimmer of gold-white particles sparkling top to bottom, an effect anybody who'd owned a television at any point in the nineties would have recognized on sight, whether or not they could have named where they'd seen it first. The sound faded. The light held its shape. Where a second ago there had been nothing at all, three actual people now stood.

It took Faith exactly one second to recognize all three of them.

They're here. She said it like saying it out loud might make it stop being true. They're not supposed to be here. How are they here?

"You're not the only one owed an explanation," ROB-Helen said, still not remotely worried about any of this.

Real Helen's eyes found her.

Real Helen hit her.

"BITCH!"

ROB-Helen hit the floor.

She lay there a moment, staring up at the ceiling, and let out a long breath.

"Really? Twice in less than a minute?"

Somewhere else entirely, in a theater running an entirely different story, Gladly hit the floor in the exact same pose, blood already welling at his lip, and had absolutely no idea why.

ROB-Helen picked herself up off the floor a second time, entirely unhurried, and took a moment to dust off both palms against her own dress, one leisurely pass smoothing out the wrinkles a punch and a half had put in it. A sequence anybody alive at the time would recognize on sight followed it, whether or not they could have named the first place they'd ever seen it.

She raised one hand to shoulder height.

And snapped her fingers.

The black gown was gone. In its place: a familiar uniform, red across the shoulders and chest, fading down into solid black below, the kind of silhouette that didn't need a single word of explanation for anyone who'd ever owned a television at all.

The room went with it. White walls and fluorescent light gave way to something warmer, wood paneling, low lamps, a fireplace crackling away in a wall that hadn't had one a second earlier. Comfortable armchairs stood arranged in a loose half circle around the hearth, and everyone was already sitting in one, Faith, Dana, Harry, Helen, all of them settled in like they'd been there the whole time and the room had simply gotten around to admitting it.

"And now, to business." ROB-Helen settled back into her own chair like she owned the room, the fireplace, and everyone currently sitting in it. "I owe you all an explanation."

"You owe us more than that." Helen, arms crossed, nowhere near finished being furious. "You owe us a fucking apology."

ROB-Helen brushed right over her, not even a flicker of acknowledgment. "Do none of you see why I did this?" Something almost wounded crept into it, buried again just as fast. "I swear, sometimes I wonder why I even try..." The rest dissolved into muttering, words too quiet and too fast to actually follow.

"What the hell do you mean?" Harry leaned forward, out of patience for anything cryptic.

ROB-Helen turned serious, all the muttering gone out of her at once.

"Look at your daughter, Harry." Dead straight, not a flicker of a joke on her face. "Now look at Faith." A beat. "Now back at your daughter."

Her body changed under that same borrowed face, shoulders squaring, stance widening into something built like it had never once worried about anything in its entire life, unmistakably lifted from one very specific decade-old commercial nobody in that room had actually managed to forget. Same face. Everything else, completely different.

Dana stared. "...What is happening right now."

The only answer she got was a whistle, short, cheerful, unmistakable, the exact same handful of notes that had closed out every single commercial that build had ever starred in, coming from everywhere in the room and nowhere in it at the same time.

ROB-Helen let the pose hold a moment longer than it needed to, entirely pleased with herself, before dropping straight back into serious.

"Do you remember what she used to look like? Before all of this?" She wasn't looking at Harry anymore. She was looking at Dana. "Dana. Have you looked in a mirror lately?"

A mirror simply appeared, the way everything in this room seemed to, propped against nothing in particular, angled just right to catch both of them at once.

Dana looked. Kept looking, because the first look didn't seem to have actually landed yet.

Two faces stared back out of the glass. Hers. And, impossibly, right alongside it, a second one that had no business being anywhere near a mirror at all. Faith didn't have a face. Not one anybody could actually see, not once, not in an entire year of this. And yet there it was, and the longer Dana looked, the less she could find the exact line where one face stopped and the other one started.

"...fuuuuuuuu!!"

Faith didn't wait for the mirror to fade. "Okay, but what about me?" Something raw crept into it, none of her usual armor bothering to show up for this part. "It can't just be because I looked like her!"

ROB-Helen turned to face her fully, all the theater gone out of her voice at once.

"You're right. There was madness to my method." A beat, and for once it didn't sound like a line. "Yours was a lesson just as important. You've been defining yourself as the Slayer for far too many years. You needed to find out how strong you were ready to become, even without that backing you up."

Nobody said anything for a beat that stopped being a beat almost immediately and turned into something closer to eternity, the fire the only thing in the whole room still willing to make a sound.

ROB-Helen dusted off her upper legs and stood, brisk about it, like the whole conversation had already wrapped itself up as far as she was concerned. "Well. It's time for everyone to go back home and..."

"Wait." Faith was on her feet before the sentence finished dying. "You can't just screw us over like that, even if you had a reason! I want something!"

ROB-Helen didn't ask what she meant. Didn't need to.

"I don't know..." Drawn out, almost playful, and then she turned toward Dana instead, and something happened to her voice that had nothing to do with the face it was still coming out of, dropping and changing in the same breath into something the whole room recognized on sound alone, the exact cadence of somebody none of them had actually heard speak for herself in this entry before tonight.

"ARE YOU READY TO BE STRONG?!"

Dana blinked. "What? Huh?" A beat, still catching up. "Well... yeah, I guess."

Something shifted through her the instant the words left her mouth. Her spine straightened without her telling it to. For one brief second she looked almost feral, shoulders dropped, weight settling low and ready into her own feet like her body had just remembered something her mind hadn't caught up to yet. Then it passed, and what was left behind wasn't feral at all. Just poised. Certain. Steady in a way she'd never once been before tonight.

"There." ROB-Helen studied her own handiwork, entirely pleased with herself. "I even gave her a little oomph."

Something moved behind Dana's eyes for just a fraction of a second, old and vast and nothing at all like a fifteen-year-old girl from Virginia, gone again before anyone could be fully sure they'd actually seen it.

"Now." ROB-Helen straightened, brisk again, like the whole conversation really was finished this time. "It's time for everyone to go home."

She raised her hand and snapped her fingers a second time.

The screen went bright white, blinding, total, for one whole second.

Then it faded to black.
