Chapter 8 of 16

Debrief

Dana sat at one end of the dining table with her back mostly to the window. Beyond her shoulder, Harry and Helen faced each other across the remains of dinner. The room around them was warm; the windows at Dana's back were black. For a while, the three of them ate in silence.

Harry drove the serving spoon down into the mashed potatoes hard enough that it cracked against the bowl underneath, a short, ugly sound that made Helen's eyes snap up to his hand before she looked back down at her own plate. By the time he lifted his head again, whatever had been in his face a second ago was gone, folded away someplace Dana couldn't see.

He chewed, swallowed, and started talking like nothing had happened at all.

"I came by your office today." Easy, conversational. "I was in the area, thought you might want to have lunch."

"Really?" Helen didn't look up. "Well, you must have just missed me."

"That's what they said." Harry watched her reach across the table for a serving dish. "They said you had to run out or something like that."

Dana kept her eyes on her own plate, cutting food she wasn't really eating, and said nothing.

"It was this rush job." Helen kept moving the spoon as she talked, like the story needed the motion to keep it going. "They needed these documents down at the district courthouse."

Harry took a drink of water and didn't answer.

"And so, of course, the big problem, our printer shut down." Helen's eyebrows went up, her free hand starting to gesture along with the story now, filling in the shape of a day that had apparently gone very badly. "So I went to the third floor to use theirs, but they've got the 1720, and of course the disk isn't formatted for the 1720, so I had to go back upstairs and reformat, then back downstairs to use their printer, and by the time I got there, 'cause I couldn't even get my car to start..." She lifted both hands briefly, the whole ordeal summed up in the gesture. "And then, well, traffic. I barely got there."

Her hands came back down. She looked at Harry and waited.

Harry held her gaze through a silence that went on a beat longer than it needed to.

"So." The corner of his mouth moved, not quite a smile. "A little excitement in an otherwise dull day." A pause. "Did it work out okay?"

Helen studied him before she answered.

"Yeah."

"Great."

She held his eyes a second longer, then looked away and started to get up. "I'll check on dessert."

"I'm done," Dana said, and stood too, crossing behind her father's chair toward the doorway.

She kept walking until the dining-room wall cut her completely out of her father's sight.

Then she ducked.

Dana folded low and moved quickly along the dark side of the hall, placing each foot where Faith told her the floor was least likely to complain. She slipped into the shadow at the foot of the stairs and settled behind the turn of the wall. From there she could see the dining-room doorway, the kitchen passage, and the hall beyond them. Every way out of the room crossed some part of her view.

She eased one eye around the edge.

Harry was still looking toward the doorway she had apparently gone through. Dana held perfectly still until he turned back toward the table.

Faith?

Yeah.

That printer story. Was that…

A lot of very specific nothing, strung together real careful so there wasn't a gap in it anywhere for him to ask a question into. A beat. Yeah. That's what that was.

Dana didn't have anything else to say to that. She stayed low behind the wall and watched instead.

Whatever her parents were doing to each other, neither one of them was leaving the dining room without Dana seeing which way they went.
