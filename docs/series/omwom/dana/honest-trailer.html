Honest Trailer

You Got to Have Faith: Honest Trailer

[DEEP, GRAVELLY TRAILER-NARRATOR VOICE]

In a world where a mid-life crisis about being a boring computer salesman was actually a cover story for being a stone-cold covert operative...

A cloud of cigar smoke rolled in from off-mic.

"Budge over there, Sparky."

A pause. The sound of someone straightening a lapel that didn't need it.

"Okay. Now, where was I?"

His face fell into shadow as he said it, the way it always seemed to the second he decided a moment had earned the treatment. All that was left to see: one eye, shining out of the dark. The other side wore the patch, scar tissue thick and old around the edges of it, catching just enough of whatever light was left to make sure nobody missed that part either.

"In a world..."

A familiar voice cut in before he could finish it.

"Wait! That's ANOTHER fourth-wall break." A pause, delighted with itself. "Hang on, let me take off my shoes. I gotta count."

Muttering followed, half under its own breath. "...and carry the four, multiply by pi, over the whole run so far, plus this one, 'cause this one's got extra topspin on it..."

"That's gotta be like a hundred and seventy-three walls now."

A beat, like the thought had only just occurred to him that introductions might be nice.

"And who am I, you might ask?"

"I'M MOTHERFUCKING DEADPOOL."

...one entirely unrelated woman is about to get strapped in for the ride, whether she asked for it or not.

She didn't ask for it.

Trilby cleared his throat, loud enough to reclaim the room.

"You've seen Die Hard on a plane. You've seen Die Hard on a boat. You've seen Die Hard on a bus, in a hockey rink, and, for reasons nobody has ever adequately explained, on a plane again, with snakes."

A pause, weighty, letting that sit.

"This is Die Hard, in your own marriage, with an actual nuclear warhead, a teenage daughter running her own version of the exact same night one floor down, and not one single member of this family looping in another until it's already much too late to do anything but improvise."

Another pause.

"This is 'Truer Lies.'" A beat, almost daring anyone to complain about it. "Because, really. Come on."

Watch as a professional spy gets made by his own daughter in a single morning.

Watch as a Slayer with a rap sheet longer than her actual sentence gets locked out of her own body for an entire book, no control transfer, no exceptions, just a mouth, and somehow still manages to almost get everyone killed with that mouth alone. Multiple times. On purpose, twice.

Watch as a fifteen-year-old girl who starts the book stealing twenty bucks from a family friend's coat ends it holding a live nuclear arming key over a twenty-story drop, and somehow that's the SECOND most dangerous thing she does that night.

Watch as an omnipotent entity wearing your mom's face bulldozes over literally every follow-up question anyone ever asks it, for an entire book, and gets away with it every single time because it's also, somehow, extremely likeable.

Watch as one anonymous man's bladder control gets tracked across four consecutive chapters, only to turn out to have been foreshadowing the entire time.

Witness as a man sentences himself to hell with a single phone call.

[BEAT]

Follow along as our heroine spends an entire hotel hallway getting dressed for a mission she doesn't know is a mission, using nothing but a vase of flowers, a stripped-down cocktail dress, and the sheer force of maternal panic, while her own teenage daughter watches from a stairwell and a studio audience of dead people takes furious mental notes on her heels.

Marvel as an entire five-man extraction team gets described, blocked, and taken apart by a fifteen-year-old and a voice in her head who have never once trained together, using nothing but "wait for the door" and "don't look in mirrors."

Sit with the fact that this book's single most tender scene is two teenagers and a dog on a bedroom floor, and its single most explosive scene is that same teenager stealing a nuclear arming key off a war criminal with her mother's stolen lipstick still on her mouth from two chapters ago.

Try to keep a straight face through a line about a bugged handbag that is, and we cannot stress this enough, real, verified, on the actual page of the actual movie: "if you need to talk, speak into the purse."

And ask yourself, honestly, how a franchise about international nuclear terrorism ends up being, at its core, a story about a girl learning she doesn't have to be just the muscle to matter.

[BEAT]

Honest Trailers Drinking Game:

Take a drink every time somebody says "that's a tic." Take two if it's followed immediately by a change of subject.

Take a drink every time somebody gets punched in this book by a woman who was completely justified in doing it.

Finish your drink the one time somebody says "Carlos" and it turns out to actually mean something.

Do NOT attempt this drinking game during the tango. You will not make it. Nobody has made it.

[BEAT]

This is a story with:
- One (1) dog, objectively the most competent member of the cast while being objectively the ugliest member of the cast
- One (1) recurring headslap of unknown but universally agreed-upon origin
- A tic-tracking subplot that got more sincere audience investment than the actual nuclear terrorism plot
- And a cast so large the Gallery itself had to start enforcing a seating chart

[BEAT]

Count along, if you want, with:

The number of times somebody in the anonymous rows gets threatened with being smacked, and the number of times it's actually somebody else who does it.

The number of separate people who, over the course of one book, get told some version of "you already know what you're capable of."

The number of dogs who are, objectively, better at their jobs than the humans around them. It's one dog. It is still worth counting.

[BEAT: MUSIC SWELLS]

Starring:

A slab of actual Austrian granite.

Horror royalty, moonlighting as a housewife.

One actress. Cloned twice. Budget reasons.

Roseanne's ex-husband, still weirdly employed.
Divorced at least twice now, by movie count alone. Honestly funnier the more you think about it.

Some guy, about to regret everything.

An old man with a cigar, a scowl, and a scar big enough to cover half his face.

Some hottie who thinks she's all that.

Gallagher on a bad hair day.

Four bodies without brains, somehow still able to walk around (mostly).

Thrift Store Evel Knievel.

One Marine Corps Harrier, borrowed without permission, returned with significantly more bullet holes.

One office building, on the receiving end of a tailfin, a missile, and an entire jet in reverse, filing the single most justified insurance claim in franchise history.

And one pair of nuts. Cracked.

And a dog. The dog is not credited. The dog should be credited. The dog is doing more acting than half this cast.
