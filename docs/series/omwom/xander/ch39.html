Chapter 39 of 43

Andrew

"Oh, I like this one," the voice from the gallery said, immediately, delighted the way one narrator recognizes another. "This kid would host his own riffing gallery if you let him near a microphone."

Andrew caught him alone in the equipment room a few days after the "Zeppo" comment, fidgeting in the doorway, awkward in the way of a man who'd clearly worked up his nerve for this conversation and was already regretting it.

"Hey, so, I wanted to apologize," Andrew said, in a rush, like the words might escape if he didn't get them out fast enough. "For the joke. At breakfast. I didn't mean anything cruel by it, I promise, it just kind of fell out of my mouth before I thought about it, and then afterward I kept thinking about it, and I realized it was actually a pretty lousy thing to say given, you know, everything you actually did out there."

Xander studied him a moment, surprised by the sincerity of it. "Appreciate that. Genuinely."

"I know what it's like, actually. Being the guy everyone underestimates." Andrew fidgeted with a strap on one of the equipment bags, not quite meeting Xander's eyes. "I spent a long time being the villain's sidekick, and then the villain, badly, and now I'm just, I don't know, the guy who takes notes and tells everybody's stories after the fact because I'm not much good in an actual fight. People don't exactly look at me like I matter either."

"Who knows," Xander said, managing something almost warm. "Maybe you'll end up running a website and a call center for things that go grr in the night."

A chair, somewhere off in the anonymous rows, went over with a clatter.

"MAN," the voice attached to it said, on its feet now, apparently, and done pretending otherwise. "Everything that comes out of that guy's mouth is a reminder of something else! This is getting old, and it is getting old FAST!"

"Sit back down."

"I don't WANT to sit back down, Fury, I want an explanation!"

"You're not getting one. Sit down."

Something in Xander's chest softened, recognizing more of himself in that admission than he expected to.

"That's rough," he said, honest. "For what it's worth, though, you and me, we're not actually the same kind of overlooked. You've got a complicated history you're working through. I just never had the flashy stuff to begin with. Doesn't make either version easy, but they're different roads."

"Yeah, I figured." Andrew finally looked up properly. "Can I ask you something? How'd you actually get people to take you seriously? Because I've been trying for, like, years now, and it still mostly doesn't work."

Xander considered the question seriously, turning over a year's worth of hard-won lessons before answering.

"Honestly? I stopped needing them to, for a while. Just did the work anyway, quiet, whether anyone was watching or not. Helped some scared kid nobody else had time for. Caught a gap in a plan nobody else bothered checking. Did it enough times that eventually it stopped being about convincing anybody and started just being who I actually was." He shrugged. "Recognition, when it finally came, it came because the work was already real, not the other way around."

"That's..." Andrew turned that over, something thoughtful crossing his face. "That's actually really good advice. I think I've been doing it backwards this whole time. Trying to get people to believe in me first, hoping the actual competence would follow."

"Yeah, doesn't really work that way, in my experience. Trust me, I tried the loud version first too. Took way too long to figure out the quiet version actually works better."

"Can I ask you something else? Kind of a weird one." Andrew fidgeted again, clearly working up to it. "Do you ever worry that people only remember the bad stuff about you? Like, no matter how much good you do, the old reputation just kind of sticks?"

Xander considered that seriously, recognizing more of himself in the question than he wanted to admit. "Yeah. All the time, actually. Zeppo's gonna follow me around for a while yet, probably. Some people are always gonna see me that way, no matter what I actually do."

"How do you deal with that?"

"Stopped needing everybody to update their opinion of me. Some will, eventually, once the work speaks loud enough. Some won't, ever, and that's on them, not on the work." Xander shrugged. "You're not gonna outrun the villain stuff by convincing every single person you've changed. You're just gonna outlast it, by being someone different for long enough that the old story stops being the only one people can tell about you."

"That's actually really comforting. In a weirdly bleak way."

"Yeah, most good advice is, in my experience."

Andrew nodded, slow, something settling into place behind his eyes. "Thanks, Xander. Actually thanks, not just, like, socially obligated thanks."

"Anytime." Xander managed a genuine smile. "And hey. No hard feelings about the Zeppo thing. Wasn't the first time I've heard it, won't be the last. Doesn't land the way it used to, mostly 'cause I know what actually happened out there, whether the room caught up to it yet or not."

"I'm gonna stop saying it. For real this time."

"Appreciate that too."

"'Some documentary narration to record,'" the gallery's voice repeated, wistful. "A kindred spirit. Somewhere out there, an entire other book is being narrated worse than this one, and this narrator salutes it."

"Worse? You haven't even seen his cut."

"I don't need to. I can feel it in my bones. It's got a laser pointer in it somewhere."

Andrew lingered a moment longer, something almost hopeful in his expression, and then, in classic Andrew fashion, seemed to remember he had somewhere else to be, some documentary narration to record or timeline to update, and excused himself with an awkward wave that Xander returned, amused, watching him go.

He's not wrong, you know, Coulson observed, once Andrew had gone. About the parallel. Two men the group habitually underestimates, for different reasons, arriving at very different outcomes.

Yeah. Xander went back to checking equipment, turning the conversation over. Guess the difference is what you actually do with being overlooked. Let it convince you you're worthless, or just, keep doing the work anyway till the room catches up.

I think that's the truest thing you've said in a while.

Yeah, well. Xander managed a small, satisfied smile, finishing his inventory check. Had a whole year of practice figuring it out.
