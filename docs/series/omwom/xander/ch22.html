Chapter 22 of 43

The Spell

The days between Buffy's return and the night everything finally came to a head moved fast and strange, the house pulling itself together with the urgency of people who knew, without anyone saying it out loud, that whatever came next was going to be the last thing, one way or another. Robin Wood healed enough to stand, slowly, leaning on whoever happened to be nearest. Faith and Buffy found something that wasn't quite friendship but functioned well enough in its place, a working truce built out of necessity and, underneath that, something like real respect. The Potentials trained harder, ate less, slept worse, and none of them complained about any of it, because complaining had stopped feeling like it mattered next to what was actually coming.

Xander spent the days doing what he'd always done, quietly, unglamorously, checking in on the ones who needed checking in on, running supply counts nobody else wanted to run, being the steady presence in a house that had learned, the hard way, how much it needed one. Coulson stayed close through most of it, present in the small unremarked way their partnership had settled into, offering a read here, a question there, mostly just company through the waiting.

"Hang on," said the voice from the gallery, thoughtful, a man checking his own math. "Has anyone actually heard one of those in a while? 'The kind of.'"

A pause, the anonymous watchers visibly conferring with each other in the low, rustling way a crowd did when nobody wanted to be the one who'd actually kept count.

"Not for several chapters, now that you mention it," someone finally offered.

"There's another one," somebody in the anonymous rows said, quiet, almost to themselves. "'The way rooms did right before something irreversible happened.' Word for word, practically. Chapter eight had that exact sentence."

"Good ear," Fury's voice said, dry. "Somebody's paying closer attention than the author was."

Willow stood in the middle of the basement, the night it finally came, with the scythe held flat across both palms, and the whole house had gone still around her, the same stillness that had settled over a different room weeks ago, only this time, for once, it was the good kind of irreversible.

"Once this starts," she said, and her voice had a steadiness to it Xander hadn't heard from her in longer than he wanted to think about, "every girl who could be a Slayer will be one. Here, and everywhere. There's no undoing it after. I need everyone to be sure."

Nobody said anything for a moment. Then, one by one, quiet nods, the whole house's fear finally finding somewhere useful to point itself.

This is the part where the actual war starts, Coulson said, quiet, present in a way that felt more like company than commentary tonight. I've sat through a lot of pre-mission briefings. This one's better than most of them. Nobody in this room is pretending it's going to be easy.

Yeah, well, Xander thought, checking the axe he'd carried since the vineyard, the weight of it familiar now in a way it hadn't been a few weeks ago, we don't really do the pretending thing 'round here. Learned that lesson kind of recently.

I noticed.

Buffy moved through the room while Willow gathered herself for the spell, checking in with each cluster of girls in turn, steady and present in the way that had made Xander walk two miles to a crypt to bring her back. She stopped by him last.

"You good?"

"As good as a one-eyed guy with a fire axe gets, probably." He managed something close to his old grin. "You?"

"Ask me tomorrow." She held his gaze a beat, something searching in it that Xander recognized now, the same quiet, careful attention she'd turned on him the night she came back, and for a second he thought she might finally say something about it. She didn't. Whatever question she was carrying stayed folded up behind her eyes, saved for later, and she squeezed his shoulder instead and moved on to the next cluster of nervous faces needing a word before the end of the world.

She's still watching you, Coulson observed.

Yeah. Xander watched her go, steady, sure, exactly the person this house needed leading it into whatever came next. She'll ask when she asks. Not tonight.

Willow began the incantation, low at first, then rising, the scythe glowing faint and gold in her hands, and somewhere out past the walls of the house, out past the whole town, girls who had never held a weapon in their lives felt something ancient and waiting finally answer a call that had been ringing, unheard, for the length of human history.

Xander felt the change ripple through the room like weather, felt three dozen Potentials straighten, all at once, into something the word Potential had never quite managed to hold.

That, Coulson said, quiet, something like genuine awe underneath the professional composure, is not something I have a comparison for. I've seen a great deal in twenty years. I don't think I've ever seen anything do that.

"First time for everybody," the gallery's voice said, and there was no dryness in it at all, just something close to wonder. "This narrator's sat through a lot of these. That one still got me."

Welcome to Tuesday, Xander said, though it wasn't Tuesday, though nothing about tonight felt remotely like an ordinary day of the week. Buffy world runs on a different scale than your world, I think.

Apparently.

"'Welcome to Tuesday,'" someone in the anonymous rows repeated, half a laugh escaping despite the moment. "Global mystical empowerment event, and the local guy's still doing the bit. I respect the commitment."

The spell settled, the gold light fading from the scythe, and the room that had held thirty scared girls a minute ago now held thirty Slayers, uncertain still, shaking still, but standing straighter than fear alone had let them stand in weeks.

"Okay," Buffy said, into the sudden, charged quiet, her voice carrying with an authority that didn't need volume to land. "Everybody knows the plan. We go in, we close the Hellmouth, we come home. All of us, if I have any say in it." A beat, and something almost gentle crept through the command in her voice. "Let's go show the First Evil what a house full of somebodies looks like."

Nobody cheered. It wasn't that kind of moment. But the room moved as one, gathering weapons, gathering courage, gathering whatever last reserves of steadiness each of them had left, and Xander fell into step beside people he'd known for years and people he'd known for days, feeling the axe settle familiar in his grip, feeling something behind his ribs that wasn't quite calm but was, at least, no longer only fear.

Ready? Coulson asked, as the house emptied out into the dark toward the school, toward whatever waited under it.

No, Xander said, honest, the old joke nowhere close to reach tonight. But that's never really stopped me before.

No, Coulson agreed, something warm and steady underneath the single word. I don't imagine it has.

They walked together, the whole house moving as one dark, determined shape through the quiet streets, toward the last night either of them could have imagined, when this had all started, as anything but the end of something enormous.
