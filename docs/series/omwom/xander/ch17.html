Chapter 17 of 43

Reunified

The house didn't erupt into applause when Buffy walked back through the front door, which was, Xander thought, exactly right, a quiet, careful welcome that matched what the moment actually called for. Willow's shoulders dropped half an inch, relief written plain across her whole body. Giles's expression did something complicated and grateful behind his glasses. A few of the Potentials looked up, uncertain, watching to see how the room would decide to feel before committing to a feeling of their own.

Faith, to her credit, didn't fight it. She met Buffy's eyes across the crowded living room, something passing between them that didn't need words, an old rivalry finally, quietly, setting itself down in favor of something more useful, and stepped back without being asked to.

"No power struggle," the voice from the gallery noted, sounding almost disappointed on principle. "No dramatic standoff. Two rivals just... agreeing, like adults."

"Shocking, I know," someone in the anonymous rows said. "Try to contain yourself."

"Somebody want to catch me up?" Buffy said, into the room, and the house exhaled, all at once, and started talking.

Xander hung back near the doorway while the debrief unfolded around him, Robin's condition, the state of the defenses, the gap in the plan that had let the third wave through. He watched Buffy absorb it all with the same steady, careful attention he remembered from before the vineyard, asking the right questions, not flinching from the bad news, already, visibly, rebuilding the thing the house had needed all along and hadn't known how to name.

It wasn't his story to center himself in tonight. He'd done the part that mattered, out at the crypt, in the quiet, where nobody but Buffy had needed to see it.

You did good tonight, Coulson said, quiet, once the room's attention had moved elsewhere.

We did good tonight, Xander corrected, and felt the shape of the correction settle comfortably, no longer a point he needed to defend so much as a fact he'd finally stopped arguing with. Your read got me to the right question. My read got us to the right door. Feels weird saying that without it turning into a whole thing.

It doesn't have to turn into a whole thing. Some nights, it can just be true.

Xander was still turning that over, some quiet, unfamiliar warmth settling into a week that had mostly held very little of it, when he noticed Buffy watching him from across the room.

Not watching the way the others were, the general attention of people checking on a friend who'd had a hard week. Something narrower than that, her eyes tracking him with the same close focus she usually reserved for a threat she couldn't quite name yet, a wrongness her instincts had caught before her conscious mind had finished cataloging it.

She didn't say anything. She held his gaze a beat too long, long enough that Xander felt it land somewhere uncomfortable, and then, deliberately, let it go, turning back to whatever Giles was explaining about the defensive perimeter, filing whatever she'd noticed away for later without pushing it into the open tonight.

She saw something, Coulson said, low, careful.

"Uh oh," murmured a voice in the anonymous rows, all the earlier lightness draining out of it fast. "Slayer senses. That's not a plot thread, that's a loaded gun on the mantelpiece."

Yeah, Xander thought back, feeling the unease of it settle slow into his chest. She did.

It made a kind of sense he didn't love. Buffy had history with exactly this shape of thing, a whole catalogue of it, voices that weren't voices, faces that weren't quite the right faces, the violation of something wearing the shell of someone she loved and finding out too late it wasn't really them at all. If anyone in this house was built to feel the uncanny valley of a second consciousness riding along behind a familiar face, it was going to be her, Slayer instinct sharpened by exactly the wrong kind of firsthand experience, and Xander understood, watching her deliberately not press the question tonight, that this wasn't the end of it. Just a pause. A thread left hanging, on purpose, for a later night that hadn't arrived yet.

You going to tell her, Coulson asked, eventually.

Not tonight. Xander watched Buffy across the room, steady again, back in the rhythm of running a debrief like she'd never left it, and felt something protective rise up around the thought of explaining any of this to her right now, on top of everything else she was already carrying. Not while she's got a house full of scared girls and a hole in the ceiling to deal with. Whenever that conversation happens, it deserves better timing than tonight.

Fair enough.

The debrief wound down eventually, the house settling into its familiar late-night murmur, chores and watches divided up, the grim, unglamorous business of tending to two dead girls and one badly wounded man folded quietly into the same evening as everyone's relief at having their general back. Xander found a quiet corner of the porch to let the last of it settle, the argument that had followed him since the vineyard finally, for the first time, feeling less like a war either of them needed to win.

Can I say something, Coulson asked, and there was something almost careful in the question, unusual for him.

Go for it.

I still don't think either one of us was fully right, back in the kitchen, that first night. About whose seeing counts more. A pause. I think we were both partly right, and I think tonight proved it better than either of us arguing it out ever could have. Your read got us the door. Mine got you to ask the right question. Neither one worked without the other.

You saying that 'cause you actually believe it, or 'cause you're trying to be nice after I spent a week being a stubborn ass about it?

Both, possibly. Mostly the first one.

"He has a hobby. It's meticulous records."

"...Fair."

"Maybe somebody needs to trim it," another voice offered, deadpan.

"...Ba-dum-tss."

Xander let out something that was almost a laugh, real, unguarded, the first one that had felt entirely earned in longer than he wanted to admit. "Yeah," he said, quiet, to the dark yard, to the house full of people rebuilding itself one uneasy night at a time behind him. "Yeah, okay. Both were partly right. I can live with that."

Good, Coulson said. Because I don't think either of us is going to stop believing our own half of it entirely. I don't think we're supposed to.

"No," Xander agreed, watching the last light go out in an upstairs window, the house finally, finally settling toward something like rest. "Probably not."

It wasn't peace, not exactly, not with a war still very much ahead of them and a Slayer across the house quietly holding a question she hadn't asked yet. But it was, for the first time since a man in a green waistcoat had rearranged both their lives without asking permission from either of them, something real enough to build on.
