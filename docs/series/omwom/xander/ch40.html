That Conversation

Three weeks into Cleveland, once the immediate business of settling eleven new arrivals had finally quieted into something like routine, Buffy found him in the building's small, sunlit kitchen and made good on her promise.

"I still owe you that conversation," she said, settling into the chair across from him, two mugs of coffee between them, one of which she slid toward him without asking.

"The delayed reckoning," the voice from the gallery murmured, settling in. "I do love it when a production actually remembers to pay off its own setups instead of just hoping the audience forgot."

"Yeah." Xander wrapped his hands around the mug, grateful for something to hold. "Figured you hadn't forgotten."

"I don't forget things like this." She studied him a moment, something gentle and careful in her expression. "I want to understand it properly, Xander. Not just the surface version. What it's actually like, having him there. What it's cost you. What it's given you. All of it."

She means that, Coulson observed, quiet. This isn't idle curiosity. She's preparing to actually know both of us, not just tolerate an arrangement she doesn't fully understand.

Yeah, I got that too.

Xander took a breath, and told her, honestly, the way he hadn't quite managed to be honest with anyone else about it, not even entirely with himself until he heard himself saying it out loud. He told her about the vineyard, about Coulson's presence in that last hour before Caleb, about the horror of the injury shared between them in a way neither of them had been prepared for. He told her about the argument that had followed for weeks, the "bag carrier" line, the stubborn refusal to admit that his own way of seeing people and Coulson's trained instincts might actually be worth the same thing. He told her about Spike, about the moment neither of them could fully explain or verify, just trusted, and how that trust had become the actual foundation everything after had been built on.

Buffy listened without interrupting, the same careful, considered attention she brought to any genuinely difficult problem, and when he finally ran out of words, she let it settle a long moment before speaking.

"Can I ask him something directly?"

"Yeah. Go ahead."

Go ahead, Xander thought, offering the same small, deliberate lending of voice he'd offered once before, at the motel outside Barstow.

"Coulson," Buffy said, direct, unflinching. "Do you ever want your own body back? Your own life? I have to imagine there's some part of you that misses that."

Coulson took the question seriously, Xander could feel that much even from the passenger's seat of his own borrowed voice, a long pause before the answer came.

"I did, at first," Coulson said, through him, honest in a way that surprised even Xander a little. "The first several weeks, I thought about it constantly, whether there was some way back, some door I hadn't found yet. I don't think about it much anymore. Not because I've given up hope of it, exactly, but because the life I've actually built here, this work, this partnership, has become something I value on its own terms, not just as a consolation for what I lost. I'm not sure that's an answer that fully satisfies the question. It's the honest one, though."

Buffy studied him a long moment, and something in her expression softened further, real understanding settling into place.

"That's a good answer," she said, finally. "Thank you for being honest instead of giving me something that sounded cleaner."

Control settled back into Xander's own hands, easy now, familiar, and he took a long sip of coffee while Buffy considered everything she'd just heard.

"I want you to know something," she said, eventually. "I've had a lot of complicated things live in my life. Not all of them turned out okay. Some of them turned out very much not okay." A small, wry smile. "This one, though. Watching you two this last year, watching what you actually built together. This is one of the good ones, Xander. I wanted you to hear that from me directly."

Something in Xander's chest went warm and a little unsteady, hearing it, a validation he hadn't realized he'd still been quietly waiting for.

"Thanks," he said, simple, meaning it entirely. "That actually means a lot."

"I mean it." She reached across the table, squeezed his hand briefly. "You did good out there, Xander. Both of you did. I'm proud of you. I don't think I say that enough, to anyone, honestly, but I especially don't think I've said it to you enough, over the years."

"Buffy Summers, getting sentimental. Never thought I'd see the day."

"Don't get used to it." But she was smiling, warm and real, and the moment held there a while, comfortable, easy, two old friends who'd survived more than either of them had ever expected to, finally, properly, catching each other up on who they'd both become.

That went well, Coulson said, once she'd gone, drawn away by some new piece of Council business needing her attention.

Yeah, Xander agreed, watching the Cleveland morning unfold outside the kitchen window, a whole new life settling comfortably into place around him. Yeah, it really did.

How does it feel? Being fully known, finally, by someone who matters this much to you?

Xander thought about that, turning it over honestly, watching steam rise off the last of his coffee.

Good, he said, finally, simple and certain. Feels really good, actually. Turns out that's kind of what I've been working toward this whole time, without quite realizing it. Not just doing the work nobody else has patience for. Being seen doing it, by the people who matter.

I think that's always been the whole point, Coulson said, warm. For both of us.

Yeah, Xander agreed, finishing his coffee, ready, finally, fully, for whatever the rest of this strange, hard-won life had left to offer either of them. Yeah, I think you're right.
