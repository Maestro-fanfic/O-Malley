The Call He Doesn't Question

The plan tightened over the next two days into something with actual edges: a defensive line, a rough rotation, Faith's read on where the Bringers would come from based on nothing Xander could point to except the plain confidence of someone who'd survived a lot of bad situations by trusting her gut and never once had to explain the math behind it out loud.

She had them running drills morning and evening, sparring pairs in the backyard, weapon checks in the garage, and there was something to be said for the energy of it, the house feeling less like a place waiting to be attacked and more like a place actively preparing to fight back.

"It's not a montage, it's a second act," someone corrected, mild. "There's a difference."

"Is there, though." Xander watched the mood shift again, cautiously, a little, the raw fear of the days right after the vineyard hardening into something closer to grim determination.

"We hold the front hall and the kitchen," Faith said, on the second evening, tracing a rough line on Giles's old floor plan with one finger while a dozen Potentials leaned in around her. "Anyone comes through, they've gotta funnel through one of two chokepoints. We stack both, we're solid."

Xander looked at the plan a beat longer than anyone else in the room seemed to, and felt something small and uneasy catch, low, half-formed, a doubt that hadn't fully turned into a thought yet.

You see it too, Coulson said, quiet, and it wasn't a question.

See what.

"Oh no," someone in the anonymous rows said, sitting forward. "He's doing the thing. The noticing thing. I hate the noticing thing, it never means good news."

"HEY," Fury's voice cut in, sharp, genuinely offended. "That's my thing to have a thing about. Nobody else gets to be tired of the noticing thing, I invented being tired of the noticing thing."

No retreat path. If the line doesn't hold, there's nowhere organized to fall back to. She's built the whole plan around holding, not around what happens if holding fails.

"Chekhov's floor plan," another voice muttered. "Somebody should frame that on the wall. Right next to the doorframe joke."

Xander looked at the floor plan again, and yeah, there it was, obvious now that it had been named, an entire strategy with no version of itself for the version where things went wrong. Two solid chokepoints, and nothing at all connecting them, no fallback route sketched anywhere on the page, no contingency beyond an implicit, unstated assumption that the line would simply hold because Faith believed it would.

He opened his mouth, half-decided to say something, and closed it again, because saying it meant standing up in a room that had just watched Buffy get quietly deposed for exactly this kind of second-guessing, meant putting his own uncertain gut up against Faith's confident one in front of thirty scared girls who needed someone in the room to sound sure.

He didn't trust his own read enough yet to spend it on that.

I'm not saying anything, Xander thought, and even to himself it sounded thinner than he wanted it to.

Xander. Coulson's voice had lost all its earlier caution. This was the first time since the vineyard he'd pushed, actually pushed, not commentary, not restraint, a real, direct disagreement laid bare. This isn't a small thing to let go. If this goes wrong, that gap is where it goes wrong.

Faith's handled worse than this. She'll be fine.

That's not the same as the plan being sound.

Look, I get that you're the professional planning guy, I do, but you've been in my head for like four days. She's actually done this. She's actually survived things neither of us have. Xander felt the old defensiveness rising, familiar now, worn smooth from use. I'm not gonna stand up and second-guess the one person in this house who still seems sure of herself, not over a hunch.

It's not a hunch. It's a gap in the plan that either of us could point to on a whiteboard.

Then it's a gap. Not the first plan in history to have one.

"Everybody stop laughing," the gallery's voice said, and it wasn't a joke this time, quiet enough that the anonymous rows actually did. "This is the argument that matters. Let it land."

Coulson didn't answer right away, and when he did, something in the quality of the silence beforehand told Xander this wasn't going to be forgotten, wasn't going to be let go the way most disagreements between them had been so far.

All right, Coulson said, finally, quiet, and there was nothing dismissive in it, nothing that sounded like giving up so much as a man filing something away for later, with real weight attached to it. I've said what I needed to say. It's your call. I hope I'm wrong.

You're wrong.

I hope so, Coulson said again, and didn't sound at all convinced.

Xander went back to helping stack sandbags at the kitchen chokepoint, working alongside two Potentials who'd started to actually trust the rhythm of the preparations, and didn't think about the conversation again for the rest of the night, mostly because he was doing his best not to, and told himself the not-thinking-about-it was a kind of confidence rather than what it actually was, which was the oldest trick in his own particular book: refuse to look directly at the thing you're afraid might be true, and it can't quite finish becoming true yet.

Later, lying awake with the house finally quiet around him, he thought about it anyway, turning the floor plan over in his head the way he'd turned the vineyard map over before the eye, looking for the version where the gap didn't matter, where Faith's instinct held the way it always seemed to, where the whole thing worked out fine and he never had to know whether he should have said something.

He didn't find that version. He found the doubt still sitting there instead, patient, unresolved, waiting for whatever came next to decide whether it had been worth listening to.

It wasn't a trick that was going to hold for very much longer.
