Chapter 16 of 43

Finding Buffy

He told the house he was going for a walk, which wasn't a lie exactly, just an incomplete one, and nobody stopped him, nobody asked where, the whole place still too raw and uncertain to spare much attention for one more person slipping out the door.

He made it two streets before Coulson finally asked the question that had apparently been sitting patiently behind everything else since the dining room.

Where would Buffy go.

"It's always the crypt," someone muttered from the anonymous rows, the weary tone of a man who had, apparently, seen this particular episode before.

Xander kept walking, hands in his pockets, the evening cooling fast the way it did this time of year. That's not you telling me. That's you asking me.

It is. Something almost like satisfaction crept into it, the plain pleasure of a technique landing the way it was supposed to. I don't know her. I've never met her, I have no history with her, nothing I could offer you here would be worth the paper it's printed on. You do know her. So. Where would she go.

Xander walked another block, turning it over properly instead of reaching for the first easy answer, the way you did when a question actually mattered. Not her mom's old house, too obvious, too much like retreating backward into a life that wasn't hers anymore. Not the school, not with everything that place had come to mean this year. Not anywhere with people in it, not tonight, not after a room full of people she'd trusted had decided, without much of a fight, that she wasn't worth trusting anymore.

You're taking a while, Coulson observed, not unkindly.

I'm actually thinking about it instead of guessing. Give me a second. He turned the question over from a few more angles, tried and discarded three more possibilities, and eventually landed somewhere he hadn't expected to, somewhere that made an uncomfortable kind of sense the longer he stayed with it.

Somewhere she could stop performing okay for once. Somewhere that had never once asked anything of her except to show up as whatever she actually was that night, hero or wreck or something in between. Somewhere she wouldn't have to explain herself to anyone who actually cared what the explanation was.

"Spike's," Xander said, out loud, before he'd fully finished deciding it, the certainty arriving a half second ahead of the reasoning behind it. "She'd go to Spike's."

"CALLED IT," the same weary voice from the anonymous rows shouted, considerably less weary now. "I called it! Somebody owes me money, this was a whole bit I had going with myself!"

"Nobody bet against you, there was no bit, you were the only participant."

"Doesn't make me less right."

You're sure.

"No," Xander admitted, already changing direction, already walking faster. "I'm not sure. I just know her, and I know that's where she goes when she doesn't want anyone to find her, which is exactly backwards logic for finding her, except I think that's kind of the point. She wants someone to look. She just doesn't want it to be easy."

That's not tracking. That's not deduction either, not really.

"Nope."

It's just knowing her.

"Yeah." Xander picked up his pace toward the cemetery, the crypt's low outline already visible against the last purple edge of the sky. "First useful thing I've had all week that didn't come out of a book, a plan, or your twenty years of training."

I noticed, Coulson said, and there was nothing competitive in it at all, just a plain, quiet kind of respect. For the record. I noticed.

✳

"All right, that's enough out of both of you," the gallery's voice said, and the anonymous rows actually quieted, a little sheepish about it. "We found her. Let's not make a mess of the part that matters."

The crypt door wasn't locked, which told Xander something before he'd even gotten it open, and Buffy was sitting on the lower step near the sarcophagus with her knees pulled up, not crying exactly, just present in the hollowed-out way of someone who'd run out of the energy required to keep performing fine.

"Go away, Xander."

"Yeah, I figured you'd say that." He didn't go away. He sat down on the opposite step instead, close enough to be there, far enough not to crowd her. "Figured I'd stick around and be ignored for a while instead. See how that goes."

"It's going to go badly."

"Probably."

"Here you are, letting it sit again," one last voice in the anonymous rows couldn't help pointing out, quiet, almost apologetic about breaking the vow of silence already. "When's that phrase gonna learn to stand on its own two feet?"

Nobody answered it. Nobody else spoke again either, true to what they'd promised.

She didn't say anything else for a long stretch, and Xander let the quiet hold, because pushing too hard, too fast, felt exactly like the wrong instrument for whatever this actually was. He could feel her walls, even without trying, a bristling quality, someone braced for an argument she'd already had a version of a dozen times that week, and his own first instinct, the easy joke, the thing that usually got him through moments like this, sat dead in his throat, wrong for once, too small for the size of what she was actually carrying.

She's not defensive because she doesn't want to talk, Coulson said, quiet, careful, the first real technical read he'd offered since they'd left the house. She's defensive because every conversation she's had this week has been someone asking her to explain herself. If you ask her a question right now, any question, she's going to hear it as one more person needing something from her she doesn't have left to give.

"So what do I do instead."

Don't ask her anything. Just tell her something true, and don't wait for a response, and let her decide on her own time whether she wants to pick it up.

Xander turned that over. It wasn't advice he'd have found on his own, not tonight, not with his own instincts running mostly on exhaustion and worry, and he felt the shape of it settle into something usable almost immediately, the timing and phrasing of it clicking into place in a way his own read on the room hadn't managed alone.

"The house is a mess," he said, out loud, not a question, not asking her to fix anything, not asking her for anything at all. "Robin's hurt bad. Faith's plan had a hole in it nobody caught in time, and I caught wind of the hole and didn't push hard enough to matter, which is on me, not her, not really. And everybody's scared, and nobody in that house right now actually knows what they're doing, including Faith, including me, including apparently everybody except one specific someone who used to be pretty good at knowing, even when she got it wrong."

Buffy didn't answer right away. But something in her posture shifted, fractionally, the walls not coming down exactly, just loosening enough at the edges to let a little more air through.

"I got people killed," she said, finally, quiet, raw in a way Xander hadn't heard from her in longer than he could remember. "I got people killed and everyone finally noticed, and I don't even know if they're wrong to be scared of trusting me again."

"Maybe they're not wrong to be scared," Xander said, and felt Coulson's quiet approval land somewhere underneath the words, confirmation he hadn't asked for and hadn't needed to. "Doesn't mean you're the wrong answer. Just means you're not the safe one. I don't actually think safe's on the table for any of us anymore, Buffy. I think it's just about who we trust to be right there in it with us when it isn't."

She looked at him properly for the first time since he'd come in, something cracking open behind her eyes that had been sealed shut since the porch.

"You came all the way out here to tell me that?"

"Came all the way out here 'cause the house is falling apart without you and I'm apparently too stubborn to just say that part first." He managed something close to a real smile, the first one in days that didn't feel like armor. "Also 'cause I'm pretty sure I know you well enough to know you needed somebody to just show up, not fix anything, not explain anything, just show up. So. Showed up."

Buffy was quiet for a long moment, and then, slowly, uncertainly, she unfolded from the step and reached a hand down toward him.

"Okay," she said. "Let's go see how bad it actually is."

Well done, Coulson said, quiet, as Xander took her hand and stood, and there was nothing performative in it, nothing that sounded like claiming credit for what had just happened. That wasn't my read landing. That was yours, all the way through. I just helped you find the door.

"Yeah," Xander said, mostly to himself, following Buffy out into the dark toward home. "Yeah. Turns out that's kind of the whole trick, isn't it."
