Chapter 25 of 43

Uneven Ground

They ended up in a motel outside Barstow, three days of driving compressed into a single exhausted decision to stop somewhere, anywhere, that wasn't a smoking hole in the ground where a town used to be. Forty-some survivors spread across a dozen rooms meant for half that many, sleeping in shifts, eating whatever the gas station down the road could provide, and slowly, unevenly, starting to reckon with what they'd actually survived.

The reckoning came in pieces, and it didn't come the same way for everyone.

Some of the newer Slayers found Xander in the parking lot the second morning, thanking him, awkward and sincere, for the plan that had gotten several of them through the vineyard and, later, for the way he'd talked half the house through the worst of the panic before the final battle. A girl named Deb, one of the ones from that very first vineyard briefing, hugged him without warning, fierce and quick, and didn't say anything at all, which somehow said more than words would have.

Other people looked straight through him. Andrew, of all people, made a joke at breakfast about "good old Zeppo, comic relief as always," not even meaning anything cruel by it, just falling back on the old, comfortable shorthand the way people did when they were too tired to update their assumptions about who anyone actually was anymore. Nobody corrected him. Xander didn't either. It wasn't worth the energy it would have cost, not this week, and some old, tired part of him had stopped expecting the correction to matter much anyway.

"'Zeppo,'" someone in the anonymous rows repeated, indignant on his behalf. "After everything. THE ZEPPO. This narrator has notes for the continuity department."

"Andrew's tired. Everyone's tired. Let it go."

"I will not be letting it go, I will be seething quietly, there's a difference."

That bothers you, Coulson observed, quiet, once they were alone again.

Less than it used to, Xander admitted, surprising himself a little with how true it actually was. I know what I did out there. I know what I've been doing all year. Doesn't mean I need every single person in this parking lot to know it too.

That's real growth, for what it's worth.

Don't get used to it. I reserve the right to be petty about it again the second I'm not this tired.

Noted.

He was sitting on the curb outside his room, working through a granola bar he didn't much want, when Buffy found him.

"Can we talk?"

"Sure." He shifted over to make room, and she sat, and for a while neither of them said anything, the parking lot's early morning quiet stretching out comfortably enough that Xander let himself believe, for a minute, that this was just going to be another version of the crypt, two tired people sitting together without needing to explain anything.

It wasn't.

"I need to ask you something," Buffy said, finally, careful in a way she hadn't been careful with him in years, "and I need you to actually answer it instead of making a joke."

"Okay." Something in his chest went tight. "Ask."

"Something's different about you." She wasn't looking at him now, staring instead at the cracked asphalt between her feet. "It's been different since the vineyard. I noticed it the night I came back, and I've been noticing it ever since, little things, the way you read people now, the way you sometimes go somewhere else mid-sentence like you're listening to something I can't hear." She finally looked up. "I've had voices in my head before that weren't mine, Xander. I've had faces that weren't the right faces wearing people I loved. I need to know if that's what's happening to you."

"So it's time for this, is it," said the same posh, affected voice from a few chapters back, quieter now, more careful with it. "British accent. Apparently that's a thing now."

"Not for this scene, it isn't," Fury's voice cut in, flat, and the accent didn't come back.

Xander felt the moment arrive, the one Coulson had known was coming, the one he'd been quietly, deliberately not preparing for because preparing for it had felt like admitting it was inevitable.

You don't have to tell her, Coulson said, quiet, careful. Not if you're not ready. This is your call, all the way, same as it's always been.

Yeah, Xander thought back, but I think I am ready. I think she's earned it more than most.

He looked at Buffy, really looked at her, three years of friendship and history sitting behind her careful, frightened eyes, and made the decision the way he'd made most of the good ones lately, not by arguing himself into it, just by recognizing, once he'd given it long enough, that it was already true.

"There's someone in my head," he said, plain, no jokes, nothing softened. "Has been since the vineyard, actually, since a little before it. His name's Coulson. He's not possessing me, he's not controlling anything, he's just, kind of, along for the ride. Watching. Sometimes talking. He's the reason I caught the retreat-path gap Faith missed. He's the reason I knew what to say to Spike during the fight. He's not a monster, Buffy. He's not the First Evil wearing a mask. He's just a guy who ended up here the same way I ended up losing an eye, which is to say, nobody asked either of us."

Buffy was quiet for a long moment, processing, her whole body gone very still the way it always did around things that could genuinely hurt her.

"Prove it," she said, finally. "Not because I don't believe you. Because I need to hear it from him, not just about him."

She wants to talk to me, Coulson said, and there was something careful in it, respectful of the weight of the request. I can do that. If you're comfortable lending me that much.

Yeah, Xander thought. Go ahead.

It wasn't quite control, not in the sense the word usually meant. Xander stayed present, aware, watching from somewhere just behind his own eyes while his mouth, briefly, wasn't entirely only his to direct, the words arriving in a cadence just slightly different from his own, more measured, more careful.

"Ms. Summers," Coulson said, through him, and it sounded almost right, almost like Xander's voice, except more formal, the professional weight behind every syllable unmistakable. "I understand this is difficult to hear secondhand, through someone you've known for years suddenly explaining something entirely outside your frame of reference. I want to be direct with you. I am not a threat to him. I have never once tried to take control of his body against his will, and I never will. Whatever trust you've built with Xander over the years, I'm asking you to extend a small piece of it to me, provisionally, until I've earned more than that on my own."

Buffy studied him, or the version of him currently borrowing Xander's mouth, with the flat, careful scrutiny of someone who had learned, the hard way, not to take anyone's word for anything without watching how they moved while they said it.

"Okay," she said, finally, quiet. "Okay. I believe you. Or I believe I don't have a reason not to yet, which is close enough for right now."

Control settled back fully into Xander's own hands, the transition seamless enough that he barely registered it happening beyond a small internal click, like a key turning back the other way.

"That's a very Buffy answer," Xander said, and managed something close to a real smile. "Cautiously optimistic, ready to revise if I turn out to be a giant liar."

"You usually are a giant liar," Buffy said, but there was warmth under it now, real and unguarded. "About small stuff, mostly. This feels like the kind of thing you wouldn't lie about."

"No," Xander agreed. "This one's real."

She sat with him a while longer, quiet, the two of them watching the sun climb properly over the motel's cracked parking lot, and eventually Giles found them there too, drawn by whatever quiet signal passed between people who'd shared a battlefield, and Buffy filled him in with the same careful, measured tone she'd used to ask her own questions.

Giles took it better than Xander expected, or maybe just differently, his whole posture shifting into the attentive stillness he reserved for genuinely novel information about the world's underlying mechanics.

"A passenger consciousness," Giles said, half to himself, already turning the concept over with the same scholarly hunger he brought to any new piece of the world's strangeness. "Not possession, not a symbiote in the Goa'uld sense, something else entirely. Fascinating. I don't suppose either of you would object to a few questions, purely in the interest of understanding the mechanics involved."

"He said 'Goa'uld,'" someone in the anonymous rows said, delighted beyond all reason. "A Watcher just made a Stargate reference not forty-eight hours after his hometown fell into the earth. This is my favorite moment in the whole production."

"Priorities. The man has priorities."

"Later, Giles," Buffy said, gentle. "Let them breathe first."

"Yes. Quite right. My apologies."

But something in Giles's reaction, the genuine scholarly interest rather than alarm, the immediate assumption that this deserved careful study rather than suspicion, settled something in Xander's chest that he hadn't realized had been braced tight since the vineyard. This, he understood, was what it looked like to be taken seriously by people who mattered, not because he'd finally proven himself loudly enough for the whole world to notice, but because two people he trusted had simply, quietly, decided his word was worth believing.

That went better than I expected, Coulson said, once Giles had wandered off, presumably to find something to write his questions down in.

Yeah, Xander agreed, watching the motel's ordinary morning traffic of exhausted, grieving survivors slowly waking up around them. Yeah, it did.
