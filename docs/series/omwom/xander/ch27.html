Chapter 27 of 43

Willow

Willow found out the way she found out most things in that house, secondhand, in fragments, until enough of them accumulated that pretending not to know stopped being an option, and when she finally cornered Xander about it, three days after Giles's notebook interrogation, there was something in her expression that Xander hadn't quite braced himself for.

It wasn't fear. It wasn't even quite surprise. It was something closer to recognition, old and complicated and entirely her own.

"This one's hers to carry," the voice from the gallery said, quiet, uncharacteristically restrained. "We're just watching. No commentary track tonight."

The anonymous rows, for once, didn't argue.

"You could've told me first," she said, settling onto the edge of his bed, arms wrapped around her knees. "Before Buffy. Before Giles. I would've understood better than either of them, probably."

"I didn't want to put that on you specifically. Given, you know. Everything."

"Everything," Willow repeated, something wry and knowing in it. "Yeah. I know a thing or two about everything, don't I."

She means the magic, Coulson said, quiet, careful. The dark period. I've picked up fragments of it from what leaked across our own boundary. I don't think I have the full picture.

Yeah. Wasn't really my story to hand over secondhand. Figured she'd tell you herself if she wanted you to know.

Willow studied him a long moment, something working behind her eyes. "Can I tell him something? Directly, I mean. Not through you paraphrasing."

"Yeah, go ahead. I'll just, you know. Listen in."

Willow took a breath, and when she spoke, she spoke slower than usual, choosing each word with the care of someone handling something that still had sharp edges even years later. "There was a time I had something living inside me too. Not a person, not like you, Coulson, something more like raw power with no boundaries left on it, and it wasn't kind, and it wasn't patient, and it very nearly ended the world because I couldn't figure out how to carry it without it carrying me instead." She wrapped her arms tighter around her knees. "I know what it's like from the inside. Having something in you that isn't entirely yours, that changes how you move through the world, that other people look at differently once they know it's there."

"Will..."

"Let me finish." She held up a hand, gentle but firm. "That's one half of it. I know what it costs to carry something. But I also know the other half, Xander. I know what it costs to watch someone I love carry something dangerous, and not be able to do anything about it except stand there and hope they come back out the other side okay."

Something cold and understanding settled in Xander's chest, seeing where this was going before she finished saying it.

"The hyena," he said, quiet.

"The hyena," Willow confirmed, and her voice cracked slightly on the word, an old wound reopening despite years of scar tissue built up over it. "I watched you turn into someone I didn't recognize, Xander. Someone with teeth in his voice and no interest in being talked back down. I didn't know if you were gone for good. Nobody could tell me for certain that you'd come back to yourself, and I sat with that uncertainty for days, watching you prowl around like something wearing your face instead of actually being you."

"I didn't know you carried that."

"Of course I carried it. You're one of my best friends, Xander. I watched something take you over once already, and I had no power to fix it, no spell, no research that helped fast enough, just fear and waiting and hoping." She wiped at her eyes, fierce and quick, unwilling to let the tears fully arrive. "So when I heard there was someone else in your head again, some part of me went straight back to that kitchen table, watching you not be you, and I needed a minute before I could actually hear the difference between then and now."

"This isn't the hyena, Will."

"I know that. I believe that, actually, now that I've had time to sit with it properly." She managed a small, watery smile. "But I needed you to know why it hit me the way it did. Twice now, I've watched something move into you that I didn't put there and couldn't take out if it went wrong. That's not a small thing for me, even when the second time turns out to actually be good."

"And again with the sitting," said the same posh, affected voice from a few chapters back, and this narrator regrets to report it did not stay quiet for long. "Bunch of lazy bastards, the whole cast of them. Not one of them can just think about something. Everybody's got to sit with it first."

Nobody else in the gallery said a word back. Some scenes earn that much restraint even from the ones who can't help themselves.

I didn't understand the full weight of that history, Coulson said, quiet, genuinely moved. Thank you for trusting me with it. I want you to know, for what it's worth, that I have no intention of ever giving you a reason to relive that fear. I'm not the hyena. I never will be.

Willow listened to Xander relay it, and something in her posture finally, fully eased, the old tension bleeding out of her shoulders.

"I believe you," she said, quiet. "I actually do. I just needed to say the fear out loud first, before I could get to the believing part."

"That's fair. More than fair." Xander reached over, squeezed her hand. "Thanks for trusting me with your half of it too. The magic stuff. I know that's not easy to hand somebody, even somebody who already sort of knew."

"Yeah, well." Willow managed a real smile now, warmer, steadier. "Turns out we've both been carrying more than either of us let on for a long time. Maybe it's good, actually, finally saying some of it out loud."

They sat together a while longer, comfortable now, the old fear finally given somewhere honest to land, and Xander felt, watching her finally relax fully into the conversation, a fresh, grateful understanding of exactly how much his friends had quietly carried for him over the years, unspoken, unacknowledged, simply because loving someone meant absorbing some of what they went through, whether you asked for that weight or not.

She's remarkable, Coulson said, once Willow had finally gone, drawn away by some new piece of Council business needing her attention.

Yeah, Xander agreed, watching the door close behind her, something warm and grateful settling into his chest. Yeah, she really is.
