What Xander Sees

"They rebuilt the school. On the Hellmouth. Again," the voice from the gallery said, weary in the way of a man who had said this before and expected to say it again. "This narrator has questions about the municipal permitting process in this town."

"Nobody in this story has ever once had questions about the municipal permitting process," someone in the anonymous rows pointed out. "That's sort of the whole joke."

The school had never looked right, even before tonight, a building that had burned once already and been rebuilt on top of the same ancient wrongness like nobody involved had thought that through very carefully. Tonight it looked like the mouth of something, which, Xander supposed, was more or less accurate, and the basement beneath it had opened into a scene out of a nightmare nobody had bothered warning them about, a churning dark well pouring out Turok-Han faster than the line of newly-minted Slayers could hold them back.

The first ten minutes were pure chaos, no room in them for anything but the next swing, the next step, the next scream that needed answering. Xander lost track of everyone around him almost immediately, caught glimpses only, Buffy cutting a path near the center of the fray, Faith and Robin Wood holding a flank together with the easy coordination of two people who'd finally stopped needing to prove anything to each other, Willow's voice somewhere behind them all, steady, holding something together that Xander didn't have the attention to spare on understanding.

Somewhere in the middle of it, wearing an amulet that glowed faint and terrible against his chest, Spike stood his ground and looked, for just a fraction of a second, like a man already saying goodbye.

Xander caught it in passing, mid-swing, the axe biting into something that shrieked and folded, and filed it away the way he filed everything these days, half-conscious, automatic, a habit built over years of watching people's faces for the gap between what they were saying and what they actually meant.

He didn't have time to think about it. The battle didn't leave room for thinking, only reacting, and it was a good ten more minutes before the tide shifted enough for Xander to catch a real breath, to look up and actually find Spike again in the chaos.

What he saw made his stomach drop.

Spike was fighting, still fighting, the amulet's light building steadily brighter against his chest, but something in the set of his shoulders had gone wrong, a hesitation creeping into moves that should have been automatic after a century of practice, and Xander, who had spent years hating this man with a thoroughness that came from genuine, hard-earned dislike, recognized the shape of it instantly, recognized it precisely because of all that history, not despite it.

He's about to break, Xander thought, and felt the certainty of it land with none of the softness that usually came with concern for someone he actually liked.

I don't see it, Coulson said, urgent, scanning what he could through Xander's eyes. He looks steady to me. Fighting well. What am I missing?

You're missing years of me watching that exact guy talk a big game right up until the second he actually believes it himself. Xander swung again, caught another Turok-Han across the throat, kept moving toward Spike through the chaos. That's not steady. That's a man about to convince himself he's not actually gonna make it, and once he believes that, he's done, doesn't matter how bright that amulet gets.

Buffy's closer. She'd know him better than either of us.

Buffy's got a hundred things to watch right now. I'm not sure she's got the specific angle on him I've got. Xander cut through two more feet of distance, close enough now to see Spike's jaw working, close enough to see a flicker in his eyes that Xander recognized from a hundred old arguments, the exact look of a man talking himself out of believing his own nerve. Trust me on this one. I've spent years learning exactly what this guy's bravado is covering. Turns out being somebody's least favorite person for that long teaches you things nobody warm and fuzzy would ever bother learning.

He reached Spike's side, planted himself there, axe still moving, still fighting, and didn't waste time on a gentle approach, because gentle had never once worked on this particular vampire and wasn't going to start now.

"You're about to fold," Xander said, flat, no softness in it at all. "Don't."

"Don't know what you're on about, whelp." Spike's voice had an edge in it that confirmed everything Xander had already read, a fraction too fast, a fraction too sharp. "Bit busy at the moment."

"You're standing there with a magic rock lighting up your chest telling yourself you're gonna burn to nothing and it won't have meant a thing." Xander swung, caught a Turok-Han reaching for Spike's blind side, kept talking without breaking rhythm. "That's what this is. I've watched you talk yourself into every stupid, self-destructive decision you've ever made for years, Spike. I know exactly what it looks like right before you convince yourself the worst version of the story's the true one."

"And what, you're gonna talk me out of it? You?" Something bitter and desperate cracked through the bravado. "Bloody hell, Harris, out of everyone in this fight, you're the last one I'd have picked to..."

"Yeah, well, lucky you, you got me anyway." Xander didn't soften it, didn't reach for comfort, because comfort had never once landed with Spike and wouldn't land now, not in the middle of a war, not from a man who'd spent years making it very clear he had no interest in Xander's approval or affection. "You wanna know what I actually think? I think you've spent a hundred years telling yourself you're not worth saving, and I think some sick part of you actually wants this to be the thing that finally proves it. Burn up, be the hero, get to stop trying. That's not sacrifice, Spike. That's just giving up with better lighting."

Something in Spike's face cracked open, raw and furious and, underneath both, terrified.

"That's not..."

"It is, though. I know it is, 'cause I've spent years watching you do exactly this, smaller versions of it, every single time you decided you weren't worth the effort of trying properly." Xander swung again, felt his arm screaming with exhaustion, kept his eyes on Spike's face. "So here's the deal. You don't get to burn out just to prove yourself right about being worthless. You hold this line 'cause we need you to hold it, not 'cause it finally lets you stop existing. There's a difference, and you know there's a difference, and I need you to actually pick the right one, right now, 'cause I am not explaining to Buffy that you checked out early."

For one long, terrible second, Spike didn't move at all, the amulet's light building higher, the battle roaring around both of them, and Xander held his ground, held the eye contact, refused to let the moment soften into something kinder than it needed to be.

Then something shifted, hard and sudden, behind Spike's eyes, the bravado settling back into place, except this time with something steadier underneath it, something that had actually chosen to be there instead of merely performing it.

"You're a right bastard, you know that?" Spike said, and there was something almost like a laugh buried under the words.

"Yeah. Been told." Xander managed a grin, real, exhausted, entirely earned. "Now hold the damn line."

Spike held it.

The amulet's light built and built, the Turok-Han army faltering as something ancient and terrible answered its call, and Spike stood his ground at the center of it, steady now, chosen now, that hesitation gone from his shoulders like it had never been there at all.

✳

The fight around them hadn't paused for any of it, and Xander turned back into the chaos the second Spike's line held, catching a Turok-Han across the knees, dragging a fallen Potential clear of a second one before it could finish what it started, the next thirty seconds and the thirty after that swallowing whatever quiet had briefly opened up between him and Spike.

It was minutes later, the tide of the whole impossible battle finally, unmistakably beginning to turn, before Coulson spoke again.

I couldn't have done that, Coulson said, quiet, once the immediate chaos of the fight had carried them a few steps further, a few more Turok-Han down. I want you to understand that clearly. No training I have covers that. I've never once in my career had to read a hundred-year-old vampire's internal despair through his fighting stance, and I have no framework for verifying you were even right. For all I know, you talked a man into holding a line he was always going to hold anyway.

Maybe, Xander admitted, breathing hard, watching Spike fight on, steady, sure. I don't have proof either. I just know what I saw, and I trusted it.

I know, Coulson said. And I backed you on it anyway. Completely, without a single piece of verification I could point to. I want to be honest about what that means, because I don't think I've done that before, not like this, not with something I had absolutely no way to confirm.

What's it mean?

It means I trust you, Coulson said, simply, no dryness left in it at all, nothing held back. Not your training, because you don't have any I recognize. Not a shared method, because we don't have one. Just you. I think that might actually be the answer to the thing we've been arguing about since the kitchen table, and I think I only just now understood it properly.

Nobody in the anonymous rows said anything. This narrator suspects, strongly, that this was the whole point of the argument all along, and doesn't intend to spoil it with a punchline now that it's finally landed.

Xander felt something settle in his chest, solid, earned, entirely unlike anything the last several weeks of argument had managed to produce between them.

"Alright," said one voice in the anonymous rows, affecting, for no reason anyone could identify, a posh British accent, "now don't start that again."

Nobody asked what "that" was. Everybody knew exactly what "that" was.

"Yeah," he said, out loud, quiet, watching Spike's light building toward whatever it was about to become, watching the tide of the whole impossible battle finally begin to turn. "Yeah. I think that's it too."

Neither of them said anything else about it. There wasn't anything left to argue, not about this, not anymore, and somewhere in the settling quiet underneath the ongoing roar of the fight, Xander understood that whatever came next, whatever this partnership turned into once the dust finally settled, it wasn't going to need the argument anymore to hold itself together.

It had found something sturdier than that to stand on instead.
