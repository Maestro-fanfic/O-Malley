Chapter 11 of 43

The Irony

Giles called everyone together the next morning, the living room packed shoulder to shoulder with Potentials still working out where to look, the kind of collective uncertainty that settled over a room once its usual center of gravity had walked out the front door the night before. Faith stood where Buffy usually stood, arms loose at her sides, entirely unbothered by the weight of thirty pairs of eyes suddenly needing somewhere to land.

"Here's the deal," she said, perched on the arm of the couch instead of standing at the front of the room the way Buffy always had, like formality was a coat she wasn't interested in wearing. "We don't have time to war-game every angle six ways from Sunday. We hit hard, we hit fast, and we don't leave anybody standing around waiting for a memo. Feels right, we go. Simple."

A hand went up near the back, one of the newer girls, hesitant. "What if it doesn't feel right to everybody at the same time?"

"New captain, same energy," someone in the anonymous rows observed. "Different flavor of confident. I respect the rebrand."

"'For now,'" another voice repeated. "She said 'for now.' Write that down too, notebook girl."

"Then you trust the person who's been doing this longer than you have." Faith's grin flashed, easy, confident, a grin that had gotten people to follow her into worse situations than this one. "That's me, for now. We clear?"

"What about backup positions?" Another voice, one of the quieter Potentials, a girl who'd taken to writing everything down in a battered spiral notebook since the day she arrived. "What if the first plan doesn't work?"

"Then we adjust on the fly. That's kind of the whole gig, sweetheart. You don't get a rehearsal for the apocalypse." Faith said it easy, no cruelty in it, just the flat certainty of someone who genuinely believed what she was saying. "Look, I get it, you're used to Blondie's whole color-coded battle plan thing. I'm not her. Never gonna be her. What I've got instead is, I don't freeze. I don't second-guess myself into a corner. Sometimes that's worth more than a plan with every box checked."

"Give her a break, she's doing the confident-captain bit, it comes with a whole voice."

"I'm not saying it's wrong, I'm saying somebody should be writing all of this down for the inevitable retrospective."

"Somebody is." A beat, pointed. "Notebook girl. Back row. Already on it."

The girl with the notebook wrote something down anyway, quick and quiet, and Xander caught the small, private gesture and understood it without needing it explained: some fears didn't go away just because you'd been told, convincingly, not to have them.

Nobody argued out loud, though. Xander watched three dozen scared teenage girls nod along like that was the most reassuring thing anyone had said to them in a week, and felt something uncomfortable settle behind his ribs that took him a second to name.

You're doing the thing where you go quiet, Coulson observed, mild.

"He got that from me," Fury's voice put in, mild in exactly the same register, entirely unbothered by the audience. "Everybody know that? The quiet-noticing thing. That's a Fury original. I should've trademarked it decades ago."

I'm doing the thing where I notice something, Xander corrected, watching Faith wave off a question about contingencies with an easy, confident we'll handle it when we get there. You know what that plan is? That's instinct. That's zero backup, zero net, purely feels right, we go. That's basically my whole philosophy of life, just said out loud by somebody with actual muscle behind it.

I noticed the resemblance too.

"Oh, HE noticed it too," someone in the anonymous rows crowed. "This is the good part. This is the part where the guy who lectures about process has to watch his own philosophy walk around wearing somebody else's face."

"Poetic justice doesn't usually get a body double, but here we are."

Bet you love that.

I have complicated feelings about it, Coulson admitted, and there was an edge in it now, real and unhidden in a way Xander hadn't heard from him since the vineyard. I've watched instinct-driven leadership work exactly often enough to respect it and fail exactly often enough to distrust it on principle. No accountability chain. No vetting on that call she just made. Authority claimed by confidence rather than earned through anything I'd recognize as process. I'd flag every part of that in a formal review.

So you don't trust her.

I don't trust the method. That's not quite the same thing, though I'll grant you it's close enough tonight that the difference might not matter.

Xander turned that over, watching Faith move through the room with the easy, physical confidence of someone who'd never once doubted her own read on a fight, answering questions with a certainty that filled the room the way Buffy's careful, considered answers never quite had. It was strange, hearing Coulson's skepticism laid out plain like that, an actual opinion instead of the careful neutrality he'd been keeping since the wave-off he hadn't made yet, and stranger still to recognize, underneath it, that Coulson's bias toward his own training was exactly the mirror of the bias Xander had spent the last several days accusing him of not understanding.

You know you're doing the same thing you keep telling me I do, Xander said. Trusting your own method because it's yours. That's not exactly neutral ground you're standing on either.

A pause. Longer than Xander expected. No, Coulson said, finally, and there was something almost surprised in the admission. It isn't. I hadn't quite let myself see it from that angle. Another beat. I'll sit with that.

"THERE it is," several voices said at once, ragged and overlapping. "The fold. The actual fold this time, not the strategic one."

"Somebody get the trophy back out, we retired it too early in chapter five."

You gonna admit I've got a point, or just carry it around unsaid forever?

Both, possibly. I find those aren't mutually exclusive either.

"'Both, possibly,'" the gallery's voice sighed. "This man's entire personality is a hedge clause."

Xander didn't push it further. It felt, for once, like enough had been said, and around him the meeting was breaking up into smaller clusters, girls pairing off to check weapons, to run drills Faith had rattled off without writing anything down, the whole house already moving to a rhythm that belonged to somebody other than the person it had belonged to a week ago.

He caught up with the notebook girl on his way out, unable to quite let it go. "Hey. You doing okay?"

"Fine." She closed the notebook, a little too quickly. "I just like keeping track of things. Helps me feel like I've got some control, even when I don't."

"That's not a bad instinct to have." Xander crouched down slightly, meeting her at eye level, an old habit by now, worn smooth from a dozen conversations just like this one. "For what it's worth, it's okay to trust Faith's confidence and still keep your own notes on the side. Those aren't opposites. Having a backup plan in your own head doesn't mean you don't believe in the person leading you."

Something in the girl's shoulders eased, just slightly. "Nobody else really gets that. Feels like everyone wants me to just, I don't know, relax and trust completely, or panic and refuse to follow anybody at all. Nobody talks about doing both."

"Yeah, well." Xander managed a small, tired smile. "Turns out doing both is basically the only sane way to survive most of this. Ask me sometime, I'll tell you all about it."

She managed a small smile back, tucking the notebook carefully into her jacket, and went to find her sparring partner for the evening's drills, steadier than she'd been a few minutes ago, even if only by a little.

Xander watched Faith work the room a while longer after that, still turning over what Coulson had said, and couldn't shake the thought, small and uncomfortable, that he was watching his own argument with Coulson play out in front of him, wearing somebody else's face, with real stakes attached this time, and no clean answer yet about whether that made him feel better about his own case or worse. Maybe, he thought, that was the actual lesson underneath all of it, not that one way of leading was right and the other wrong, but that a house full of frightened people needed room for both kinds of confidence to exist side by side, Faith's loud, instinctive certainty and the quieter, more careful kind that kept its own notes on the side, just in case.
