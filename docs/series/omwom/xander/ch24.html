What's Left

The tide turned, and then it kept turning, and somewhere in the middle of that slow, grinding shift from losing to maybe-not-losing, Xander lost track of where anyone actually was.

That was the thing about a battle this size, he was learning, in real time, in the worst possible classroom. It didn't hold still long enough to be understood. It was a hundred small fights happening at once, each one its own entire war with its own entire stakes, and a person could only ever really live inside the one directly in front of them, trusting, with no way to verify it, that the others were going somewhere survivable.

He heard Anya's voice before he found her, sharp and furious, cutting through the chaos with the same blend of terror and stubbornness that had carried her through worse fights than this one. She was holding a line near the gymnasium doors with two Slayers barely a week into their power, directing them with the brisk, impatient competence of a woman who had spent a thousand years learning how to survive and had no intention of losing that education now, tonight, to a bunch of ancient, eyeless things that smelled like the inside of the earth.

She's doing well, Coulson observed, some part of his attention tracking the fight even through everything else demanding it. Genuinely well. Whatever she was before this, she's earned her place in this fight tonight.

She's terrifying and she knows exactly what she's doing, Xander thought back, cutting down a Turok-Han that had gotten too close, and she's gonna be insufferable about it for weeks if we all make it out of here.

He never got to find out if she would have been.

The gallery said nothing. Not even the anonymous rows, ordinarily quick with something to fill a silence, offered so much as a rustle. This narrator will not be riffing on this scene, tonight or on any future viewing.

He didn't see it happen. That was, later, one of the small cruelties he carried out of that night, that the moment itself came to him secondhand, relayed in fragments by people who had been closer, who had watched a section of ceiling give way at exactly the wrong angle, exactly the wrong second, burying her under rubble that hadn't given anyone, not her, not the girls she'd been protecting, any warning at all.

He felt it differently. He felt the fight around him change shape, felt a ragged cry go up from somewhere near the gymnasium doors, and something in him, some old, worn instinct sharpened by years of losing people in exactly this kind of chaos, simply knew, before anyone told him, before he'd even fully processed what the sound meant, that the world had just gotten smaller by one person he loved.

Xander. Coulson's voice, careful, present, not trying to fix anything, just there. I'm here.

He didn't answer. There wasn't an answer available yet, nothing that could survive being put into words while a battle was still happening around him, while thirty more people still needed him functional, still needed his axe swinging and his feet moving and whatever was left of his attention pointed at staying alive long enough to grieve properly later.

So he kept fighting. He didn't know, afterward, how he'd managed it, how a person's body kept doing the necessary thing while the rest of them had gone somewhere else entirely, but it did, hour after hour, an axe rising and falling with a mechanical precision that had nothing left of him in it at all.

Coulson didn't try to talk him through it. That was, Xander understood only much later, its own kind of gift, a restraint that came from some deep well of hard experience, knowing that grief this raw didn't want company yet, wanted only enough presence to know it wasn't alone, nothing more demanding than that. He stayed close and quiet, an unmentioned shoulder, there without announcing itself, the same unglamorous, load-bearing not-doing-anything the whole strange partnership had kept coming back to since the very first night.

✳

The battle ended the way battles like this ended, not with a single clean victory so much as a slow, exhausted realization that the enemy had stopped coming, that the tide really had turned, that somehow, impossibly, more of them were still standing than weren't.

The school began to come apart around them almost immediately after, the whole structure groaning under stresses no ordinary building was built to survive, and Buffy's voice, hoarse and commanding, cut through the ringing aftermath of the fight with an urgency that pulled everyone's attention back to the one thing left that mattered: get out, get everyone out, right now, don't wait to see how bad it gets.

Xander helped carry the wounded up through crumbling stairwells that hadn't finished deciding whether they'd hold, past cracks spreading through walls like something enormous underneath the school was finally, fully waking up and stretching. Outside, the whole of Sunnydale had started to follow the school's example, streets buckling, houses folding in on themselves, an entire town giving up its long, uneasy truce with the thing that had always lived beneath it.

They made it to the cars with minutes to spare, a battered, bloody convoy pulling away as the ground behind them opened wide and swallowed the last of everything any of them had ever called home.

Xander sat in the back of Giles's car, the axe finally set down, finally still, watching Sunnydale collapse in the rearview mirror the way you watched something you already knew, on some level, you were never coming back to.

I'm sorry, Coulson said, quiet, after a long stretch of road had passed under them in silence. About Anya. I didn't know her well. I know that doesn't help. I'm still sorry.

Xander didn't answer right away, watching the dust cloud where a town used to be finally disappear behind them entirely.

"Yeah," he said, eventually, out loud, his voice rough and unfamiliar even to himself. "Yeah. Me too."

Nobody in the car said much of anything else for a long while, the whole convoy moving forward through the dark toward wherever came next, carrying more silence than any of them had words left to fill, and somewhere behind his ribs, underneath the exhaustion and the grief and the strange, disorienting relief of simply still being alive, Xander felt the shape of a life he didn't recognize yet beginning, whether he was ready for it or not.
