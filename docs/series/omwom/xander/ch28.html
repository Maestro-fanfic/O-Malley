The Posting

The Council, such as it was now, three exhausted adults and a stack of borrowed office space in a building nobody could quite explain who owned, spent the better part of a week working out what came next for everyone still standing.

There were a lot of newly-Called girls out there, scattered across a world that had, until very recently, been entirely unprepared for even one Slayer at a time, let alone thousands all waking up to the same impossible truth on the same impossible night. Some kind of coordinated response was needed, fast, before panic or exploitation or simple bad luck claimed girls the world hadn't even finished processing existed yet.

Xander sat in on the planning meeting mostly by accident, having wandered in looking for Giles and ended up staying once the conversation turned toward logistics he found he actually had opinions about.

"The map with the pins," the voice from the gallery said, delighted. "Every organization in every story like this eventually gets a map with pins in it. This narrator has never once seen anyone actually explain who orders the pins."

"We need people in the field," Giles was saying, spreading a world map across the makeshift conference table, red pins already dotting a dozen countries where reports had already started trickling in. "Not just watchers in the traditional sense. Someone who can find these girls, assess what they need, and provide it, without frightening them further or exposing them to unnecessary risk."

"Someone steady," Willow added. "Someone who won't panic if the first conversation goes badly, and won't push too hard if it needs to go slow instead."

Giles's eyes found Xander across the table, and something in his expression shifted, thoughtful, deliberate.

"Xander," he said. "I'd like you to consider Africa."

"NEW CONTINENT," someone in the anonymous rows announced, sitting bolt upright, thrilled. "We're leaving the sound stage! Somebody find out if the budget stretches to actual location shooting!"

"It's a book. It stretches to however many words the author feels like typing."

"Don't ruin this for me."

The room went quiet the way rooms did when a decision that mattered was being made out loud instead of behind closed doors.

"Me?"

"You." Giles set down the pin he'd been holding, met Xander's eye directly. "I've watched what you did with Molly before the vineyard. I've watched what you did with the girl at the crypt, with Buffy, though that's rather a different category of person to be steadying. And I know, now, what you've been carrying alongside all of it since the vineyard itself." A brief, meaningful pause. "I don't think that's a coincidence, Xander. I think you have precisely the temperament this work requires, and I would rather send someone who's actually good at this than someone who merely has the free time."

Xander felt something warm and uncertain settle in his chest, the unfamiliar weight of being chosen for a reason rather than by default.

He means that, Coulson said, quiet, and there was real conviction in it. I've sat through a great many personnel assignments dressed up as compliments that weren't actually compliments. This isn't one of them. He's seen exactly what you're capable of, and he's betting on it deliberately.

Yeah, Xander thought, watching Giles's face for any sign of the old dismissal, the old dad-adjacent affection that had never quite extended to actually respecting him. He didn't find it. Yeah, I think you're right.

Not everyone in the building shared Giles's read, though nobody was crass enough to say so directly. Xander caught the shape of it anyway, in the way a couple of the other adults exchanged a glance when the assignment was announced more broadly, a look that didn't need words to communicate its actual content: convenient, that look said, useful that the awkward one, the Zeppo, the extra body without an obvious specialty, gets shipped somewhere far away where nobody has to figure out what to do with him at headquarters. Nobody said it out loud. Xander wasn't sure anyone involved would have even fully admitted it to themselves. But he caught it anyway, the same way he caught most things these days, and it sat next to Giles's genuine, considered faith in him without either one canceling the other out.

You noticed that too, Coulson said, quiet.

Hard not to. Xander kept his face neutral, professional, the same face he'd been wearing all week for people who needed him steady. Can't tell if it actually matters, though. Like, does it matter why they're sending me, if the sending itself is the right call either way?

I don't know, Coulson admitted. I've turned that exact question over plenty of times myself, over the years, whether the reason behind an assignment changes what the assignment actually means once you're the one doing the work. I've never landed on a clean answer. I don't think there is one.

Comforting.

I try.

Xander accepted the posting two days later, and spent the rest of that week saying goodbyes that felt heavier than he'd expected them to, given that nobody involved was actually dying, just scattering, the way people did after a war finally ended and there was suddenly nothing left holding everyone in the same room.

Buffy found him the morning he left, packing the last of his things into a single duffel bag that felt absurdly small for the size of what he was walking into.

"You sure about this?" she asked, leaning in the doorway.

"No," Xander admitted, honest. "But I think that's kind of the point. Being sure was never really my thing anyway."

"You'll be good at it. I already know that." She crossed the room and hugged him, fierce and brief. "Just, come back sometimes, okay? Don't disappear on us entirely."

"Nobody in the anonymous rows is riffing on the goodbye scene," someone noted, quiet. "Even we have limits."

"Wouldn't dream of it." He held onto the hug a beat longer than strictly necessary. "You take care of this place. I'm trusting you with it."

"Always have been. Even when I didn't deserve it."

"Especially then." He managed a small, genuine smile. "That's kind of what trust is for."

She left him to finish packing, and Xander zipped the duffel bag closed with a finality that felt bigger than the bag itself, a man closing one chapter of his life to make room for whatever came next, and found himself, a week after that, on a plane he'd never expected to be on, headed toward a continent he'd never expected to see, carrying nothing but a bag, a voice in his head, and a job description that amounted, mostly, to go find the scared girls and be the steady thing nobody else has time to be.

It was, he thought, watching Los Angeles shrink away below the wing, a job that would have made perfect sense to him a year ago, and one he never would have believed, back then, that anyone would trust him to actually do well.

Ready? Coulson asked, as the plane leveled out over open ocean, the whole unknown shape of the next several months waiting somewhere on the other side of the horizon.

No, Xander said, honest, and found, underneath the honesty, something that felt almost like anticipation. But that's never really stopped me before.

No, Coulson agreed, warm underneath the dryness. I don't imagine it has.
