The Settlement

Not every report led him somewhere he needed to fix something, and the reminder, when it came, arrived as a genuine, welcome surprise.

The village sat tucked into a valley two days' walk from the nearest road, self-contained in a way that read, at first glance, almost suspiciously peaceful, and Xander approached it with the same careful vigilance he brought to every unknown situation these days, half-expecting the calm to be a mask for something worse underneath.

"Here we go," the voice from the gallery said, settling in for what it clearly expected to be a twist. "Too peaceful. Somebody's hiding a body under the well. Called it."

It wasn't.

"...Huh," the gallery admitted, after a beat. "Well. Good for them, I suppose."

The girl's name was Nomvula, and she met him at the village's edge with an escort of four adults who introduced themselves, unhurried and unbothered, as though a strange American showing up asking after a Slayer was a perfectly ordinary Tuesday occurrence.

This is different, Coulson observed, quiet, cataloging the scene with the same trained attention he brought to every arrival. No fear in the greeting. No urgency. They're not hiding her. They're presenting her.

Yeah, Xander agreed, watching Nomvula stand easy and confident among the adults flanking her, none of the hunched, guarded posture he'd learned to expect. This is new.

"You could just... let it be nice."

"I could. I'm choosing not to, on principle, but I could."

"She has been strong since she was small," one of the elders explained, once introductions had settled, pride plain and unguarded in her voice. "We did not understand it at first. Now we understand it is a gift, and we protect what she is rather than fear it."

"You knew something was different about her? Before the Calling, I mean?"

"She has always run faster than the boys. Always seen further, heard sooner." The elder smiled, warm. "When the whole village dreamed the same dream, months ago, of a woman speaking of a well and a scythe and a burden being shared, we understood. We simply waited for you, or someone like you, to arrive and confirm it."

Xander felt something loosen in his chest, an old tension he carried into every one of these visits, the readiness for crisis simply finding nothing here to be ready for.

Nomvula herself watched the exchange with open curiosity rather than fear, occasionally cutting in with a sharp, confident question of her own. "The other girls you find. Are they all afraid?"

"Some. A lot of them, honestly. Most didn't have what you've got here." Xander gestured at the village around them, the easy, unguarded normalcy of it. "You're lucky. I don't say that to a lot of the girls I meet out here."

"I know I am lucky." Nomvula's expression sobered, some real weight settling behind her confidence. "I think about the others sometimes. The ones who are not."

She's got real empathy, Coulson observed, something warm underneath the professional read. Not just confidence. Actual concern for people whose circumstances she's never had to share.

Yeah. Xander watched her a moment longer, recognizing a familiar combination: strength paired with genuine care for people less fortunate. Good combination to have, that. Hope the Network finds a use for it eventually.

They spent the better part of two days in the village, not because anything required fixing, but because the elders, generous and curious in equal measure, wanted to understand more about what their daughter was becoming, what the wider world of Slayers actually looked like, what resources and community existed beyond their own valley.

The first evening, they fed him until he genuinely couldn't manage another bite, a rich, spiced stew served alongside bread still warm from a communal oven, and the whole village turned out for it, children weaving between the fires while the adults peppered him with questions, some practical, some clearly just curious about the strange American who'd walked two days into their valley on nothing but a rumor and a dream nobody else had explained properly.

"Tell us about the others," one of the younger women asked, settling nearby with a baby balanced easily on one hip. "The other girls like Nomvula. Where do they live? Are they safe, the way she is safe?"

Xander considered his answer carefully, unwilling to lie and unwilling, too, to needlessly frighten a room full of people who had, so far, done everything right. "Some are as safe as she is. Some aren't, not yet. Part of my job's finding the ones who need help getting there."

"And you help them. Alone?"

"Not alone." He didn't elaborate further, and nobody pushed, though he caught Nomvula watching him with the same sharp, assessing curiosity she'd shown since their first meeting, filing the deflection away without comment.

The second day, Nomvula insisted on showing him the whole valley properly, moving with an easy, confident stride through terrain that would have exhausted him twice over without her patient pace-setting, naming plants and warning him off a nest of something venomous tucked into a rock crevice before he got anywhere near it.

"You'd have stepped right on it," she said, amused, watching him give the crevice a wide, grateful berth.

"Add it to the list," someone in the anonymous rows said. "Vineyards, doorframes, now venomous crevices. This narrator's starting to think the man attracts hazards specifically."

"He does have one working eye. Depth perception's still a work in progress."

"That's not an excuse, that's foreshadowing for a sequel where he finally learns to duck."

"Good thing I've got you, then."

"Good thing," she agreed, something warm and easy in it, and Xander understood, watching her navigate her own home with total, unhurried mastery, that this was what confidence actually looked like when it had never once been starved of the resources it needed to grow properly, an entirely different animal from the hard-won, scar-tissue confidence he'd watched so many other girls build out of nothing but necessity.

By the time they'd circled back to the village, the light going gold and long over the valley, Xander found himself, for once, not cataloging threats or gaps in a plan, just present, unhurried, genuinely enjoying a walk with a girl who didn't need saving from anything at all.

You look relaxed, Coulson observed, quiet, something almost surprised in it. I don't think I've seen that from you out here before. Not like this.

Yeah. Xander watched Nomvula greet a group of younger children running to meet her, scooping the smallest one up with easy, practiced strength. Guess it's nice, every once in a while, getting to just visit instead of rescue.

Xander answered every question patiently over those two days, and found, somewhere in the unhurried rhythm of it, a version of his job he didn't often get to practice: not rescue, not crisis management, just connection, information, the quiet work of linking one safe corner of the world to a larger network that might, someday, need to call on the steady confidence Nomvula already had in abundance.

"You will come back?" Nomvula asked, the morning he finally prepared to leave. "When you need me. For the others."

"Yeah." Xander felt something warm and certain settle into the promise. "When we need you. Wouldn't ask before then, though. This is your home. Your choice, always, when and if you leave it."

"Good." She managed a small, confident smile. "I like that you asked instead of assumed."

You're getting good at that too, Coulson observed, once they were back on the road, the village fading behind them into the valley's green folds. Reading which situations need rescue and which just need acknowledgment.

Yeah, well. Xander glanced back once, watching the last of the village's rooftops disappear from view. Wish they were all like that one. Makes the job feel a little less like running from one fire to the next.

They're not, Coulson agreed, quiet. But it's good to be reminded, every so often, what it looks like when it goes right without you having to fix anything at all.
