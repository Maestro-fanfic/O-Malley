The House Falls

"Nobody's riffing on this one," Fury's voice said, quiet, before the anonymous rows could try. "We already told you where the gap was. This part's just watching it matter."

They came in through the back around midnight, the way they always did, a dozen Bringers moving with the kind of flat, purposeful silence that belonged to men who'd given up their eyes and most of their humanity along with them, and for the first four minutes, Faith's plan held exactly the way she'd promised it would.

"Kitchen's solid!" someone shouted, and it was, three Potentials and a length of rebar making short, brutal work of the first wave through the back door, the chokepoint doing exactly the job Faith had drawn it to do on Giles's old floor plan.

Xander, stationed at the front hall with two others and a fire axe he'd never expected to actually need, felt something in his chest loosen half a degree. Maybe he'd been wrong. Maybe the gap Coulson had spotted was never going to matter, one of those flaws that only counted if the universe bothered to find it. Around him, girls who'd spent two days running drills moved with something that almost looked like confidence, weapons up, positions held, the house's newest rhythm doing exactly what it had been built to do.

The universe bothered.

It came from the side, not the front or the back, a third group nobody had accounted for pouring in through the dining room's bay window, glass exploding inward in a spray that caught two Potentials before anyone had time to process the new direction. The front hall and the kitchen held, exactly as planned, two solid walls doing exactly their job, and the house between them, the actual living space where thirty scared girls had been sleeping and eating and trying to remember what safe used to feel like, came apart from the middle outward with nowhere organized to fall back to, because nobody had planned for what happened once the line, somehow, everywhere at once, stopped being a line and became a house full of chaos with three separate directions of threat and one single, panicked, unrehearsed idea of what to do about it.

This is it, Coulson said, and his voice had gone hard and fast, urgent in a register Xander hadn't heard from him since the vineyard. This is the gap. There's nowhere for anyone to fall back to. You need to get people moving toward an actual choke, not just away from what's closest.

Working on it! Xander swung the axe wide, caught a Bringer across the shoulder, felt the impact travel up his arm in a way that would ache badly tomorrow if there was a tomorrow that let him notice. Around him the hall had turned into the scrambling, directionless chaos Coulson had warned about days ago, girls running toward each other instead of toward anywhere useful, Faith's voice somewhere upstairs shouting orders that arrived a half second too late to matter, the whole careful architecture of the plan dissolving into the oldest and worst shape a fight could take, everyone for themselves, nobody's back covered, nobody's exit clear.

He saw Robin Wood go down near the stairs, a Bringer's blade catching him low and wrong, saw two Potentials try to drag him clear and get tangled instead in a hallway that had no organized route out of it, only doorways leading to other rooms leading to other doorways, an entire house that had been built for living in and not one single corridor of it built for retreating through.

Xander, the study, there's a window that leads to the side yard, get people funneling there, it's the closest thing to an actual exit this house has left...

"The study!" Xander shouted, loud as he could over the chaos, pointing with the axe. "Everybody, the study, out the side window, go, go!"

It worked, mostly, a trickle of girls finding the direction and following it, but it was minutes too late, built on the fly instead of planned, the improvised fix to a hole that should have been closed before the fighting ever started, and even as bodies started moving the right way at last, Xander felt a sick recognition arrive in real time, no delay, no distance, landing exactly as hard as Coulson had warned it would three days ago on a floor plan nobody had wanted to argue with out loud.

You told me, Xander thought, breathing hard, watching Robin Wood get half-carried, half-dragged toward the study window by two girls who should never have had to learn to do that. You actually told me.

This isn't the moment for that conversation. Coulson's voice was flat, controlled, doing the thing professionals did when there wasn't room yet for the feeling underneath the words. Get people to the window. We'll have the other conversation later.

Xander swung the axe again, caught another Bringer, felt his own arm going numb with the effort of it, and kept moving people toward the study, toward the one exit that existed now only because Coulson had found it after the fact, a patch stitched onto a plan that had never accounted for needing one, and somewhere underneath the adrenaline and the noise and the narrow, focused work of surviving the next thirty seconds, the shape of his own mistake sat in his chest like a stone he was going to be carrying for a long time.

The fighting ran another ten minutes past that, the Bringers finally thinning, finally breaking, whatever purpose had sent them retreating as suddenly and silently as it had arrived, leaving the house full of broken glass and blood and a ringing quiet that came after violence finally stopped needing anyone's attention.

They counted the cost in the gray hour before dawn, the house full of shattered glass and blood that hadn't fully dried yet, and the hollow quiet that came after violence had finally, finally stopped needing anyone's attention.

Robin Wood was alive. Barely, according to the two Potentials with the most first-aid training doing what they could with what they had, but alive, which was more than the house could say for everyone. Two more girls hadn't made it, names Xander had only just learned in the last week, faces he was going to remember for a lot longer than that.

Xander sat on the porch steps, the axe still in his hand because he hadn't quite managed to put it down yet, and didn't have a joke ready, didn't reach for one, didn't try.

I should've said it louder, he thought, eventually, into the quiet. When you flagged it. I should've made a bigger deal out of it instead of just letting it drop because I didn't want to be the guy second-guessing Faith in front of everybody.

Maybe, Coulson said, quiet. I don't know that louder would have changed the outcome. Faith might not have listened regardless. I don't say that to let you off the hook. I say it because I don't actually know, and I'm not going to pretend certainty I don't have just to make this conversation feel more resolved than it is.

That's not really making me feel better.

I'm not trying to make you feel better. I'm trying to be honest with you, which I think, right now, matters more.

Xander looked out at the wrecked yard, the broken bay window, the first gray edge of morning starting to find its way over the fence, and felt the weight of the last several hours settle into something that wasn't going away with sleep or jokes or time, not for a good while.

"Yeah," he said, out loud, to the empty porch, to the house behind him full of people trying to put themselves back together. "Yeah. Okay."

It wasn't an apology. It wasn't quite an acceptance either. It was just the sound of a boy carrying the unglamorous weight of having been wrong about something that mattered, and finding, for the first time in his life, that there wasn't a joke anywhere close enough to reach for.

"Yeah," Fury's voice said, quiet, no performance left in it at all. "Now you're learning, kid."
