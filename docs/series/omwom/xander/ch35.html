The First Handoff

It caught up with him three days later, on the road between villages, and Xander understood immediately, watching the truck ahead of him block the narrow dirt track, that the quiet resolution at the mother's doorstep hadn't been the whole story after all.

There'd been someone else with a stake in that girl. He learned that now, too late to have planned around it, four men climbing out of the blocking truck with the loose, confident violence of people used to getting their way through simple physical intimidation, one of them the girl's uncle, Xander recognized him now from a photograph he'd been shown days ago and dismissed as irrelevant, a man who had apparently been counting on that daughter's labor, or her fear, or something else entirely that nobody had bothered explaining to Xander before he'd walked into that house and disrupted whatever arrangement had existed before he got there.

"Something wicked this way comes," someone in the anonymous rows murmured, quiet, no flourish left in the delivery at all, just an old line reached for because it already knew the shape of this.

This isn't a negotiation, Coulson said, immediately, flat and urgent, reading the men's posture with the trained speed of someone who'd assessed a hundred situations exactly like this one. Look at their hands. Look at how they're spacing themselves around the truck. This is a beating, or worse, and it's coming in the next thirty seconds.

Yeah, I'm getting that too. Xander kept his own hands loose, visible, buying whatever fraction of a second he could with the appearance of calm. Options?

Talking won't work. I don't think you have the vocabulary for a negotiation this fast-moving even if it would, and I don't think these men are interested in one regardless. If this goes physical, and it's about to, you're not equipped for four-on-one. I am. Not confident I'd win outright, but I'd give us considerably better odds than you'd have alone.

Xander felt the offer land differently than it had at the vineyard, all those months ago, when handing over control to a man he'd known for twenty minutes had felt like an impossible ask. This wasn't twenty minutes anymore. This was months of arguments and reconciliations and a man who had backed his instinct about Spike with nothing but trust to stand on, and the decision, when it actually arrived, didn't feel like a surrender at all.

Do it, Xander thought, clear, deliberate, the choice entirely his own to make. Take it.

The transfer happened in a way neither of them had fully anticipated, a smooth, seamless handoff, Xander's own awareness sliding sideways rather than vanishing, still fully present, still watching, still feeling everything, just no longer the one directing the muscles that mattered.

Coulson moved.

"No flips," someone in the anonymous rows observed, sounding faintly betrayed. "I was promised competence. I was not told it would be this boring to watch."

"That's the point. Boring is the professional's compliment to himself."

It wasn't flashy. That was the first thing Xander noticed, watching from somewhere just behind his own eyes, a strange, vertiginous experience of witnessing his own body do things he'd never trained it to do, efficient, economical, every motion built around ending a confrontation as fast as possible rather than looking impressive doing it. The first man went down with a strike to the throat that dropped him gagging before he'd even fully raised his hands. The second took a redirected shove that used his own momentum to put him face-first into the dirt. Xander's body moved through the remaining two with the same brisk, professional efficiency, disabling rather than destroying, restraint that could only belong to a man who had clearly spent a career learning exactly how much force a situation actually required and not one ounce more.

The uncle, last standing, backed away with his hands up, shouting something Xander's limited grasp of the language couldn't fully parse, though the tone translated well enough on its own: fear, and fury, and the helplessness of a man who had expected an easy target and found something else entirely waiting for him instead.

Coulson didn't press the advantage. He let the man retreat, let all four of them scatter back toward their truck and the safety of distance, and only once they were genuinely gone, genuinely no longer a threat, did Xander feel control settle back fully into his own hands, the transition as seamless leaving as it had been arriving.

He stood there a moment, breathing hard, his own body feeling suddenly, strangely unfamiliar, like a coat he'd just gotten back after loaning it to someone else for an afternoon.

You okay? Coulson asked, quiet, genuine concern under the question.

I don't know yet. Xander flexed his own hands, watched them move exactly the way they always had, and found the ordinariness of that somehow more unsettling than anything dramatic could have been. That was weird. Good weird, I think, given the alternative was probably getting jumped by four guys with something to prove. But weird.

I understand. For what it's worth, I felt strange about it too. I've spent twenty years being the one who acts. Being handed a body to act with, rather than relying on my own, is a different thing entirely than I expected it to be, even having known the mechanic existed in theory.

Did it feel good? Being able to actually do something instead of just talking me through it?

Coulson considered that for a long moment before answering. Yes, he said, finally, honest. And it also felt like a genuine trust I'm not sure I've earned yet, being given a body that isn't mine and asked to bring it back exactly as I found it. I'd like to be worthy of being asked again. I don't take it lightly that you did.

Xander turned that over, walking the rest of the way to his own waiting truck on legs that still felt faintly, strangely borrowed.

You did bring it back exactly as you found it, Xander said, eventually, checking himself over out of habit, finding nothing worse than the ordinary aches of a long day's walk. No damage. No weirdness left over. Just, you know. A very efficient beating of four guys who deserved it.

I'll take the compliment.

It wasn't really a compliment. More like a receipt.

I'll take that too.

"A receipt," the gallery's voice repeated, delighted. "Twenty years of field training and the review he gets is an itemized receipt. This narrator's never seen a man take an insult with this much grace."

"It's not an insult if he agrees with it."

"That's what makes it worse for him, actually."

Xander managed something close to a real laugh at that, the tension of the last several minutes finally draining out of him, and climbed into the truck to finish the drive to the next village, carrying with him a new, unfamiliar knowledge about exactly how far this partnership was actually willing to go, and exactly how much he'd come to trust it, without ever quite deciding to, one small handed-over moment at a time.
