Chapter 1: Nothing to Report



"Coulson" (POV):

The requisition form wanted to know, in triplicate, why Strike Team Delta needed a fourth grappling rig when the last three had, per the incident report, "not been lost, technically."

"Technically," Coulson said, "is doing a lot of work in that sentence."

"It's accurate," Fury said, without looking up from his own stack. "Barton didn't lose it. He threw it at a helicopter."

"On purpose?"

"Does it matter?"

"For the form? Enormously." Coulson clicked his pen closed, then open again. Across the table, two junior analysts had stopped pretending not to listen. "If it was tactical, I can code it as consumed ordnance and nobody above pay grade eleven ever thinks about it again. If it was Barton being Barton, it comes out of his equipment allowance and I get to have a conversation with him that neither of us enjoys."

"Code it tactical."

"It hit a Duane Reade."

"Did it stop the helicopter?"

"It was never going to stop the helicopter, Nick, it's a grappling rig."

"Then code it as suppressive."

Coulson looked at him for a long moment, wearing the look he reserved for men who outranked him and knew it. Then he wrote something down, because some hills were not worth the paperwork required to die on them, and this was one of an increasingly long list.

He was three words into asking whether anyone had actually confirmed the Duane Reade was empty when the pen stopped meaning anything in his hand.

He didn't feel himself fall. That was the detail that would have bothered him most, later, if there had been a later that worked the way laters were supposed to. There was no lightheadedness, no tunnel closing in from the edges, none of the small mercies a body usually offered on its way down. One second he was a man mid-sentence with a pen and an opinion about suppressive fire classifications. The next, he simply wasn't managing his own weight anymore, and the floor was a fact rather than a destination.

"Coulson."

Not loud. Fury didn't do loud when it mattered, he'd told him that once, early, back when Coulson had still been finding out which of the older agent's habits were performance and which were load-bearing. Loud was for hallways. This was something else, low and immediate, the sound of a man moving before he'd finished deciding to.

Fury was down on one knee with two fingers at Coulson's throat before either analyst had gotten out of their chair, faster than a man his rank had any professional obligation to be. He wasn't shouting orders so much as issuing them, flat and fast, medical, now, somebody get me a name and a location, a voice that made rooms rearrange themselves around it without anyone deciding to comply.

It didn't matter. Coulson could have told him that, if he'd had a mouth left to tell it with. Whatever this was, it wasn't going to answer to a pulse check or a directive shouted at a doorway. Fury's hands were doing everything right, and everything right was going to add up to exactly nothing, because the last thing Coulson heard, underneath the sound of a man refusing to be helpless about it, was that his own heartbeat hadn't stopped.

It just wasn't his to listen to anymore.

***

There were no grappling rigs in the room he woke into. No Fury, no Duane Reade, no floor that had any business holding him up after what had just happened to it.

There was, unaccountably, a requisition form. A whole stack of them, in fact, sitting in someone else's hands behind him, being read with the unhurried attention of a man who had never once in his life had to justify a grappling rig to anybody.

There was a chair that looked like every debrief chair he'd ever sat in and wasn't quite any of them, off by degrees he couldn't name, the wood grain running the wrong direction, or the light falling from nowhere he could find a fixture for. A folder sat on the table in front of him, closed, unlabeled. He didn't remember sitting down. He was fairly sure he hadn't.

Coulson did not reach for the folder. He did not touch the door behind him, though he clocked its location, its hinge side, the fact that it had no visible handle from this angle. He inventoried exits before he inventoried feelings, always had, and the fact that his hands weren't shaking told him less about his own composure than it told him this situation hadn't earned a reaction yet.

It felt wrong the way a forged signature felt wrong, not obviously, not at first glance. Everything present and accounted for and somehow still off, a room built by someone who had only ever heard SHIELD facilities described secondhand and gotten every measurement half an inch out of true.

"You're doing the thing," Fury said, from behind him. "Cataloging."

Coulson didn't startle. He'd trained that reflex out of himself a decade ago, in rooms considerably less strange than this one. He turned, unhurried, and found Fury seated in a second chair that he was almost certain had not been there when he inventoried the room the first time, a requisition form open across one knee like the most natural reading material in the world.

"Sir."

"Don't sir me. You're not sure I've earned it yet." Fury said it without heat, almost pleased, a man watching someone else solve a problem he already knew the shape of. "Go on. Ask."

"How long have you been sitting there."

"Wrong question. Try again." No mark went onto the form for that one. Coulson noted the absence with the same part of his brain that had, moments ago, been arguing about grappling rigs, and found it more unsettling than any answer would have been.

Coulson considered him. The uniform was right. The voice was right, that particular flatness that could turn a joke and a threat into the exact same sentence. But there was a stillness to him that didn't track, an ease that Nick Fury, in Coulson's considerable professional experience of the man, did not extend to rooms he hadn't already secured.

"You're not him."

"There's the form I was expecting from you." Fury, or the thing wearing him, allowed something almost like approval to cross his face, and made a small, unhurried mark on the requisition without looking down to find the box. Coulson caught it anyway, upside down and briefly, before the page turned: SITUATIONAL AWARENESS, checked off like a delivery inspector confirming the crate hadn't shattered in transit. "Not scared. Suspicious. Good. Scared I could work with. Suspicious means you're still doing your job."

"What happened to me."

"You dropped. Mid-sentence, I understand, something about a helicopter. Real Fury is on the floor right now doing everything right and getting nowhere, which I imagine is eating at him more than losing you would, if you want the honest read."

Something in Coulson's chest did a slow, cold turn that had nothing to do with tactics. "Is he."

"He's fine. He'll be furious, and he'll be thorough, and in about eleven minutes he's going to run out of things to be thorough about, and that's going to be worse for him than anything I could tell you to pass along. I'm not going to let you pass anything along. That's not a cruelty. It's just not how this works."

"How what works."

"That," said not-Fury, "is the actual question. Progress." Another mark went down, unhurried, and this time Coulson was close enough to read it before the page turned: CORRECT LINE OF INQUIRY, ticked off with the same satisfied economy of motion a man might use signing off on a shipment that had, against expectation, arrived intact.

Coulson didn't sit. He'd noted the second chair, the one that had appeared for his host, and made a private decision not to match the posture of a thing he hadn't identified yet. His eyes dropped, briefly, to the form still open on the stranger's knee. Wrong letterhead, no seal he recognized, no agency he could place, but the format was unmistakable, boxes, line items, a signature block waiting to be filled, in principle.

REQUISITION: ONE (1), PHIL J. COULSON. QUANTITY: ONE. JUSTIFICATION: PENDING. DESTINATION: PASSENGER SEAT, SEE ATTACHED. PRIORITY: URGENT, RETROACTIVELY. RETURN POLICY: NON-REFUNDABLE, SEE PASSENGER-SEAT CLAUSE. APPROVED BY:

The line wasn't blank. A signature sat in the approval box, small and precise, the kind of tight economical hand that came from twenty years of countersigning other people's paperwork instead of writing anything of his own. He knew it the way he knew his own face in a mirror, immediately and without having to check.

It was his.

He had never seen this form before in his life.

"You have a name?" Coulson asked, and was quietly pleased to hear that his voice hadn't changed at all. "Or should I address the paperwork instead? Since apparently I already did."

"You did. Eventually. Paperwork's funny that way, in my line of work." He tapped the signature line without looking down at it, entirely unbothered by the question underneath the question. "Keeps me honest, having you sign off on yourself. Several names, if you're asking properly. Rob O'Make O'Malley, if you want the one that's actually mine. Facilitator. Importer. On my better days, a corrector of tragically understaffed narratives." He said it like a line he enjoyed enough to keep using, unbothered by how little of it would mean anything to the man across from him. "You can call me whatever's comfortable. I answer to most things when the face fits."

"The face doesn't fit. That's the part I can't source." Coulson kept his voice level, cataloging again, not the room this time but the man, the half-second delays, the references that arrived a beat early or late, the sense of a photograph developed from a memory rather than a life. "You feel familiar. You're not."

"No," O'Malley agreed, and for just a moment the amusement thinned enough to show something underneath it that wasn't unkind, exactly, but wasn't comfortable either. "I'm not."

"What do you want."

"Wrong question again, but I'll forgive it, you're new at this." O'Malley leaned forward, elbows on knees, and the borrowed face went, for the first time since Coulson had turned around, entirely serious. "There's a boy. Not a soldier, not an asset, not anyone your organization has ever had a file on or ever will. He's about to lose something he's never once been told he was allowed to need, and in about an hour, give or take, he's going to need someone in his corner who already knows what it costs to matter without anyone noticing you're doing it."

Coulson said nothing. It was, he would think later, the first professional silence of an entirely new career, though he had no way of knowing that yet.

"You've spent your whole life being underestimated on purpose," O'Malley said. "Good suit, good manners, everyone in the room deciding you're the furniture right up until the moment it costs them. I've called you my Eye more than once, back when I was wearing a face that had the standing to say it." A beat, and something almost gentle crept into the words despite everything dry about the delivery. "But there's someone else who's going to need that Eye more than he knows."

He closed the folder on that line, and stamped something across the front in the same unhurried motion, a single word Coulson caught only as a blur of red ink upside down: APPROVED.

The sentence wasn't finished landing before the room was gone.

Chapter 2: The Hour Before



*In our last episode:* a mid-level SHIELD paper-pusher gets recruited into a fantasy crossover by dropping dead in the middle of a very important discussion about grappling rigs, because nothing says "chosen one" like a man whose last conscious act on his own Earth was arguing about equipment allowances.

Somewhere behind a door with entirely too many other doors, a folder gets stamped, a joke about eyebrows does not happen yet, and our protagonist gets handed his entire new personality in one line: *"I've called you my Eye more than once."* Riveting stuff. Ten out of ten, no notes, except several notes, several of which are about the paperwork.

*"He requisitioned himself,"* said one voice from the gallery, delighted, plainly a man who'd filled out entirely too many procurement forms in a past professional life. *"I've read a great many recruitment files. That's the only one I've ever seen with its own chain of custody."*

A ripple of agreement went through the anonymous watchers crowded into the seats behind him, a low, appreciative murmur, the sound a crowd made when a joke landed exactly as intended. Somebody, unseen, asked whether the beverage machine took requests. Nobody answered. Nobody ever did.

*Now:* cut to Sunnydale, California, a town whose primary industry, as far as this narrator can determine, is dying, and our hero rides shotgun toward the vineyard where he is, this movie assures us, about to have a genuinely terrible day.

The drive out to the vineyard took twenty minutes nobody talked much through, six of them crammed into Giles's ancient sedan with weapons wrapped in beach towels on their laps like the world's least convincing family road trip, and Xander spent most of it with his forehead against the window, watching Sunnydale's tired little houses give way to hills that hadn't seen rain in weeks.

Buffy drove. Faith rode shotgun, boots up on the dash in a way that would have gotten anyone else yelled at, and in the back, wedged between Xander and a girl named Chloe who hadn't said six words since she'd been Called three days ago, Molly sat with both hands wrapped around a stake like she was afraid it might try to leave without her. Across from them, a sharp-eyed girl named Caridad watched the whole car with the quiet, assessing calm of someone who'd already decided panicking wasn't going to be her particular contribution to the day.

"You built a model," Faith said, twisting around from the front seat, half amused. "Like an actual model. With popsicle sticks."

*"A model,"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, delighted. *"Popsicle sticks. This production had a craft budget and spent it on a teenager's diorama."*

*"Cheaper than CGI,"* another voice pointed out.

*"Everything's cheaper than CGI."*

"I like to be thorough." Xander didn't look away from the window. "Some of us don't get super strength as a backup plan when thorough doesn't work out."

*"There it is. That's the whole show in one sentence. Somebody frame that."*

"Didn't say it was a bad idea." Faith's grin flashed, gone as fast as it came. "Just didn't expect Watcher's Council levels of arts and crafts out of you, is all."

"Giles was thrilled. You should've seen his face."

"I did see his face. Looked like Christmas came early and brought glue."

Buffy caught Xander's eye in the rearview mirror, something steady and grateful in it that didn't need words, and he held it a beat before looking away, because if he thought too hard about the fact that people were trusting a plan he'd built with a shoebox lid and four hundred imagined versions of today, he was going to start doubting the whole thing right there in the back seat, and there wasn't room in this car for that particular flavor of panic.

The car parked half a mile out, in the dead lot of an abandoned feed store, the kind of place that had gone under sometime around when Sunnydale's whole economy had started quietly organizing itself around vampire attacks and unexplained disappearances. From there it was on foot, single file along a dry irrigation ditch, the vineyard's rows rising up ahead of them in the gray early light like something out of a war movie nobody had asked to star in.

Buffy split the group at the tree line. Herself and Faith with the main strike team, circling wide toward what scouting had suggested was Caleb's actual base of operations, the old winery building itself. A second, smaller group toward the east rows, feeling out the perimeter. And Xander, at his own insistence, staying back with the four Potentials too green or too recently arrived to be trusted anywhere near the real fighting yet, running last-minute logistics, making sure everyone actually understood the plan instead of just nodding along because nodding was easier than admitting you were scared.

"You've got maybe an hour before we need eyes forward," Buffy told him, low, just between them, her hand briefly on his shoulder. "Get them settled. Get yourself settled too, okay? You've been weird since yesterday."

"I'm always weird. It's kind of my whole thing."

"Weirder than usual." She studied him a second longer, something searching in it he didn't have an answer for yet, and then Faith was calling her name from up the ridge and the moment closed over, gone, filed away for whenever there was time to come back to it.

There wasn't going to be time. Xander understood that already, some cold, certain part of him that had learned, over the years, to recognize the shape of a day that wasn't going to give anybody the luxury of finishing their sentences.

He found the third row exactly where his model said it would be, the light already going gray and strange under the vine canopy, and unfolded the hand-drawn map against a fence post while Molly and the others gathered around, quiet, waiting.

"Okay," he said, mostly to himself, and then, because Molly was watching him with the desperate attention of someone who needed him to have an answer, "okay, so, here's the thing about grapes."

*"'Here's the thing about grapes,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, savoring it. *"I've sat through action-movie briefings with worse opening lines than that. This kid should be teaching a seminar."*

*"He is, technically. Look at the audience."*

Molly didn't laugh. She was maybe sixteen, hollow-eyed in the way all of them were now, three weeks off a plane from somewhere with a name Xander kept meaning to look up and never did, and behind her the other three Potentials had arranged themselves in the loose, unconscious half-circle people made around whoever seemed like they might actually know something.

"The thing about grapes," Xander said again, "is nobody plants them in a straight line because it looks nice. They're planted so a truck can get down the row without murdering half the crop. Which means every row you're looking at is basically a hallway. Caleb's people can only come at us single file, or close to it, once we're between the vines. That's not nothing."

"You built a model," Molly said. Not quite a question.

"I built a model. Popsicle sticks and a shoebox lid, very high-tech, very Watcher's Council, I'm sure Giles is thrilled." He tapped the map. "Point is, I've walked this ground in my head about four hundred times since Tuesday. I know where the light's bad. I know where the ground's soft enough to trip on. None of that makes today safe. It just means when it stops being safe, you'll already know which way you were planning to run."

"That's not the same as safe."

"No," Xander agreed. "It's really not. I'm not going to stand here and tell you it is, because you'd know I was lying, and then you'd stop trusting anything else I said, and I need you to trust the next part more than the first part." He crouched down to her eye level, the way you did with kids, except she wasn't a kid, not really, not anymore, none of them were once the scythe or whatever passed for one found them. "The next part is: if it goes bad, you don't look for me. You don't look for Buffy. You look for the nearest gap in the vines and you go through it low and fast and you do not stop to see if anyone's behind you. Guilt is a luxury item. You can afford it later."

Molly's mouth did something that wasn't quite a smile. "You're weird for a guy with no powers."

"I prefer 'unconventionally credentialed.'"

That got the almost-smile a little further along, and behind her, one of the other Potentials, a quiet girl named Deb who'd barely spoken since arriving, actually laughed, short and surprised, like she hadn't expected to have one left in her this morning. Caridad, beside her, didn't laugh, but something in her sharp, watchful expression eased half a degree, as if she were quietly filing away that the funny one might actually know what he was doing. It wasn't much. It felt, right then, like the only useful thing he'd built all week, more useful than the shoebox, more useful than the four hundred imaginary walks down four hundred imaginary rows.

He sent the other three off to double-check the gap points he'd marked, an actual task, something with edges to it, small enough to give scared hands somewhere to put themselves, and kept Molly back a moment longer, because something in her had been holding on a little too tight since the car.

That was when the ground tilted.

Not literally. Nothing under his feet actually moved. But something in the architecture of his own skull shifted half an inch to the left and then, just as fast, corrected itself, that odd disorientation of hearing a word you were sure you'd only thought, echo back at you from somewhere that wasn't your own voice at all.

*Where am I.*

*"Oh, HERE we go,"* the gallery's voice said, sitting up so fast this narrator could hear it happen. *"New arrival. Everybody quiet, this is the good part."*

Not his own thought. He knew his own thoughts, had spent a lifetime getting intimately, uncomfortably familiar with every single one of them, and this one arrived shaped wrong, too calm for the volume of wrongness it should have been carrying, a man's voice, unfamiliar, running some kind of quiet triage on a situation he clearly hadn't chosen to be in.

*Okay,* Xander thought back, with the weary competence of a man who had once had a hyena spirit take up temporary residence behind his eyes and was, therefore, considerably less rattled by this flavor of impossible than a reasonable person had any right to be. *New guy. Nice of you to knock.*

*"'Nice of you to knock,'"* someone repeated, wheezing. *"He's not even fazed. Fifteen years old, hyena spirit, apparently a whole BACKLOG of impossible. This kid's résumé is unreal."*

The voice, when it came again, had gone very still in a way that felt less like fear and more like a man doing inventory under fire. *You can hear that.*

*"Yes, obviously, we all can, that's the premise, keep up."*

*Loud and clear. You want to tell me who you are, or should I just start narrating the vineyard tour and see if you flinch at anything?*

*"HA. There it is. First joke under pressure. Somebody start a tally."*

*Phillip Coulson.* A beat, the mental equivalent of someone squaring their shoulders. *I don't have a framework for this. I'm going to need you to be patient with me for approximately as long as it takes me to build one, which, going on past performance, should be under a minute.*

*"'Going on past performance,'"* another voice echoed, delighted. *"The man's been conscious in a stranger's skull for ninety seconds and he's already citing his own track record. That's a professional."*

*Buddy, I don't have a framework for anything that's happened to me since I was fifteen.* Xander kept his face doing the things a face was supposed to do while he was mid-conversation with a Potential, which took more effort than he would have guessed, given how many other things his brain was apparently equipped to run in parallel these days. *Welcome to the crowded house. There's a spirit hyena joke I could make here but I don't actually know you well enough yet to know if you'd get it.*

*Try me.*

*"Oh, he's GAME. Look at that. Thirty seconds in and he's already leaning into the bit."*

*Later. Kind of in the middle of something.*

*"'Kind of.' Note it."*

*"We're noting everything tonight, aren't we."*

He glanced back at Molly, who was watching him with the alarm of someone who had just watched a grown man's eyes go somewhere else entirely for three full seconds. "Sorry. Where was I."

"You went somewhere," Molly said carefully. "Somewhere that wasn't here."

"Yeah." Xander rubbed the back of his neck. There wasn't a version of the truth here that would help her more than it would scare her, not with less than an hour left before people started arriving with knives, so he reached for the smallest true thing available instead. "Turns out I've got company today. Long story, not the important one. The important one's still the vines and the running and the not looking back for me."

Molly considered this with the flat, fast-adjusting calm of someone who had already recalibrated for stranger things this month than her mentor briefly going somewhere else mid-sentence. "Is the company going to help?"

"Working on finding out," Xander said, which was, as far as he could tell, entirely true.

*She trusts you,* Coulson said, and there was something in the observation that wasn't quite surprise, more the sound of a hypothesis holding up under early testing. *Actually trusts you. Not the performance of it. I've spent a career reading the difference and that wasn't performance.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander watched Molly go back to turning the stake over in her hands, methodical, a fidget that was really just a way of keeping her hands from finding something worse to do. *Not a lot of competition for the job around here. Buffy's got the whole army to run. Giles has the whole history of everything to keep straight. Willow's basically a walking apocalypse-prevention system these days. Somebody's gotta be the guy who remembers which sixteen-year-old needs a pep talk before the knives show up, and it's not exactly a role people are lining up for.*

*That's not a complaint. I want to be clear I heard that as information, not a complaint.*

*It wasn't a complaint.* And it mostly wasn't, which was, if Xander was being honest with the part of his head that apparently now had an audience, more interesting than the fact that a stranger had said it back to him accurately. *It's just the job. Somebody's gotta watch the people nobody else has time to watch. Turns out I'm decent at it. Turns out decent at it doesn't come with a title.*

*I know exactly what that job looks like from the inside,* Coulson said, and there was something dry underneath it, self-aware in a way that read like a man who'd made peace with an old joke a long time ago. *I've spent the better part of two decades being extremely good at a job most of the building assumes is filing.*

*"'Most of the building assumes is filing,'"* someone repeated, delighted. *"This narrator has met that guy. Every organization has that guy. Never trust the quiet one with the binder."*

*Huh.* Xander turned that over. *You're a filing guy who got teleported into a Buffy fic. This day keeps getting weirder.*

*"'A Buffy fic,'"* several voices said at once, and then, overlapping, a ragged little chorus of *"he knows,"* *"does he know,"* *"how does he know,"* before someone finally shouted them all down.

*I'm not going to ask what that means.*

*"Smart man. Ignorance is the only thing keeping him functional right now."*

*Smart. Save it for later, we've got, what, forty minutes?*

*Thirty-eight.*

*You're timing it?*

*"Of course he's timing it. Look at him. That's a man who has never once in his life not known exactly how much time is left."*

*Occupational habit.* A pause, and something in the quality of the silence shifted, careful in a new direction. *Can I ask you something. About her.* He meant Molly, Xander understood immediately, without needing the clarification. *How long have you known her.*

*Three weeks. Since she landed.* Xander watched her check her stake grip for the fifth time in as many minutes, the small compulsive ritual of someone keeping her hands busy so her head didn't have room to go somewhere worse. *You don't need years for this part. You just need to actually be there for the three weeks you get.*

*That's a better answer than most people twice your age would give me.*

*I get that a lot. The 'better answer than expected' thing. Not usually meant as a compliment.*

*I meant it as one.*

Xander didn't have a quick joke ready for that, which was, itself, unusual enough to notice.

He crouched back down to Molly's level instead, tapped the map one more time, walked her through the third row and the gap by the broken irrigation post and the exact spot where the light went bad enough that anyone chasing her would lose a full second finding their footing, a second that might, with reasonable luck, be the whole difference. She listened the way she'd been listening the whole conversation, hungry for it in a way that had nothing to do with tactics and everything to do with somebody bothering to explain instead of just ordering. When the others drifted back from checking the gap points, he ran through it again for all four of them together, patient, unhurried, until even Chloe, who still hadn't said much of anything, managed a small, determined nod.

Thirty minutes became twenty. Twenty became twelve, time that didn't announce itself shrinking so much as simply arrived at almost nothing, all at once, the way water finally boils after appearing to sit still for ages.

*You're good at this,* Coulson said, quiet, somewhere around the eight-minute mark, and it didn't feel like flattery so much as a man updating a file in real time. *I want you to know I'm not saying that to manage you. I don't have a reason to manage you yet. I'm saying it because it's true and because, from where I'm sitting, I don't think anyone's told you.*

*Careful,* Xander said, and meant it mostly as a joke, mostly. *Keep talking like that and I might start liking you.*

*Would that be so terrible.*

*Ask me again in an hour.*

*"What? What'd he say?"*

*"Nothing. Never mind. Watch the row."*

He didn't know, saying it, how literally the hour would come to matter, or that the last easy thing he'd think for a long while was going to be that exact sentence, tossed off light, half a joke, an offhand line that filled a silence he didn't yet know was about to end.

Molly's head came up first. She had good ears, better than his, better than most of the Potentials, one of the few genuine advantages the Calling seemed to have handed her early. "Someone's coming," she said, low, and the fear in it was different from the fear she'd been carrying for the last twenty minutes, sharper, more immediate, the difference between being afraid of a thing and being afraid of this thing, right now, close.

Xander stood. Down the third row, the one with the good sightline he'd built the whole plan around, a shape was walking toward them, unhurried, taking his time about it in a way that had nothing to do with confidence and everything to do with certainty.

*Xander,* Coulson said, and the dryness had gone out of him entirely, replaced by something flat and focused, a professional's attention locking onto a threat, a register Xander would only much later learn to recognize on sight, *that's him, isn't it.*

*Yeah,* Xander said, and felt Molly's hand find his sleeve, gripping hard enough to hurt. *That's him.*

*"ffffuuuuuuuu..."* someone in the anonymous rows managed, before the sound cut off entirely, along with everything else, the whole gallery, this narrator included, holding its breath for whatever came next.

Chapter 3: The Vineyard



He didn't look like much, was the thing. Xander had built four hundred imaginary versions of this moment over the past week and every single one of them had gotten the shape of Caleb wrong, had made him bigger, more obviously monstrous, something the eye could file away as *dangerous* on sight. The real thing walking down the third row was just a man in a black shirt with his sleeves rolled up, moving like he had nowhere else he needed to be today, which was somehow so much worse.

"Everybody, positions," Xander said, low, and the other three Potentials scattered without needing to be told twice, Chloe and Deb toward the gap points he'd drilled into them, Caridad moving low and fast between the vines with a quiet economy that made him think, distantly, that she'd absorbed more of his drilling than she'd ever let on, gone from sight so completely he lost track of her entirely, which, right now, was exactly what he wanted.

Molly didn't move.

"Molly." He kept his voice even, kept his eyes on Caleb, who hadn't hurried a single step since they'd spotted him. "Gap by the irrigation post. Go."

"I'm not leaving you."

"You're leaving me. That was the deal. Go."

She went, and it cost her something to do it, he could feel that much even without a shared skull to confirm it, the sound a person's resolve made tearing when it did the smart thing instead of the loyal thing. Good. That was the whole point of the plan. That was the entire reason he'd spent a week with popsicle sticks and a shoebox lid, so that this exact moment would have a right answer already loaded into her, one she didn't have to invent from scratch while terrified.

"Well now," Caleb said, and his voice carried easy across the whole quiet vineyard, folksy and unbothered, the voice of a man remarking on the weather. "Look what wandered onto my land."

*"Ah,"* said the voice from the gallery, dry, entirely unsurprised. *"The folksy-menace monologue. Every single time. You'd think, across however many of these I've sat through, at least one of them would skip the pleasantries and get straight to the violence. Efficiency, gentlemen. It's not just for filing cabinets."* A pause, considering. *"Full marks for the delivery, though. Genuinely unsettling. I'll allow it."*

Somewhere in the anonymous rows behind him, somebody hissed at the screen the way an audience hissed at a slasher-movie mistake, the universal sound of *don't you dare*, aimed at a scene that was, this narrator regrets to inform you, already well past the point of listening.

*Xander.* Coulson's voice had gone tight, urgent in a register Xander hadn't heard from him yet in their brief acquaintance. *You have a plan for this part specifically, or was the plan entirely about her.*

*The plan was entirely about her,* Xander admitted, backing up half a step as Caleb kept coming, unhurried, closing ground he didn't seem to feel any need to close quickly. *I'm mostly a delay tactic at this stage. Delay tactics don't usually get their own shoebox.*

*"Gallows humor under fire,"* someone in the anonymous rows noted, appreciative despite themselves. *"Give the kid credit, the material's landing even at the worst possible moment."*

"You're the funny one," Caleb said, conversational, like he was placing an order. "Heard about you. Heard you talk a lot and don't much back it up." He stopped, finally, close enough now that Xander could see he wasn't even winded, wasn't even bothering to look like a man who considered this a fight. "Let's see what's actually behind all that talking, hm?"

*Xander, listen to me.* Coulson's voice had dropped into something Xander would later understand was the exact register of a man calculating angles, distances, the small mathematics of violence he'd apparently spent a career fluent in. *If he goes for you, and you can't get clear in time, give me the wheel. I have training you don't. I might be able to buy you the half second you need.*

*Buddy, I appreciate the offer, genuinely, five stars, would recommend,* Xander said, already backing again, already knowing from the set of Caleb's shoulders that backing wasn't going to be enough, *but that's not really how this works. I don't just hand the keys to a guy I met twenty minutes ago.*

*"'Five stars, would recommend,'"* someone repeated, half a laugh, half something more nervous. *"That's the last joke for a while, isn't it. This narrator can feel it."*

*Then don't hand them. Just know I'm here. I'm not going anywhere.*

Xander tried anyway, because trying was the only thing he had, a feint toward the gap between the fourth and fifth rows, a plan already half-formed in his head about drawing Caleb further from the others, buying Molly and the rest another minute of distance.

It bought him nothing.

Caleb moved.

He didn't wind up for it, didn't telegraph, just closed the last six feet between them like the distance had personally offended him, and Xander's whole plan, every four hundred imagined versions of this scene, collapsed into the single, useless realization that he had built an excellent strategy for getting other people clear and no strategy whatsoever for getting himself clear once the man in front of him decided the conversation was over.

A hand closed around his jaw.

*No,* Coulson said, and it wasn't a plea, it was an order, the kind of flat immovable refusal that belonged to a man who had thrown himself between danger and other people more times than he'd ever bothered counting, and something in him reached, hard, instinctive, for a body that had never once in twenty years failed to answer that particular reach.

Nothing happened.

Not nothing like a door being locked. Nothing like the door not existing at all, like reaching for a limb that had never been his to move, no resistance, no wall, just a total absence of anything to push against. Coulson had built his entire career on the certainty that when something needed doing with his hands, his hands would do it, and for the first time in that career, in this life, in whatever this now was, they didn't, because they weren't his, because they had never for one second actually been his, no matter how loudly some old animal part of him insisted otherwise.

Xander's whole body went rigid under a grip that felt, distantly, aware, that this was a monstrous amount of pressure applied by someone who wasn't remotely trying yet.

"First-a all," Caleb said, easy, almost gentle about it, the terrible gentleness of a man who had done this before and found it satisfying, "let's just get a good look at you."

A thumb found the corner of Xander's eye.

*"MotherFUCKER!!!"* somebody in the anonymous rows screamed, no composure left to lose, none of the earlier hedging, none of the critic's remove, just the sound of a person watching something unforgivable happen to someone they'd started, against their better judgment, to care about.

Nobody hushed it this time. Nobody made a joke about volume, or decorum, or keeping it down for the people in the back row. This narrator, for what it's worth, would have screamed the same thing, if this narrator had a mouth to scream it with.

What happened next didn't have a clean shape in Xander's own head afterward, not one word followed by another, just pressure becoming pain becoming something too large for either word to hold, his own scream sounding distant and disconnected from the mouth making it, the world tilting sideways into a red that wasn't a color so much as a total absence of anything else.

And underneath it, riding along inside a body he couldn't move, couldn't protect, couldn't so much as flinch out of the way, Coulson felt every bit of it.

Not an echo. Not information about pain relayed at a comfortable remove, the way a report might describe an injury to someone who hadn't been there. He was there. He was fully, completely there, present in a nervous system screaming at a volume he had never once in a life full of genuine violence experienced from the inside, because every other time pain had found him, he had at least had the small mercy of a body that was unambiguously his to own the damage. This body wasn't his. The pain didn't care. It arrived exactly the same, full intensity, no distance, no mercy, the horror of being fully present for something you had tried, with everything you had, to stop, and failing so completely that your failure didn't even register as a failure to the universe, just a null result, nothing happened, because there had never been anything there to try with in the first place.

*Xander,* Coulson said, or tried to, or thought, the boundary between those had stopped meaning much of anything in that particular second, *Xander, I'm here, I'm not, I can't, I'm...*

He didn't have a way to finish it. There wasn't a way to finish it. He had spent twenty years being the man who found the fix, the angle, the play nobody else had considered, and here, now, inside the one body where all of that expertise weighed exactly nothing, there was no fix, no angle, no play, just a boy's scream and his own useless, screaming want to make it stop, both of them equally incapable of doing one single thing about it.

Two rows over, close enough to freeze at the sound but too far to matter, Molly stopped running.

She turned, one half of one second, long enough to see Xander go down under Caleb's hand, long enough for the sound to reach her and lodge somewhere it was never going to fully come loose from again, long enough for the plan he'd built her, the good plan, the plan built on popsicle sticks and four hundred imagined versions of a much smaller disaster, to become, in that exact half second, the only thing keeping her feet moving instead of turning back.

She didn't turn back.

*"Good girl,"* somebody in the gallery said, quiet, none of the earlier delight left in it, just relief with nowhere else to go.

He'd told her not to. It was, distantly, underneath everything else happening to him, the one thing in the whole vineyard that had gone according to plan.

Caleb released him a moment later, unhurried about that too, stepping back to consider the wreck of him with something that might, on a kinder man's face, have passed for satisfaction. "There now," he said, almost gentle, wiping his thumb clean on his trousers like the whole thing had cost him nothing at all. "That's a start."

He didn't finish it. Something further down the row, shouting, the clash of steel on something that wasn't steel, pulled his attention away, and he went to it without hurrying, without a backward glance at the boy curled in the dirt behind him, already forgotten in favor of whatever came next.

Xander didn't remember much of the next several minutes. Hands finding him, someone's voice repeating his name like a question that needed answering, the whole vineyard tilting and roaring around him in a way that had nothing to do with the fight still going on somewhere past the edge of what he could process. He remembered, dimly, being moved. He remembered a voice, not out loud, staying with him through all of it, not offering anything useful, because there wasn't anything useful left to offer, just staying, present, refusing to go anywhere, the one small mercy either of them had left to give the other.

Chapter 4: Revello Drive



*Previously:* our folksy antagonist made good on every promise his introduction implied, our protagonist lost an eye in the process, and the newly-recruited SHIELD handler learned, in the worst possible classroom, exactly where the limits of his new arrangement actually sat.

Nobody argued. Even the beverage machine, several rows back, seemed to know better than to hum.

He came back to himself properly somewhere in the back of Giles's car, jolting over the same dry ruts they'd driven in on, someone's jacket pressed hard against the side of his face and someone else's hand gripping his so tight it hurt in a small, distant way that barely registered against everything else.

"Stay with us," Buffy kept saying, low, urgent, from somewhere near his head. "Xander. Stay with us."

He wanted to tell her he wasn't going anywhere, that going anywhere wasn't really on the table given how thoroughly the world had narrowed down to a single burning point behind his left eye, but talking turned out to require a coordination he didn't have available yet, so he just held onto her hand and let the car eat up the miles back toward town.

The vineyard had cost more than his eye. He learned that in pieces over the drive, Faith's voice clipped and furious in the front seat relaying a body count that hadn't finished counting yet, two Potentials down and not getting back up, a third torn up bad enough that somebody was already talking about a hospital they couldn't actually risk using. Molly made it. He held onto that fact the way he held onto Buffy's hand, small and solid against everything else trying to pull him under.

The kitchen at Revello Drive smelled like antiseptic and burnt coffee by the time Willow got the last of the gauze taped into place, the house around them full of the low, awful murmur of people counting their own losses in whispers, trying not to let the newest arrivals hear just how bad the night had actually gone.

"So on the bright side," Xander said, to nobody in particular, to Willow's hands still shaking slightly as she smoothed down the final edge of the bandage, "I was never using that eye for anything important. Depth perception's overrated. Who needs to judge distance? Not me. Not anymore. I'm basically a Cyclops now, except with worse superpowers and a much smaller support team."

"Xander." Willow's voice cracked on the second syllable.

"I'm fine. I'm hilarious. Those are basically the same thing right now, it's a package deal."

He wasn't fine. Nobody in the room believed he was fine, least of all the voice that had gone very, very quiet somewhere behind his working eye since the vineyard, present but not talking, standing very still the way someone did when they didn't trust what would come out if they opened their mouth.

Willow finished the tape, pressed her palm briefly to his good cheek, a touch that didn't ask anything of him, and left him alone in the kitchen with a glass of water he wasn't going to drink and the low murmur of the rest of the house rebuilding itself around a version of tonight that had, somehow, other casualties to grieve too, other things that had gone wrong at other parts of the vineyard that he hadn't seen and wasn't ready yet to ask about.

The quiet stretched.

*You still in there?* Xander thought, eventually, when the silence had gone on long enough to start feeling less like respect and more like something worse. *Or did you check out somewhere back at the third row?*

Nothing, for a second. Then, low, unsteady in a way that didn't match anything Xander had heard from him yet: *I'm here.*

*You don't sound here.*

*I know.*

Another silence. Xander waited it out, because there wasn't much else to do with one working eye and a kitchen full of people carefully not looking at him too long, and eventually the voice started again, and it didn't sound like it was talking to him at all.

*There was a room,* Coulson said, or thought, the distinction had stopped mattering somewhere around the vineyard, *early on. First real op I ran point on, low-level, nothing that should have mattered to anyone above me. I was twenty-six. There were four other agents in the debrief, all of them bigger than me, all of them with better files, the kind of men a room naturally arranges itself around without anyone deciding to. I didn't say much. I never said much in those rooms. Learned early that saying less and noticing more got you further than the other way around, though I don't think I understood yet why that was true, just that it worked.*

Xander didn't say anything. Something in the rhythm of it told him not to, the way you didn't interrupt a man walking across a narrow ledge.

*There was a discrepancy in the intel,* Coulson went on. *Small. A timestamp that didn't match a radio log by about ninety seconds, nothing anyone in that room had a reason to look for, because ninety seconds isn't the kind of thing four confident men go looking for when the bigger picture already looks solved. I looked for it. Not because I was smarter than anyone in that room. Because looking at small things nobody else was watching was the only tool I had, so I used it constantly, out of necessity more than talent, and eventually it stopped being a compensating habit and just became the thing I was actually good at.*

*What was the ninety seconds,* Xander asked, quiet, mostly to keep the thread going, mostly because some part of him needed the story to keep moving more than he needed to understand exactly why.

*A mole,* Coulson said. *Feeding a timestamp that was almost right. Almost is the whole game, in that line of work. Almost gets people killed just as dead as an obvious lie does, it just takes longer and costs more when it finally lands. I flagged it. Turned out to matter. Turned out to matter a great deal, actually, more than a twenty-six-year-old with no real file to speak of had any business being responsible for.*

*And Fury started calling you his Eye.*

*"Of course I did!"* the voice from the gallery broke in, sudden and delighted, all the earlier quiet gone out of it at once, a man recognizing his own line coming back at him secondhand. *"Best nickname I ever gave anybody. Still stand by it."*

*Half a joke, at first.* Something in the telling shifted, warmer and more exposed at once, a man handing over something he hadn't planned on handing over. *He has a habit of it, nicknames, most of them barbed enough to leave a mark. This one never quite turned into a title. Just something he said, now and then, when it was the two of us and nobody else needed to hear it. I never asked him to stop. I don't think I ever told anyone what it meant to me that he kept using it.*

*You're telling me now.*

A pause, longer this time, and Xander got the distinct sense, even without a body to read, of a man realizing exactly what he'd just done.

*I wasn't telling you anything,* Coulson said, and there was something almost startled in it, the sound of a composed man catching himself mid-fall. *I was talking. There's a difference. I don't usually do the second one out loud, or whatever the equivalent of out loud is, currently, for either of us.*

*Yeah,* Xander said. *I noticed.*

He had. That was the thing nobody in the kitchen would have guessed, looking at him, one eye gone, half his face wrapped in gauze, cracking jokes that weren't landing because nobody had the heart to laugh at them tonight. He'd noticed exactly what had just happened, the crack in something that had, up until ninety seconds ago, presented itself as entirely, professionally composed. A man who talked for a living, who'd probably talked his way out of a hundred rooms that should have gone worse, had just, without meaning to, without deciding to, let something slip that he'd apparently been carrying quietly, unspoken, for the better part of two decades.

Losing the eye hadn't touched that part of him. If anything, it had sharpened it, the way exhaustion sometimes stripped everything down to the parts that were actually load-bearing. He'd have missed it a week ago, probably, too busy performing okay to actually watch for the thing underneath somebody else's performance of okay.

*You didn't have to tell me that,* Xander said, eventually, quiet, careful with it the way you were careful with something you'd caught somebody dropping by accident.

*No,* Coulson agreed. *I didn't.*

*Why'd you do it, then.*

There was a long pause before the answer came, long enough that Xander thought maybe there wasn't going to be one.

*Because you're sitting in that chair with half your face gone and everyone in this house too scared to actually look at you, and I didn't have hands to hold, or a shoulder to offer, or one single useful thing I could physically do for you,* Coulson said, finally, quiet, none of the earlier dryness left in it at all. *This was the only thing I had. So I gave it to you. I'm not entirely sure I meant to. I'm not going to pretend that makes it less true.*

*"You know,"* the voice from the gallery said, quiet now too, none of the earlier delight left in it, *"this is the reason I gave Coulson this job."*

Down the hall, someone was crying, quiet and worn out, one of the newer Potentials being walked upstairs by a girl who'd survived the night herself and had somehow found the reserves left to comfort someone else. Xander listened to it fade toward the second floor, turning over what Coulson had just given him, the water glass sweating a ring onto the table, one working eye fixed on nothing in particular.

"Okay," he said, out loud, quiet enough that nobody else in the kitchen turned to look. "Okay. Noted."

It wasn't much of a response. It was, for that particular night, all either of them had left to give.

Chapter 5: Explain That



The house went quiet somewhere around two in the morning, the way a place got quiet when too many people crammed into too few rooms finally ran out of adrenaline all at once. Sleeping bags covered most of the living room floor, a tangle of exhausted, grieving girls who'd fought a war today and would be expected to get up and maybe fight another piece of it tomorrow, and somewhere above all of it, in the small room he still technically shared with nobody because nobody had wanted to make him share it tonight, Xander lay on his back in the dark.

The ceiling swam slightly whenever he turned his head too fast. He was still relearning, minute by minute, how far away the corners of a room actually were, some old, confident map of space folding itself down to half its usual reliability and refusing, so far, to redraw itself into anything he trusted.

He wasn't tired. He was fairly sure he wasn't going to be tired again for a long time, in the way that mattered, the kind of tired sleep actually fixed.

*"There it is again,"* said the voice from the gallery, mild, unhurried, the tone of a man who had, in fact, been keeping count. *"'The kind of.' Third one tonight. I'd almost think somebody back home has a favorite scaffold."*

A pause, considering, and then, dryly: *"Someone fetch the beverage. This one's earned."*

Somewhere behind him, an anonymous watcher made a small, satisfied noise, a running joke finally getting its due, and nothing further was said about it, though this narrator notes, for the record, that nothing further being said about it did not mean nothing further was going to happen about it.

*You awake,* he thought, into the quiet, not really expecting silence to be the answer.

*I don't sleep the way you do. I'm not sure I sleep at all, currently.* A pause. *So, yes.*

*"'I'm not sure I sleep at all,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, delighted. *"Buy this man a nightlight."*

*Good. So, you gonna explain that?*

*Explain what.*

*The kitchen. The whole "twenty-six years old, ninety seconds, my Eye" thing.* Xander laced his hands behind his head, stared at the ceiling that kept insisting on being further away than it was. *You don't strike me as a guy who just hands that kind of thing out. And I don't think you meant to hand it to me either. So. Explain it.*

*"There it is,"* someone murmured. *"Kid's better at this than the professional. Write that down."*

There was a real pause this time, long enough that Xander started to wonder if the answer was just going to be silence again, the professional retreat he'd half expected the first time and hadn't gotten.

*I don't have a clean answer,* Coulson said, finally, and it sounded like the truth, unpolished in a way his earlier telling hadn't quite been. *I told you because you were sitting there with half your face gone and I had nothing else to give you, and somewhere in the middle of not having anything else to give, that came out instead. I've turned it over a dozen times in the last few hours trying to find the version where I meant to do that deliberately, and I can't. It just happened.*

*You've had feelings leak out of you before. Rooms of professional liars. I bet you're better at holding the line than that.*

*I am,* Coulson agreed. *Considerably better, as a rule.*

*"Modest,"* someone snorted.

*"He's not wrong, though,"* another voice put in. *"Twenty years. Give the man his due."*

*"I'm just saying, 'considerably better, as a rule' is a very funny thing to say two seconds after admitting you cried in a kitchen."*

*"He didn't cry."*

*"He might as well have."*

*So why not this time.*

Another pause, shorter. *I don't know yet. I'd like to, for what it's worth. I don't enjoy not knowing my own reasons.*

Xander turned that over, watching the ceiling do its slow, dishonest drift. It would have been easier, somehow, if the answer had come out smooth, some line about strategy or bonding or whatever a trained professional was supposed to say to a scared kid with a bandage over half his face. The not-knowing was harder to argue with. He didn't have a joke built for not-knowing.

*Here's the thing,* Xander said, eventually, and felt the shape of the real thing arriving before he'd fully decided to say it. *You told me that story like it was supposed to mean something to me. Like, hey, look, we're the same, huh? Guy in the room nobody's watching, guy who notices the ninety seconds nobody else looks for.* His jaw tightened, and it wasn't the bandage causing it. *I'm not a spy. I'm the guy who carries bags. There's a difference.*

*"Ohh, he's mad,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, with real relish. *"I love it when he's mad."*

*Is there.*

*"Two words. Devastating. This narrator's seen entire arguments lose to a well-placed 'is there.'"*

*Yeah, there's a difference.* He heard his own voice come out sharper than he meant it, didn't soften it. *You've got training. An organization. Twenty years of somebody paying you to get good at noticing things, backing you up when you're right, catching you when you're wrong. I've got nothing behind me. No file. No handler. No org chart. Just, whatever I managed to figure out on my own, mostly by almost dying a bunch of times and paying really close attention to what almost killed me. That's not the same skill wearing a different uniform. That's a completely different thing that happens to look similar from a distance.*

*"Somebody give the boy a monologue trophy."*

*"Shh, he's not done being wrong yet."*

*I don't think it's as different as you want it to be.*

*You don't get a vote on that. You've known me for, what, twelve hours?*

*Fair,* Coulson said, and didn't push further, didn't argue, didn't do the thing Xander had half-braced for, the smooth professional reframe that would make the objection sound smaller than it was. *I'll grant you the twelve hours. I'm not going to pretend I've earned the right to tell you what your own life is made of.*

*"He folded,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, almost offended on Coulson's behalf. *"Twenty years of training and the man just folded."*

*"That's not folding. That's strategy. Watch."*

*But you don't actually agree with me.*

*"Called it,"* said the same voice that had called the strategy a beat earlier, smug about it.

*No,* Coulson said, plainly. *I don't. I think you built something real out of nothing, which is harder than what I did, not easier, and I think some part of you needs it to be a lesser thing so you don't have to carry what it would mean if it wasn't. I'm not going to argue you out of that tonight. I don't think arguing would work, and I don't think tonight's the night to try.*

*"Oof,"* several voices said, in ragged, overlapping unison.

*"That's not a comeback. That's a diagnosis."*

*"Somebody check on the kid."*

Xander didn't have a comeback for that either. He lay there a while longer, letting the ceiling keep its slow, unconvincing drift, letting the silence sit instead of filling it with something clever, and found, somewhere under the resentment, that the not-arguing bothered him more than an actual argument would have.

*You've done this before,* Xander said, eventually, turning it a different direction. *Talked somebody down off a ledge they didn't know they were standing on. That's a whole skill set, isn't it. Reading somebody, knowing exactly how hard to push and when to back off.*

*It is.*

*So which is this. You reading me, or you actually meaning it.*

*Both, I think,* Coulson said, unhurried. *I don't consider those mutually exclusive. I read people for a living because it matters that I get them right, not because getting them right lets me manage them into whatever I've already decided they should think. If I only meant the reading and not the substance underneath it, I don't think it would have cost me anything to say it in the kitchen. It did cost me something. That's usually how I tell the difference myself.*

Xander let that sit, unwilling to grant it outright, unwilling, too, to argue it away.

*You're not gonna let it go, though,* Xander said, eventually. Not quite a question.

*No,* Coulson agreed. *I'm not.*

*Great.* Xander closed his working eye, felt the exhaustion finally start pulling at the edges of him, the real kind, the kind that might actually work tonight. *Can't wait for round two.*

*Get some sleep, Xander.*

*Bossy for a guy who's been in my head half a day.*

*I contain multitudes.*

*"Hey!"* someone in the anonymous rows said, sitting bolt upright. *"That's Xander's line!"*

*"It's Whitman's line, technically,"* another voice corrected, prim about it.

*"It's whoever says it best's line, and right now that's not the guy who's supposed to be quoting poetry at two in the morning."*

*"...He's picking it up already, isn't he,"* the gallery's voice said, slow, something almost fond creeping into the observation. *"That's not a bit. That's actual bleed. Somebody make a note."*

Nobody made a note, out loud, but this narrator did.

Xander almost laughed at that, the first almost-laugh since the vineyard, small and involuntary and gone as fast as it came, and somewhere behind his ribs, in the hollow place where a fight he hadn't fully finished having was still quietly refusing to resolve itself, he understood without being able to say why that this argument, unlike most of the arguments in his life, wasn't going to end with either of them winning.

He fell asleep somewhere in the middle of trying to figure out whether that thought was supposed to be comforting.

Chapter 6: Practice



The days right after the vineyard settled into a strange, uneven rhythm, grief and recovery and the ordinary business of a crowded house all tangled together, and somewhere in the middle of it, Xander and Coulson started, without either of them quite deciding to, actually practicing.

It started with small things. Coulson, watching Xander struggle to judge the distance to a doorframe for the third time in an hour, started calling out corrections, quiet, patient, a running commentary that slowly, gradually, retrained the reflexes Xander's remaining eye hadn't caught up to yet.

*"Ah,"* said the voice from the gallery, settling in with the satisfaction of a man who recognized a genre convention when he saw one. *"The training montage. No soundtrack cue, sadly, budget's what it is, but I appreciate the effort regardless."*

*"Where's the part where he does one-armed push-ups on a mountain,"* asked someone from the anonymous rows, not entirely joking.

"Two feet left of where you think it is," Coulson said, one afternoon, watching Xander reach for a glass on the kitchen counter and miss it by an inch.

*"'Two feet left,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated. *"The man narrates depth perception corrections like he's calling in an airstrike."*

"I know that now. After the fact. Very helpful."

*"HA."*

"You'll know it beforehand eventually. That's how retraining works. It's tedious before it's automatic."

"You sound like you've done this before. The retraining-somebody's-reflexes thing."

"I've overseen physical therapy for injured field agents more times than I'd like to count," Coulson said. "It's not glamorous work. It's also some of the most important work in the whole recovery process, and most people rush through it because it's boring, which just means it takes longer in the end."

"Great. So I'm your project now."

*"Oh, here we go..."*

"You're not a project. You're someone I have a vested interest in not walking into any more doorframes."

*"'A vested interest.' In doorframes. This is either the most romantic thing I've heard all week or the least, and I genuinely cannot decide which."*

*"Why not both."*

Xander laughed at that, actually laughed, the first real one since the vineyard that didn't feel forced, and reached for the glass again, slower this time, more deliberate, and got it on the first try.

*"There it is,"* someone said, quieter, more pleased than the joke really warranted. *"First real laugh. Somebody mark the date."*

"See," Coulson said, something almost pleased in it. "Tedious. Then automatic."

It became a kind of game between them, over those first quiet days, Coulson calling out small challenges, Xander working through them with the same stubborn persistence he brought to everything once he'd actually decided to commit to it. Depth perception drills disguised as chores. Reaction-time exercises disguised as helping stack sandbags nobody needed stacked yet. A whole private curriculum neither of them ever called by that name, built entirely out of the ordinary business of a house getting through an ordinary week.

*You're better at this than I expected,* Coulson observed, one evening, watching Xander catch a tossed apple out of the air on the first try, no hesitation, no missed judgment of distance at all.

*Yeah, well. Turns out I've got a pretty good teacher. Even if he's kind of a jerk about corrections.*

*I'm precise. There's a difference.*

*Sure there is.*

They fell into an easy back-and-forth over those days that had nothing to do with the bigger argument still sitting unresolved between them, the whose-seeing-counts-more fight neither of them had actually finished having. This was smaller, simpler, two people building something together one small correction at a time, and Xander found, somewhere in the middle of it, that he'd started looking forward to Coulson's voice in his head instead of merely tolerating it.

"You ever gonna tell me more about your actual life?" Xander asked, one evening, sitting on the back steps with nothing particular to do for once. "Not the professional stuff. The actual stuff. Family, hobbies, whether you've got a dog somewhere missing you."

*"The Getting-To-Know-You Scene,"* the gallery's voice announced, settling in properly. *"Every buddy story needs one. This narrator has high hopes."*

*No dog,* Coulson said, something wry in it. *The job doesn't really accommodate one. Family's complicated, the way most people's families are complicated. I had a partner, once. It didn't work out, mostly because of the job, the hours, the secrecy I couldn't ever fully set down even at home.*

*"Oh. Oh, that took a turn."*

*"You asked for the actual stuff. This is the actual stuff. Sit with it."*

*That's rough.*

*It was, at the time. I've made my peace with it. The work mattered to me more than I think I ever fully admitted to her, and I don't know that I'd choose differently even now, knowing how it ended.*

*Hobbies, though? Please tell me you've got a hobby. Something normal. Stamp collecting. Bird watching. Anything.*

*"Please let it be stamps,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, with real feeling. *"I need this man to collect stamps."*

*I keep meticulous records,* Coulson said, dry. *Of nearly everything. Some people would call that a personality flaw. I've always considered it closer to a hobby.*

*"Close enough,"* the same voice sighed, satisfied anyway. *"I'll allow it."*

Xander laughed again, easier this time, and something in the moment settled into a comfortable, companionable quiet, the two of them sitting together, in whatever sense that meant for two consciousnesses sharing one set of eyes, watching the evening settle over a house that was, slowly, finding its way back toward something like normal.

*Thanks,* Xander said, eventually, quiet, meaning it.

*For what?*

*The drills. The patience. The bad jokes about your own personality flaws.* Xander watched the last light fade over the yard. *Guess I didn't expect any of this to actually be, I don't know. Nice. Figured it'd just be a war the whole time.*

*I'm glad it isn't,* Coulson said, warm underneath the words. *I wasn't expecting that either, if I'm honest.*

It wouldn't stay this simple for long. Both of them understood that, somewhere underneath the easy quiet of the evening, some old instinct warning them that whatever peace the house had found was going to be temporary, that the argument they hadn't finished having was still there, waiting, and that the real test of this partnership hadn't actually arrived yet.

But for one evening, sitting on the back steps with nothing more urgent than a thrown apple and a bad joke between them, it was enough just to have found something worth protecting.

Chapter 7: The Mood Shifts



A few days had done what a few days could, which wasn't much. Xander had traded the heavy bandage for a lighter one, had started bumping into doorframes on his left with a consistency that was almost funny if you squinted, and the house on Revello Drive had gone on being crowded and loud and full of girls who flinched at sounds that hadn't used to mean anything.

He'd found a rhythm to it, of a sort. Mornings meant helping Giles inventory whatever weapons hadn't been lost at the vineyard, a task that mostly consisted of counting stakes and pretending the count mattered as much as everyone treated it like it did. Afternoons meant checking in with the newer Potentials, the ones still learning which hallway led where, still flinching at doors slamming two rooms away, the kind of flinch that took months to train out of a person even once the actual danger was gone. Evenings meant sitting somewhere quiet with Coulson, the two of them working out, slowly, awkwardly, what exactly this new arrangement of theirs was actually supposed to look like day to day.

*You favor your right side when you walk,* Coulson observed, one such evening, watching Xander navigate the upstairs hallway carefully, like a man who'd clipped his shoulder on the same doorframe twice already that week. *You're compensating for the depth perception loss by over-correcting toward your working eye. It'll get worse before it gets better, unless you consciously slow down and let your brain relearn the spacing.*

*"Still narrating the walking,"* someone in the anonymous rows noted, fond despite themselves. *"I've made my peace with it."*

*Is this the part where you give me handler notes on how to walk?*

*This is the part where I point out you've walked into that doorframe twice in four minutes and I'd rather not watch you do it a third time.*

*Noted. Also, rude.*

*Accurate. Both things can be true.*

*"'Both things can be true,'"* the gallery's voice repeated, dry. *"Somebody cross-stitch that. Frame it. Hang it somewhere he'll see it every single morning."*

It was, in its own strange way, becoming almost companionable, the back-and-forth of it, the low-grade sniping that had replaced the raw, exposed vulnerability of that first night in the kitchen with something steadier, something two people built when they were stuck together long enough to stop performing for each other and start just existing side by side instead.

Which was why Xander noticed the room changing before anyone said a word about it. Some part of him had gotten quietly, unexpectedly good at noticing things lately, sharper than before, or maybe just more practiced, now that he had an audience who actually seemed to value it instead of writing it off as him being paranoid.

It wasn't dramatic. That was the thing nobody would think to describe afterward, when they tried to explain how it had happened, how a house full of people who trusted their general had ended up somewhere else entirely by the end of the night. It was smaller than that. A glance that used to land on Buffy automatically, checking in, seeking direction, now sliding somewhere else first, toward Faith, toward each other, a half-second recalibration nobody had decided on out loud. A sentence that used to end with *Buffy'll know what to do* now trailing off before it got there, replaced by something more careful, more hedged. The way conversations went quiet when Buffy walked into a room, not out of respect anymore, but out of something closer to caution.

Xander felt the temperature drop the way you felt weather changing before the sky actually did anything about it.

*"Ohh, I know this part,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, sitting up straighter. *"This is the part where everyone pretends nothing's happening while everything's happening."*

*"That's not a genre convention, that's just Tuesday in an ensemble drama."*

*You feel that,* he thought, quiet, sitting on the stairs with a mug of coffee he wasn't drinking, watching the living room reorganize itself in small, unspoken increments.

*I do,* Coulson said, and there was nothing dry in it, no performance, just plain confirmation. *Something's shifting. Nobody's said anything yet. It's in the room anyway.*

*Yeah.*

Neither of them said more than that, for a while. It wasn't restraint exactly, not the deliberate kind Coulson had been practicing since the vineyard, more that there wasn't anything useful to add to a thing they'd both already clocked at the same moment, arriving at the identical read from entirely different training, and something about that quiet agreement felt more solid than either of them commenting on it would have.

Xander watched the room a while longer, cataloging it the way he'd caught himself doing more and more lately: Anya, tight-jawed and pacing near the kitchen doorway, the pacing of someone building up to something rather than working something off. Willow, hovering near her, sensing it too, trying and failing to head it off with small talk that kept dying halfway out of her mouth. Giles, buried in a book he wasn't actually reading, the pages not turning fast enough to be genuine. Faith, over by the window, arms crossed, watching the room the way a woman watched weather she already knew the shape of.

*"Pan around the room,"* Fury's voice narrated, low, the director's habit still there under everything else he'd become. *"Everybody's doing the thing. The not-doing-the-thing thing."*

*"Giles hasn't turned that page in four minutes,"* someone in the anonymous rows reported, like a sports commentator. *"Four minutes. I've been counting."*

*"Somebody get that man a bookmark. He's not fooling anyone."*

And Buffy, moving through it all like she hadn't noticed, or like she'd noticed and decided the only way through was to keep moving like she hadn't, checking supply lists, asking Giles about patrol schedules, doing every single thing a leader was supposed to do while the ground quietly, steadily, shifted underneath her.

*She knows,* Xander thought, watching her.

*I think she does,* Coulson agreed. *I think that's why she's working so hard right now. People who already sense the floor going soft tend to move faster on the surface, not slower. It's a way of arguing with the feeling without having to say it out loud.*

*You've seen this before too. Not just the fear-curdling-into-a-decision thing. This part. The part right before.*

*More times than I'd like,* Coulson said, quiet. *It rarely announces itself. That's what makes it dangerous. By the time anyone names it, it's usually already decided.*

Xander turned that over, coffee gone properly cold in his hands, watching Anya finally break away from the kitchen doorway and cross the room toward Buffy with the flat, deliberate stride of someone who had run out of patience for waiting to be asked.

*"Here we go,"* the gallery's voice said, and this time there was no dryness left in it at all, none of the earlier play. *"Everybody quiet down. Let's actually watch this one."*

Nobody in the anonymous rows argued. Even the beverage machine, several rows back, seemed to understand this wasn't the moment.

*Here it comes,* Xander thought, and felt something brace behind his ribs that he didn't have a joke ready for.

Chapter 8: Empty Places



"Buffy." Anya's voice climbed before anyone had decided the conversation was actually happening yet, sharp enough to cut through the low hum of the living room and pull every eye in the house toward her. "I want to talk about the vineyard."

"Okay." Buffy set down the supply clipboard, careful, unhurried, a calm that plainly cost her real effort to hold. "Talk."

"People died," Anya said, not shouting, worse than shouting, flat and furious in a way that filled the room without needing volume. "People we brought with us died because of a plan that didn't work, and I would like someone to explain to me why we keep trusting the person whose plans keep not working."

*"There's no trope name for this one,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, the joking entirely gone out of it. *"Fear doing this to a room isn't a genre convention. It's just what fear does. Let's watch."*

Nobody in the anonymous rows said anything further. This narrator, for once, had nothing clever to add either.

"That's not fair," Willow said, too fast, the reflexive defense of someone who hadn't finished deciding whether she believed it.

"Isn't it?" Anya's eyes found Buffy across the room. "Isn't it exactly fair? You said Caleb would be manageable. You said the numbers worked. Half of us didn't come back from that vineyard, Buffy, and Xander's missing an eye, and I would like, just once, for someone to say the word wrong out loud instead of pretending we're all still fine with the plan just because the plan came from you."

Xander felt his own mouth open, felt the shape of a defense already forming, and felt it die there, unfinished, because the terrible thing under Anya's fury wasn't that she was being unfair. It was that she wasn't, not entirely, not in the way that would have made this easy to argue against.

*Say something,* Coulson said, low, urgent, the first real push he'd offered since the vineyard. *You know this isn't the whole truth. You were there. Tell them what actually happened.*

*And say what,* Xander thought back, and heard the bitterness in his own head before he'd decided to let it show. *That I built the plan too? That I'm the one who told her the vineyard timing would work? Doesn't exactly help her case, buddy.*

*It's still the truth.*

*The truth doesn't always help.*

Around the room, other voices were rising, a Potential Xander only half-recognized asking, quiet but audible, whether anyone actually knew what tonight's plan even was, whether there was a plan at all beyond hoping the last one hadn't taught them anything. Another girl near the stairs was crying, silent, the exhaustion of someone who'd used up everything she had just getting through the last week and had nothing left over for whatever came next.

"I'm not saying it to be cruel," Anya went on, quieter now, which was somehow worse. "I'm saying it because somebody has to, and because I watched two girls I didn't even know the last names of get carried out of that vineyard, and I don't think either of them would want us pretending everything's fine just because pretending is more comfortable than admitting we're scared."

"I'm not asking anyone to pretend," Buffy said, and her voice had gone very steady, very careful, holding a great deal very still to keep it that way. "I know it went wrong. I know people died. I don't have a version of tonight where that isn't true."

"Then what do we do differently?" Anya pressed. "Because I don't think you actually know. I think you're going to tell us to trust you again, and I don't think trust is a plan."

Giles closed his book, finally, set it aside, and said something careful and measured about process, about reconvening, about not making decisions in the heat of grief, and it didn't land, not really, the room too far gone into its own fear to be steered by careful measured words from a man everyone respected but nobody, right now, was in the mood to be managed by.

It was Faith's voice that actually cut through, low and certain in a way that carried further than Anya's fury had.

"Maybe it's time somebody else calls it for a while."

The room went still the way rooms did right before something irreversible happened.

Buffy didn't argue. That was, Xander thought later, the worst part, the part that stayed with him longest, that she didn't fight it, didn't defend herself, just stood there absorbing it with the flat, exhausted acceptance of someone who had already, privately, wondered the same thing about herself. Her eyes moved once around the room, slow, cataloging every face that wouldn't quite meet hers, and something in her posture settled, resigned, before anyone had actually finished deciding anything.

Nobody voted. Nobody needed to. The room simply, quietly, reorganized itself around a different center of gravity, glances that used to find her automatically now finding Faith instead, half a decision made by thirty small, silent recalibrations rather than one loud one.

"Okay," Buffy said, finally, quiet. "Okay."

She went upstairs without another word. Xander watched her go, watched the set of her shoulders, straight, controlled, determined not to let the room see anything more than she'd already given it, and felt the useless, cowardly weight of his own silence settle deeper into his chest with every step she took.

Twenty minutes later she came back down with a bag over her shoulder and nowhere in particular she'd said she was going, and nobody stopped her, and the door closed behind her with a sound that felt bigger than a door should.

Xander hadn't said one word in her defense the entire time.

*I saw this exact shape before,* Coulson said, quiet, into the silence that followed, and it wasn't advice, wasn't a push, just a man annotating something he recognized in his bones. *Chain of command breaking down under pressure. Fear finding the nearest available target and calling it a decision. I've watched rooms do this to good commanding officers more times than I'd like to count. It never looks like malice while it's happening. It just looks like relief that someone finally said the thing everyone was already thinking.*

*You gonna tell me that makes it okay?*

*No,* Coulson said. *I'm telling you I recognize it. Recognizing it and approving of it aren't the same thing, and for what it's worth, I don't think I approve of much that just happened in that room.* A pause, careful. *I also think you're allowed to have not known what to say. That's not the same as having done nothing wrong. But it's also not nothing.*

Around him, the house was already reorganizing itself, Faith accepting a question from one of the Potentials with the easy, unbothered confidence of someone stepping into a role she'd half-expected to land in eventually, Giles retreating to his books, still in the way of a man deciding whether to fight a losing battle tonight or save his strength for tomorrow, Willow disappearing toward the kitchen with her arms wrapped around herself.

Xander sat where he was for a long while, not moving, the coffee mug gone cold and forgotten in his hands, watching the shape of the house rearrange itself around an absence that had, until twenty minutes ago, been the thing holding all of it together.

Nobody in the anonymous rows had said a single word since Faith's line about somebody else calling it. This narrator checked, twice, in case a joke had been sitting there unnoticed. There wasn't one. Some scenes just don't have one in them.

Chapter 9: After



He got up eventually, because sitting on the stairs with a cold coffee mug had stopped being a place to process anything and started being a place to be seen not processing anything, and went looking for somewhere quieter to be alone with the thing sitting in his chest.

He found it in the backyard, the one corner of the property that hadn't filled up yet with Potentials trying to look busy while the whole house recalibrated around its new center. The evening had gone cool and gray, the kind of Sunnydale dusk that never quite committed to being either day or night, and Xander sat down against the fence and let it finally catch up with him.

*"Nobody's watching this one for laughs,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, before the anonymous rows had even settled into their seats properly. *"Just watching."*

What arrived wasn't one clean feeling. It was three at once, tangled, none of them winning.

Guilt came first, sharp and specific, for the silence he'd kept when a word from him might have changed the room's mind, or might not have, he'd never actually know now which. He kept replaying the moment Anya had turned to Buffy, the half-second where he could have stood, could have said *I built that plan too, if you're going to be angry at someone be angry at both of us*, and hadn't, had let his own cowardice dress itself up as tact, as not wanting to make things worse, when the truth, underneath all of that, was simpler and uglier: he hadn't trusted his own voice enough yet to spend it defending anyone, not even her.

Relief came next, quieter, uglier, a shameful little gladness that the decision hadn't required him to take a side out loud, that the room had reorganized itself without ever actually asking him where he stood. He hated that relief more than he hated the guilt. The guilt, at least, felt like it belonged to someone trying to be better. The relief just felt like the truest, least flattering thing about him.

And underneath both of those, something messier: a half-agreement with the room's fear that he didn't want to admit to, the private, unspoken worry that maybe Buffy's plan really had been the thing that cost people their lives, that maybe the room wasn't entirely wrong to be scared of trusting it again. He'd built half of that plan himself. If it had been flawed, the flaw was partly his too, and some small, cowardly part of him was glad the room had aimed its fear at her instead of splitting it evenly.

*You don't get to feel just one of those,* he thought, mostly to himself, mostly just to hear the thought land somewhere. *That'd almost be easier.*

*You don't,* Coulson agreed. *For what it's worth, I don't think there's a version of this where you only feel one thing. Guilt and relief aren't opposites. They're just both true at once, and your job right now isn't to pick which one wins. It's to survive carrying both without deciding either one gets to be the whole verdict on who you are.*

*That's a very professional-sounding way of saying I feel like garbage.*

*It's the professional version, yes.* Something almost gentle came through underneath the dryness. *I've said versions of that sentence to people in considerably worse circumstances than this one. It's not advice about Buffy. It's not a read on what you should have said in there, or what you should do next. I don't think either of us is in a position to know that yet. It's just, you're allowed to feel three things you can't reconcile, and none of them makes you a liar for feeling the other two.*

*You do this a lot? The emotional-triage thing?*

*More than I'd like to admit,* Coulson said. *It's not a skill you get taught. You just watch enough people fall apart in enough debriefing rooms that eventually you learn what actually helps and what just sounds like it should.*

*And what actually helps.*

*Mostly, someone staying in the room instead of leaving once things get uncomfortable.* A pause, something careful in it. *I don't have anywhere else to be, Xander. I want you to know that's not a small thing, for me to say. I've spent a career being the person who gets sent somewhere else the second the situation stops needing tact and starts needing exit strategies. I don't have an exit strategy from this. I'm not going anywhere you don't take me.*

Xander let that settle a while, back against the fence, the last of the daylight going gold and then gray over the yard, and didn't have a joke ready, for once, didn't reach for one.

Somewhere behind him, the house's low murmur shifted, a door opening, someone's voice asking whether anyone had seen where Buffy had gone, whether someone should follow her, and the question hung there, unanswered, nobody quite willing yet to be the one who went after the woman the house had just, quietly, without a vote, decided not to trust.

"Okay," he said, out loud, quiet enough that nobody in the busy house behind him would hear it. "Okay."

It wasn't a resolution. It wasn't even close to one. But it was, for that particular evening, the only true thing he had left to offer either of them.

Nobody in the anonymous rows had said a word the whole scene. This narrator isn't going to pretend that was a hard call to make.

He stayed out there until full dark, until the cold actually started to matter, and went back inside only when Willow found him and told him, gently, that Giles wanted everyone together to talk about what came next, now that what came next didn't have Buffy standing at the front of the room to explain it.

Chapter 10: Anya



She found him three days after the confrontation that had sent Buffy out the door, sitting alone in the garage sharpening a stake that didn't particularly need sharpening, and Xander felt his whole body go tense at the sight of her before she'd said a single word.

"I'm not here to fight," Anya said, settling onto an overturned crate across from him without waiting for an invitation. "I wanted to say something, and I've been putting it off because saying things is uncomfortable and I don't like being uncomfortable, but Willow says that's not actually a good enough reason to avoid a difficult conversation, so. Here I am."

*"Finally,"* said the voice from the gallery, warming considerably. *"A character who states her intentions directly instead of circling them for six scenes. This narrator has been begging for this energy since the pilot."*

Scattered, approving applause rippled through the anonymous rows, an audience that had, evidently, sat through a great many circling-for-six-scenes conversations in its time.

"Okay." Xander set the stake down carefully. "I'm listening."

"I was too hard on you. Not on Buffy, I stand by everything I said to Buffy, she made choices and people died and I don't think grief obligates me to pretend otherwise." Anya's hands twisted together in her lap, an old, human gesture that still looked slightly foreign on her sometimes, even after all these years. "But you were standing right there, and you'd just lost an eye, and I didn't say anything to you at all, like your pain wasn't even worth acknowledging in the moment. That wasn't fair. I've been thinking about it since, and I wanted you to know I noticed the unfairness, even if I didn't correct it in the moment."

Xander felt something in his chest loosen, an old tension he hadn't fully realized he'd still been carrying.

"Thanks," he said, simple. "That actually means a lot."

"I also want to say, separately, that I've noticed you've been different lately. Not bad different. Just, different." Anya studied him with the same blunt directness that had always been one of her most disarming qualities, an ex-demon's total unfamiliarity with the social convention of not simply asking what she wanted to know. "Is there something you want to tell me? I won't be upset either way. I've dated a Key made of pure energy that used to be a mystical ball of light, Xander. My tolerance for strange is considerably higher than most people's."

*She's going to find out eventually,* Coulson observed, quiet. *Might as well be honest with her too, if you're comfortable with it.*

*Yeah.* Xander turned it over, and found, somewhat to his own surprise, that he trusted her enough to say it plainly, the same way he'd trusted Buffy. *There's someone in my head. Has been since the vineyard. Not possessing me, just, along for the ride. His name's Coulson.*

Anya considered this information with the same flat, practical acceptance she brought to most impossible things.

"Is he a demon?"

*"Reasonable opening question,"* someone in the anonymous rows allowed. *"This is a woman who's dated worse."*

"No. Just a guy. Government spy type, from a world that's, honestly, kind of its own whole separate thing from ours. Long story."

"Does he have opinions about my jewelry business plans? I've been considering diversifying into protective amulets now that regular commerce seems to be resuming somewhat, and I'd value an outside perspective from someone presumably familiar with actual government procurement processes."

*"There it is,"* another voice said, delighted. *"Zero transition. Interdimensional passenger to procurement consulting in one breath. I love her so much."*

Xander laughed, surprised and genuine, the first real laugh he'd managed around Anya since the confrontation.

*Tell her I'd be happy to review a business plan, if she puts one together,* Coulson said, something warm and amused underneath the offer. *I have, believe it or not, actually reviewed procurement proposals before. It's not glamorous work, but I know what I'm doing.*

Xander relayed it, and watched Anya's whole face light up with genuine, uncomplicated delight, plainly thrilled that somebody was taking her business ambitions seriously instead of treating them as an eccentric quirk to be humored.

"I like him already," Anya said, decisive. "Tell him I'll have a full proposal ready within the week. I've been thinking about the amulets for some time and I have very strong opinions about margin structures."

*"'Very strong opinions about margin structures,'"* someone repeated, reverent. *"Best line in the episode. Somebody nominate this woman for something."*

*"She's not getting an award, she's getting a spreadsheet, and honestly that might mean more to her."*

"I'll pass that along."

They sat together a while longer, easy now, the tension of the last few days finally, properly resolved between them, and Xander found himself grateful, in a way he didn't often let himself fully feel, for the prickly, unfiltered honesty that had always made Anya who she was.

"I'm glad we talked," he said, eventually, meaning it.

"Me too." Anya stood, brushing off her jeans with brisk efficiency. "I don't like unresolved tension. It's bad for business relationships and personal ones alike. Efficiency matters in both."

"Never change, Anya."

"I have no current plans to." She paused at the garage door, glancing back at him with something almost soft underneath the usual bluntness. "For what it's worth, Xander. I think you've gotten better this year. Not just the eye thing. All of it. I don't say things like that often, because compliments feel strange to give, but I wanted you to have heard it from me directly, in case I forget to say it again."

She left before he could fully respond, and Xander sat alone in the garage a while longer, the stake forgotten in his lap, something warm and unexpectedly grateful settling into his chest.

*That was good,* Coulson said, quiet.

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, watching the garage door swing shut behind her. *Yeah, it really was.*

He didn't know, sitting there, how few more conversations like this one he'd get to have with her, how little time remained before the last battle claimed her in a moment too fast and too chaotic for anyone to properly say goodbye. He didn't know that this quiet, prickly, generous reconciliation in a garage full of half-sharpened stakes was going to become one of the things he held onto hardest, afterward, when there wasn't anything left to hold onto but memory.

*"Wow, foreshadowing much,"* someone in the anonymous rows started to say, and got hushed, fast, by half a dozen voices at once before the sentence finished landing.

The gallery didn't riff any further on that part. Whatever they knew, or half-knew, or simply felt coming the way an audience sometimes feels a story's shape before the story admits it, they kept it to themselves after that, and let the scene end warm.

He just sat there, grateful, for a friendship that had survived worse than a bad week, and went back to sharpening a stake that still, stubbornly, didn't need it.

Chapter 11: The Irony



Giles called everyone together the next morning, the living room packed shoulder to shoulder with Potentials still working out where to look, the kind of collective uncertainty that settled over a room once its usual center of gravity had walked out the front door the night before. Faith stood where Buffy usually stood, arms loose at her sides, entirely unbothered by the weight of thirty pairs of eyes suddenly needing somewhere to land.

"Here's the deal," she said, perched on the arm of the couch instead of standing at the front of the room the way Buffy always had, like formality was a coat she wasn't interested in wearing. "We don't have time to war-game every angle six ways from Sunday. We hit hard, we hit fast, and we don't leave anybody standing around waiting for a memo. Feels right, we go. Simple."

A hand went up near the back, one of the newer girls, hesitant. "What if it doesn't feel right to everybody at the same time?"

*"New captain, same energy,"* someone in the anonymous rows observed. *"Different flavor of confident. I respect the rebrand."*

*"'For now,'"* another voice repeated. *"She said 'for now.' Write that down too, notebook girl."*

"Then you trust the person who's been doing this longer than you have." Faith's grin flashed, easy, confident, a grin that had gotten people to follow her into worse situations than this one. "That's me, for now. We clear?"

"What about backup positions?" Another voice, one of the quieter Potentials, a girl who'd taken to writing everything down in a battered spiral notebook since the day she arrived. "What if the first plan doesn't work?"

"Then we adjust on the fly. That's kind of the whole gig, sweetheart. You don't get a rehearsal for the apocalypse." Faith said it easy, no cruelty in it, just the flat certainty of someone who genuinely believed what she was saying. "Look, I get it, you're used to Blondie's whole color-coded battle plan thing. I'm not her. Never gonna be her. What I've got instead is, I don't freeze. I don't second-guess myself into a corner. Sometimes that's worth more than a plan with every box checked."

*"Give her a break, she's doing the confident-captain bit, it comes with a whole voice."*

*"I'm not saying it's wrong, I'm saying somebody should be writing all of this down for the inevitable retrospective."*

*"Somebody is."* A beat, pointed. *"Notebook girl. Back row. Already on it."*

The girl with the notebook wrote something down anyway, quick and quiet, and Xander caught the small, private gesture and understood it without needing it explained: some fears didn't go away just because you'd been told, convincingly, not to have them.

Nobody argued out loud, though. Xander watched three dozen scared teenage girls nod along like that was the most reassuring thing anyone had said to them in a week, and felt something uncomfortable settle behind his ribs that took him a second to name.

*You're doing the thing where you go quiet,* Coulson observed, mild.

*"He got that from me,"* Fury's voice put in, mild in exactly the same register, entirely unbothered by the audience. *"Everybody know that? The quiet-noticing thing. That's a Fury original. I should've trademarked it decades ago."*

*I'm doing the thing where I notice something,* Xander corrected, watching Faith wave off a question about contingencies with an easy, confident *we'll handle it when we get there.* *You know what that plan is? That's instinct. That's zero backup, zero net, purely feels right, we go. That's basically my whole philosophy of life, just said out loud by somebody with actual muscle behind it.*

*I noticed the resemblance too.*

*"Oh, HE noticed it too,"* someone in the anonymous rows crowed. *"This is the good part. This is the part where the guy who lectures about process has to watch his own philosophy walk around wearing somebody else's face."*

*"Poetic justice doesn't usually get a body double, but here we are."*

*Bet you love that.*

*I have complicated feelings about it,* Coulson admitted, and there was an edge in it now, real and unhidden in a way Xander hadn't heard from him since the vineyard. *I've watched instinct-driven leadership work exactly often enough to respect it and fail exactly often enough to distrust it on principle. No accountability chain. No vetting on that call she just made. Authority claimed by confidence rather than earned through anything I'd recognize as process. I'd flag every part of that in a formal review.*

*So you don't trust her.*

*I don't trust the method. That's not quite the same thing, though I'll grant you it's close enough tonight that the difference might not matter.*

Xander turned that over, watching Faith move through the room with the easy, physical confidence of someone who'd never once doubted her own read on a fight, answering questions with a certainty that filled the room the way Buffy's careful, considered answers never quite had. It was strange, hearing Coulson's skepticism laid out plain like that, an actual opinion instead of the careful neutrality he'd been keeping since the wave-off he hadn't made yet, and stranger still to recognize, underneath it, that Coulson's bias toward his own training was exactly the mirror of the bias Xander had spent the last several days accusing him of not understanding.

*You know you're doing the same thing you keep telling me I do,* Xander said. *Trusting your own method because it's yours. That's not exactly neutral ground you're standing on either.*

A pause. Longer than Xander expected. *No,* Coulson said, finally, and there was something almost surprised in the admission. *It isn't. I hadn't quite let myself see it from that angle.* Another beat. *I'll sit with that.*

*"THERE it is,"* several voices said at once, ragged and overlapping. *"The fold. The actual fold this time, not the strategic one."*

*"Somebody get the trophy back out, we retired it too early in chapter five."*

*You gonna admit I've got a point, or just carry it around unsaid forever?*

*Both, possibly. I find those aren't mutually exclusive either.*

*"'Both, possibly,'"* the gallery's voice sighed. *"This man's entire personality is a hedge clause."*

Xander didn't push it further. It felt, for once, like enough had been said, and around him the meeting was breaking up into smaller clusters, girls pairing off to check weapons, to run drills Faith had rattled off without writing anything down, the whole house already moving to a rhythm that belonged to somebody other than the person it had belonged to a week ago.

He caught up with the notebook girl on his way out, unable to quite let it go. "Hey. You doing okay?"

"Fine." She closed the notebook, a little too quickly. "I just like keeping track of things. Helps me feel like I've got some control, even when I don't."

"That's not a bad instinct to have." Xander crouched down slightly, meeting her at eye level, an old habit by now, worn smooth from a dozen conversations just like this one. "For what it's worth, it's okay to trust Faith's confidence and still keep your own notes on the side. Those aren't opposites. Having a backup plan in your own head doesn't mean you don't believe in the person leading you."

Something in the girl's shoulders eased, just slightly. "Nobody else really gets that. Feels like everyone wants me to just, I don't know, relax and trust completely, or panic and refuse to follow anybody at all. Nobody talks about doing both."

"Yeah, well." Xander managed a small, tired smile. "Turns out doing both is basically the only sane way to survive most of this. Ask me sometime, I'll tell you all about it."

She managed a small smile back, tucking the notebook carefully into her jacket, and went to find her sparring partner for the evening's drills, steadier than she'd been a few minutes ago, even if only by a little.

Xander watched Faith work the room a while longer after that, still turning over what Coulson had said, and couldn't shake the thought, small and uncomfortable, that he was watching his own argument with Coulson play out in front of him, wearing somebody else's face, with real stakes attached this time, and no clean answer yet about whether that made him feel better about his own case or worse. Maybe, he thought, that was the actual lesson underneath all of it, not that one way of leading was right and the other wrong, but that a house full of frightened people needed room for both kinds of confidence to exist side by side, Faith's loud, instinctive certainty and the quieter, more careful kind that kept its own notes on the side, just in case.

Chapter 12: The Call He Doesn't Question



The plan tightened over the next two days into something with actual edges: a defensive line, a rough rotation, Faith's read on where the Bringers would come from based on nothing Xander could point to except the plain confidence of someone who'd survived a lot of bad situations by trusting her gut and never once had to explain the math behind it out loud.

She had them running drills morning and evening, sparring pairs in the backyard, weapon checks in the garage, and there was something to be said for the energy of it, the house feeling less like a place waiting to be attacked and more like a place actively preparing to fight back.

*"It's not a montage, it's a second act,"* someone corrected, mild. *"There's a difference."*

*"Is there, though."* Xander watched the mood shift again, cautiously, a little, the raw fear of the days right after the vineyard hardening into something closer to grim determination.

"We hold the front hall and the kitchen," Faith said, on the second evening, tracing a rough line on Giles's old floor plan with one finger while a dozen Potentials leaned in around her. "Anyone comes through, they've gotta funnel through one of two chokepoints. We stack both, we're solid."

Xander looked at the plan a beat longer than anyone else in the room seemed to, and felt something small and uneasy catch, low, half-formed, a doubt that hadn't fully turned into a thought yet.

*You see it too,* Coulson said, quiet, and it wasn't a question.

*See what.*

*"Oh no,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, sitting forward. *"He's doing the thing. The noticing thing. I hate the noticing thing, it never means good news."*

*"HEY,"* Fury's voice cut in, sharp, genuinely offended. *"That's my thing to have a thing about. Nobody else gets to be tired of the noticing thing, I invented being tired of the noticing thing."*

*No retreat path. If the line doesn't hold, there's nowhere organized to fall back to. She's built the whole plan around holding, not around what happens if holding fails.*

*"Chekhov's floor plan,"* another voice muttered. *"Somebody should frame that on the wall. Right next to the doorframe joke."*

Xander looked at the floor plan again, and yeah, there it was, obvious now that it had been named, an entire strategy with no version of itself for the version where things went wrong. Two solid chokepoints, and nothing at all connecting them, no fallback route sketched anywhere on the page, no contingency beyond an implicit, unstated assumption that the line would simply hold because Faith believed it would.

He opened his mouth, half-decided to say something, and closed it again, because saying it meant standing up in a room that had just watched Buffy get quietly deposed for exactly this kind of second-guessing, meant putting his own uncertain gut up against Faith's confident one in front of thirty scared girls who needed someone in the room to sound sure.

He didn't trust his own read enough yet to spend it on that.

*I'm not saying anything,* Xander thought, and even to himself it sounded thinner than he wanted it to.

*Xander.* Coulson's voice had lost all its earlier caution. This was the first time since the vineyard he'd pushed, actually pushed, not commentary, not restraint, a real, direct disagreement laid bare. *This isn't a small thing to let go. If this goes wrong, that gap is where it goes wrong.*

*Faith's handled worse than this. She'll be fine.*

*That's not the same as the plan being sound.*

*Look, I get that you're the professional planning guy, I do, but you've been in my head for like four days. She's actually done this. She's actually survived things neither of us have.* Xander felt the old defensiveness rising, familiar now, worn smooth from use. *I'm not gonna stand up and second-guess the one person in this house who still seems sure of herself, not over a hunch.*

*It's not a hunch. It's a gap in the plan that either of us could point to on a whiteboard.*

*Then it's a gap. Not the first plan in history to have one.*

*"Everybody stop laughing,"* the gallery's voice said, and it wasn't a joke this time, quiet enough that the anonymous rows actually did. *"This is the argument that matters. Let it land."*

Coulson didn't answer right away, and when he did, something in the quality of the silence beforehand told Xander this wasn't going to be forgotten, wasn't going to be let go the way most disagreements between them had been so far.

*All right,* Coulson said, finally, quiet, and there was nothing dismissive in it, nothing that sounded like giving up so much as a man filing something away for later, with real weight attached to it. *I've said what I needed to say. It's your call. I hope I'm wrong.*

*You're wrong.*

*I hope so,* Coulson said again, and didn't sound at all convinced.

Xander went back to helping stack sandbags at the kitchen chokepoint, working alongside two Potentials who'd started to actually trust the rhythm of the preparations, and didn't think about the conversation again for the rest of the night, mostly because he was doing his best not to, and told himself the not-thinking-about-it was a kind of confidence rather than what it actually was, which was the oldest trick in his own particular book: refuse to look directly at the thing you're afraid might be true, and it can't quite finish becoming true yet.

Later, lying awake with the house finally quiet around him, he thought about it anyway, turning the floor plan over in his head the way he'd turned the vineyard map over before the eye, looking for the version where the gap didn't matter, where Faith's instinct held the way it always seemed to, where the whole thing worked out fine and he never had to know whether he should have said something.

He didn't find that version. He found the doubt still sitting there instead, patient, unresolved, waiting for whatever came next to decide whether it had been worth listening to.

It wasn't a trick that was going to hold for very much longer.

Chapter 13: The House Falls



*"Nobody's riffing on this one,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, before the anonymous rows could try. *"We already told you where the gap was. This part's just watching it matter."*

They came in through the back around midnight, the way they always did, a dozen Bringers moving with the kind of flat, purposeful silence that belonged to men who'd given up their eyes and most of their humanity along with them, and for the first four minutes, Faith's plan held exactly the way she'd promised it would.

"Kitchen's solid!" someone shouted, and it was, three Potentials and a length of rebar making short, brutal work of the first wave through the back door, the chokepoint doing exactly the job Faith had drawn it to do on Giles's old floor plan.

Xander, stationed at the front hall with two others and a fire axe he'd never expected to actually need, felt something in his chest loosen half a degree. Maybe he'd been wrong. Maybe the gap Coulson had spotted was never going to matter, one of those flaws that only counted if the universe bothered to find it. Around him, girls who'd spent two days running drills moved with something that almost looked like confidence, weapons up, positions held, the house's newest rhythm doing exactly what it had been built to do.

The universe bothered.

It came from the side, not the front or the back, a third group nobody had accounted for pouring in through the dining room's bay window, glass exploding inward in a spray that caught two Potentials before anyone had time to process the new direction. The front hall and the kitchen held, exactly as planned, two solid walls doing exactly their job, and the house between them, the actual living space where thirty scared girls had been sleeping and eating and trying to remember what safe used to feel like, came apart from the middle outward with nowhere organized to fall back to, because nobody had planned for what happened once the line, somehow, everywhere at once, stopped being a line and became a house full of chaos with three separate directions of threat and one single, panicked, unrehearsed idea of what to do about it.

*This is it,* Coulson said, and his voice had gone hard and fast, urgent in a register Xander hadn't heard from him since the vineyard. *This is the gap. There's nowhere for anyone to fall back to. You need to get people moving toward an actual choke, not just away from what's closest.*

*Working on it!* Xander swung the axe wide, caught a Bringer across the shoulder, felt the impact travel up his arm in a way that would ache badly tomorrow if there was a tomorrow that let him notice. Around him the hall had turned into the scrambling, directionless chaos Coulson had warned about days ago, girls running toward each other instead of toward anywhere useful, Faith's voice somewhere upstairs shouting orders that arrived a half second too late to matter, the whole careful architecture of the plan dissolving into the oldest and worst shape a fight could take, everyone for themselves, nobody's back covered, nobody's exit clear.

He saw Robin Wood go down near the stairs, a Bringer's blade catching him low and wrong, saw two Potentials try to drag him clear and get tangled instead in a hallway that had no organized route out of it, only doorways leading to other rooms leading to other doorways, an entire house that had been built for living in and not one single corridor of it built for retreating through.

*Xander, the study, there's a window that leads to the side yard, get people funneling there, it's the closest thing to an actual exit this house has left...*

"The study!" Xander shouted, loud as he could over the chaos, pointing with the axe. "Everybody, the study, out the side window, go, go!"

It worked, mostly, a trickle of girls finding the direction and following it, but it was minutes too late, built on the fly instead of planned, the improvised fix to a hole that should have been closed before the fighting ever started, and even as bodies started moving the right way at last, Xander felt a sick recognition arrive in real time, no delay, no distance, landing exactly as hard as Coulson had warned it would three days ago on a floor plan nobody had wanted to argue with out loud.

*You told me,* Xander thought, breathing hard, watching Robin Wood get half-carried, half-dragged toward the study window by two girls who should never have had to learn to do that. *You actually told me.*

*This isn't the moment for that conversation.* Coulson's voice was flat, controlled, doing the thing professionals did when there wasn't room yet for the feeling underneath the words. *Get people to the window. We'll have the other conversation later.*

Xander swung the axe again, caught another Bringer, felt his own arm going numb with the effort of it, and kept moving people toward the study, toward the one exit that existed now only because Coulson had found it after the fact, a patch stitched onto a plan that had never accounted for needing one, and somewhere underneath the adrenaline and the noise and the narrow, focused work of surviving the next thirty seconds, the shape of his own mistake sat in his chest like a stone he was going to be carrying for a long time.

The fighting ran another ten minutes past that, the Bringers finally thinning, finally breaking, whatever purpose had sent them retreating as suddenly and silently as it had arrived, leaving the house full of broken glass and blood and a ringing quiet that came after violence finally stopped needing anyone's attention.

---

They counted the cost in the gray hour before dawn, the house full of shattered glass and blood that hadn't fully dried yet, and the hollow quiet that came after violence had finally, finally stopped needing anyone's attention.

Robin Wood was alive. Barely, according to the two Potentials with the most first-aid training doing what they could with what they had, but alive, which was more than the house could say for everyone. Two more girls hadn't made it, names Xander had only just learned in the last week, faces he was going to remember for a lot longer than that.

Xander sat on the porch steps, the axe still in his hand because he hadn't quite managed to put it down yet, and didn't have a joke ready, didn't reach for one, didn't try.

*I should've said it louder,* he thought, eventually, into the quiet. *When you flagged it. I should've made a bigger deal out of it instead of just letting it drop because I didn't want to be the guy second-guessing Faith in front of everybody.*

*Maybe,* Coulson said, quiet. *I don't know that louder would have changed the outcome. Faith might not have listened regardless. I don't say that to let you off the hook. I say it because I don't actually know, and I'm not going to pretend certainty I don't have just to make this conversation feel more resolved than it is.*

*That's not really making me feel better.*

*I'm not trying to make you feel better. I'm trying to be honest with you, which I think, right now, matters more.*

Xander looked out at the wrecked yard, the broken bay window, the first gray edge of morning starting to find its way over the fence, and felt the weight of the last several hours settle into something that wasn't going away with sleep or jokes or time, not for a good while.

"Yeah," he said, out loud, to the empty porch, to the house behind him full of people trying to put themselves back together. "Yeah. Okay."

It wasn't an apology. It wasn't quite an acceptance either. It was just the sound of a boy carrying the unglamorous weight of having been wrong about something that mattered, and finding, for the first time in his life, that there wasn't a joke anywhere close enough to reach for.

*"Yeah,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, no performance left in it at all. *"Now you're learning, kid."*

Chapter 14: Dug In



By midmorning the house had gone from wrecked to grimly functional, someone had boarded over the bay window, someone else had gotten a pot of coffee going despite the general lack of anyone in a state to drink it, and Xander was sweeping glass out of the dining room with the joyless focus of a man who badly wanted a task simple enough not to think about anything else while he did it.

*"Aftermath episode,"* the gallery's voice said, quieter than usual, none of its earlier appetite for jokes in it. *"These are always the hard ones to sit through. Watch how carefully everyone avoids saying the actual number out loud."*

Nobody in the anonymous rows had anything to add to that.

*We need to talk about what happens next,* Coulson said, and there was nothing tentative in it now, no restraint at all, the professional urgency Xander had first heard at the vineyard fully returned. *A real plan. Chain of command that doesn't collapse the first time someone's confidence outruns their preparation. Contingencies built in from the start, not stitched on after people are already bleeding.*

"I know what happened," Xander said out loud, low, sweeping harder than the glass strictly required. "I was there. I don't need the after-action report."

*I'm not giving you an after-action report. I'm telling you what needs to change, and it needs to come from someone in this house, and I don't think it's going to be Faith.*

"So what, now you want to run things? Twelve hours ago you were the guy telling me you didn't have a plan, just an opinion nobody asked for."

*I have more than an opinion. I have twenty years of watching exactly this kind of failure happen in exactly this kind of vacuum, and I know what fixes it.*

"Yeah, well, maybe I'm not real interested in your fix right now." The words came out sharper than Xander meant them to, sharper than the moment strictly called for, and some distant, more reasonable part of him noted the disproportion even as the rest of him kept swinging the broom like it owed him something. "Funny how you were right about the retreat path and now suddenly you've got the whole playbook. Real convenient timing."

*That's not fair.*

"Maybe not. Don't much care right now."

He knew, even as he said it, that the anger wasn't really about Coulson's timing, wasn't really about the fix or the playbook or any of the specific words being traded between them. It was about being wrong, freshly and publicly wrong, in a way that had cost Robin Wood most of his blood and nearly cost the house a great deal more, and some old, familiar part of Xander, the part that had been digging in against being told what to do since long before any of this started, had decided that admitting Coulson's approach might actually be the right one now meant admitting the whole argument they'd been circling since the kitchen table, not just this one call, and some stubborn, wounded corner of him needed Coulson to be wrong about something, anything, badly enough to keep swinging at shadows rather than let that happen.

*You're digging in,* Coulson observed, and there wasn't judgment in it, just a flat, careful read. *I understand why. I'd probably do something similar in your position. I still think you're wrong to do it right now, but I understand it.*

"Great. Glad we're on the same page about how wrong I am."

*That's not what I said.*

"Feels like what you meant."

He swept the last of the glass into a dustpan he didn't remember picking up, dumped it into a garbage bag someone had already half-filled with the wreckage of the night before, and kept moving, kept his hands busy, because stillness right now felt like an invitation for the rest of it to catch up with him.

Willow passed through the dining room while he worked, arms full of first-aid supplies, and paused just long enough to catch his eye. "You okay?"

"Peachy," Xander said, not looking up from the broom. "Just doing some light home renovation. Very therapeutic."

"Xander."

"I'm okay, Will. Really." He managed something close to a real look at her, softening it. "Go take care of Robin. I've got this."

She lingered a moment longer, clearly unconvinced, but the house had a hundred urgent things needing her attention this morning, and she squeezed his shoulder once and moved on, leaving him alone again with the broom and the argument still simmering behind his ribs.

*You want to know what actually bothers me,* Xander said, eventually, the anger not gone but banked lower, simmering instead of boiling. *It's not that you were right. It's that being right doesn't feel like anything. I thought if I ever actually proved my gut mattered, it'd feel like something. Instead two people are dead and Robin's barely breathing and all I've got is you, very calmly, very professionally, telling me what to fix next.*

*I don't have a better feeling to offer you,* Coulson said, quiet. *I wish I did. In my experience, being right about a danger nobody listened to never feels like winning. It just feels like carrying the weight of having seen it coming and not been enough to stop it. That doesn't go away. You just get more practiced at carrying it.*

*That's supposed to help?*

*No,* Coulson admitted. *I don't think there's a version of this conversation that helps. I think there's a version that's honest, and I'd rather give you that than something that sounds better and isn't true.*

*You said you're used to carrying it. How many times, exactly.*

*More than I'd like to count,* Coulson said, quiet. *A missed threat in Prague, once, early in my career, that cost two field agents their lives. A miscalculated timeline in a hostage situation I still think about more than I'd admit under normal circumstances. Being right doesn't inoculate you against the cost of it happening anyway. If anything, it makes the cost feel more personal, because you know exactly how it could have gone differently, and it didn't.*

*How do you keep doing the job, knowing that.*

*Because the alternative is not showing up the next time, and the next time might be the one where being right actually saves someone.* A pause, something almost gentle underneath the professional flatness. *You don't get to stop caring just because caring hurts. You just learn to carry it forward instead of letting it stop you.*

Xander didn't answer that right away, standing in the middle of a dining room still gritty with glass dust, the argument having burned through most of its own fuel without either of them actually resolving anything, and found, underneath the exhaustion, that the honesty landed harder than any reassurance would have.

"Yeah," he said, finally, out loud, quiet, mostly to himself. "Yeah, okay. Forward it is."

*"'Into the valley of Death rode the six hundred,'"* someone in the anonymous rows murmured, half under their breath, not really a joke, just an old line borrowed because none of their own quite fit. Nobody asked them to explain it. Nobody needed to.

Chapter 15: The Turn



Coulson didn't push further after that, not right away, and the silence that followed had a different quality to it than the silences that had come before, the kind of quiet that meant less restraint and more a man actually reconsidering his approach in real time.

*All right,* Coulson said, finally, quiet. *I'm going to stop pushing.*

Xander kept sweeping, waiting for the catch, the qualifier, the *but* that usually followed a sentence like that.

*Not because I've changed my mind,* Coulson went on. *I haven't. I still think this house needs real structure or it's going to lose people it doesn't have to lose. But I've spent enough years managing people who dig in under pressure to know that pushing harder right now doesn't change your mind, it just costs something, real damage, not just wasted breath, and I'd rather back off and be wrong about the timing than be right about the structure and cause harm getting there.*

"You're doing the thing again."

*What thing.*

*"Oh, they're back,"* someone in the anonymous rows murmured, quiet, testing whether it was allowed yet. *"Cautiously optimistic about the return of bickering."*

*"And we're back to using my thing,"* Fury's voice put in, satisfied, before catching himself. *"Wait. That didn't come out right."*

*"It really didn't."*

"The thing where you back off and it somehow makes me feel worse than if you'd just kept arguing." Xander stopped sweeping, leaned the broom against the wall, looked at nothing in particular with the one eye that still worked. "You're supposed to be the professional planning guy. Plan around me being stubborn, then. Isn't that what all your training's for?"

*It is,* Coulson said. *I just did.*

Xander didn't have a response for that, not right away, and the quiet that followed wasn't angry anymore, just tired, an exhaustion that came from a fight running out of fuel before it ran out of feeling.

He went back to sweeping, slower now, and somewhere in the rhythm of it, glass scraping against hardwood, his mind wandered loose from the argument entirely, toward the actual problem underneath it, the one Coulson's structure and Faith's instinct had both, in their own ways, failed to solve last night. A house full of scared girls with no one steady enough to hold it together. A leadership vacuum that hadn't been filled so much as occupied, first by Faith's confidence, and confidence alone had turned out not to be enough, not without something underneath it the house could actually trust when things went sideways.

He thought about Buffy on the porch with a bag over her shoulder, walking away without a single person in the house finding a reason to stop her.

He thought about the way she used to read a room before deciding anything, slower than Faith, more careful, sometimes frustratingly so, but rarely wrong about the thing that actually mattered once the dust settled.

He thought about how, even now, exhausted and half-wrecked, part of him still expected to hear her footsteps on the stairs, still braced automatically for her voice cutting through a crisis with the calm she brought that nobody else in the house, not Faith, not Giles, not even Coulson with all his professional composure, seemed able to replicate.

He thought about who this house actually needed, not who was loudest, not who was most confident, not who had the cleanest theoretical structure, but who the house itself, in its bones, already trusted, however imperfectly, however recently betrayed that trust had felt.

"We need Buffy," Xander said, out loud, quiet, mostly to himself, and felt the rightness of it land somewhere underneath the argument he'd just finished having, arrived at on his own, not handed to him, not argued into him, just found, the way he found most things, by circling the actual shape of the problem until the answer stopped hiding from him.

*I agree,* Coulson said, and there was nothing in it that sounded like winning, no I-told-you-so, no quiet claim on the idea. *For what it's worth, I don't think I'd have gotten there on my own. Not this way. Not this fast.*

"Yeah, well." Xander picked the broom back up, found one last corner of glass to chase across the floor. "Somebody had to notice something you couldn't plan for."

*Apparently so.*

*So what now. I just, what, go find her? Nobody's even said her name out loud since last night.*

*That's worth noticing too,* Coulson said. *A house this scared of its own fear doesn't want to admit it might have made a mistake. Someone's going to have to be willing to say it plainly before anyone goes looking for her. I don't think that has to be a whole speech. I think it just has to be true, and said by somebody the room will actually hear it from.*

Xander looked around the dining room, the last of the glass finally swept clean, the house beginning to stir awake around him with the low, uncertain business of people trying to figure out what today was even supposed to look like.

He found Giles first, in the study, surrounded by the chaos of a man who'd been up all night cross-referencing something that had, in the end, given him no useful answers.

"Can I say something that might not be popular?" Xander asked, hovering in the doorway.

*"Low bar tonight,"* someone in the anonymous rows noted. *"Man's been cross-referencing dead ends since midnight, he'd welcome a strongly worded grocery list at this point."*

Giles looked up, weary. "At this point, I'd welcome almost anything that isn't another dead end."

"We need Buffy back."

*"Called that one three chapters ago,"* the gallery's voice said, quiet, not gloating, more relieved than anything. *"Feels good to be right about the hopeful thing for once."*

Giles was quiet a long moment, removing his glasses, cleaning them with the same slow, deliberate motion he always used when buying himself time to think properly. "I rather agree," he said, finally. "I confess I've been hesitant to say so myself. It felt presumptuous, given how the room decided things the other night."

"Yeah, well. Sometimes the room's wrong. Doesn't mean the people in it are bad people. Just means fear makes for lousy decision-making, and somebody's gotta be willing to say that out loud eventually."

"Indeed." Giles managed a small, tired smile. "I'll speak to Faith, if you're willing to be the one who actually goes and finds her. I suspect that particular errand will land better coming from you than from either of us."

"Yeah." Xander felt the shape of the task settle into place, real now, no longer just a thought turning over in his head. "Yeah, I can do that."

It wasn't a truce, not exactly, between him and Coulson, not yet. But it was, for the first time since the vineyard, something that felt less like two men proving a point at each other and more like two men who'd actually landed, independently, on the same answer, from two entirely different directions, and neither one of them needed to say out loud how much that was starting to matter.

Chapter 16: Finding Buffy



He told the house he was going for a walk, which wasn't a lie exactly, just an incomplete one, and nobody stopped him, nobody asked where, the whole place still too raw and uncertain to spare much attention for one more person slipping out the door.

He made it two streets before Coulson finally asked the question that had apparently been sitting patiently behind everything else since the dining room.

*Where would Buffy go.*

*"It's always the crypt,"* someone muttered from the anonymous rows, the weary tone of a man who had, apparently, seen this particular episode before.

Xander kept walking, hands in his pockets, the evening cooling fast the way it did this time of year. *That's not you telling me. That's you asking me.*

*It is.* Something almost like satisfaction crept into it, the plain pleasure of a technique landing the way it was supposed to. *I don't know her. I've never met her, I have no history with her, nothing I could offer you here would be worth the paper it's printed on. You do know her. So. Where would she go.*

Xander walked another block, turning it over properly instead of reaching for the first easy answer, the way you did when a question actually mattered. Not her mom's old house, too obvious, too much like retreating backward into a life that wasn't hers anymore. Not the school, not with everything that place had come to mean this year. Not anywhere with people in it, not tonight, not after a room full of people she'd trusted had decided, without much of a fight, that she wasn't worth trusting anymore.

*You're taking a while,* Coulson observed, not unkindly.

*I'm actually thinking about it instead of guessing. Give me a second.* He turned the question over from a few more angles, tried and discarded three more possibilities, and eventually landed somewhere he hadn't expected to, somewhere that made an uncomfortable kind of sense the longer he stayed with it.

Somewhere she could stop performing okay for once. Somewhere that had never once asked anything of her except to show up as whatever she actually was that night, hero or wreck or something in between. Somewhere she wouldn't have to explain herself to anyone who actually cared what the explanation was.

"Spike's," Xander said, out loud, before he'd fully finished deciding it, the certainty arriving a half second ahead of the reasoning behind it. "She'd go to Spike's."

*"CALLED IT,"* the same weary voice from the anonymous rows shouted, considerably less weary now. *"I called it! Somebody owes me money, this was a whole bit I had going with myself!"*

*"Nobody bet against you, there was no bit, you were the only participant."*

*"Doesn't make me less right."*

*You're sure.*

"No," Xander admitted, already changing direction, already walking faster. "I'm not sure. I just know her, and I know that's where she goes when she doesn't want anyone to find her, which is exactly backwards logic for finding her, except I think that's kind of the point. She wants someone to look. She just doesn't want it to be easy."

*That's not tracking. That's not deduction either, not really.*

"Nope."

*It's just knowing her.*

"Yeah." Xander picked up his pace toward the cemetery, the crypt's low outline already visible against the last purple edge of the sky. "First useful thing I've had all week that didn't come out of a book, a plan, or your twenty years of training."

*I noticed,* Coulson said, and there was nothing competitive in it at all, just a plain, quiet kind of respect. *For the record. I noticed.*

---

*"All right, that's enough out of both of you,"* the gallery's voice said, and the anonymous rows actually quieted, a little sheepish about it. *"We found her. Let's not make a mess of the part that matters."*

The crypt door wasn't locked, which told Xander something before he'd even gotten it open, and Buffy was sitting on the lower step near the sarcophagus with her knees pulled up, not crying exactly, just present in the hollowed-out way of someone who'd run out of the energy required to keep performing fine.

"Go away, Xander."

"Yeah, I figured you'd say that." He didn't go away. He sat down on the opposite step instead, close enough to be there, far enough not to crowd her. "Figured I'd stick around and be ignored for a while instead. See how that goes."

"It's going to go badly."

"Probably."

*"Here you are, letting it sit again,"* one last voice in the anonymous rows couldn't help pointing out, quiet, almost apologetic about breaking the vow of silence already. *"When's that phrase gonna learn to stand on its own two feet?"*

Nobody answered it. Nobody else spoke again either, true to what they'd promised.

She didn't say anything else for a long stretch, and Xander let the quiet hold, because pushing too hard, too fast, felt exactly like the wrong instrument for whatever this actually was. He could feel her walls, even without trying, a bristling quality, someone braced for an argument she'd already had a version of a dozen times that week, and his own first instinct, the easy joke, the thing that usually got him through moments like this, sat dead in his throat, wrong for once, too small for the size of what she was actually carrying.

*She's not defensive because she doesn't want to talk,* Coulson said, quiet, careful, the first real technical read he'd offered since they'd left the house. *She's defensive because every conversation she's had this week has been someone asking her to explain herself. If you ask her a question right now, any question, she's going to hear it as one more person needing something from her she doesn't have left to give.*

"So what do I do instead."

*Don't ask her anything. Just tell her something true, and don't wait for a response, and let her decide on her own time whether she wants to pick it up.*

Xander turned that over. It wasn't advice he'd have found on his own, not tonight, not with his own instincts running mostly on exhaustion and worry, and he felt the shape of it settle into something usable almost immediately, the timing and phrasing of it clicking into place in a way his own read on the room hadn't managed alone.

"The house is a mess," he said, out loud, not a question, not asking her to fix anything, not asking her for anything at all. "Robin's hurt bad. Faith's plan had a hole in it nobody caught in time, and I caught wind of the hole and didn't push hard enough to matter, which is on me, not her, not really. And everybody's scared, and nobody in that house right now actually knows what they're doing, including Faith, including me, including apparently everybody except one specific someone who used to be pretty good at knowing, even when she got it wrong."

Buffy didn't answer right away. But something in her posture shifted, fractionally, the walls not coming down exactly, just loosening enough at the edges to let a little more air through.

"I got people killed," she said, finally, quiet, raw in a way Xander hadn't heard from her in longer than he could remember. "I got people killed and everyone finally noticed, and I don't even know if they're wrong to be scared of trusting me again."

"Maybe they're not wrong to be scared," Xander said, and felt Coulson's quiet approval land somewhere underneath the words, confirmation he hadn't asked for and hadn't needed to. "Doesn't mean you're the wrong answer. Just means you're not the safe one. I don't actually think safe's on the table for any of us anymore, Buffy. I think it's just about who we trust to be right there in it with us when it isn't."

She looked at him properly for the first time since he'd come in, something cracking open behind her eyes that had been sealed shut since the porch.

"You came all the way out here to tell me that?"

"Came all the way out here 'cause the house is falling apart without you and I'm apparently too stubborn to just say that part first." He managed something close to a real smile, the first one in days that didn't feel like armor. "Also 'cause I'm pretty sure I know you well enough to know you needed somebody to just show up, not fix anything, not explain anything, just show up. So. Showed up."

Buffy was quiet for a long moment, and then, slowly, uncertainly, she unfolded from the step and reached a hand down toward him.

"Okay," she said. "Let's go see how bad it actually is."

*Well done,* Coulson said, quiet, as Xander took her hand and stood, and there was nothing performative in it, nothing that sounded like claiming credit for what had just happened. *That wasn't my read landing. That was yours, all the way through. I just helped you find the door.*

"Yeah," Xander said, mostly to himself, following Buffy out into the dark toward home. "Yeah. Turns out that's kind of the whole trick, isn't it."

Chapter 17: Reunified



The house didn't erupt into applause when Buffy walked back through the front door, which was, Xander thought, exactly right, a quiet, careful welcome that matched what the moment actually called for. Willow's shoulders dropped half an inch, relief written plain across her whole body. Giles's expression did something complicated and grateful behind his glasses. A few of the Potentials looked up, uncertain, watching to see how the room would decide to feel before committing to a feeling of their own.

Faith, to her credit, didn't fight it. She met Buffy's eyes across the crowded living room, something passing between them that didn't need words, an old rivalry finally, quietly, setting itself down in favor of something more useful, and stepped back without being asked to.

*"No power struggle,"* the voice from the gallery noted, sounding almost disappointed on principle. *"No dramatic standoff. Two rivals just... agreeing, like adults."*

*"Shocking, I know,"* someone in the anonymous rows said. *"Try to contain yourself."*

"Somebody want to catch me up?" Buffy said, into the room, and the house exhaled, all at once, and started talking.

Xander hung back near the doorway while the debrief unfolded around him, Robin's condition, the state of the defenses, the gap in the plan that had let the third wave through. He watched Buffy absorb it all with the same steady, careful attention he remembered from before the vineyard, asking the right questions, not flinching from the bad news, already, visibly, rebuilding the thing the house had needed all along and hadn't known how to name.

It wasn't his story to center himself in tonight. He'd done the part that mattered, out at the crypt, in the quiet, where nobody but Buffy had needed to see it.

*You did good tonight,* Coulson said, quiet, once the room's attention had moved elsewhere.

*We did good tonight,* Xander corrected, and felt the shape of the correction settle comfortably, no longer a point he needed to defend so much as a fact he'd finally stopped arguing with. *Your read got me to the right question. My read got us to the right door. Feels weird saying that without it turning into a whole thing.*

*It doesn't have to turn into a whole thing. Some nights, it can just be true.*

Xander was still turning that over, some quiet, unfamiliar warmth settling into a week that had mostly held very little of it, when he noticed Buffy watching him from across the room.

Not watching the way the others were, the general attention of people checking on a friend who'd had a hard week. Something narrower than that, her eyes tracking him with the same close focus she usually reserved for a threat she couldn't quite name yet, a wrongness her instincts had caught before her conscious mind had finished cataloging it.

She didn't say anything. She held his gaze a beat too long, long enough that Xander felt it land somewhere uncomfortable, and then, deliberately, let it go, turning back to whatever Giles was explaining about the defensive perimeter, filing whatever she'd noticed away for later without pushing it into the open tonight.

*She saw something,* Coulson said, low, careful.

*"Uh oh,"* murmured a voice in the anonymous rows, all the earlier lightness draining out of it fast. *"Slayer senses. That's not a plot thread, that's a loaded gun on the mantelpiece."*

*Yeah,* Xander thought back, feeling the unease of it settle slow into his chest. *She did.*

It made a kind of sense he didn't love. Buffy had history with exactly this shape of thing, a whole catalogue of it, voices that weren't voices, faces that weren't quite the right faces, the violation of something wearing the shell of someone she loved and finding out too late it wasn't really them at all. If anyone in this house was built to feel the uncanny valley of a second consciousness riding along behind a familiar face, it was going to be her, Slayer instinct sharpened by exactly the wrong kind of firsthand experience, and Xander understood, watching her deliberately not press the question tonight, that this wasn't the end of it. Just a pause. A thread left hanging, on purpose, for a later night that hadn't arrived yet.

*You going to tell her,* Coulson asked, eventually.

*Not tonight.* Xander watched Buffy across the room, steady again, back in the rhythm of running a debrief like she'd never left it, and felt something protective rise up around the thought of explaining any of this to her right now, on top of everything else she was already carrying. *Not while she's got a house full of scared girls and a hole in the ceiling to deal with. Whenever that conversation happens, it deserves better timing than tonight.*

*Fair enough.*

The debrief wound down eventually, the house settling into its familiar late-night murmur, chores and watches divided up, the grim, unglamorous business of tending to two dead girls and one badly wounded man folded quietly into the same evening as everyone's relief at having their general back. Xander found a quiet corner of the porch to let the last of it settle, the argument that had followed him since the vineyard finally, for the first time, feeling less like a war either of them needed to win.

*Can I say something,* Coulson asked, and there was something almost careful in the question, unusual for him.

*Go for it.*

*I still don't think either one of us was fully right, back in the kitchen, that first night. About whose seeing counts more.* A pause. *I think we were both partly right, and I think tonight proved it better than either of us arguing it out ever could have. Your read got us the door. Mine got you to ask the right question. Neither one worked without the other.*

*You saying that 'cause you actually believe it, or 'cause you're trying to be nice after I spent a week being a stubborn ass about it?*

*Both, possibly. Mostly the first one.*

*"He has a hobby. It's meticulous records."*

*"...Fair."*

*"Maybe somebody needs to trim it,"* another voice offered, deadpan.

*"...Ba-dum-tss."*

Xander let out something that was almost a laugh, real, unguarded, the first one that had felt entirely earned in longer than he wanted to admit. "Yeah," he said, quiet, to the dark yard, to the house full of people rebuilding itself one uneasy night at a time behind him. "Yeah, okay. Both were partly right. I can live with that."

*Good,* Coulson said. *Because I don't think either of us is going to stop believing our own half of it entirely. I don't think we're supposed to.*

"No," Xander agreed, watching the last light go out in an upstairs window, the house finally, finally settling toward something like rest. "Probably not."

It wasn't peace, not exactly, not with a war still very much ahead of them and a Slayer across the house quietly holding a question she hadn't asked yet. But it was, for the first time since a man in a green waistcoat had rearranged both their lives without asking permission from either of them, something real enough to build on.

Chapter 18: Dawn



He found her on the back steps one evening, during that quiet stretch of weeks after Buffy's return, picking at the frayed edge of her jeans, restless in the way of someone who'd been sitting with an uncomfortable thought for longer than she wanted to admit.

"Hey, Dawnie." Xander settled beside her, careful, casual. "You've been quiet lately. Quieter than usual, I mean, which is saying something given how loud this house normally is."

*"Radical,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, entirely deadpan.

"I'm fine." The old, automatic deflection, the kind every teenager in this house had learned to reach for by instinct. Then, after a moment, more honest: "Okay, not fine. Just, kind of invisible, I guess. Everyone else in this house has a whole superpowered destiny thing going on. I'm just Buffy's sister. The one without the special blood anymore, without the Key thing mattering the way it used to. Just, regular Dawn."

"Hey." Xander bumped her shoulder gently with his own. "Regular Dawn saved this whole town more than once with nothing but her own brain and her own nerve. Don't let anybody, including yourself, forget that part."

*"And it's pep-talk-boy to the rescue,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, warm despite the ribbing.

"Doesn't feel like nerve. Feels like standing on the sidelines while everyone else does the actual fighting."

"Yeah, well." Xander turned that over, recognizing the shape of it more intimately than she probably realized. "I know a little something about standing on the sidelines while everyone else has the flashy superpowers. Spent most of my life feeling exactly like that, actually."

Dawn glanced at him, surprised. "You? You fight right alongside everybody. You've got the axe and everything."

"Now, yeah. Took a long time to get here, though. A lot of years feeling like the extra guy nobody quite needed, tagging along because I refused to leave, not because anybody actually thought I mattered." He watched the evening settle gold and quiet over the yard. "Here's the thing I figured out, though, eventually. Mattering doesn't have to look like the flashy stuff. Doesn't have to be a punch or a spell or superhuman strength. Sometimes it's just being the person who shows up, who pays attention, who's actually there when someone needs somebody steady. That's not sidelines, Dawn. That's the actual foundation everything else gets built on."

"You really believe that?"

"Took me way too long to believe it about myself. But yeah. I really do."

"Can I tell you something kind of embarrassing?" Dawn said, after a moment.

*"Every single scene on this porch starts with somebody asking permission to be honest,"* the gallery's voice noted, not unkindly. *"This narrator finds that charming and slightly tragic in equal measure."*

"Always."

"Sometimes I miss being the Key. Isn't that awful? Like, actively dangerous mystical energy that could end the world, and I miss it, because at least it meant something. At least people needed to protect me for a reason bigger than just, you know, being somebody's kid sister."

*"Oof,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, quiet. *"Nobody laugh at that one. That one's real."*

"That's not awful. That's just honest." Xander turned to look at her properly. "But here's the thing, Dawnie. Being needed for a mystical emergency isn't the same as mattering. You matter 'cause of who you actually are, not 'cause of some cosmic accident of birth. The Key stuff, that was never really you. This is you. The girl who's smart and stubborn and cares enough about this whole mess of a family to still be sitting out here worrying about her place in it."

"You really believe that."

"I really do."

Dawn was quiet a while, turning that over, and something in her posture slowly eased, the tight, defensive set of her shoulders loosening by degrees.

"You know," she said, eventually, "you've gotten different this year. Wiser, maybe. Or just, I don't know. Steadier. Is that from losing the eye?"

Xander felt the moment brush up against a secret he wasn't ready to hand her yet, not tonight, not without more thought about whether it was even his to give, and found a true answer that didn't require the whole truth to be honest.

"Losing the eye taught me a lot," he said, carefully. "About what actually matters versus what I thought mattered. Guess some of that's bound to show, even if I can't always explain the whole how of it."

"Cryptic. I like it. Very mysterious older-brother-figure energy."

*"'Mysterious older-brother-figure energy,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, delighted. *"She's not wrong. Somebody put that on a business card."*

*"He'd need a business."*

*"Give him time."*

Dawn managed a real smile, finally, warm and unguarded. "Thanks for this, by the way. The pep talk. I needed it more than I wanted to admit."

"Anytime, Dawnie. That's kind of my whole deal these days."

She leaned her head briefly against his shoulder, quick and affectionate, and Xander sat with her a while longer in the gathering dark, watching the house's lights come on one by one behind them, grateful, in a way he didn't often let himself fully feel, for a girl who'd grown up faster and harder than anyone her age should have to, and had somehow, despite all of it, held onto a heart generous enough to still worry about whether she mattered.

*You're good at that,* Coulson observed, quiet, once Dawn had gone back inside.

*Yeah, well.* Xander watched the last of the daylight fade. *Somebody's gotta remind the overlooked ones they're not actually invisible. Figure I'm about as qualified for that job as anybody in this house.*

*I'd agree with that assessment,* Coulson said, warm. *Wholeheartedly.*

Chapter 19: The Weeks Between



Robin Wood's recovery became the house's unofficial clock for a while, a slow, steady metric everyone tracked without quite admitting they were doing it. First he could sit up. Then he could stand, briefly, leaning hard on whoever happened to be nearest. Then, three weeks after the Bringers' attack, he made it down the stairs under his own power, and the whole house held its breath through every step like his legs were a shared, fragile hope none of them wanted to jinx by celebrating too soon.

*"Recovery arc, and unless this narrator is very much mistaken, the beginnings of a subplot,"* the voice from the gallery said, settling in with real interest. *"Watch the eyeline. Watch it very closely."*

*"You say that about every two characters standing near each other,"* someone in the anonymous rows muttered.

*"And I am rarely wrong."*

"You're supposed to be resting," Faith told him, the day he insisted on sitting in on a strategy session instead of staying in bed.

"I've been resting for three weeks. I'm going to lose my mind before I lose the wound." Robin settled carefully into a chair, wincing only a little. "Besides, somebody around here should know how the actual Council used to run these things, before it was mostly improvisation and hoping for the best."

"Improvisation and hoping for the best's gotten us this far," Faith said, but there wasn't any real edge in it, and Xander, watching from across the room, caught something new in the exchange, an easy familiarity that hadn't existed between the two of them before the attack, forged in the shared near-catastrophe that either broke people apart or welded them together with no in-between.

*They're good for each other,* Coulson observed, quiet.

*"TOLD you,"* the gallery's voice crowed, smug beyond all reasonable proportion. *"Watch the eyeline, I said. Nobody listens to this narrator."*

*"We're listening now, congratulations, you get a medal."*

*Yeah.* Xander watched Faith lean over Robin's shoulder to look at whatever notes he was making, something almost gentle in how carefully she avoided jostling his still-healing side. *Didn't see that coming, honestly. Guess almost dying together does that to people.*

*It has a way of clarifying things, yes.*

The house found its new rhythm slowly, over those weeks, Buffy and Faith settling into something that wasn't quite the old dynamic and wasn't quite a new one either, a genuine partnership built on hard-won mutual respect rather than rivalry. Buffy ran strategy. Faith ran the physical training, the sparring, the practical business of turning thirty newly-Called girls into something that could actually survive what was coming. Giles researched, endlessly, patiently, piecing together everything he could find about the First Evil's endgame from sources that had never expected to be consulted about an apocalypse this size. Willow, growing stronger and steadier by the day, began quietly, carefully, preparing the spell that would eventually change everything.

Xander found his own rhythm too, somewhere in the gaps between all of it, the same unglamorous, essential work he'd always done, checking in on the girls nobody else had time to check in on, running supply logistics, being the calm, steady presence a house full of frightened people needed more than they needed another general.

*You've settled into something,* Coulson observed, one quiet evening, watching Xander patch a stake-throwing form some girl had been struggling with for days.

*Yeah?*

*You're not performing competence anymore. You're just being competent. There's a difference, and it's a significant one.*

*"Somebody frame that one too,"* someone in the anonymous rows said. *"We're running out of wall space in this narrator's head, but frame it anyway."*

*When'd that happen?*

*I'm not entirely sure. Sometime in the last few weeks, I think. It's been gradual enough that I'm not sure either of us would have noticed it happening in real time.*

Xander turned that over, watching the girl finally land three throws in a row, her whole face lighting up with the pride of someone who'd worked hard for something and finally gotten it. It felt true, the observation, in a way that surprised him a little, some old, anxious part of himself that had spent years needing to prove its worth loudly, constantly, finally quieting down into something steadier.

*"And here we go with this again,"* the gallery's voice said, wary. *"'With the [feeling] of someone who'd [thing].' I'm starting to feel like one of those buzzards in* The Jungle Book. *Say it with me next time and we'll all four finish the sentence together."*

*"Next thing you know he'll be talking in a posh British accent,"* someone in the anonymous rows predicted, darkly.

*"Don't tempt this narrator."*

*Guess I stopped needing to convince myself,* he said, eventually. *Somewhere between the vineyard and now. Don't know exactly when.*

*I think it happened in pieces,* Coulson said. *The way most real changes do.*

They had one real scare, in the middle of those quiet weeks, a Bringer scouting party spotted at the edge of the property one night, three of them, testing the house's defenses in a way that suggested Caleb, or whatever remained of the First Evil's coordination, hadn't given up on Sunnydale entirely.

It was over almost before it started, this time, the house's new rhythm proving itself in real time: Faith and two Slayers intercepting the scouts before they got anywhere near the actual perimeter, clean, efficient, no chaos, no scrambling, no gap in the plan for anyone to fall through. Xander watched it from the porch, axe in hand but never needed, and felt something settle in his chest that had nothing to do with relief and everything to do with quiet, hard-won pride.

*That's what it's supposed to look like,* Coulson said, watching Faith and the others return, unhurt, business handled with the calm efficiency of people who'd actually learned from their mistakes. *No wasted motion. No panic. Just competence, applied correctly, the first time.*

*Feels good,* Xander admitted. *Watching it work right, for once.*

*It should. You had a hand in building it, even if nobody's going to stand up and say so out loud.*

*Don't need them to.* Xander watched the house settle back into its evening routine, the scare already fading into just another night's business, handled and done. *Turns out that's the whole thing I've been learning all year. Doesn't have to be loud to matter.*

*"And that's the part nobody can teach you,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, no dryness in it at all. *"Kid either gets there himself or he doesn't."*

The weeks kept moving, the house growing steadier, more prepared, more genuinely ready than it had ever been before, right up until the night Willow finally judged the moment ready, the scythe finally glowing gold in her hands, and the whole quiet, careful preparation of those in-between weeks tipped, all at once, into the last night any of them would spend as anything other than what they were about to become.

Chapter 20: Full Circle



*"A callback,"* the voice from the gallery said, pleased, the plain pleasure of a man watching a screenwriter actually do their homework. *"This narrator appreciates a production that remembers its own cast list."*

He found Molly in the training yard, running through the same defensive drills she'd been running for weeks, her form sharper now than it had been at the vineyard, more confident, the fear that had once lived in every line of her body replaced by something steadier, hard-won.

"You've gotten good," Xander said, watching her land a clean series of strikes against the practice dummy.

Molly glanced over, wiping sweat from her forehead. "Had a good teacher. Popsicle sticks and everything."

"Hey, that model held up under real pressure. I stand by the shoebox lid."

*"The shoebox lid,"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, warm. *"Chapter three's plan finally getting its due credit. This narrator's been waiting for this."*

She managed a real laugh, the first genuinely light sound he'd heard from her in longer than he wanted to admit, and joined him on the bench at the yard's edge, breathing hard from the drills.

"Can I ask you something?" Molly said, eventually, turning serious. "About the vineyard. About after."

"Yeah, go ahead."

"I've felt guilty. This whole time. For running when you told me to. For not looking back, not trying to help you." She picked at the edge of her sleeve, an old, nervous habit. "I know you told me to go. I know it was the plan. Doesn't mean the guilt actually listened to the plan."

Something in Xander's chest ached, hearing it, recognizing the shape of a burden he understood better than she probably realized.

"Hey. Look at me." He waited until she did. "You did exactly what you were supposed to do. You survived. That wasn't cowardice, Molly, that was the whole point of every hour I spent walking you through that plan. "Doesn't make the guilt go away."

*Tell her about the mother,* Coulson said, quiet, unexpected. *Not the specifics. Just the shape of it. I think it might actually help her more than more reassurance would.*

Xander considered that, and found, turning it over, that he agreed. "Can I tell you something? Something that happened out there that I haven't told anybody yet."

Molly looked up, curious. "Yeah, of course."

"I've had guilt like yours more than once since the vineyard. Had a moment, actually, where I had to make somebody else's mother believe I was doing right by her kid, even though every part of the exchange felt wrong on the surface. "Only thing that's ever actually helped me is what I just told you. Carrying both. The guilt and the knowing you did right. Not picking one to be true."

"That actually helps," Molly said, quiet. "Knowing it's not just me, I mean. Knowing you carry it too, even now, even after everything you've figured out since."

"Yeah. Guess neither of us gets to graduate out of carrying it. Just gets more familiar, the longer you practice."

"No," Xander agreed, honest. "It doesn't. Guilt's stubborn like that. Doesn't care how logical the reasons were." He bumped her shoulder gently. "But you can carry the guilt and still know, underneath it, that you did the right thing. Those aren't opposites. I've had a lot of practice lately, carrying two true things that don't cancel each other out."

Molly turned that over, something in her posture slowly easing. "How do you do it? Carry stuff like that and still, I don't know, function. Still show up every day like it's not eating you alive underneath."

"Practice, mostly. And having people around who remind you the guilt doesn't get to be the only true thing about you." He glanced sideways at her. "You've got people like that now too, you know. This whole house. Me, if you ever need it."

*"And now it's shoulder-boy to the rescue,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, gentle about it, no mockery in it at all. *"Somebody give pep-talk-boy a partner credit."*

"Yeah." Molly managed a small, grateful smile. "Yeah, I know. Thanks, Xander. For everything, actually. The vineyard, the training, all of it. I don't think I've said that properly."

"Didn't need you to. But I'll take it anyway."

They sat together a while longer, comfortable, the training yard's evening quiet settling around them, and Xander felt a fierce, unexpected pride watching the girl beside him, the same terrified sixteen-year-old from the vineyard now standing steady on her own two feet, ready, as ready as anyone could be, for whatever tomorrow's battle actually held.

*She's come a long way,* Coulson observed, quiet, genuine warmth in it.

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, watching her stretch, preparing for one more round of drills before the light fully failed. *Yeah, she really has. Guess that's the whole job, right? Not just getting people through the worst night. Sticking around long enough to watch 'em actually become who they're supposed to be after.*

*I think that might be the truest thing you've said all week.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander stood, offering Molly a hand up for one more round. *Had a whole year of practice figuring that part out too.*

Chapter 21: The Morning Of



The last ordinary morning began quiet, the house waking slowly around a day everyone understood, without needing to say it aloud, was going to be the last one like it for a long while, one way or another.

Even the anonymous rows, restless as they usually were, seemed content to just watch for a while.

Xander found Robin Wood on the porch first, still moving carefully, still favoring the side that hadn't fully healed, watching the sunrise still, the way a man did when he was making his peace with whatever the day held.

"You don't have to fight today," Xander said, settling beside him. "Nobody would think less of you, sitting this one out."

"I know." Robin didn't look away from the horizon. "I'm fighting anyway. Some things you don't sit out, whatever the cost."

"Yeah." Xander understood that better than he might have a year ago. "Yeah, I get that."

He found Willow next, in the makeshift ritual space she'd assembled in the basement, running through the spell's components one final time with the meticulous, anxious care of someone who understood, fully, the weight of what she was about to attempt.

"You ready?" he asked, quiet, from the doorway.

"No." She looked up, something raw and honest in her expression. "I don't think 'ready' is actually available today. I've done the preparation. I've checked everything six times. But ready implies some kind of certainty I just don't have, Xander. I'm about to change what it means to be a Slayer, permanently, for every girl on the planet who could be one, and there's no version of that where I get to feel fully certain first."

"For what it's worth, I trust you completely. Whether that helps or not."

"It helps." She managed a small, tired smile. "It actually helps a lot."

He found Buffy last, alone in what had briefly been her room, checking weapons with the same careful, exhausted focus she'd brought to every crisis this whole terrible year.

"Hey," Xander said, leaning in the doorway.

"Hey yourself." She didn't look up from the sword she was checking. "You holding together?"

"As well as anyone can be, I think. You?"

"Ask me tonight." She set the sword down, finally, and looked at him properly, something searching and warm in her expression. "I meant to ask you something, before all this. About the thing you're carrying. The passenger."

"Yeah, I figured you hadn't forgotten."

"I haven't." She studied him a long moment. "I don't need the whole explanation right now. Today's not the day for it. I just wanted you to know, before whatever happens today happens, that I see it. Whatever it is. I trust you to have made the right call, letting it happen, whatever it turns out to actually be."

Something in Xander's chest went tight and grateful at once. "Thanks. That actually means more than you know."

"We'll talk properly. After." Buffy squeezed his shoulder, brief and warm. "I promise."

*"Well, THAT'S different,"* someone in the anonymous rows murmured, quiet, remembering the same watchful look from a few chapters back. Nobody pushed the observation any further than that.

*She's a good friend,* Coulson observed, quiet, once Xander had stepped back out into the hallway.

*Yeah,* Xander agreed. *Best one I've got, probably. Present company excluded, obviously.*

*Obviously.*

The house gathered slowly through the morning, thirty-some Potentials and the handful of adults who'd carried them this far, breakfast eaten in a quiet that had nothing defeated in it, only the focus of people preparing themselves properly for something enormous. Xander moved through it the way he'd learned to move through crises all year, checking in, steadying, being the calm, patient presence a frightened house needed more than one more general barking orders.

He found Molly last, of all people, sitting alone near the kitchen, running her thumb along the edge of a blade with the same nervous energy she'd shown at the vineyard months ago.

"Hey," Xander said, sitting beside her. "How you holding up?"

"Scared." She didn't try to hide it, which felt like its own kind of progress from the girl he'd met at the vineyard. "Excited too, though. Is that weird?"

"Not weird at all. Sounds about right, actually." He bumped her shoulder gently with his own. "You've come a long way since the vineyard, you know that? Whatever happens today, I want you to know I'm proud of who you've turned into."

Molly's eyes went bright, unexpectedly emotional. "Thanks, Xander. That means a lot, coming from you."

"Good. Meant it to."

*You're good at this,* Coulson observed, quiet, watching the whole morning unfold, one small, steadying conversation after another, each one its own small gift given freely before a day that might not leave room for many more of them.

*Yeah, well.* Xander watched Molly rejoin the others, steadier now, some of the fear settled into something more manageable. *Turns out that's the whole job, right? Making sure everybody knows they mattered, whatever happens next.*

*I think that might be the truest thing you've ever said.*

The morning wound down toward the moment Willow had been preparing for, the whole house gathering, slowly, into the basement where the spell would finally be cast, and Xander walked in among them, carrying with him every quiet conversation of the morning, every small, steadying gift exchanged before whatever came next, ready, as ready as anyone could be, for the last night of the world as they'd all known it.

Chapter 22: The Spell



The days between Buffy's return and the night everything finally came to a head moved fast and strange, the house pulling itself together with the urgency of people who knew, without anyone saying it out loud, that whatever came next was going to be the last thing, one way or another. Robin Wood healed enough to stand, slowly, leaning on whoever happened to be nearest. Faith and Buffy found something that wasn't quite friendship but functioned well enough in its place, a working truce built out of necessity and, underneath that, something like real respect. The Potentials trained harder, ate less, slept worse, and none of them complained about any of it, because complaining had stopped feeling like it mattered next to what was actually coming.

Xander spent the days doing what he'd always done, quietly, unglamorously, checking in on the ones who needed checking in on, running supply counts nobody else wanted to run, being the steady presence in a house that had learned, the hard way, how much it needed one. Coulson stayed close through most of it, present in the small unremarked way their partnership had settled into, offering a read here, a question there, mostly just company through the waiting.

*"Hang on,"* said the voice from the gallery, thoughtful, a man checking his own math. *"Has anyone actually heard one of those in a while? 'The kind of.'"*

A pause, the anonymous watchers visibly conferring with each other in the low, rustling way a crowd did when nobody wanted to be the one who'd actually kept count.

*"Not for several chapters, now that you mention it,"* someone finally offered.

*"There's another one,"* somebody in the anonymous rows said, quiet, almost to themselves. *"'The way rooms did right before something irreversible happened.' Word for word, practically. Chapter eight had that exact sentence."*

*"Good ear,"* Fury's voice said, dry. *"Somebody's paying closer attention than the author was."*

Willow stood in the middle of the basement, the night it finally came, with the scythe held flat across both palms, and the whole house had gone still around her, the same stillness that had settled over a different room weeks ago, only this time, for once, it was the good kind of irreversible.

"Once this starts," she said, and her voice had a steadiness to it Xander hadn't heard from her in longer than he wanted to think about, "every girl who could be a Slayer will be one. Here, and everywhere. There's no undoing it after. I need everyone to be sure."

Nobody said anything for a moment. Then, one by one, quiet nods, the whole house's fear finally finding somewhere useful to point itself.

*This is the part where the actual war starts,* Coulson said, quiet, present in a way that felt more like company than commentary tonight. *I've sat through a lot of pre-mission briefings. This one's better than most of them. Nobody in this room is pretending it's going to be easy.*

*Yeah, well,* Xander thought, checking the axe he'd carried since the vineyard, the weight of it familiar now in a way it hadn't been a few weeks ago, *we don't really do the pretending thing 'round here. Learned that lesson kind of recently.*

*I noticed.*

Buffy moved through the room while Willow gathered herself for the spell, checking in with each cluster of girls in turn, steady and present in the way that had made Xander walk two miles to a crypt to bring her back. She stopped by him last.

"You good?"

"As good as a one-eyed guy with a fire axe gets, probably." He managed something close to his old grin. "You?"

"Ask me tomorrow." She held his gaze a beat, something searching in it that Xander recognized now, the same quiet, careful attention she'd turned on him the night she came back, and for a second he thought she might finally say something about it. She didn't. Whatever question she was carrying stayed folded up behind her eyes, saved for later, and she squeezed his shoulder instead and moved on to the next cluster of nervous faces needing a word before the end of the world.

*She's still watching you,* Coulson observed.

*Yeah.* Xander watched her go, steady, sure, exactly the person this house needed leading it into whatever came next. *She'll ask when she asks. Not tonight.*

Willow began the incantation, low at first, then rising, the scythe glowing faint and gold in her hands, and somewhere out past the walls of the house, out past the whole town, girls who had never held a weapon in their lives felt something ancient and waiting finally answer a call that had been ringing, unheard, for the length of human history.

Xander felt the change ripple through the room like weather, felt three dozen Potentials straighten, all at once, into something the word Potential had never quite managed to hold.

*That,* Coulson said, quiet, something like genuine awe underneath the professional composure, *is not something I have a comparison for. I've seen a great deal in twenty years. I don't think I've ever seen anything do that.*

*"First time for everybody,"* the gallery's voice said, and there was no dryness in it at all, just something close to wonder. *"This narrator's sat through a lot of these. That one still got me."*

*Welcome to Tuesday,* Xander said, though it wasn't Tuesday, though nothing about tonight felt remotely like an ordinary day of the week. *Buffy world runs on a different scale than your world, I think.*

*Apparently.*

*"'Welcome to Tuesday,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, half a laugh escaping despite the moment. *"Global mystical empowerment event, and the local guy's still doing the bit. I respect the commitment."*

The spell settled, the gold light fading from the scythe, and the room that had held thirty scared girls a minute ago now held thirty Slayers, uncertain still, shaking still, but standing straighter than fear alone had let them stand in weeks.

"Okay," Buffy said, into the sudden, charged quiet, her voice carrying with an authority that didn't need volume to land. "Everybody knows the plan. We go in, we close the Hellmouth, we come home. All of us, if I have any say in it." A beat, and something almost gentle crept through the command in her voice. "Let's go show the First Evil what a house full of somebodies looks like."

Nobody cheered. It wasn't that kind of moment. But the room moved as one, gathering weapons, gathering courage, gathering whatever last reserves of steadiness each of them had left, and Xander fell into step beside people he'd known for years and people he'd known for days, feeling the axe settle familiar in his grip, feeling something behind his ribs that wasn't quite calm but was, at least, no longer only fear.

*Ready?* Coulson asked, as the house emptied out into the dark toward the school, toward whatever waited under it.

*No,* Xander said, honest, the old joke nowhere close to reach tonight. *But that's never really stopped me before.*

*No,* Coulson agreed, something warm and steady underneath the single word. *I don't imagine it has.*

They walked together, the whole house moving as one dark, determined shape through the quiet streets, toward the last night either of them could have imagined, when this had all started, as anything but the end of something enormous.

Chapter 23: What Xander Sees



*"They rebuilt the school. On the Hellmouth. Again,"* the voice from the gallery said, weary in the way of a man who had said this before and expected to say it again. *"This narrator has questions about the municipal permitting process in this town."*

*"Nobody in this story has ever once had questions about the municipal permitting process,"* someone in the anonymous rows pointed out. *"That's sort of the whole joke."*

The school had never looked right, even before tonight, a building that had burned once already and been rebuilt on top of the same ancient wrongness like nobody involved had thought that through very carefully. Tonight it looked like the mouth of something, which, Xander supposed, was more or less accurate, and the basement beneath it had opened into a scene out of a nightmare nobody had bothered warning them about, a churning dark well pouring out Turok-Han faster than the line of newly-minted Slayers could hold them back.

The first ten minutes were pure chaos, no room in them for anything but the next swing, the next step, the next scream that needed answering. Xander lost track of everyone around him almost immediately, caught glimpses only, Buffy cutting a path near the center of the fray, Faith and Robin Wood holding a flank together with the easy coordination of two people who'd finally stopped needing to prove anything to each other, Willow's voice somewhere behind them all, steady, holding something together that Xander didn't have the attention to spare on understanding.

Somewhere in the middle of it, wearing an amulet that glowed faint and terrible against his chest, Spike stood his ground and looked, for just a fraction of a second, like a man already saying goodbye.

Xander caught it in passing, mid-swing, the axe biting into something that shrieked and folded, and filed it away the way he filed everything these days, half-conscious, automatic, a habit built over years of watching people's faces for the gap between what they were saying and what they actually meant.

He didn't have time to think about it. The battle didn't leave room for thinking, only reacting, and it was a good ten more minutes before the tide shifted enough for Xander to catch a real breath, to look up and actually find Spike again in the chaos.

What he saw made his stomach drop.

Spike was fighting, still fighting, the amulet's light building steadily brighter against his chest, but something in the set of his shoulders had gone wrong, a hesitation creeping into moves that should have been automatic after a century of practice, and Xander, who had spent years hating this man with a thoroughness that came from genuine, hard-earned dislike, recognized the shape of it instantly, recognized it precisely because of all that history, not despite it.

*He's about to break,* Xander thought, and felt the certainty of it land with none of the softness that usually came with concern for someone he actually liked.

*I don't see it,* Coulson said, urgent, scanning what he could through Xander's eyes. *He looks steady to me. Fighting well. What am I missing?*

*You're missing years of me watching that exact guy talk a big game right up until the second he actually believes it himself.* Xander swung again, caught another Turok-Han across the throat, kept moving toward Spike through the chaos. *That's not steady. That's a man about to convince himself he's not actually gonna make it, and once he believes that, he's done, doesn't matter how bright that amulet gets.*

*Buffy's closer. She'd know him better than either of us.*

*Buffy's got a hundred things to watch right now. I'm not sure she's got the specific angle on him I've got.* Xander cut through two more feet of distance, close enough now to see Spike's jaw working, close enough to see a flicker in his eyes that Xander recognized from a hundred old arguments, the exact look of a man talking himself out of believing his own nerve. *Trust me on this one. I've spent years learning exactly what this guy's bravado is covering. Turns out being somebody's least favorite person for that long teaches you things nobody warm and fuzzy would ever bother learning.*

He reached Spike's side, planted himself there, axe still moving, still fighting, and didn't waste time on a gentle approach, because gentle had never once worked on this particular vampire and wasn't going to start now.

"You're about to fold," Xander said, flat, no softness in it at all. "Don't."

"Don't know what you're on about, whelp." Spike's voice had an edge in it that confirmed everything Xander had already read, a fraction too fast, a fraction too sharp. "Bit busy at the moment."

"You're standing there with a magic rock lighting up your chest telling yourself you're gonna burn to nothing and it won't have meant a thing." Xander swung, caught a Turok-Han reaching for Spike's blind side, kept talking without breaking rhythm. "That's what this is. I've watched you talk yourself into every stupid, self-destructive decision you've ever made for years, Spike. I know exactly what it looks like right before you convince yourself the worst version of the story's the true one."

"And what, you're gonna talk me out of it? You?" Something bitter and desperate cracked through the bravado. "Bloody hell, Harris, out of everyone in this fight, you're the last one I'd have picked to..."

"Yeah, well, lucky you, you got me anyway." Xander didn't soften it, didn't reach for comfort, because comfort had never once landed with Spike and wouldn't land now, not in the middle of a war, not from a man who'd spent years making it very clear he had no interest in Xander's approval or affection. "You wanna know what I actually think? I think you've spent a hundred years telling yourself you're not worth saving, and I think some sick part of you actually wants this to be the thing that finally proves it. Burn up, be the hero, get to stop trying. That's not sacrifice, Spike. That's just giving up with better lighting."

Something in Spike's face cracked open, raw and furious and, underneath both, terrified.

"That's not..."

"It is, though. I know it is, 'cause I've spent years watching you do exactly this, smaller versions of it, every single time you decided you weren't worth the effort of trying properly." Xander swung again, felt his arm screaming with exhaustion, kept his eyes on Spike's face. "So here's the deal. You don't get to burn out just to prove yourself right about being worthless. You hold this line 'cause we need you to hold it, not 'cause it finally lets you stop existing. There's a difference, and you know there's a difference, and I need you to actually pick the right one, right now, 'cause I am not explaining to Buffy that you checked out early."

For one long, terrible second, Spike didn't move at all, the amulet's light building higher, the battle roaring around both of them, and Xander held his ground, held the eye contact, refused to let the moment soften into something kinder than it needed to be.

Then something shifted, hard and sudden, behind Spike's eyes, the bravado settling back into place, except this time with something steadier underneath it, something that had actually chosen to be there instead of merely performing it.

"You're a right bastard, you know that?" Spike said, and there was something almost like a laugh buried under the words.

"Yeah. Been told." Xander managed a grin, real, exhausted, entirely earned. "Now hold the damn line."

Spike held it.

The amulet's light built and built, the Turok-Han army faltering as something ancient and terrible answered its call, and Spike stood his ground at the center of it, steady now, chosen now, that hesitation gone from his shoulders like it had never been there at all.

---

The fight around them hadn't paused for any of it, and Xander turned back into the chaos the second Spike's line held, catching a Turok-Han across the knees, dragging a fallen Potential clear of a second one before it could finish what it started, the next thirty seconds and the thirty after that swallowing whatever quiet had briefly opened up between him and Spike.

It was minutes later, the tide of the whole impossible battle finally, unmistakably beginning to turn, before Coulson spoke again.

*I couldn't have done that,* Coulson said, quiet, once the immediate chaos of the fight had carried them a few steps further, a few more Turok-Han down. *I want you to understand that clearly. No training I have covers that. I've never once in my career had to read a hundred-year-old vampire's internal despair through his fighting stance, and I have no framework for verifying you were even right. For all I know, you talked a man into holding a line he was always going to hold anyway.*

*Maybe,* Xander admitted, breathing hard, watching Spike fight on, steady, sure. *I don't have proof either. I just know what I saw, and I trusted it.*

*I know,* Coulson said. *And I backed you on it anyway. Completely, without a single piece of verification I could point to. I want to be honest about what that means, because I don't think I've done that before, not like this, not with something I had absolutely no way to confirm.*

*What's it mean?*

*It means I trust you,* Coulson said, simply, no dryness left in it at all, nothing held back. *Not your training, because you don't have any I recognize. Not a shared method, because we don't have one. Just you. I think that might actually be the answer to the thing we've been arguing about since the kitchen table, and I think I only just now understood it properly.*

Nobody in the anonymous rows said anything. This narrator suspects, strongly, that this was the whole point of the argument all along, and doesn't intend to spoil it with a punchline now that it's finally landed.

Xander felt something settle in his chest, solid, earned, entirely unlike anything the last several weeks of argument had managed to produce between them.

*"Alright,"* said one voice in the anonymous rows, affecting, for no reason anyone could identify, a posh British accent, *"now don't start that again."*

Nobody asked what "that" was. Everybody knew exactly what "that" was.

"Yeah," he said, out loud, quiet, watching Spike's light building toward whatever it was about to become, watching the tide of the whole impossible battle finally begin to turn. "Yeah. I think that's it too."

Neither of them said anything else about it. There wasn't anything left to argue, not about this, not anymore, and somewhere in the settling quiet underneath the ongoing roar of the fight, Xander understood that whatever came next, whatever this partnership turned into once the dust finally settled, it wasn't going to need the argument anymore to hold itself together.

It had found something sturdier than that to stand on instead.

Chapter 24: What's Left



The tide turned, and then it kept turning, and somewhere in the middle of that slow, grinding shift from losing to maybe-not-losing, Xander lost track of where anyone actually was.

That was the thing about a battle this size, he was learning, in real time, in the worst possible classroom. It didn't hold still long enough to be understood. It was a hundred small fights happening at once, each one its own entire war with its own entire stakes, and a person could only ever really live inside the one directly in front of them, trusting, with no way to verify it, that the others were going somewhere survivable.

He heard Anya's voice before he found her, sharp and furious, cutting through the chaos with the same blend of terror and stubbornness that had carried her through worse fights than this one. She was holding a line near the gymnasium doors with two Slayers barely a week into their power, directing them with the brisk, impatient competence of a woman who had spent a thousand years learning how to survive and had no intention of losing that education now, tonight, to a bunch of ancient, eyeless things that smelled like the inside of the earth.

*She's doing well,* Coulson observed, some part of his attention tracking the fight even through everything else demanding it. *Genuinely well. Whatever she was before this, she's earned her place in this fight tonight.*

*She's terrifying and she knows exactly what she's doing,* Xander thought back, cutting down a Turok-Han that had gotten too close, *and she's gonna be insufferable about it for weeks if we all make it out of here.*

He never got to find out if she would have been.

The gallery said nothing. Not even the anonymous rows, ordinarily quick with something to fill a silence, offered so much as a rustle. This narrator will not be riffing on this scene, tonight or on any future viewing.

He didn't see it happen. That was, later, one of the small cruelties he carried out of that night, that the moment itself came to him secondhand, relayed in fragments by people who had been closer, who had watched a section of ceiling give way at exactly the wrong angle, exactly the wrong second, burying her under rubble that hadn't given anyone, not her, not the girls she'd been protecting, any warning at all.

He felt it differently. He felt the fight around him change shape, felt a ragged cry go up from somewhere near the gymnasium doors, and something in him, some old, worn instinct sharpened by years of losing people in exactly this kind of chaos, simply knew, before anyone told him, before he'd even fully processed what the sound meant, that the world had just gotten smaller by one person he loved.

*Xander.* Coulson's voice, careful, present, not trying to fix anything, just there. *I'm here.*

He didn't answer. There wasn't an answer available yet, nothing that could survive being put into words while a battle was still happening around him, while thirty more people still needed him functional, still needed his axe swinging and his feet moving and whatever was left of his attention pointed at staying alive long enough to grieve properly later.

So he kept fighting. He didn't know, afterward, how he'd managed it, how a person's body kept doing the necessary thing while the rest of them had gone somewhere else entirely, but it did, hour after hour, an axe rising and falling with a mechanical precision that had nothing left of him in it at all.

Coulson didn't try to talk him through it. That was, Xander understood only much later, its own kind of gift, a restraint that came from some deep well of hard experience, knowing that grief this raw didn't want company yet, wanted only enough presence to know it wasn't alone, nothing more demanding than that. He stayed close and quiet, an unmentioned shoulder, there without announcing itself, the same unglamorous, load-bearing not-doing-anything the whole strange partnership had kept coming back to since the very first night.

---

The battle ended the way battles like this ended, not with a single clean victory so much as a slow, exhausted realization that the enemy had stopped coming, that the tide really had turned, that somehow, impossibly, more of them were still standing than weren't.

The school began to come apart around them almost immediately after, the whole structure groaning under stresses no ordinary building was built to survive, and Buffy's voice, hoarse and commanding, cut through the ringing aftermath of the fight with an urgency that pulled everyone's attention back to the one thing left that mattered: get out, get everyone out, right now, don't wait to see how bad it gets.

Xander helped carry the wounded up through crumbling stairwells that hadn't finished deciding whether they'd hold, past cracks spreading through walls like something enormous underneath the school was finally, fully waking up and stretching. Outside, the whole of Sunnydale had started to follow the school's example, streets buckling, houses folding in on themselves, an entire town giving up its long, uneasy truce with the thing that had always lived beneath it.

They made it to the cars with minutes to spare, a battered, bloody convoy pulling away as the ground behind them opened wide and swallowed the last of everything any of them had ever called home.

Xander sat in the back of Giles's car, the axe finally set down, finally still, watching Sunnydale collapse in the rearview mirror the way you watched something you already knew, on some level, you were never coming back to.

*I'm sorry,* Coulson said, quiet, after a long stretch of road had passed under them in silence. *About Anya. I didn't know her well. I know that doesn't help. I'm still sorry.*

Xander didn't answer right away, watching the dust cloud where a town used to be finally disappear behind them entirely.

"Yeah," he said, eventually, out loud, his voice rough and unfamiliar even to himself. "Yeah. Me too."

Nobody in the car said much of anything else for a long while, the whole convoy moving forward through the dark toward wherever came next, carrying more silence than any of them had words left to fill, and somewhere behind his ribs, underneath the exhaustion and the grief and the strange, disorienting relief of simply still being alive, Xander felt the shape of a life he didn't recognize yet beginning, whether he was ready for it or not.

Chapter 25: Uneven Ground



They ended up in a motel outside Barstow, three days of driving compressed into a single exhausted decision to stop somewhere, anywhere, that wasn't a smoking hole in the ground where a town used to be. Forty-some survivors spread across a dozen rooms meant for half that many, sleeping in shifts, eating whatever the gas station down the road could provide, and slowly, unevenly, starting to reckon with what they'd actually survived.

The reckoning came in pieces, and it didn't come the same way for everyone.

Some of the newer Slayers found Xander in the parking lot the second morning, thanking him, awkward and sincere, for the plan that had gotten several of them through the vineyard and, later, for the way he'd talked half the house through the worst of the panic before the final battle. A girl named Deb, one of the ones from that very first vineyard briefing, hugged him without warning, fierce and quick, and didn't say anything at all, which somehow said more than words would have.

Other people looked straight through him. Andrew, of all people, made a joke at breakfast about "good old Zeppo, comic relief as always," not even meaning anything cruel by it, just falling back on the old, comfortable shorthand the way people did when they were too tired to update their assumptions about who anyone actually was anymore. Nobody corrected him. Xander didn't either. It wasn't worth the energy it would have cost, not this week, and some old, tired part of him had stopped expecting the correction to matter much anyway.

*"'Zeppo,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, indignant on his behalf. *"After everything. THE ZEPPO. This narrator has notes for the continuity department."*

*"Andrew's tired. Everyone's tired. Let it go."*

*"I will not be letting it go, I will be seething quietly, there's a difference."*

*That bothers you,* Coulson observed, quiet, once they were alone again.

*Less than it used to,* Xander admitted, surprising himself a little with how true it actually was. *I know what I did out there. I know what I've been doing all year. Doesn't mean I need every single person in this parking lot to know it too.*

*That's real growth, for what it's worth.*

*Don't get used to it. I reserve the right to be petty about it again the second I'm not this tired.*

*Noted.*

He was sitting on the curb outside his room, working through a granola bar he didn't much want, when Buffy found him.

"Can we talk?"

"Sure." He shifted over to make room, and she sat, and for a while neither of them said anything, the parking lot's early morning quiet stretching out comfortably enough that Xander let himself believe, for a minute, that this was just going to be another version of the crypt, two tired people sitting together without needing to explain anything.

It wasn't.

"I need to ask you something," Buffy said, finally, careful in a way she hadn't been careful with him in years, "and I need you to actually answer it instead of making a joke."

"Okay." Something in his chest went tight. "Ask."

"Something's different about you." She wasn't looking at him now, staring instead at the cracked asphalt between her feet. "It's been different since the vineyard. I noticed it the night I came back, and I've been noticing it ever since, little things, the way you read people now, the way you sometimes go somewhere else mid-sentence like you're listening to something I can't hear." She finally looked up. "I've had voices in my head before that weren't mine, Xander. I've had faces that weren't the right faces wearing people I loved. I need to know if that's what's happening to you."

*"So it's time for this, is it,"* said the same posh, affected voice from a few chapters back, quieter now, more careful with it. *"British accent. Apparently that's a thing now."*

*"Not for this scene, it isn't,"* Fury's voice cut in, flat, and the accent didn't come back.

Xander felt the moment arrive, the one Coulson had known was coming, the one he'd been quietly, deliberately not preparing for because preparing for it had felt like admitting it was inevitable.

*You don't have to tell her,* Coulson said, quiet, careful. *Not if you're not ready. This is your call, all the way, same as it's always been.*

*Yeah,* Xander thought back, *but I think I am ready. I think she's earned it more than most.*

He looked at Buffy, really looked at her, three years of friendship and history sitting behind her careful, frightened eyes, and made the decision the way he'd made most of the good ones lately, not by arguing himself into it, just by recognizing, once he'd given it long enough, that it was already true.

"There's someone in my head," he said, plain, no jokes, nothing softened. "Has been since the vineyard, actually, since a little before it. His name's Coulson. Chapter 28: The Posting

Chapter 29: Arrival



*"New setting, new arc, and would you look at that,"* the voice from the gallery said, settling in with real appetite. *"Another montage. Third one, by this narrator's count, and this narrator is now formally on the record as keeping a count."*

Not the dramatic kind. Nobody died because Xander said the wrong thing in his first few weeks in the field, nobody's plan collapsed under the weight of his mistakes the way Faith's had. *Yeah, well.* Xander watched the light fade slow over hills he was starting, despite everything, to think of as almost familiar. *Turns out it's kind of my whole thing.*

Chapter 30: The Settlement



Not every report led him somewhere he needed to fix something, and the reminder, when it came, arrived as a genuine, welcome surprise.

The village sat tucked into a valley two days' walk from the nearest road, self-contained in a way that read, at first glance, almost suspiciously peaceful, and Xander approached it with the same careful vigilance he brought to every unknown situation these days, half-expecting the calm to be a mask for something worse underneath.

*"Here we go,"* the voice from the gallery said, settling in for what it clearly expected to be a twist. *"Too peaceful. Somebody's hiding a body under the well. Called it."*

It wasn't.

*"...Huh,"* the gallery admitted, after a beat. *"Well. Good for them, I suppose."*

The girl's name was Nomvula, and she met him at the village's edge with an escort of four adults who introduced themselves, unhurried and unbothered, as though a strange American showing up asking after a Slayer was a perfectly ordinary Tuesday occurrence.

*This is different,* Coulson observed, quiet, cataloging the scene with the same trained attention he brought to every arrival. *No fear in the greeting. No urgency. They're not hiding her. They're presenting her.*

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, watching Nomvula stand easy and confident among the adults flanking her, none of the hunched, guarded posture he'd learned to expect. *This is new.*

*"You could just... let it be nice."*

*"I could. I'm choosing not to, on principle, but I could."*

"She has been strong since she was small," one of the elders explained, once introductions had settled, pride plain and unguarded in her voice. "We did not understand it at first. Now we understand it is a gift, and we protect what she is rather than fear it."

"You knew something was different about her? Before the Calling, I mean?"

"She has always run faster than the boys. Always seen further, heard sooner." The elder smiled, warm. "When the whole village dreamed the same dream, months ago, of a woman speaking of a well and a scythe and a burden being shared, we understood. We simply waited for you, or someone like you, to arrive and confirm it."

Xander felt something loosen in his chest, an old tension he carried into every one of these visits, the readiness for crisis simply finding nothing here to be ready for.

Nomvula herself watched the exchange with open curiosity rather than fear, occasionally cutting in with a sharp, confident question of her own. "The other girls you find. Are they all afraid?"

"Some. A lot of them, honestly. Most didn't have what you've got here." Xander gestured at the village around them, the easy, unguarded normalcy of it. "You're lucky. I don't say that to a lot of the girls I meet out here."

"I know I am lucky." Nomvula's expression sobered, some real weight settling behind her confidence. "I think about the others sometimes. The ones who are not."

*She's got real empathy,* Coulson observed, something warm underneath the professional read. *Not just confidence. Actual concern for people whose circumstances she's never had to share.*

*Yeah.* Xander watched her a moment longer, recognizing a familiar combination: strength paired with genuine care for people less fortunate. *Good combination to have, that. Hope the Network finds a use for it eventually.*

They spent the better part of two days in the village, not because anything required fixing, but because the elders, generous and curious in equal measure, wanted to understand more about what their daughter was becoming, what the wider world of Slayers actually looked like, what resources and community existed beyond their own valley.

The first evening, they fed him until he genuinely couldn't manage another bite, a rich, spiced stew served alongside bread still warm from a communal oven, and the whole village turned out for it, children weaving between the fires while the adults peppered him with questions, some practical, some clearly just curious about the strange American who'd walked two days into their valley on nothing but a rumor and a dream nobody else had explained properly.

"Tell us about the others," one of the younger women asked, settling nearby with a baby balanced easily on one hip. "The other girls like Nomvula. Where do they live? Are they safe, the way she is safe?"

Xander considered his answer carefully, unwilling to lie and unwilling, too, to needlessly frighten a room full of people who had, so far, done everything right. "Some are as safe as she is. Some aren't, not yet. Part of my job's finding the ones who need help getting there."

"And you help them. Alone?"

"Not alone." He didn't elaborate further, and nobody pushed, though he caught Nomvula watching him with the same sharp, assessing curiosity she'd shown since their first meeting, filing the deflection away without comment.

The second day, Nomvula insisted on showing him the whole valley properly, moving with an easy, confident stride through terrain that would have exhausted him twice over without her patient pace-setting, naming plants and warning him off a nest of something venomous tucked into a rock crevice before he got anywhere near it.

"You'd have stepped right on it," she said, amused, watching him give the crevice a wide, grateful berth.

*"Add it to the list,"* someone in the anonymous rows said. *"Vineyards, doorframes, now venomous crevices. This narrator's starting to think the man attracts hazards specifically."*

*"He does have one working eye. Depth perception's still a work in progress."*

*"That's not an excuse, that's foreshadowing for a sequel where he finally learns to duck."*

"Good thing I've got you, then."

"Good thing," she agreed, something warm and easy in it, and Xander understood, watching her navigate her own home with total, unhurried mastery, that this was what confidence actually looked like when it had never once been starved of the resources it needed to grow properly, an entirely different animal from the hard-won, scar-tissue confidence he'd watched so many other girls build out of nothing but necessity.

By the time they'd circled back to the village, the light going gold and long over the valley, Xander found himself, for once, not cataloging threats or gaps in a plan, just present, unhurried, genuinely enjoying a walk with a girl who didn't need saving from anything at all.

*You look relaxed,* Coulson observed, quiet, something almost surprised in it. *I don't think I've seen that from you out here before. Not like this.*

*Yeah.* Xander watched Nomvula greet a group of younger children running to meet her, scooping the smallest one up with easy, practiced strength. *Guess it's nice, every once in a while, getting to just visit instead of rescue.*

Xander answered every question patiently over those two days, and found, somewhere in the unhurried rhythm of it, a version of his job he didn't often get to practice: not rescue, not crisis management, just connection, information, the quiet work of linking one safe corner of the world to a larger network that might, someday, need to call on the steady confidence Nomvula already had in abundance.

"You will come back?" Nomvula asked, the morning he finally prepared to leave. "When you need me. For the others."

"Yeah." Xander felt something warm and certain settle into the promise. "When we need you. Wouldn't ask before then, though. This is your home. Your choice, always, when and if you leave it."

"Good." She managed a small, confident smile. "I like that you asked instead of assumed."

*You're getting good at that too,* Coulson observed, once they were back on the road, the village fading behind them into the valley's green folds. *Reading which situations need rescue and which just need acknowledgment.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander glanced back once, watching the last of the village's rooftops disappear from view. *Wish they were all like that one. Makes the job feel a little less like running from one fire to the next.*

*They're not,* Coulson agreed, quiet. *But it's good to be reminded, every so often, what it looks like when it goes right without you having to fix anything at all.*

Chapter 31: Something Wild



The report that brought him to the sixth village didn't sound like the others. Not a girl in danger, not a family afraid of what she'd become. A lion, and a girl playing with it right now, out past the tree line, and nobody in the village able to get close enough to stop her.

The village headman met him at the edge of the settlement at a dead run, no ceremony left in him at all. "Her name is Zanele. Good girl, quiet girl, worked with the goats her whole life and never caused anyone a moment's trouble." He gestured wildly toward the tree line, a fresh sound carrying from it even as he spoke, something between a roar and a shriek, cut short. "It is still going. She will not stop, she will not listen, and I do not think that animal has very much longer."

"How long's this been happening?"

"Minutes only. I ran the moment I heard it start."

Xander was already moving before the headman finished the sentence, Coulson's attention sharpening alongside his own the way it always did heading into an unknown, faster now, both of them clocking the same word doing all the work: *still.*

*Move,* Coulson said, urgent in a register that had nothing careful left in it. *Whatever this is, it's happening right now. You might still be in time to change how it ends.*

*Yeah. Getting that.*

He heard it before he smelled it, before he saw any of it, a sound too large and too wrong to belong to anything he wanted to be running toward, and broke through the last of the tree line into a clearing where a lion, alive, bleeding, staggering, tried one more time to drag itself upright, and a girl half its size let it get almost all the way there before she knocked it back down again, almost gentle about it, the way you'd swat a fly you weren't actually trying to kill yet.

She was laughing.

Not the hysterical, cracking laugh of shock, the kind he'd heard from more than one girl in the immediate aftermath of surviving something that should have killed her. This was something else, low and delighted, a girl entirely, unnervingly present in the moment, watching the animal try and fail to get its legs back under it with something like genuine fascination, and even as Xander cleared the last of the trees, even as he was close enough now to see her face properly, she didn't stop.

*"Man,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, quiet, no joke anywhere in it. *"That's some scary shit right there."*

*"Yeah,"* Fury's voice agreed, and there was nothing light in it either. *"Seen it once before. In something much bigger. And much greener."* A pause, flat, unreadable. *"Wasn't the worst thing I've seen, though."*

*This is different,* Coulson said, careful, cataloging fast. *Not shock. Not trauma response. She's not dissociating. She's engaged. Present.* A pause, grimmer. *And she didn't finish this quickly. Whatever's happening here, it isn't over adrenaline anymore. This is concerning in a different direction than what we usually see out here.*

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, already closing the distance, no time left for the slow, careful approach he'd have preferred. "Zanele!" Sharp, loud, cutting clean through whatever she'd been lost in. "Finish it. Now. Not for fun this time, for real, right now."

She froze, hand still raised for the next hit, and something in his voice must have reached the part of her that was still listening, because the next blow, when it landed, landed different, final, and the animal went still under it and stayed that way.

Her hands finally stilled with it. She looked up, surprised he already knew her name, and there was nothing dazed in her eyes at all, just a fierce, bright satisfaction that unsettled him more than fear would have. "It came at the goats," she said, and grinned, quick and involuntary, the grin of someone remembering something genuinely fun. "And I went to hit it, and it was like, I don't know, like swatting at something a hundred times slower than me. So I didn't hit it hard. Hit it just hard enough. And it got up, and I did it again, because I wanted to see if I could still do it exactly that easy a second time. And a third. It never got faster. I never got tired. So I just, kept going. Kept playing." She looked back down at her own hands, flexing them slowly, something like wonder underneath the delight. "I've never felt anything like that. Do you understand? My whole life I've been the small one, the quiet one, the one nobody worried about because nobody thought I could do anything. And that thing couldn't even touch me, and I could have stopped anytime I wanted and just didn't, and it felt..."

"Good," Xander finished, quiet, when she didn't. "It felt good."

"Yes." She said it like a confession, watching his face for judgment. "Is that wrong? To feel that? I keep waiting to feel sick about it and I just don't."

Xander sat down carefully a few feet away, giving her space, giving himself a moment to actually think through an answer instead of reaching for something easy.

*Careful here,* Coulson said, quiet. *Shame won't help her. Neither will simple permission. This needs to be honest about both halves of it.*

*Yeah, I know.*

"It's not wrong to feel powerful," Xander said, finally. "First time you find out what you can actually do, after a whole life of people underestimating you? That's gonna feel incredible. I'm not gonna sit here and tell you it shouldn't." He picked up a stray twig, turned it over in his hands, giving himself something to do while he found the rest of it. "But here's the thing. The power doesn't care what it's pointed at. Doesn't come with a built-in sense of when enough's enough. That part's on you to build yourself, and it doesn't come automatic just 'cause the strength did."

"So I should feel bad."

"No. I said careful, not bad." He met her eyes, steady. "There's a difference between enjoying that you're strong and enjoying that something died. I think you're feeling the first one. I need you to make sure it stays the first one, though, because the shard, the power, whatever you want to call it, it likes a good fight. Rewards it. Makes the next one feel even better than the last, if you let it. That's not you being broken. That's just the deal you got handed. Doesn't mean you get to stop paying attention to where the line is."

The girl considered that, some of the bright, unbothered satisfaction settling into something more thoughtful. "How do you know all this? You don't have the power yourself."

*"Well,"* Fury's voice said, dry, something almost like relief buried under the dryness. *"At least that means we're not getting a rematch with the worse one. Big, green, and considerably less interested in a conversation."*

*"...I don't want to know."*

*"No,"* Fury agreed. *"You really don't."*

"Had a friend once who did. Watched her walk that exact line, the hard way, with nobody around to tell her where it actually was." Xander thought of Faith, briefly, an old memory surfacing with an ache that hadn't fully faded even now. "I'd rather you not have to learn it the same way she did."

*Well handled,* Coulson said, quiet, once Zanele had finally, slowly, started to help him with the practical business of getting the carcass properly dealt with, the meat salvaged for the village rather than wasted, the whole aftermath handled with a steadiness that hadn't been available to her twenty minutes ago.

*Yeah.* Xander watched her work, some of the wildness settling into something more grounded, present but no longer quite so unbothered. *Hope so. Kid's got real power in her. Just needs somebody to help her build the fence around it before it builds itself in a direction nobody wants.*

*I think you just did exactly that.*

He stayed in the village three more days after that, longer than strictly necessary, watching Zanele carefully in the aftermath, checking in each morning and evening, making sure the bright, unnerving satisfaction from that first hour hadn't curdled into something worse once the initial shock properly wore off.

It didn't. If anything, she grew steadier with every day, thoughtful in a way that suggested she'd actually taken his warning to heart rather than simply nodding along to make him stop worrying.

"I keep thinking about what you said," she told him, the morning before he finally left, sitting with him at the edge of the same clearing, entirely different now in the daylight, empty and unremarkable. "About the fence. I think I understand it better now. It's not that I can't enjoy it. It's that I have to decide, every time, whether it deserves to be enjoyed."

"That's exactly it." Xander felt something like real pride settle in his chest, watching her articulate back a lesson he wasn't always sure he'd landed properly. "You're gonna do just fine, Zanele."

"Will you come back? To check?"

"If you want me to. Not gonna hover, though. You've got good instincts. Just wanted to make sure the first rush of it didn't run away with you before you had the chance to actually think it through."

"It almost did," she admitted, quiet. "Having someone show up right when it mattered, that helped more than you probably know."

*Yeah, well.* Xander managed a tired, genuine smile, watching the last of the daylight fade over a clearing that had, days ago, held something considerably more dangerous than a girl learning her own strength. *Had a whole year of practice, building fences. Guess it's finally coming in handy.*

Chapter 32: The Call



The satellite phone rang at an hour that meant trouble before he'd even answered it, and Xander fumbled it awake in the dark of his rented room, half-braced for bad news about the compound, about one of the girls, about anything except the voice that actually greeted him.

"Hey," Faith said, and something in the single syllable was wrong, worn thin in a way he'd never once heard from her, not even at the worst of the war.

"Faith?" He sat up fully, all the sleep gone out of him at once. "What's wrong?"

"Nothing. Everything. I don't know." A long, ragged breath crackled down the line. "I left Robin. Tonight. Didn't really think it through, just, grabbed what I could carry and walked."

*Give her room,* Coulson said, quiet, immediate. *Don't fill the silence. Let her get to it at her own pace.*

Xander held the phone, held the silence, waited.

"You're not gonna ask what happened?"

"Figured you'd tell me when you were ready. Not gonna push."

Something that might have been a laugh, small and broken, came down the line. "Yeah. That's kind of why I called you and not, I don't know, literally anyone else in this whole operation."

"How'd you find out? Whatever it was."

"Overheard him. Stupid, right? Straight out of a bad movie." She let out a breath that wasn't quite a laugh. "He was on the phone in his study, door not quite shut, and I was just gonna ask if he wanted tea, and instead I heard him laughing with somebody about how far he'd come, using words like 'asset' and 'PR value' and 'the wild one, finally settled,' like he was describing a horse he'd broken instead of a person he'd been sleeping next to for a year."

Another breath, steadier this time. "I found out he's been working an angle. Whole time. Politicking with the Council behind my back, telling people he's the one who tamed the wild Slayer, like I'm some kind of trophy he landed instead of an actual person he's supposed to, I don't know, actually give a damn about."

"Faith..."

"Let me finish. I need to actually say this out loud once, all the way through, or I'm gonna choke on it." Her voice hardened, old defenses locking back into place even through the hurt. "I confronted him. Tonight. Told him exactly what I'd heard, word for word, gave him the chance to tell me it was a lie. He didn't. Got angry instead. Real angry. Raised his hand like he was gonna..." She stopped, and Xander felt something cold and furious settle in his own chest, waiting. "He didn't. I want to be clear, he didn't actually hit me. But he raised it, and I saw exactly what he was capable of in that one second, and that was enough. That was more than enough."

*"Man,"* a new voice said, one nobody in the gallery had heard from before, brash and half a beat too eager, *"that's a lot of pain in a very pretty package. Come to think of it, that package looks very fa..."*

The voice stopped itself. Dead, mid-word, the easy leer draining out of it in real time as whatever had been about to follow that sentence apparently caught up with the man saying it, some private recognition landing a half-second too late to stop the first half of the thought but plenty fast enough to strangle the second half in its crib.

*"...Never mind,"* the same voice said, considerably smaller now. *"Forget I said anything. That's, huh. That's somebody's kid. That's actually somebody's kid, isn't it. Christ."*

*"GIBSON."* Fury's voice, flat, immediate, though there was something almost merciful in the timing of it, cutting in right as the man clearly needed something else to focus on. *"Not tonight. Not this scene. Sit down."*

*"Sitting down. Already sitting down. Extremely sat."*

Nobody asked who Gibson was, or what he'd almost finished saying, or whose kid he meant. This narrator suspects there will be time for all three later. Tonight was not it.

*Xander,* Coulson said, low, urgent. *Stay steady for her. Whatever you're feeling right now, she needs steady, not fury, not right this second.*

Xander breathed through it, banked the fury down where it could wait its turn, though it didn't go anywhere, just sat there, patient, a debt he fully intended to collect on someday if the opportunity ever presented itself. "You did the right thing. Leaving. Right then, no packing, no waiting around to see if he'd apologize his way back into your good graces."

"Didn't feel like a choice, honestly. Felt like my legs just decided for me before my brain caught up." A shaky breath. "I keep replaying it. The look on his face when I said his name out loud, before I even told him what I'd heard. Like he already knew, already had the excuse half-built. That's the part that gets me, Xander. He had a *speech* ready. Like he'd been waiting for the day I'd catch him, rehearsing it."

"That's its own kind of answer, isn't it. Man doesn't build a defense speech for something he's not already guilty of."

"Yeah. Figured that out somewhere around minute two of him giving it to me."

"Yeah. I know that logically. Doesn't stop it from hurting like hell." Her voice cracked properly this time, the wall finally giving. "I thought he was different, Xander. Thought I'd actually found somebody who saw me as, I don't know, a person, not an asset, not a story he could tell about himself. Guess I was wrong about that too."

"You weren't wrong to want it. That's not the same as being wrong about him." Xander felt his way through the words carefully, wanting to get this exactly right. "Some people are good at looking like the real thing right up until it costs them something to actually be it. That's on him, Faith. Every bit of that's on him. Not on you for believing somebody who was good at lying."

Silence, for a long moment, just her breathing, steadier now, something in it slowly settling.

"Why'd you call me and not Buffy? Or Willow?"

"Because you're the only one who's never once needed anything from me." Faith said it plain, no defenses left to hide behind. "Everybody else, there's history, there's old stuff, there's them needing me to be a certain version of okay so they don't have to worry. You've just always kind of, taken me as I come. Never asked for anything back. Figured you were the one person who'd actually let me fall apart without needing me to apologize for it after."

*"Man,"* Gibson's voice said, quiet, none of the earlier leer left anywhere in it, *"I'm starting to feel all gooey inside."*

*"Shut up, you."* But there wasn't any heat in it, just Fury, sounding almost fond of the man he'd told to sit down twenty minutes ago.

Something warm and complicated settled in Xander's chest, hearing that, a feeling he didn't have a name ready for yet and didn't try to force one onto.

"You can fall apart as long as you need to," he said, quiet, meaning it entirely. "I'm not going anywhere. Literally can't, actually, I'm on a different continent, but you know what I mean."

That got a real laugh out of her, small but genuine, the first crack of actual light through the whole conversation. "Yeah. I know what you mean."

"Where are you now? Physically, I mean. You said you walked."

"Bus station. Cleveland's a big enough city, there's always a bus station open somewhere." A pause, and when she spoke again some of the rawness had receded, replaced by something more like her usual dry, deflecting humor, worn thin but present. "Don't worry, I'm not actually sleeping on a bench like some tragic movie montage. Got a room. Just needed somewhere quiet to make this call without an audience."

"What happens now? With the house. The rebuild."

"Guess I finish what I started, professionally, and figure out the personal stuff on my own time. Not gonna let him chase me off a project I put actual sweat into." Something fierce came back into her voice, familiar, steadying. "Might take some time off after, though. Head out somewhere nobody's expecting me to be anybody's anything for a while."

"That sounds smart, actually."

"Don't sound so surprised."

"I'm not surprised. I'm impressed. Different thing." He managed a small smile she couldn't see but might have heard anyway, in his voice. "You're allowed to take care of yourself, Faith. Not just everybody else. Been meaning to tell you that for a while, actually, long before tonight gave me the opening."

*"Yeah,"* Fury's voice said, quiet, nothing dry left in it at all. *"The girl's got the right stuff."*

She was quiet a moment, and when she spoke again her voice had gone soft in a way he'd rarely heard from her. "When'd you get so good at this? The saying-the-right-thing stuff. Feels like it snuck up on you sometime in the last year."

"Had a lot of practice. Bunch of scared girls, one very patient roommate, and a whole continent's worth of learning how to actually listen instead of just waiting for my turn to talk." He shrugged, though there was no one there to see it. "Turns out it's a skill like anything else. You just gotta actually work at it."

They talked another hour after that, the specifics of Robin's betrayal giving way slowly to smaller things, the girls Xander was working with, the half-finished renovation on the Cleveland house's east wing, a story about Faith accidentally starting a minor kitchen fire that had nothing to do with anything and made them both laugh anyway, an old friendship finding its footing again in the dark, easier with every passing minute, and by the time Xander finally set the phone down, the sky outside his window had started, faintly, toward gray.

*That was Faith,* Coulson said, quiet, something thoughtful in the observation. *Really Faith. Not the armor she usually wears for everyone else.*

*Yeah.* Xander lay back down, sleep nowhere close to reach now, turning the whole conversation over in his head. *Guess we've both had a year of figuring out who actually sees us instead of the story other people tell about us.*

*I think that might be more significant than you're letting yourself notice right now.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander closed his eyes, exhaustion finally catching up despite everything. *Ask me again sometime when I've had actual sleep. Tonight I'm just glad she called somebody who'd actually pick up.*

Chapter 33: First Blood



Not every threat out here wore a human face, and Xander learned that properly, for the first time, on a warm evening in the fourth month, when a girl named Thandiwe felt something in the compound's outer field that none of his careful, hard-won language skills had a word ready for.

"Something's wrong with the goats," she said, first, hesitant, still new enough to her own instincts that she didn't yet trust them fully. She'd been with him six weeks, quick and clever and still carrying a wariness that hadn't fully softened, though it was softening, slowly, the way it always did once a girl started to believe she was actually safe.

*She's not wrong,* Coulson said, immediately, some part of his own trained awareness picking up on something Xander's slower senses hadn't caught yet. *Listen. The insects have stopped.*

Xander went still, and yeah, there it was, the whole evening chorus of the field gone abruptly, unnervingly quiet, a silence that meant something predatory had entered the ecosystem and every smaller creature in it had gone to ground at once.

"Everybody inside," Xander said, low, urgent, already moving the other girls toward the compound's main building. "Thandiwe, with me."

She hesitated, fear and pride warring visibly across her face. "I can help."

"I know you can. That's why you're with me and not hiding with the others." He kept his voice steady, calm, the same register he'd learned mattered most in exactly this kind of moment. "First rule out here, though. You listen to what I say, when I say it, until you've got more experience under your belt to argue with. Deal?"

"Deal."

They found it at the field's edge, something old and wrong, vaguely humanoid but wearing its shape the way a coat wore a hanger, ill-fitting, provisional, a low, hungry growl building in a throat that hadn't been built by anything Xander recognized from any bestiary Giles had ever shown him.

*I don't have a name for this,* Coulson admitted, fast, professional, cataloging the threat with the same speed he'd once brought to a vineyard full of danger. *Whatever it is, it's not moving like the things you've described from home. Slower. More deliberate. I'd guess strength over speed.*

*"'I'd guess strength over speed,'"* someone in the anonymous rows repeated, delighted despite the tension. *"The man's narrating a cryptid encounter like a scouting report. I love him."*

*Great. Love a mystery monster.* Xander kept his stance loose, weapon ready, watching Thandiwe out of the corner of his eye, watching her fear settle, slowly, into something closer to focus. *Thandiwe. This is you. First real fight. I'm right here, but this one's yours to take the first swing on.*

"Me?"

"You. You've got the strength now. You've got the instincts, whether you trust 'em yet or not. Only way you actually believe that is doing it once, with somebody watching your back who isn't gonna let it go too far wrong." He met her eyes, steady, certain. "You've got this."

She didn't have this, not entirely, not yet, her first strike wild and off-balance, more instinct than technique. But it connected, staggering the creature back a step, and something in her whole posture changed in that instant, the fear transmuting, right there in front of him, into something fiercer, more certain.

*Now,* Coulson said, sharp. *Second strike, low, it's overcorrecting for the first hit.*

Xander relayed it without breaking his own focus, watching Thandiwe adjust, watching her land the second strike clean, decisive, the creature finally going down for good under a third blow that carried none of the wildness of the first.

Silence, after, the field's insects slowly, cautiously resuming their evening chorus, and Thandiwe stood over the fallen creature breathing hard, shaking, her whole body flooded with an adrenaline she'd never once had reason to feel before tonight.

*"There it is,"* the gallery's voice said, warm now, all the earlier tension gone out of it. *"First win. Every single one of these matters, even the ones without a monologuing villain attached."*

"I did it," she said, disbelieving, looking at her own hands like they belonged to someone else. "I actually did it."

"You actually did it." Xander let himself grin, real and unguarded, watching the fear finally, fully drain out of her posture, replaced by something he recognized immediately, having felt its early version himself once, a long time ago, in a vineyard that felt like another lifetime now. "How's it feel?"

"Terrifying." A slow, disbelieving laugh escaped her. "Also, kind of incredible? Is that bad? Should I not feel like that's incredible?"

"Nah." Xander clapped a hand on her shoulder, steady, warm. "That's exactly how it's supposed to feel. Scared and a little bit amazing, both at once. Means you're taking it seriously and you're not letting it own you. That's the whole balance."

They walked back to the compound together, Thandiwe practically vibrating with the aftermath of it, already asking a dozen questions about what else was out there, what else she might face, what else she needed to learn, and Xander answered each one patiently, unhurried, watching a girl who'd arrived six weeks ago carrying nothing but fear slowly, visibly, become someone who might actually survive the life that had been handed to her.

*That was a good night,* Coulson said, quiet, once she'd finally run out of questions and gone ahead to tell the others.

*"Your guy really has a knack for understatement, doesn't he,"* someone in the anonymous rows said to Fury, not unkindly.

*"Shut up, you!"* But there wasn't any real bite in it, just a man who'd apparently gotten used to fielding that exact complaint about that exact agent for a very long time.

*Yeah.* Xander watched her go, watched the compound's lights welcome her back, warm and safe. *Yeah, it really was. Don't get enough of those out here. Good to remember they happen too, not just the hard ones.*

*Agreed.* A pause, something almost fond in it. *You're good at this part too, you know. Not just the crisis management. The teaching. The watching someone become who they're supposed to be.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander looked up at a sky thick with more stars than he'd ever seen back home, feeling, for once, entirely at peace with the strange, hard-won shape his life had taken. *Turns out that's kind of the whole job, isn't it. Not just keeping people safe. Helping 'em actually get to be something, once they are.*

Chapter 34: A Girl Like Molly



The report came through a contact in the fourth village, third month in, relayed in the careful, indirect language people used when they were afraid of saying a true thing too plainly: a household two valleys over, a girl nobody had seen in public in some time, whispers about restraints, about a family too desperate or too frightened to explain themselves to outsiders.

Xander went alone, which had become his habit by now, some instinct telling him a smaller, quieter presence got further than an entourage ever would.

*You're tense,* Coulson observed, as they walked the last mile on foot, the truck he'd hired left behind at a fork in the road too narrow for it to manage.

*Yeah, well.* Xander kept his pace steady, unhurried, the way he'd learned mattered in these situations, urgency read as threat here as often as it read as help. *Reports like this don't usually end with everybody just having a nice misunderstanding.*

*No,* Coulson agreed. *They usually don't.*

The house, when he found it, was small and poor, poverty that wasn't a moral failing so much as a simple, grinding fact of the world, and Xander felt his stomach drop the second he stepped through the low doorway and saw her.

She was young, younger than Molly had been, restrained in the corner of the single room with something that functioned as chains even if it hadn't started life as anything so deliberate, wrists bound tight enough to have left real marks. The specific reason for the restraint, Xander understood almost immediately, watching the mother's face crumple at the sight of a stranger seeing her daughter this way, wasn't going to be something spoken plainly, not today, maybe not ever, and he made the deliberate choice, right there in the doorway, not to press for it. Whatever had driven a family to this point, fear of something the girl had done, fear of something that might be done to her, fear of a power in her that nobody in this house had a name for yet, the specifics mattered less right now than getting her somewhere safe.

The girl herself didn't speak a word of the language Xander had spent three months learning.

*New Slayer, then,* Coulson said, quiet, careful, watching her track the room with an alertness that had nothing calm about it. *That kind of vigilance isn't ordinary fear. That's someone whose body already knows something's changed and hasn't been given a framework to understand it.*

*Yeah.* Xander crouched slowly, kept his hands visible, kept his voice low and even, the exact register he'd learned to use with a scared Potential a lifetime ago in a vineyard he still dreamed about sometimes. He tried three languages he had scraps of before finding one that got a flicker of recognition, halting, imperfect, enough to start with.

The mother watched him work, wary, exhausted, her whole body braced for judgment she'd clearly been expecting since the moment he walked in.

*This is where it gets difficult,* Coulson said. *She's terrified of you, but she's also terrified of what happens if you leave without taking the girl. You need to give her a way to save face here, not just an order to comply with.*

*Yeah, I know.* Xander had already been circling the same conclusion. *Doesn't mean I love the version of this where it ends up looking like a transaction.*

*It doesn't have to be ugly to be a transaction. Sometimes a transaction is just the only shape dignity's allowed to take, in a situation with no other framework available.*

Xander understood, watching the mother's hands twist together, understood in the specific way you understood a thing once you'd actually seen it up close instead of just imagining it from a distance: this wasn't a woman selling her daughter for profit, wasn't cruelty dressed up as commerce. This was a woman who had run out of every other option she could name, who needed, desperately, some framework, any framework, that let her believe she was doing right by her daughter rather than abandoning her, and the only framework this desperate a situation had left her was the oldest one there was, an exchange, something given for something taken.

*"There's that specific tic again,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, quiet, no humor reaching for it at all. Nobody answered. Nobody made it into anything more than what it was.

He had medicine in his pack, real, needed, the kind of thing the Network's supply lines had started routing through his territory precisely because emergencies like this one kept surfacing faster than anyone back home had planned for. He set it on the table between them, unhurried, and named it plainly, medicine for a sickness he'd heard was moving through these hills, medicine this family, he suspected, badly needed regardless of anything happening with the daughter.

The mother's eyes went to it, then to her daughter, then, finally, to Xander's face, searching for whatever truth lived behind the offer.

*Say something about the girl now,* Coulson said, quiet. *Not a command. A promise. She needs to hear that this ends somewhere safe, specifically, not just somewhere else.*

Xander found the words, slow, careful, explaining as best his limited vocabulary allowed that there were others like her daughter, that there was a place, safety, people who understood what was happening to her even if nobody here yet had a name for it, that this wasn't an ending, it was a beginning she couldn't see the shape of yet.

The mother wept, finally, unlocking the restraints herself with hands that shook, and said something too fast and too broken for Xander's imperfect grasp of the language to fully catch, though he understood the shape of it well enough without every word: gratitude, and grief, and the relief of a person finally allowed to stop carrying something alone.

The girl didn't speak on the walk back to the truck. She flinched at every unfamiliar sound, kept a careful distance even from Xander's steady, unhurried pace, and Xander didn't push any of it, didn't reach for her, didn't try to fill the silence with reassurance that would have meant nothing without a shared language to carry it.

He just walked beside her, at her pace, the same steady presence he'd learned to be for Molly a lifetime ago, no shared language required for that part, apparently, just the willingness to actually be there.

*You did well,* Coulson said, once they were finally settled in the truck, the girl asleep against the window within minutes, exhaustion finally overtaking whatever vigilance had kept her upright through the whole ordeal.

*Yeah,* Xander said, watching her sleep, watching the hills roll by outside, the same steadying work he'd started with Molly finally, fully, coming into its own out here where nobody back home would ever know to credit him for it. *Yeah. This one, I think we did.*

Chapter 35: The First Handoff



It caught up with him three days later, on the road between villages, and Xander understood immediately, watching the truck ahead of him block the narrow dirt track, that the quiet resolution at the mother's doorstep hadn't been the whole story after all.

There'd been someone else with a stake in that girl. He learned that now, too late to have planned around it, four men climbing out of the blocking truck with the loose, confident violence of people used to getting their way through simple physical intimidation, one of them the girl's uncle, Xander recognized him now from a photograph he'd been shown days ago and dismissed as irrelevant, a man who had apparently been counting on that daughter's labor, or her fear, or something else entirely that nobody had bothered explaining to Xander before he'd walked into that house and disrupted whatever arrangement had existed before he got there.

*"Something wicked this way comes,"* someone in the anonymous rows murmured, quiet, no flourish left in the delivery at all, just an old line reached for because it already knew the shape of this.

*This isn't a negotiation,* Coulson said, immediately, flat and urgent, reading the men's posture with the trained speed of someone who'd assessed a hundred situations exactly like this one. *Look at their hands. Look at how they're spacing themselves around the truck. This is a beating, or worse, and it's coming in the next thirty seconds.*

*Yeah, I'm getting that too.* Xander kept his own hands loose, visible, buying whatever fraction of a second he could with the appearance of calm. *Options?*

*Talking won't work. I don't think you have the vocabulary for a negotiation this fast-moving even if it would, and I don't think these men are interested in one regardless. If this goes physical, and it's about to, you're not equipped for four-on-one. I am. Not confident I'd win outright, but I'd give us considerably better odds than you'd have alone.*

Xander felt the offer land differently than it had at the vineyard, all those months ago, when handing over control to a man he'd known for twenty minutes had felt like an impossible ask. This wasn't twenty minutes anymore. This was months of arguments and reconciliations and a man who had backed his instinct about Spike with nothing but trust to stand on, and the decision, when it actually arrived, didn't feel like a surrender at all.

*Do it,* Xander thought, clear, deliberate, the choice entirely his own to make. *Take it.*

The transfer happened in a way neither of them had fully anticipated, a smooth, seamless handoff, Xander's own awareness sliding sideways rather than vanishing, still fully present, still watching, still feeling everything, just no longer the one directing the muscles that mattered.

Coulson moved.

*"No flips,"* someone in the anonymous rows observed, sounding faintly betrayed. *"I was promised competence. I was not told it would be this boring to watch."*

*"That's the point. Boring is the professional's compliment to himself."*

It wasn't flashy. That was the first thing Xander noticed, watching from somewhere just behind his own eyes, a strange, vertiginous experience of witnessing his own body do things he'd never trained it to do, efficient, economical, every motion built around ending a confrontation as fast as possible rather than looking impressive doing it. The first man went down with a strike to the throat that dropped him gagging before he'd even fully raised his hands. The second took a redirected shove that used his own momentum to put him face-first into the dirt. Xander's body moved through the remaining two with the same brisk, professional efficiency, disabling rather than destroying, restraint that could only belong to a man who had clearly spent a career learning exactly how much force a situation actually required and not one ounce more.

The uncle, last standing, backed away with his hands up, shouting something Xander's limited grasp of the language couldn't fully parse, though the tone translated well enough on its own: fear, and fury, and the helplessness of a man who had expected an easy target and found something else entirely waiting for him instead.

Coulson didn't press the advantage. He let the man retreat, let all four of them scatter back toward their truck and the safety of distance, and only once they were genuinely gone, genuinely no longer a threat, did Xander feel control settle back fully into his own hands, the transition as seamless leaving as it had been arriving.

He stood there a moment, breathing hard, his own body feeling suddenly, strangely unfamiliar, like a coat he'd just gotten back after loaning it to someone else for an afternoon.

*You okay?* Coulson asked, quiet, genuine concern under the question.

*I don't know yet.* Xander flexed his own hands, watched them move exactly the way they always had, and found the ordinariness of that somehow more unsettling than anything dramatic could have been. *That was weird. Good weird, I think, given the alternative was probably getting jumped by four guys with something to prove. But weird.*

*I understand. For what it's worth, I felt strange about it too. I've spent twenty years being the one who acts. Being handed a body to act with, rather than relying on my own, is a different thing entirely than I expected it to be, even having known the mechanic existed in theory.*

*Did it feel good? Being able to actually do something instead of just talking me through it?*

Coulson considered that for a long moment before answering. *Yes,* he said, finally, honest. *And it also felt like a genuine trust I'm not sure I've earned yet, being given a body that isn't mine and asked to bring it back exactly as I found it. I'd like to be worthy of being asked again. I don't take it lightly that you did.*

Xander turned that over, walking the rest of the way to his own waiting truck on legs that still felt faintly, strangely borrowed.

*You did bring it back exactly as you found it,* Xander said, eventually, checking himself over out of habit, finding nothing worse than the ordinary aches of a long day's walk. *No damage. No weirdness left over. Just, you know. A very efficient beating of four guys who deserved it.*

*I'll take the compliment.*

*It wasn't really a compliment. More like a receipt.*

*I'll take that too.*

*"A receipt,"* the gallery's voice repeated, delighted. *"Twenty years of field training and the review he gets is an itemized receipt. This narrator's never seen a man take an insult with this much grace."*

*"It's not an insult if he agrees with it."*

*"That's what makes it worse for him, actually."*

Xander managed something close to a real laugh at that, the tension of the last several minutes finally draining out of him, and climbed into the truck to finish the drive to the next village, carrying with him a new, unfamiliar knowledge about exactly how far this partnership was actually willing to go, and exactly how much he'd come to trust it, without ever quite deciding to, one small handed-over moment at a time.

Chapter 36: Too Many Girls



By the fifth month, Xander wasn't traveling alone anymore.

*"And here's where the premise finally runs into arithmetic,"* the voice from the gallery said. *"Every found-family story hits this wall eventually. Turns out 'gather everyone who needs you' doesn't scale. Who could've predicted."*

*"Everyone,"* someone in the anonymous rows said. *"Everyone could've predicted it."*

It had happened gradually, the way most real changes did, one girl becoming two, two becoming a small, cautious cluster, each new arrival needing somewhere to actually go once she'd been found, steadied, and readied for whatever came next. He'd set up a kind of way station in the largest of the villages he worked, a compound loaned to him by a local family grateful for something he'd done for them early on, and it had filled, slowly and then not so slowly, until by the fifth month he had eleven girls under his informal care at once, ranging from barely-Called to nearly two months into learning what their new strength actually meant.

It should have felt like a victory. In a lot of ways it was one, eleven girls who might otherwise have been lost to fear, exploitation, or simple bad luck, all of them safer now than they'd been the day he found them.

It was also, Coulson pointed out one evening, watching the compound's evening routine unfold from the shade of the porch, a genuine, serious problem.

*Eleven Slayers in one place, no formal security, minimal defenses, and a growing local reputation that anyone with an interest in finding them wouldn't need much effort to trace,* he said, flat, professional, the exact register he'd used the night he'd first flagged the retreat-path gap Xander had ignored. *I want to be direct about this, because I don't think you've fully clocked the shape of the risk yet. You've built something wonderful here. You've also built something that reads, to anyone thinking tactically, as a single, high-value target instead of eleven separate, harder-to-find ones.*

Xander felt something cold settle in his stomach, recognizing, even before Coulson finished the thought, the shape of a mistake he'd made once before, at a house on Revello Drive, in a plan built entirely around holding rather than around what happened if holding failed.

*You're saying this is the retreat-path thing again.*

*"OH,"* several voices in the anonymous rows said at once, a ragged, overlapping chorus. *"The callback! The actual structural callback! Somebody get the frame back out, we're gonna need it for this one too."*

*"At this rate the wall's gonna run out of room before the book does."*

*I'm saying it's the same category of failure. Concentration without contingency. It worked while the group was small enough not to be worth anyone's notice. It stops working the moment the group becomes large enough to be worth someone's while.*

*How much time do we have?*

*I don't know precisely. I know it's not open-ended.* A pause, careful. *I also want to say, this isn't a criticism of what you've built. Every single decision that got you here was the right one, individually. Find the girl, bring her somewhere safe, repeat. Nobody planned for the aggregate. That's not a failure of judgment. It's just what happens when good instincts compound faster than the infrastructure around them.*

Xander turned that over a long while, watching the compound's evening routine, girls training in loose pairs in the fading light, others cooking, others simply sitting together in the easy companionship of people who'd found something like family in the strangest possible circumstances. It was good. It was real. And it was, exactly as Coulson said, a problem waiting to happen.

*What do we do,* Xander asked, eventually, no longer resisting the read the way he might have, once, a lifetime ago, in a kitchen in Sunnydale arguing about whose version of seeing counted more.

*"You call Giles, you idiot!"* someone in the anonymous rows shouted, the exact pitch of an audience member who has lost all patience with a protagonist standing three feet from an obvious answer.

*"He's going to. Give the scene a second to breathe."*

*"I've given it plenty of seconds! Call the man!"*

*We need somewhere organized to consolidate this,* Coulson said. *Somewhere with real defenses, real infrastructure, not an improvised compound in a village that was never built to hold this concentration of the exact power that draws exactly the wrong kind of attention.*

*The Council's been talking about Cleveland.* Xander turned the thought over as it arrived, the shape of it settling into place with the same quiet certainty he'd felt outside the crypt, all those months ago, arriving at an answer that felt discovered rather than argued into existence. *New Hellmouth. Real base of operations, actual defenses, actual coordination instead of everybody improvising in their own corner of the world.*

*That fits,* Coulson agreed. *A defensible position instead of an accumulating one. I'd support that recommendation.*

*Yeah.* Xander watched the girls a while longer, already turning over, in his head, the practical shape of what moving eleven of them halfway across the world would actually require. *Yeah, me too.*

It wasn't a decision either of them reached alone, exactly, the way the best decisions between them rarely were anymore. It was the two of them looking at the same problem from two different angles, Coulson's trained operational instinct naming the risk plainly, Xander's own read on what the girls actually needed to feel safe rather than simply be safe, and landing, together, on an answer neither of them would have found quite as cleanly on their own.

He called Giles that same night, before he let himself second-guess any of it, the satellite phone's connection crackling through half a second of delay that made every exchange feel like talking across more distance than it actually was.

"It's late there," Giles said, by way of hello, already alert in the way of a man who'd learned, over the last year, that a call at this hour from Xander specifically usually meant something worth losing sleep over.

"Yeah, sorry. Couldn't wait till morning, this one's been sitting wrong in my chest all evening." Xander stepped out of earshot of the compound, the evening chorus of insects the only company for what he needed to say next. "I've got eleven Slayers in one place, Giles. Eleven, in a compound that used to be somebody's storage shed, no real security, and I just had it explained to me, in pretty stark terms, exactly how bad an idea that's about to become."

A pause, papers rustling faintly on the other end, the sound of a man already reaching for a pen before he'd finished processing the problem. "Eleven. Good Lord. I confess I hadn't tracked the number climbing quite that high."

"Neither had I, honestly, not till somebody made me actually add it up." Xander glanced back at the compound's lit windows, the ordinary evening business of thirty-some lives he'd have to explain this to eventually. "I need somewhere real to send them. Somewhere with actual walls, actual coordination, not me improvising security out of whatever a village happens to have lying around."

"I take it you have a specific somewhere in mind," Giles said, dry, already half a step ahead of him.

"Cleveland." Xander said it plain, no more circling around it. "New Hellmouth, actual infrastructure, and last I heard the Council's putting real money into building something permanent there anyway. Feels less like inventing a new plan and more like plugging into one that already exists."

Giles was quiet a moment, and Xander could hear, even across an ocean and a bad connection, the sound of a careful man actually weighing something rather than simply agreeing to be agreeable. "It's sound," Giles said, finally. "More than sound, honestly, it's rather overdue. We've been discussing consolidation in the abstract for weeks without anyone actually proposing a number to build toward. Eleven gives the conversation a concrete shape it's been lacking."

"So it's a yes."

"It's a yes, contingent on logistics neither of us can solve over a satellite phone at this hour. Housing, transport, integration with the Slayers already settling in. I'll need a fortnight, perhaps three weeks, to have something real in place rather than a promise." A pause, and something almost fond crept into his voice. "For what it's worth, Xander, I'm glad you called instead of simply managing this alone. That's not always been your instinct."

"Yeah, well." Xander watched the compound's lights a while longer, feeling something ease in his chest that had been sitting tight there since Coulson first named the problem. "Had a good teacher, somewhere along the way, about not carrying everything by myself. Guess it finally stuck."

A pause on the line, longer than the satellite delay could account for on its own. "For what it's worth," Giles said, quieter than before, none of the usual careful formality left in it, "I'm proud of the man you've become, son."

*"...Hang on."* The gallery's voice again, but changed now, something almost startled in it. *"That's the first time that word's been used. Either direction. This whole book, this whole year, and that's the first time."*

Nobody in the anonymous rows argued the point, or tried to follow it with a joke.

Xander didn't have anything ready for that either. It took him a moment to find his own voice again. "...Thanks, Giles. That actually means a lot."

*"Aw,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, soft. *"There's the moral of the episode. Somebody write that one down."*

*"Nobody's writing it down. It's not a fortune cookie, it's a phone call."*

*"It could be both."*

They talked another twenty minutes after that, working through the rough shape of a plan neither of them would finish that night, and by the time Xander finally lowered the phone, the compound had gone quiet around him, thirty-some lives sleeping easy in a place that was, he now understood clearly, on borrowed time in the best possible way.

*"There. Happy now?"*

*"Ecstatic."*

He spent the next several weeks preparing the move, quiet, methodical work, securing transport, coordinating with the skeleton Council staff already setting up shop in Cleveland, breaking the news to eleven girls who'd only just started to feel settled that settled, for now, meant somewhere new again.

Most of them took it better than he expected. A few didn't, and he spent long evenings with those few, patient, unhurried, the same steady presence he'd been building his whole reputation on out here, helping them understand that this wasn't another uprooting, another loss, but a step toward something more permanent than anything he'd been able to offer them so far.

"We're not running," he told the last, most reluctant holdout, a fierce girl named Priya who'd been with him since nearly the beginning. "We're going somewhere that can actually hold all of us properly. Somewhere with walls that mean something and people who know what they're doing. This isn't the end of what we built here. It's the next part of it."

Priya studied him a long moment, scrutinizing him the way someone did when deciding whether to trust a promise, and finally, slowly, nodded.

"Okay," she said. "I trust you."

*That's the whole job, isn't it,* Coulson said, quiet, once she'd gone to pack. *Not the tactics. Not the defenses. Just, somehow, earning that.*

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, watching the compound begin its slow, deliberate dismantling around him, eleven lives about to be reorganized around a single, careful decision he'd made in partnership with a man who'd once been a stranger in his head and had somehow, over the better part of a year, become something closer to family. *Yeah. That's the whole job.*

Chapter 37: Cleveland



The new headquarters didn't look like much from the outside, a big, sagging former fraternity house on the edge of a university district nobody had ever accused of being glamorous, chosen entirely for the same grim, practical reason Sunnydale had once mattered: a Hellmouth, quieter than the one they'd lost but real, sitting patient underneath the foundation, waiting for someone to build a life on top of it and defend it properly this time. A set of Greek letters still hung crooked over the front door, nobody had quite gotten around to taking them down yet, and somebody had papered over most of the ground floor's more enthusiastic old decor rather than dealing with it properly.

Xander saw it first from the window of the van, eleven girls crowded in around him, exhausted and travel-worn and, underneath all of it, cautiously hopeful in a way he'd worked months to earn from every single one of them.

"That's it?" Priya asked, unimpressed, peering past him at the big, tired-looking house with the crooked letters over the door.

"That's it," Xander confirmed. "I know it doesn't look like much. Give it a chance."

*Nervous?* Coulson asked, quiet, as the van pulled through the gate.

*A little.* Xander watched the building grow larger through the windshield, watched a handful of figures gather near the entrance, alerted by whatever advance word had traveled ahead of their arrival. *Been a long time since I walked into a room where people already knew me before I got there. Not sure what version of me they're expecting.*

*I imagine they're expecting exactly who you are. I don't think that's ever really been in doubt, even when the room itself hasn't caught up to seeing it.*

The van stopped. Xander climbed out first, stretching muscles stiff from the long drive, and looked up to find the small welcoming party had resolved itself into faces he knew.

Buffy stood at the front of it, arms crossed, a smile breaking slowly across her face as she took in the sight of the van, the eleven exhausted, hopeful girls climbing out behind him, the whole improbable shape of what he'd built out of nothing but steadiness and patience and a year of unglamorous work nobody back home had been there to see.

"You brought an army," she said, something warm and genuinely proud in her voice.

*"Big damn hero moment,"* the voice from the gallery said, sitting up. *"Roll the drums. Somebody find this narrator a drum."*

*"There's no drum budget."*

*"There is never a drum budget in this production and I am tired of pretending that's fine."*

"I brought some scared kids who needed somewhere safe to land," Xander corrected, though he couldn't quite keep the pleased flush out of his own voice either. "Turned out there were eleven of them, is all."

Willow was there too, steadier than he'd seen her in years, real color in her face, real calm in the way she held herself, and she crossed the distance between them without hesitation and pulled him into a fierce, unhurried hug.

"You look good," she said, pulling back to study him properly, the eye patch he'd finally settled on, the sun-weathered evidence of months spent somewhere far from anything familiar. "Different. Good different."

"Yeah, well. Turns out I'm decent at this whole steadying-people thing when nobody's actively trying to kill me every five minutes."

A sharp-eyed young woman pushed through the small crowd next, a clipboard of her own tucked under one arm, already counting heads with the brisk, practiced efficiency of someone whose actual job this was. It took Xander a full second to place her, older now, steadier, nothing left of the watchful, half-scared Potential who'd once ridden beside him in the back of Giles's car.

"Caridad?"

"You remember." Something pleased flickered across her face before the efficiency reasserted itself. "Eleven, Giles said. I'll need names, ages, any medical concerns, and I'll want them settled by rooms before dinner, not after, or we'll be sorting out arguments about bunks until midnight." She was already moving down the line of girls, assessing, warm but no-nonsense, the kind of authority that came from having actually run a house full of exhausted Slayers for months rather than simply being told to. "I run the place day to day. Someone has to, and Giles is hopeless past sundown, and Buffy's got the whole organization to worry about besides."

"You ended up running the house."

*"The quiet one from chapter three,"* someone in the anonymous rows said, delighted. *"Look at her go. Nobody saw this coming except everybody who was paying attention."*

"Somebody had to, and it turned out to be the thing I was actually good at." Caridad's eyes found his, something knowing in them. "Funny how that works out, isn't it. Turns out the vineyard taught me a few things too."

*"Hang on,"* the same voice said again, slower this time, something working behind it. *"Frat house. Caridad running the place day to day. That vineyard girl, of all the vineyard girls, ending up right here, right now, doing exactly this. This narrator's been keeping notes on odder coincidences than this and calling them nothing, but that's a lot of things lining up in a row all at once."*

*"Lining up toward what, exactly?"*

*"...I don't know. I can't quite see the shape of it yet. But somebody's driving this arrangement, and this narrator doesn't think it's us."*

Nobody had an answer for that. This narrator, for the record, isn't offering one either.

Giles approached last, unhurried, and studied the assembled girls with the same careful, scholarly attention he brought to anything genuinely new, before turning that same attention fully on Xander himself.

"Eleven," Giles said, quiet, something like real vindication in his voice, the satisfaction of a man whose considered judgment had, in fact, been correct. "In under a year. Do you have any idea how significant that number actually is, given the resources you had available to you?"

"I had time, patience, and a very good translator eventually." Xander glanced sideways, a private, small smile only Coulson would understand the full weight of. "Turns out that's most of the job."

"It's rather more than most of the job," Giles said. "I'd like to hear the full account, when you're ready to give it. All of it."

Xander noticed, then, the absence that had been sitting quietly at the edge of his attention since he'd stepped out of the van. "Faith not around?"

"Off doing her own thing," Buffy said, easy, unbothered. "She was here for most of the rebuild, actually. Put more sweat into this building than almost anybody, hauling drywall and rewiring half the place herself when the contractors ran behind schedule. Took off a few weeks back, though. Last I heard, somewhere in the Midwest, running her own little operation the way only Faith would run it. She checks in. Mostly."

Somewhere near the back of the small welcoming crowd, a couple of the newer recruits, people Xander didn't recognize, people who'd only ever known him by reputation rather than personal history, watched the whole reunion with open curiosity. He caught one of them lean toward another and murmur something, and though he couldn't quite catch the words, the shape of it was familiar enough, an old nickname surfacing out of habit, the Zeppo, still alive somewhere in the building's collective memory even after everything.

*"That word again,"* the same indignant voice from a few chapters back muttered. *"I have half a mind to write a strongly worded letter to nobody in particular."*

*"Wait for it."*

It didn't land the way it used to. He noticed that, distantly, with something like surprise. It used to cost him something, hearing that word, a small private wound reopened every time. Now it just sat there, true once and increasingly irrelevant, a label some people would keep reaching for out of habit long after the reality had outgrown it, and that no longer mattered nearly as much as it once had.

*How's that feel,* Coulson asked, quiet, catching the moment the same way he caught most things.

*Weird.* Xander turned the observation over honestly. *Used to be the thing that could ruin a whole day for me. Now it's just... noise. Background noise from people who haven't caught up yet. Doesn't change what actually happened out there.*

*No,* Coulson agreed. *It doesn't.*

*"Told you to wait for it,"* the same voice said, satisfied, and settled back into its seat, letter apparently unwritten, grievance apparently retired for good.

Eleven girls, trailing behind him into the building that was about to become their new home, wasn't a speech. It wasn't a medal, wasn't a formal announcement, wasn't anyone standing up to declare that the Zeppo had, in fact, been worth something all along. *Yeah,* Xander agreed, watching the light fade outside a window that looked out on a city he'd never expected to end up in, carrying a life he'd never expected to build, alongside a partner he'd never expected to trust this completely. *Yeah. Longest one I've ever had.*

*Worth it?*

Chapter 38: Growing Pains



The eleven girls from Africa didn't fold seamlessly into Cleveland's existing population, and Xander should have expected that, probably, if he'd thought about it properly instead of just being relieved to have finally gotten everyone somewhere safe.

The friction started small. Different languages, different customs, different assumptions about how a shared living space was supposed to work, all of it compressed into hallways and common rooms that had never been designed to hold this much difference at once. Priya got into a sharp, unhappy argument with one of the original Sunnydale survivors over kitchen scheduling that had nothing to do with kitchens and everything to do with two groups of scared, displaced girls each feeling like outsiders in a space the other one had claimed first.

It wasn't just Priya. Small misunderstandings kept surfacing everywhere Xander looked, a training exercise that meant something entirely different to a girl raised on one continent's fighting traditions than to one raised on another's, a joke that landed as playful teasing to one group and genuine insult to the other, mealtimes that turned tense over nothing more complicated than unfamiliar food and homesickness for meals nobody here knew how to properly recreate. One of the younger girls asked him, out of nowhere one Tuesday, whether he could help her put together a small cake to bring to school, some tradition from home she couldn't fully explain and didn't seem to expect him to understand, just needed, badly, for someone to help her have.

*"Okay, this is getting far too specific,"* the same voice said, flat, something almost unsettled under the dryness this time. *"A cake. For school. Why does that detail feel like it's been lifted wholesale out of somewhere else entirely."*

*"You said yourself you couldn't see the shape of it yet."*

*"I still can't. I just keep bumping into the edges of it."*

"This isn't working the way I thought it would," Xander admitted, late one evening, watching two more small conflicts play out across the common room in the space of an hour.

*It rarely does, this fast,* Coulson said. *You built something real out there, a genuine community with its own rhythms and trust. *Doesn't make it feel better watching it happen.*

*No,* Coulson agreed. *I don't imagine it does.*

"I don't belong here," Priya said, miserable, curled up on her bed in the small shared room she'd been assigned. "Everyone already knows each other. Everyone already has their people. I'm just the new girl from somewhere far away that nobody actually understands."

"I actually do."

*You did this,* Coulson observed, one evening, watching the common room's easy, mixed chatter, girls from two different continents laughing together over something Xander couldn't quite catch from across the room.

*We did this,* Xander corrected, easy now, no longer needing to defend the distinction at all. *Same as always. Your read on where the friction was actually coming from, my read on how to actually fix it without making anybody feel like they lost.*

*I'll take the credit split. It's accurate.*

*"Look at them, negotiating credit splits like a normal old married couple,"* the gallery's voice said, warm. *"This narrator did not expect to get invested, and yet."*

Xander watched the room a while longer, feeling something settle in his chest that had nothing to do with crisis management and everything to do with simple, quiet satisfaction, the reward of watching something you'd built with your own patient, unglamorous hands actually take root and grow.

*Worth all the friction?* Coulson asked, quiet.

*Yeah,* Xander said, watching Priya throw her head back laughing at something one of the Cleveland girls had said, entirely at home now in a room that had felt like enemy territory just weeks ago. *Yeah. Every bit of it.*

Chapter 39: Andrew

*He's not wrong, you know,* Coulson observed, once Andrew had gone. *About the parallel. Two men the group habitually underestimates, for different reasons, arriving at very different outcomes.*

*Yeah, well.* Xander managed a small, satisfied smile, finishing his inventory check. *Had a whole year of practice figuring it out.*

Chapter 40: That Conversation

*Yeah,* Xander agreed, finishing his coffee, ready, finally, fully, for whatever the rest of this strange, hard-won life had left to offer either of them. *Yeah, I think you're right.*

Chapter 42: The Waiting Room



That first night in Cleveland, exhausted in a way that went bone-deep, past muscle and into something closer to soul, Xander fell asleep faster than he had in months, and found himself, without any sense of transition, standing in a room he'd never actually seen before but somehow, immediately, recognized.

Six doors. A coffee machine offering something labeled, in cheerful block letters, PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE. Diplomas on the wall from universities that didn't exist anywhere real. And, standing beside him, looking every bit as startled as Xander felt, a man he'd never once seen with his own eyes despite spending the better part of a year sharing a body with him.

"Coulson?" Xander turned, taking him in properly for the first time, the good suit, the steady, watchful eyes, the quiet competence that had carried them both through a year neither of them had asked for. "That you?"

"That's me." Coulson looked down at his own hands, flexed them slowly, the wonder of it plain on his face even through the professional composure. "I don't believe we've actually met. Properly, I mean. Face to face."

"Yeah, no, this is new." Xander looked around the impossible room again, taking in the details with real delight, a man who'd spent a year absorbing enough of Coulson's own world, secondhand, to actually recognize significance when it walked through a door. "Wait. Wait, is that..."

A seventh figure occupied the room's single armchair, unhurried, a green waistcoat catching the light, and Xander felt something in him go momentarily, helplessly starstruck, because even without ever having seen the real man, even knowing, on some level, that this specific face was borrowed rather than genuine, some part of him that had absorbed a full year of Coulson's own quiet, complicated respect for the person wearing it couldn't help reacting.

"Is that Fury?"

Coulson opened his mouth, hesitated, and Xander watched him make the quick, deliberate choice not to correct him, a small private kindness extended without needing to be asked for.

"Something like him," Coulson said instead, careful.

"Dude." Xander stared, unable to help himself. "You worked for that guy? For real? That's, that's actually incredible, I've heard so much about him secondhand I feel like I basically already know him..."

"Gentlemen." Not-Fury's voice cut through, dry, amused, entirely unhurried. Xander blinked, processing that, and felt something click into place, an old, half-forgotten memory of a line Coulson had once mentioned in passing, months ago, back at Revello Drive, about a waiting room and a recruitment he'd never fully explained.

"You're the guy," Xander said, slow. "The one who dropped him into my head."

"Rob O'Make O'Malley," the figure confirmed, with a small, theatrical bow that managed to be both entirely sincere and deeply unserious at once. "Facilitator. Importer. Occasional corrector of tragically understaffed narratives. And, on this particular occasion, host of what I believe is your first proper reunion."

"Reunion," Xander repeated, looking back at Coulson, really looking at him now, a whole year of shared consciousness suddenly given a face and a body and a physical presence he'd never once actually seen. "This is wild. This is so wild. I've been living in your head for a year and I didn't even know you had, like, actual eyebrows."

"I have quite ordinary eyebrows," Coulson said, dry, and something in his face had gone soft in a way Xander had never once felt from the inside, an expression he'd never had access to before, warm and a little overwhelmed all at once.

O'Malley, tactfully, said nothing further, occupying himself instead with something that looked very much like paperwork, giving the two of them room to have whatever this was without an audience pressing in on it.

"I owe you an apology," Coulson said, once the moment had settled, turning to face Xander directly. "For the wave-off, back at the house. For not pushing harder when I should have. I've replayed that conversation more times than I'd care to admit, wondering if a different approach might have saved Robin Wood some of what he went through."

"I owe you one too," Xander said, immediate, matching the honesty. "For dismissing you. For needing you to be wrong about something just so I didn't have to admit I'd been wrong about you. That wasn't fair. Wasn't smart, either, considering how right you turned out to be."

"We were both stubborn."

"We were both really stubborn." Xander managed something close to a laugh. "In our own defense, I think we've gotten a lot better at the not-stubborn thing since."

"We have," Coulson agreed, warm. "I want to say thank you, too, while we're doing this properly, face to face, for the first time. For trusting me with your body at the vineyard. For trusting my read on Spike backing yours, on the amulet. For a year of letting a stranger share the most private space a person has, and somehow, despite every reason not to, treating me like family instead of an intrusion."

"Yeah, well." Xander felt his own throat go tight, unexpectedly. "Turns out you're pretty good family, for a guy who showed up uninvited."

"I'll take that as the highest compliment you're capable of giving."

"It was meant as one."

They stood together a moment longer, the strange impossible room holding still around them, and Xander understood, without needing it explained, that this wasn't goodbye, not really, not in any sense that mattered. Waking life still had them sharing one body, one set of senses, one long partnership that showed no signs of ending. But here, in this room, in whatever this actually was, they got something waking life had never quite been able to offer either of them: the simple, physical fact of two separate faces, finally, looking at each other properly.

"This a one-time thing?" Xander asked, eventually, as the room's edges began, faintly, to soften, the way dreams did when they sensed themselves ending.

O'Malley looked up from his paperwork, something knowing in his expression. "I find," he said, mild, "that certain doors, once opened properly, have a habit of staying accessible. Should either of you wish to find your way back here again, I suspect you'll manage it. This room isn't going anywhere. Neither, I imagine, are the two of you."

"Standing invitation," Xander said, looking at Coulson, something warm and certain settling into place. "I like that."

"So do I," Coulson agreed.

Somewhere behind the closing door, in rows that had never once been visible from inside the dream itself, the anonymous watchers who had sat through the whole strange, stubborn, hard-won year of it rose quietly, the way an audience did when it had gotten, against considerable odds, the ending it had been rooting for all along. This narrator will not be riffing on the credits. Some things earn the silence instead.

The room dissolved slowly around them, the six doors and the impossible diplomas and the man in the green waistcoat fading like morning mist burning off, and somewhere in the last, soft edges of the dream, Xander heard Coulson's voice, warm, unmistakably fond, following him back toward waking.

*See you again soon,* Coulson said. *Not goodbye. Just, until next time.*

*Yeah,* Xander thought, feeling the dream give way at last to the ordinary dark of his new room in Cleveland, a whole life waiting for him on the other side of sleep, eleven girls settling into their own new beginnings down the hall, a city and a Hellmouth and a future neither of them could have imagined a year ago, all of it his now, all of it theirs. *Yeah. Until next time.*

Chapter 43: The Honest Trailer



The waiting room dissolved. What came after it did not, technically, have a room to dissolve into, but it had a screen, somehow, and a single spotlit podium, and a voice that dropped into the register this narrator had been saving for exactly one occasion.

*"In a world,"* the voice from the gallery began, and somewhere in the anonymous rows a low, appreciative murmur went up, the sound of a crowd recognizing an old ritual finally being observed properly, *"where one man's job was noticing things nobody else was watching... and another man's job was noticing things nobody else was watching... ROB O'Make O'Malley looked at the both of them and said, 'what if I put them in the same skull and see what happens.'"*

*"This,"* the voice went on, *"is* O'Make Way for Xander.*"*

A beat.

*"Also known as: The One-Eyed Guy and the Guy That Works for a One-Eye Guy."*

A pause, considering its own handiwork.

*"...Huh,"* the same voice said, quieter, genuine surprise leaking through the delivery. *"Didn't clock that symmetry until just now. That's actually the better subtitle."*

*"Somebody take it up with the publisher."*

*"You are the publisher."*

*"Then consider it under review."*

The screen, or whatever was serving as one, ran a highlight reel nobody present had asked for and everybody, this narrator included, was going to sit through anyway.

*"Watch as our hero,"* the voice intoned, deadpan, *"loses an eye in the cold open, and instead of getting so much as a training montage to prepare him for it, gets handed a fully grown federal employee instead. Free of charge. No refunds. No exchanges. Not even a receipt, though God knows the man tried to give him one later."*

*"'A receipt,'"* somebody repeated, delighted, from several chapters' worth of memory. *"Never letting that go."*

*"Watch,"* the gallery's voice continued, *"as this fully grown federal employee immediately demonstrates his complete professional composure by crying, quietly, in a kitchen, over a nickname. Twenty years of training. One kitchen. Advantage: kitchen."*

*"In fairness,"* someone offered, *"it was a really good nickname."*

*"It was! That's what made it worse!"*

The reel rolled on, unbothered by objections, unbothered by anything.

*"Witness,"* the voice said, *"a vineyard. Witness a folksy man in a black shirt deliver possibly the least hurried murder-adjacent monologue in the history of murder-adjacent monologues. Witness our professional passenger reach for a body that was never his to reach for, discover, in real time, that twenty years of throwing himself between people and danger means exactly nothing when the body in question belongs to somebody else. This narrator does not have footage of what came next that this narrator is prepared to make jokes about. Some things you sit through instead of riffing on. We sat through it."*

Nobody in the anonymous rows said anything to fill that pause. Nobody had, the first time either.

*"Witness,"* the voice picked back up, gentler now but not slower, *"a popsicle-stick model of a vineyard. A shoebox lid. A plan built entirely around getting one specific girl clear, executed so well it got credited, out loud, chapters later, by the very girl it was built for. This is a movie that will make you cry about a shoebox lid. This narrator did not see that coming and would like it on the record that this narrator is not sorry about it."*

*"Witness,"* the voice went on, picking up speed, *"an argument about whose kind of noticing things counts more, conducted by two men who are, and this is important, both extremely right and both extremely stubborn about it, for approximately eleven chapters, until the movie finally, mercifully, lets them both be right at the same time instead of making either one of them lose."*

*"Groundbreaking,"* somebody in the anonymous rows said, dry. *"A story where nobody has to be wrong for somebody else to be right. Somebody alert the entire rest of the genre."*

*"Witness a doorframe. Witness that doorframe again. Witness it a third time, at which point this narrator began to suspect the doorframe was doing it on purpose."*

*"Witness 'I contain multitudes,' stolen fair and square from a dead poet by a live one-eyed guy, live, on the page, in front of everybody, and witness this narrator's own gallery catch him doing it in real time. That's not narration. That's surveillance. We were good at our jobs that night."*

*"Witness,"* the voice said, and something shifted under it, not quite dry anymore, *"a house falling apart because a gap in a plan got waved off by exactly the person who should have listened. Witness that same person carry it, quietly, for the rest of the book, not as a plot device, just as a thing a person carries. This narrator would like to point out that most stories let their hero learn a lesson and then never mention it again. This one just kept mentioning it. Every single retreat-path joke you heard from chapter twelve onward, that's not a running gag. That's a wound the story refused to let scar over clean."*

*"Witness Anya,"* the voice said, and stopped there, and let the stop be the whole sentence.

The anonymous rows, for once, offered nothing at all. This narrator did not ask them to.

*"Witness,"* the voice resumed, quieter, *"a continent. Witness a man who couldn't hold a conversation in the local language for the first six weeks somehow become the guy villages sent word ahead for. Witness eleven girls, individually rescued, individually steadied, individually taught that the fence around their own power is a thing they build themselves, one patient conversation at a time, by a guy the home team was still occasionally calling 'the Zeppo' the whole time he was doing it."*

*"Witness,"* the voice said, *"a very competent handoff of one body to one very competent professional, four very unfortunate men, and one extremely dry exchange about receipts that this narrator has now referenced three separate times in one chapter and regrets nothing."*

*"Witness a woman leave a bad man in the middle of the night with nothing but a bag and a phone call to the one person in her whole life who'd never once needed anything back from her. This narrator is not going to make a joke here either. Some chapters you just carry."*

*"And witness,"* the voice said, building now, the register climbing back toward something almost triumphant, *"eleven girls walking into a converted frat house on a lesser Hellmouth behind a one-eyed man with a federal employee riding shotgun in his skull, and absolutely nobody in that doorway needing it explained to them what they were looking at."*

The reel ended. The spotlight held a moment on the empty podium.

*"Honest Trailers Drinking Game,"* the voice announced, and somewhere in the anonymous rows a rustle went up, glasses already being found.

*"Take a drink every time somebody in this book gets called 'the Zeppo,' out loud, to his face, by somebody who should honestly know better by now."*

*"Take two if it's said by somebody who's about to be proven wrong within the same scene."*

*"Finish your drink the one time somebody actually apologizes for it. There's exactly one. Savor it."*

*"Do NOT attempt this drinking game during the Caleb chapter. Nobody has made it. This narrator has checked."*

A beat.

*"This is a story with,"* the voice went on:

*"One (1) eye, permanently retired from active duty.*

*One (1) receipt, offered in complete sincerity, never once redeemed.*

*A doorframe with more screen time than several credited characters.*

*A shoebox lid that made grown gallery members go quiet.*

*And a body count of exactly zero doorframes actually walked into, despite three separate close calls."*

*"Count along, if you want,"* the voice offered, *"with the number of times somebody calls him 'the guy who carries the bags,' and the number of times, by the end, that phrase stops sounding like an insult and starts sounding like a job description he'd frame."*

*"Count the eleven girls. Actually count them. This narrator did. It's eleven. Every single one gets a name. That's not an accident."*

[BEAT: MUSIC SWELLS]

*"Starring,"* the voice intoned, downshifting into the register reserved for the part everybody actually came for:

*"The funniest guy in the ensemble for seven straight years, whose reward for it was 'plucky comic relief' on the resume and 'the Zeppo' as the character's own nickname. Poetic, in a way that had to hurt a little."*

*"Clark Gregg, dying on screen so many times across one franchise that the studio eventually just gave him a television show to justify all the resurrecting."*

*"The Slayer herself, twenty-plus years deep into being asked when she's coming back to it, and entitled, at this point, to be a little tired of the question."*

*"The one who went on to prove, repeatedly, that Sunnydale's redhead was never the warm-up act, whatever the marketing for her other franchises tried to claim about who the funny one was."*

*"Selling you instant coffee for a decade before he ever sold you Watcher gravitas, and somehow making both pitches equally convincing. That's not nothing. That's a résumé."*

*"A thousand years of vengeance demon, played with a delivery precise enough to curdle milk at forty paces, from an actress who never once got asked back for the genre work that delivery should have guaranteed her."*

*"Cheekbones doing the heavy lifting of an entire personality since day one, and somehow still less iconic than the bleach job on top of them."*

*"And the director in the big chair, insisting, to this day, that there was a plan the whole time. There was not, in fact, a plan the whole time. Ask anybody who was actually in the room."*

*"Hang on,"* a voice cut in from the gallery, before the card could even finish loading. *"We haven't litigated this. Pitch the alternates first, we're not skipping the part where we argue."*

*A show of hands, apparently, because the narrator sighed and read them out anyway.* *"'Two Guys Who Notice Things.' Blunt. Accurate. Somebody's very proud of it."*

Fury's voice, from somewhere near the front: *"'The Cavalry and the Zeppo.' Both of those are real nicknames. Real people gave them those, I didn't have to invent a single word."* A scattered, appreciative murmur went up. Somewhere in the back row, somebody muttered that it was actually good.

*"'The Most Qualified Guys in the Room, Finally.' Somebody's still bitter about season one."*

A pause.

*"Yeah,"* the narrator said, quieter now. *"We looked at all of it. We're not using any of it."*

[BEAT: HONEST TITLE CARD, styled to match the show's own title design]

THE EYE OF THE ONE WHO SEES

[BEAT: CREDITS ROLL]

*Directed by: a woman who swears there was a plan the whole time.*

*Written by: an outline that grew several unplanned chapters and is not sorry about a single one of them.*

*Starring: One (1) Very Good Bag Carrier, One (1) Extremely Professional Crier.*

*Also Starring: A Receipt That Never Once Got Cashed In.*

*Retreat Path Consultant: nobody, that was the whole problem.*

*Doorframe Wrangler: still working on it.*

*Special Thanks: to every Potential who got a name and a scene and mattered, and to the eleven who made it all the way to Cleveland.*

*"So remember,"* the voice from the gallery said, settling into the old sign-off cadence, *"if you enjoyed this story, or if you're just here because Fury made you watch it,"* a small pause, self-amused, *"you're not alone. This has been another Honest Trailer, for* O'Make Way for Xander: The Eye of the One Who Sees. *Starring: One (1) Very Good Bag Carrier, One (1) Extremely Professional Crier, a Shoebox Lid, Several Doorframes, a Receipt, and Eleven Girls Who Made the Whole Thing Worth It."*

*"Also starring,"* someone in the anonymous rows added, unable to help themselves, *"a genuinely unexpected amount of pride, for something none of us actually made happen, and yet."*

A pause. Then, from the gallery's own voice, warmer than it had let itself be all chapter:

*"...Yeah. That too. Somebody note it down. This one, I'm not letting the beverage machine take the credit for."*

The screen went dark. Somewhere behind it, unseen, unhurried, a man who had once given a boy a nickname he never fully explained folded his hands, satisfied, and let the credits roll.
