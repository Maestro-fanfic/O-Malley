Chapter 25 of 29

What Gets Out

By morning, everybody knew Max Anders was Kaiser.

The first public version of the story had begun assembling before Taylor left the MedHall loading dock.

News helicopters held beyond the PRT cordon and filmed the roof through rain. Reporters shouted questions at people still wearing triage tags. Guests wrapped in silver emergency blankets described giant women at the doors, a metal animal in the atrium, and insects that had shown them where to run. No two accounts agreed on the order. Several disagreed on which floor they had occupied.

All of them agreed about the man with blades coming from his hands.

The news began with the footage that required the least explanation: MedHall's front wall bowed outward, water pouring through broken glass, emergency lights pulsing behind smoke. A section of mezzanine hung into the atrium. The chandelier lay across the dance floor in a glittering ruin large enough to be visible from the street.

Then came Kaiser.

Three separate cameras had caught him in the service corridor with blades along both arms. One angle showed Miss Militia entering behind him. Another showed Armsmaster closing from the clinic. The third, recorded by a firefighter's helmet camera through the rising loading shutter, caught the moment Kaiser looked from one blocked route to the other and understood that the performance was over.

The networks played that moment repeatedly.

They played it while Taylor was lifted into an ambulance. They played it on the waiting-room television while a nurse cut the rest of the green dress away from her ankle. They played it without sound in the imaging department, Kaiser sinking to his knees above a caption bar while a technician checked Taylor's spine for metal fragments.

Every replay made the distance between Max Anders and Kaiser look smaller.

MAX ANDERS ARRESTED AFTER MEDHALL SIEGE, the chyron read.

EMPIRE EIGHTY-EIGHT LEADERSHIP EXPOSED.

PRT CONFIRMS EXPLOSIVES, HOSTAGES, PARAHUMAN INVOLVEMENT.

MEDHALL RECORDS SEIZED IN FALSE-FLAG INVESTIGATION.

That fourth line appeared after sunrise.

Danny sat on the couch wearing borrowed hospital scrubs because his suit remained sealed in an evidence bag. Taylor wore an oversized Dockworkers sweatshirt over bandages at her shoulder and hip. Neither had slept.

Her right ankle was not broken. The doctor had repeated that twice as if the absence of a fracture converted it into good news. Severe sprain, lacerations to both feet, six stitches across her back, bruising from the stun charge, mild hearing loss expected to resolve, no retained glass. She had been given crutches and told not to put weight on the joint.

The crutches leaned against the couch untouched.

Danny had bruised ribs, a cut above one eyebrow he did not remember receiving, and a shoulder strained when he caught the concealed door against Fenja. He kept testing the shoulder when he thought Taylor was watching the television.

She kept noticing.

The television volume stayed low. Every few minutes one of them raised it, listened to a detail, and lowered it again.

"Authorities credit an unidentified person inside the building with providing the first accurate threat assessment," the anchor said. "The caller, referred to over emergency channels only as Tower, warned responding teams that Anders was directing the hostage takers while presenting himself publicly as a captive. The PRT has declined to identify Tower, citing an active protective investigation."

The segment used a silhouette that looked nothing like Taylor. Short hair, broad shoulders, adult proportions. Someone at the PRT had either guessed very badly or protected her on purpose.

Danny looked at Taylor.

Taylor looked at the television.

Protective investigation, McClane said. That's government for “we know exactly who she is and we're trying not to say it where the Nazis can hear.”

Comforting.

More comforting than them putting your school picture on screen.

Danny reached for the remote, paused the image, and studied the false silhouette.

"They know?"

"Miss Militia does. Armsmaster. Some of the troopers. Probably more."

"Your name?"

"Yes."

"Your school?"

Taylor had not asked.

McClane answered first. Assume they know everything that can be put on a form. You'll be pleasantly surprised if they don't.

"Probably."

Danny set the remote down with exaggerated care. "We're discussing that when they come back."

They had already come once.

Miss Militia had found them between radiology and discharge with her scarf removed and rain drying on her uniform. Without the cloth and weapons she looked younger, which made the exhaustion around her eyes harder to ignore. Armsmaster remained in the hall, visible through the open door and pretending the placement was about guarding it.

"No civilian fatalities," Miss Militia had said before anything else. "The donor from the clinic is stable. The server from the north collapse has a broken pelvis and internal injuries, but surgery went well. Trooper Ortega has a concussion and ligament damage. Roof One has frost exposure and a fractured wrist. Both are expected to recover."

Taylor had counted everyone outside. She still needed the words.

"The people under the mezzanine?"

Miss Militia's pause answered before she did. "Three of Anders's armed personnel died in the collapse. A fourth died during the initial takeover."

The fourth had been listed as MedHall security because he wore a confiscated blazer and carried one of the guards' rifles. Taylor corrected that before Miss Militia finished the sentence.

"He wasn't security. He came through the loading dock with them. He took the rifle from the real guard."

Armsmaster appeared in the doorway. "Location?"

Taylor gave it. Coat-check passage, eleven feet from the north stair, body moved behind the display wall after the takeover.

Armsmaster transmitted the correction immediately. The real guard, Raymond Vale, was located among the wounded in the laundry and removed from the suspect list before sunrise.

He remained in the doorway after the transmission ended.

"My initial assessment of Anders was wrong," he said.

Danny's head came up from the molded hospital chair.

Armsmaster continued before either of them answered. "I treated the camera feed as stronger evidence than the source inside the structure. I allowed the apparent hostage role to outweigh repeated observations that did not fit it. That delayed our response and contributed to the roof team entering an anticipated ambush."

It was not an apology shaped to invite forgiveness. It was an incident finding delivered by a man who intended to put his own name on it.

Taylor preferred that.

"Miss Militia listened," she said.

"Yes. She did."

Miss Militia glanced toward him, then back to Taylor. "The designation Tower stays inside the operational record. Your name will be restricted to the investigation team, medical liaison, and command review. The surviving Empire capes know they were opposed by someone using insects. They may have your physical description. They do not get confirmation from us."

"What about everyone I helped?"

"We cannot stop them describing you. We can ask them not to identify you. Several have already asked us how to protect your name."

Danny leaned forward despite his ribs. "And what do you want from her?"

Miss Militia understood the question beneath the question.

"Tonight? A recorded statement when her doctor clears it, with you present. After that, a secure way to contact her if the people who escaped create an immediate threat. No recruitment conversation today. No decision made while either of you is exhausted or frightened."

"Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow she is still fifteen, Mr. Hebert. You remain her father. Any conversation includes both of you unless Taylor asks otherwise."

Danny's shoulders lowered by a fraction.

Armsmaster placed a sealed radio handset on the bedside table. "One button reaches Protectorate dispatch under the Tower identifier. It does not transmit until activated."

Taylor looked at it.

Better than a business card, McClane said. Harder to lose in the wash.

"Thank you," she said.

Armsmaster nodded once. "Trooper Ortega asked us to tell you the same. Less formally."

The casualty count appeared next.

No civilian deaths.

Thirty-two treated at the scene. Eleven hospitalized. Two PRT troopers injured, one seriously. Four members of Kaiser's armed contingent dead, including the sound-board operator caught beneath the stage collapse. An earlier report identifying one body as MedHall security was withdrawn. Nine attackers arrested. Hookwolf, Cricket, Fenja, and Menja escaped during the confusion around the north-support detonation. Alabaster was discovered in the service elevator after striking the doors for forty-three uninterrupted minutes.

The anchor managed to read that last detail without changing expression.

Danny did not.

He laughed once, exhausted and startled by the sound. Taylor joined him. The laughter hurt her shoulder and became something dangerously close to crying before either of them acknowledged the transition.

Danny turned off the television.

Silence settled through the house.

Outside, a truck changed gears on the avenue. Pipes clicked in the kitchen wall. After a night in which every sound had meant a door, a weapon, or a person moving where they should not, the house's ordinary noises arrived with edges.

Taylor extended her swarm through the rooms without meaning to. Three moths rested beneath the porch light. Ants moved through a crack beside the sink. A spider occupied the upper corner of the front window. No one waited in the yard. No radio shifted in the bushes. No steel grew behind the walls.

McClane said nothing about the check.

"You trapped a supervillain in an elevator," he said.

"Temporarily."

"Forty-three minutes sounds pretty committed."

"He kept resetting."

"Of course he did."

Danny looked toward the muted television. "You know that's going to be the part everyone remembers."

"Not the chandelier?"

"Elevator's funnier."

"The chandelier was three tons."

"You counted?"

Taylor gave him a look.

Danny's tired smile appeared and vanished. "Right. Stupid question."

They had not yet had the conversation. They had started it six times at the hospital and been interrupted by doctors, PRT interviews, insurance forms, and one furious Dockworkers representative who arrived prepared to organize a search party before learning Danny was already safe.

The pause now felt different. Nothing remained to interrupt them except choice.

Danny turned the television back on instead.

The next report was not about the capes.

An aerial photograph showed MedHall's western service wing with a red box drawn around the locked room Taylor had found through a fly. A second image showed the loading dock after investigators opened the false wall beside it. Banker boxes, portable drive duplicators, industrial shredders, and two rolling fireproof cabinets had been arranged behind the wall on a track leading directly to the dock.

"Investigators now believe the apparent hostage escape demand concealed an effort to remove and destroy MedHall corporate records," the anchor said. "Sources close to the inquiry confirm that a state forensic audit of MedHall's community-outreach subsidiaries was scheduled to begin this morning. The records were under a preservation order and could not legally be moved or destroyed."

The screen changed to a diagram.

The locked equipment room sat beside the archive. Kaiser's hidden corridor connected both to the sound booth and loading dock. The west and north charges bracketed the archive wing. The armored vehicle demanded during negotiations would have arrived at the only door from which the fireproof cabinets could be rolled out without crossing a public camera.

The terrorists were supposed to leave with the evidence.

Max Anders was supposed to be rescued from the wreckage as their most famous hostage.

If the vehicles were stopped, the charges would destroy the archive and the people positioned beside it. Either outcome erased the audit trail. One preserved Kaiser as a civic survivor. The other gave him a public tragedy from which to demand broader security powers and emergency control over the charities whose records had vanished.

"Authorities have recovered partial ledgers connecting MedHall grants to a network of real-estate and security contractors," the anchor continued. "Several are now alleged Empire Eighty-Eight fronts. Investigators have not announced a total, but one source described the diverted funds as being in the eight figures."

Danny stared at the diagram.

"The loading dock," he said.

Taylor remembered Anders stepping into the cold corridor for a private exchange. Something passed hand to hand while security explained it away as a discreet pledge.

"Access credential," she said. "Or the preservation seal. Whatever opened the archive."

And you saw the handoff before any of it started, McClane said. Didn't know what it meant. Knew it didn't fit.

The reporter outside MedHall held up a scorched strip of paper inside an evidence sleeve. "Investigators say the operation began earlier than planned after Anders's security team detected unauthorized surveillance inside the gala. As a result, most of the archive had not yet been transferred when the takeover began."

Taylor had not merely interrupted the siege.

She had forced Kaiser to choose between abandoning the reason for it and beginning before he could make the evidence disappear.

He had chosen control.

It had cost him both.

Marcus Thorne appeared outside Brockton Bay General with a bandage across his forehead and a fresh notebook in his hand.

"Mr. Thorne," a reporter called, "you were one of the five hostages placed beside an explosive device. Did Max Anders speak to you before the takeover?"

Thorne looked directly into the cameras.

"I gave his staff information about a young guest I believed had witnessed a newsworthy exchange. I did that in return for access to Anders. I did not understand the danger, but failure to understand does not remove responsibility. That guest and her father later saved my life. I will not identify either of them."

He closed the notebook.

"The Bulletin will publish a full account of MedHall's financial and political relationships. My byline will not appear on it."

The clip ended.

Danny leaned back. "Didn't expect that."

Neither did I, McClane admitted.

Taylor watched the next segment assemble the night from public pieces. Guests described insects forming arrows through dark hallways. A firefighter described the east doors opening against a giant's weight. The defense attorney told cameras that a teenage girl in a green dress had understood the building better than every adult inside it and then refused to give her name.

The councilwoman spoke from a hospital entrance with her bruised cheek uncovered. She described returning to the hostage floor on purpose and stopped a reporter who tried to call it courage.

"It was a job somebody had to do," she said. "The young woman inside had already done six impossible jobs. I managed one ordinary one."

The catering supervisor gave an interview beside three employees who had refused transport. He told the cameras about the second kitchen route. The dishwasher stood at the edge of the group and said nothing until a reporter asked whether he had seen Tower.

"Saw a kid trying to survive," he answered. "That's all you need."

The anesthesiologist did not speak to reporters. A hospital representative confirmed that she had refused to leave her patient until firefighters reached the clinic and had continued treatment through the north-support detonation.

Luis Ortega appeared only as a still photograph taken before the operation. The PRT statement credited him with maintaining interior communications after injury. It did not mention that a barefoot teenager had pulled him out of Hookwolf's stairwell.

That omission felt less like theft than shelter.

The footage showed the chandelier falling from four angles.

On the third replay, the network added dramatic music.

"Oh, now they find a soundtrack department," Gladly said.

Fury told him to shut up, but without much force.

Below the spectacle, the city began reacting to what the archive contained.

The MedHall board suspended six executives before breakfast. State investigators sealed three subsidiary offices. Police and PRT teams served warrants on two private-security firms and a waterfront construction company before employees could reach work. A federal agency announced it was joining the financial investigation and declined to explain why its representatives had already been in Brockton Bay before the siege began.

The Dockworkers Association issued a statement demanding an independent review of every MedHall-linked contract on the waterfront. Danny read his own organization's name in the scrolling text and blinked.

"They work fast when I'm unconscious."

"You weren't unconscious."

"Felt like it."

The Empire did not disappear with Kaiser in custody. News footage showed spray-painted threats outside MedHall clinics and police tape around a storefront damaged before dawn. Hookwolf, Cricket, Fenja, and Menja remained free. The respectable structure Anders had built was cracking in public, but the people who had enforced it still had weapons.

Victory did not make the next problem smaller.

It made the next problem visible.

What got out was spectacular and incomplete.

The public knew Kaiser had planned the building control in advance. It knew E88 capes had guarded the hostages. It knew an inside caller had corrected the official response. It knew Max Anders had ordered a charge detonated while pretending to be one of the people it threatened.

It knew the siege had been camouflage for the MedHall archive removal and that Taylor's premature discovery had forced the operation to begin before the records could be taken.

It did not know Taylor controlled the insects.

It did not know John McClane existed behind her eyes.

It did not know Danny had told Kaiser to go through him without ever using those words.

Those parts remained in the living room.

"The PRT will come back," Danny said.

"I know."

"And the Empire people who got away."

"I know."

"You were doing this before last night. The bugs."

Taylor pulled the sweatshirt sleeves over her hands.

"Yeah."

Danny nodded as if confirming something he had already worked out in the hospital corridor.

"And John?"

McClane went still.

Taylor could have lied. The previous night had taught her a hundred better ways to do it.

"Still here."

Danny looked toward the empty chair beside her, guessed wrong, then adjusted his gaze back to Taylor.

"Tell him thank you."

Tell him he did pretty good himself.

"He says you did pretty good yourself."

Danny's mouth moved around a smile he was not ready to let become one.

The television returned to MedHall. Dawn illuminated the broken glass and wet stone. Investigators in white coveralls moved through the atrium with evidence markers. The building no longer looked like a stage set waiting to be destroyed.

It looked like a place that had survived being used for what it was built to hide.

Taylor watched workers carry the Max Anders nameplate out through the loading dock.

That, more than the arrest footage, made the ending feel real.
