Hard Landing

The roof access door came down three flights of stairs.

Taylor felt its path through insects scattered across the north side of MedHall: the charge lifting steel out of its frame, the door striking the first landing edge, turning once, and crushing the railing beneath it. The blast blew roof gravel through the opening in a black wave. Smoke followed.

Two PRT troopers had entered the stair enclosure before it detonated.

The first went over the railing with the door. His safety line caught after twelve feet and slammed him into the concrete wall. The second fell backward onto the roof, one leg trapped beneath twisted framing.

Hookwolf entered the stairwell from below.

Cricket stepped onto the roof.

Ambush, McClane said. One team split into two problems, and each problem gets a cape.

The radio dissolved into overlapping voices.

"Roof Two is down!"

"North access compromised. We have an interior blast."

"All units hold positions."

"Roof One, report."

Miss Militia cut through them. "Tower, status of hostile movement?"

Taylor keyed twice, then realized yes or no meant nothing.

She transmitted openly. "Cricket on roof. Hookwolf in north stairs. Two PRT down, one hanging inside, one pinned outside."

The sound-board operator answered before Miss Militia could. "Your inside source has now compromised the safety of your personnel twice. Silence her channel, or the next consequence will be taken from the hostages."

He means your dad, McClane said.

Taylor was already moving toward the north service stairs.

"Tower, do not approach," Miss Militia said. "Recovery team is staging."

Taylor counted the people staging outside. Counted Hookwolf's progress upward. Counted the rise and fall of the hanging trooper's chest through a fly caught in his harness.

They won't reach him first.

No, McClane said.

"Tower, acknowledge."

Taylor turned the radio off.

That's gonna hurt the relationship.

You turned yours off too.

Yeah. Usually right before doing something everybody told me not to do. I'm not criticizing. I'm recognizing the move.

Taylor put weight on her right foot and nearly fell.

The ankle had swollen beneath the strap of her shoe. She crouched in the fire passage, removed both shoes, and pushed the right one beneath a radiator where no pursuing guard would notice it. A length of torn table linen became a compression wrap. McClane talked her through crossing it over the joint, pulling tight beneath the heel, and knotting it above the swelling.

Can you walk?

Taylor stood. Pain climbed her leg, but the joint held.

Yes.

That's not what I asked.

It's the answer we have time for.

She kept the left shoe on for four steps before the uneven height made the injured ankle worse. That one went beneath a linen cart. She ran barefoot toward the stairs, the marble cold and gritty beneath her feet.

The north corridor had been cleared of staff. Emergency lighting painted the floor in alternating red and shadow. Taylor ran with one hand against the wall and her swarm thirty feet ahead.

The blast had shaken the entire northern face of MedHall. Ceiling dust hung beneath the emergency lights. A sprinkler pipe had split near the stair enclosure and sprayed one wall in a silver fan. Water carried plaster, roof gravel, and a thread of blood down the corridor toward Taylor.

In the atrium, the hostages had thrown themselves flat when the explosion hit. Guards hauled them back into rows while the sound-board operator announced that the Protectorate had attacked the building. Anders helped the councilwoman sit up. He brushed white dust from her shoulder with the tenderness of a man cameras were meant to trust.

In the clinic, Danny braced both hands over the injured donor as a light fixture swung above the table. The anesthesiologist shouted for the guard to unlock the door. He did not.

Taylor felt all of it and kept moving.

Hookwolf reached the landing beneath the dangling trooper. The man hung unconscious, helmet gone, rifle suspended from its sling. Hookwolf extended one bladed arm toward the safety line.

Taylor sent every insect in the stairwell into the smoke detector above him.

The local horn sounded.

Hookwolf looked up. Not startled. Irritated.

The stairwell fire doors released from their magnetic catches. Three floors of weighted steel swung closed at once. The door above Hookwolf struck his extended arm and wedged against the blades. The door below shut cleanly, dividing him from the rest of the stairs.

It would not hold him. It did not need to.

Taylor entered on the ground floor, climbed one flight, and found the safety line anchored around the central rail. The trooper hung above her in drifting dust.

His line creaked each time his body turned. One glove dragged against the wall, leaving a black mark in the concrete dust. A laminated identification tab had torn loose from his vest and hung against his throat.

ORTEGA, L.

Taylor sent flies across his face. Air moved through his nose. Blood ran from his hairline, crossed one closed eyelid, and dropped into the open stairwell.

Can you lower him?

Not with that knot.

Don't untie it. Rail's already bent. Use the buckle on the harness to shorten the drop, then cut the anchor when you've got his weight.

Taylor climbed onto the damaged railing. The open stairwell fell beneath her farther than she allowed herself to measure. She reached the trooper's shoulder, caught the rear handle of his armor, and pulled him close enough to hook one arm through the harness.

He outweighed her by at least eighty pounds.

Her wrapped ankle trembled on the rail. She tried to take his weight and the first pull moved him less than an inch. The harness handle cut into her palm. The safety line drew taut above them and rotated Ortega away from the landing.

Use the turn, McClane said. Don't fight it. Swing him.

Taylor let the line carry him out, then pulled when he came back. His shoulder struck the landing edge. She caught the armor before he rebounded. On the second swing his chest reached concrete. On the third she got his upper body across the lip.

The railing shifted beneath her foot.

One anchor bolt tore free with a sharp metallic crack.

Taylor dropped flat across the landing and held Ortega by the harness while the rail leaned into the open shaft. Her injured foot slipped between two bars. For a second it carried both of them.

She screamed once, through closed teeth, and hauled until Ortega's weight came off her ankle.

The fire door above screamed. Hookwolf's arm widened inside it. Steel peeled around him.

The alarm horn continued above the tearing metal, absurdly small and orderly. Smoke detector. Fire door release. Please use the nearest marked exit. A system built to move civilians through an emergency kept announcing rules to a man who had turned himself into a demolition tool.

Taylor found the harness adjuster and hauled. The line shortened by inches. The trooper rose until his boots touched the landing edge.

"Wake up," she whispered. "Please wake up."

He did not.

She got one boot onto concrete, then the other. His weight shifted from the open shaft to Taylor all at once and drove her to both knees.

The fire door tore free.

Cut and drag, McClane said.

Taylor drew the trauma shears she had kept from the security office. The safety line resisted the first bite. She sawed twice, three times, while Hookwolf stepped through the ruined frame above.

The cord parted.

Taylor dragged the trooper through the door below and pulled its manual latch. Hookwolf struck the other side before she cleared the landing. The whole frame bowed inward.

The impact drove the latch tongue halfway out of its socket. Taylor planted both bare feet against the stair edge and pulled Ortega by the shoulder straps. His armor caught against the door closer. Hookwolf hit the frame again. Concrete dust jumped from every seam.

Taylor sent beetles into the closer arm and packed the hinge until its joint refused to fold. The door could no longer open far enough for Hookwolf's largest blades. A narrow spike punched through beside Taylor's face and swept sideways, searching.

She flattened herself over Ortega. The blade passed above her back, cut through his rifle sling, and carved a groove across the landing wall.

The rifle fell between them.

Taylor caught it before it slid through the gap beneath the door.

The trooper's rifle caught on the threshold. Taylor freed it, considered leaving it, then took it because leaving a weapon for Hookwolf felt worse.

She hauled the man down one stair at a time, his armor striking each edge. Her bugs spread through adjoining corridors looking for another route. The second-floor door opened into the mezzanine lounge, where armed guards now held the guests Taylor had tried to evacuate. The ground-floor door opened too close to the atrium.

Basement was the only choice.

Behind them, Hookwolf tore through the latched door.

He did not descend at once. Metal struck the ruined frame in short, controlled blows. Taylor understood why when her insects found him reducing the width of his transformed body to fit the stairwell without trapping himself. Even his violence had procedure.

That bought them one landing.

The trooper coughed.

"Easy," Taylor said. "You're inside MedHall."

His eyes opened without focusing. "Team?"

"One on the roof. Cricket has them."

He tried to rise. His left leg folded.

"Can't."

"Have to."

His voice belonged to someone much farther away than his body. He reached for the stair rail and closed his hand on air. Taylor caught his wrist.

"Your name is Ortega. You're in the north stairs. Your safety line caught you. I need you to look at me."

His eyes found her, lost her, then returned.

"Civilian?"

"Yes."

"You're bleeding."

Taylor looked down. The sole of her left foot had opened somewhere between the kitchen and the stairs. She had not felt it separately from the ankle.

"So are you. Up."

Taylor put his arm over her shoulders. Together they reached the basement landing as metal struck concrete above.

The recovered trooper found the radio control on his vest. "Roof Two to command. Inside, mobile with assistance. Roof One pinned. Hookwolf north stairs."

Static answered first. Then three people began speaking over one another.

"Roof Two, authenticate."

"Medical status?"

"Do not use the north stairs."

Ortega squeezed his eyes shut. "Challenge seven. Response Hawthorne. Possible concussion, left knee unstable. I have Tower with me."

Miss Militia answered. "Identity of assistance?"

The trooper looked at Taylor.

She shook her head.

"Tower," he said.

Silence held the channel for one beat.

"Understood," Miss Militia said. "Follow Tower's directions."

Relationship might survive, McClane said.

Taylor guided the trooper into the mechanical corridor and hid him with the MedHall guards in the laundry room. One guard took the rifle. The other began cutting away the trooper's pant leg.

The laundry had ceased being a hiding place and become a casualty station. A catering worker lay beneath two tablecloths with an oxygen mask held over her face. One of MedHall's guards sat against a dryer with his dislocated shoulder strapped to his chest. Towels had been sorted into clean stacks, bloodied stacks, and strips ready for bandages.

Ortega stopped at the sight of them.

"How many?"

"Eight here," the older guard said. "More upstairs."

"Fourteen catering in the kitchen," Taylor added. "Twenty-one in the mezzanine lounge. Main group in the atrium."

Ortega looked at her bare, bleeding feet, the torn dress, the radio at her waist, and finally the insect-drawn map unfolded across a laundry hamper.

"You made this?"

"Most of it."

He sat because his knee stopped giving him a choice.

"Roof One," the trooper said again.

Taylor reached through the building.

Cricket had not killed the second member. She had removed his radio, bound his hands, and dragged him to the skylight above the atrium where the outside cameras could see him kneeling.

The sound-board operator spoke over the public channel. "You attempted entry. One of your personnel is now our prisoner. Five civilians will be moved to the demolition points. Any further approach will be answered at one of those points."

Armed men entered the hostage rows and selected people.

They did not choose randomly. Taylor saw the pattern as soon as the first guard pointed. Each person had already made trouble, held influence, or mattered to someone who had.

The first was the defense attorney who had objected to the phone search.

The second was the councilwoman.

The third was Marcus Thorne.

The fourth was the injured man's wife.

The fifth was Danny.

The guard from the clinic unlocked the treatment room and crooked two fingers at him.

"You're going back out."

Danny looked at the donor on the table. The anesthesiologist had closed the scalp wound with adhesive strips, but his skin had gone gray beneath the blood.

"He needs an ambulance."

"He needed you to follow directions."

The guard caught Danny above the elbow. Danny did not pull away. He took one step, stopped beside the anesthesiologist, and placed the unopened dressing in her hand before allowing himself to be moved.

In the atrium, the five were made to stand in a line while everyone else remained on the floor. The injured man's wife looked toward the clinic door and asked whether her husband was alive. No one answered. Thorne tried to move between her and the nearest weapon. A guard struck him behind the knee and forced him down.

Danny found Anders watching from the front row.

"You," Danny said.

Anders let confusion cross his face for the cameras.

The guard shoved Danny toward the west support.

They took him toward the west support beneath the mezzanine, the charge Armsmaster had not seen on camera until Taylor reported it.

Anders remained seated with the hostages. His face showed horror at exactly the level the cameras required.

His fingers did not move.

They did not need to. The order had already been given.

Taylor turned her radio back on.

"Miss Militia."

"Tower."

"My father is one of the five."

The admission left the laundry room silent.

Miss Militia answered without asking Taylor's age, name, or why a civilian was doing any of this.

"Then we get all five away from those charges. Tell me what you need."

Taylor looked at the paper map, at the wounded trooper, at the stolen rifle, and at the trauma shears that had nearly failed on one safety line.

"I need the building plans," she said. "The real ones. Electrical, ventilation, alarms, every service route. And I need you to stop listening to Anders."

Outside, Armsmaster began to object.

Miss Militia cut across him on the channel.

"Plans transmitting now."
