Chapter 20 of 29

Approach Control

The first voice from outside arrived through the radio Taylor had stolen.

"MedHall command, this is Armsmaster of the Brockton Bay Protectorate. We have established a perimeter. State your demands and release any injured civilians requiring medical attention."

The transmission was calm, amplified, and clean enough that Taylor could hear the faint electronic edge added by a helmet microphone. It was followed by silence throughout the building. Guards stopped in corridors. Cricket paused on the mezzanine. Even the hostages seemed to feel that someone outside had finally named what was happening, though the radio words could not have reached them.

Max Anders lowered his head among the captives and tapped two fingers against his knee.

The man nearest the atrium's sound board lifted his radio.

"Protectorate, your people will remain beyond the outer drive. Any attempt to enter the building will be treated as an attack on the civilians inside. We will provide demands when we are ready."

His voice had been flattened through a modulator. The words came out sexless and mechanical.

There, McClane said. That's the front man. Everybody outside talks to him. Everybody inside takes orders from the hostage sitting on the floor.

Taylor crouched behind a stack of banquet chairs in a storage room off the western service hall. The radio rested against one ear. Through her bugs, MedHall had become a diagram drawn in people.

One hundred and sixty-three hostages sat or knelt in the atrium. Twenty-one more were held in the mezzanine lounge after the service-stair group Taylor had freed ran into a locked fire door below. Fourteen catering workers waited in a kitchen cold room with the refrigeration turned off. Seven people, including the two security guards, hid in the basement laundry.

Danny sat near the stage with the injured man's head in his lap.

Taylor counted again.

The numbers did not change.

You keep recounting, you're gonna get the same answer, McClane said.

I might have missed someone.

Then count routes, not people. People stay alive if the routes work.

It was the kind of advice that sounded heartless until she understood it. McClane was not telling her to care less. He was telling her where caring could become useful.

Taylor unfolded a linen inventory sheet on the floor and began drawing MedHall from memory. Atrium at center. Grand stairs northeast. Kitchen and loading dock west. Laundry beneath the southeast service corridor. Mechanical room below the stage. She marked locked doors with Xs and groups of hostages with circles.

Three exits from the atrium, McClane said. Main doors, east doors, loading corridor. Main's covered from the mezzanine. East is covered by the two big ones. Loading corridor puts everybody past the kitchen and through a door narrow enough to turn a crowd into a pile.

There are fire stairs from the mezzanine.

Locked below. We learned that one.

Parking bridge.

Cricket owns it.

Taylor drew another X.

On the radio, Armsmaster answered the modulated voice. "We need proof of life and a head count."

"You will receive both."

"We also received a partial emergency call identifying Empire Eighty-Eight capes inside the building."

Every guard channel went silent.

Taylor stopped drawing.

The modulated voice replied, "Then you understand the cost of testing us."

Not a denial.

Not confirmation either.

He just took your warning and made it part of his threat, McClane said. That's good. Means he's fast. Also means the people outside still don't know who's wearing which hat.

A second voice entered the channel, lower than Armsmaster's and female.

"Inside caller, if you can hear this frequency, key your radio twice. Do not speak."

Taylor stared at the transmit button.

Trap? she asked.

Maybe. Could be the bad guys checking whether the stolen set's still live. Could be somebody outside figured out their radio traffic is getting repeated.

"Inside caller," the woman said again. "Two clicks only."

Taylor pressed twice.

Across the building, six radios answered with bursts of static as men shifted their hands toward transmit buttons and stopped. The sound was too small to identify a set.

"Received," the woman said. "My name is Miss Militia. Keep this channel. Do not answer again until I address you as Tower."

I like her, McClane said.

You heard two sentences.

First one didn't get you killed. Second gave you a code word. That's a strong opening.

The gallery had gone quiet. No wagers. No reviewer's voice. Taylor was grateful for it.

She folded the map into quarters and put it inside her dress at the waist. Then she moved.

The storage room opened onto a staff corridor running behind the atrium wall. Decorative panels separated it from the main room. During the gala, servers had passed through concealed doors with trays. Now armed men used the same route to move behind the hostages without crossing their sightlines.

Taylor crawled beneath a row of garment racks and sent bugs ahead.

At the far end of the corridor, two men rolled something heavy toward the atrium. Not weapons. Black equipment cases, each the size of a footlocker, drawn from the locked room her reconnaissance had found earlier.

They opened the first case behind the stage.

Inside were shaped charges, wire, and three compact control units.

McClane went still.

Those aren't for the doors.

How do you know?

Too much charge. Wrong placement. They're putting one against the building columns.

Taylor watched a man lift a gray block from the foam lining and carry it toward the west support beneath the mezzanine.

Why would they bring down their own building?

They might not intend to. Explosives do two jobs in a hostage situation. They can blow something up, or they can make the people outside believe you will. Right now he needs everybody staring at the detonator while he does whatever tonight was built to do.

You said he was reacting to us.

The lockdown was. The hardware wasn't.

The conclusion changed the shape of the night again. Kaiser had not merely kept dangerous people near him at a gala. He had prepared MedHall to be seized. Taylor and McClane had tripped the plan early, or forced him to change its purpose, but the cases had been waiting behind the locked door long before the first bug crossed the loading corridor.

There had always been a siege coming.

They had only made it start before Kaiser was ready.

One of the men with the equipment cases turned toward Taylor's corridor.

She flattened herself beneath the garment bags. Polished shoes approached, stopped close enough that she could see water beaded along the soles from the atrium floor.

The man keyed his radio. "Found movement behind west service."

Taylor held every bug still.

"Human?"

"Not sure. Door moved."

The muzzle of his weapon pushed between two hanging coats.

Taylor sent three cockroaches across the opposite wall.

The man's eyes tracked them. His gun followed.

She came out behind him, caught the handle of the equipment case, and shoved it into the backs of his knees. He fell across the lid. Taylor ran before he finished turning, through the concealed service door and into the atrium itself.

For one terrible second there was nowhere to hide.

Hostages covered the floor in ordered rows. Fenja stood near the east doors. Alabaster faced the stage. Armed men watched every aisle. The room Taylor had spent all night learning now offered no empty space at all.

Then Danny stood up.

"He needs a doctor," he shouted, pointing at the unconscious man beside him. "You want proof of life for the people outside, start by keeping him alive."

Every head near the stage turned toward him.

Taylor crossed behind the last row of hostages, bent nearly double, and slipped beneath a banquet table whose cloth still reached the floor.

He didn't know he was covering you, McClane said.

I know.

Still counts.

Anders rose among the captives with both hands visible. "He's right. This man needs help. Whatever point you're trying to make, a dead donor won't improve it."

The armed men watched their supposed hostage speak. One looked toward the sound-board operator for instruction.

Anders touched his thumb to the second joint of his forefinger.

The operator nodded. "Bring the injured man forward. The union representative comes with him."

Taylor's stomach dropped.

Danny helped lift the injured man. Another hostage took the legs. They carried him toward the stage under three guns.

Anders stepped aside to make room, gracious even now.

As Danny passed him, Anders said something too low for human hearing.

A fly clinging beneath his collar caught it.

"Your daughter has made this evening much more complicated than it needed to be."

Danny missed a step.

Taylor stopped breathing.

Anders smiled for the room and took the injured man's hand as cameras near the stage came alive for the promised proof-of-life feed.

"Tower," Miss Militia said through the radio hidden against Taylor's ribs. "If you can hear me, we need your threat map."

Taylor watched her father realize that the respected man beside him knew exactly who she was.

We need a new route, she thought.

McClane's answer came hard and immediate.

No. We need to take his building away from him.
