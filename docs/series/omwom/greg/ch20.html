Old Friend

Bra'tac arrived through the gate two hours after Hammond's briefing ended, summoned by a message Teal'c had sent the moment he'd been released from the room, and the old Jaffa's face, when Teal'c met him in the gate room, already carried the stillness of someone who'd heard the news before he'd finished walking through the event horizon.

::hey wait! I know this guy! I KNOW this guy!::

::You are mistaken,:: Teal'c began. ::That is Bra'tac. An old friend, and a genuinely real one.::

::No, I mean it, this is THE guy. The desert. The whole 'you require discipline, not to silence what you feel, to aim it' thing. Even the stick joke, and yeah, I know how weird it is that a four-hundred-year-old warrior is the one who made the stick joke, believe me, I've had time to sit with that. Gladly said literally one line. 'That didn't turn out like you thought it would, did it?' That's it. That's his whole contribution. Everything else, the entire recruitment, the real speech, 'Mr. Veder,' all of it, was him. I just never had a face to put with the voice before. Now I do.::

Teal'c said nothing for a moment, weighing the claim rather than dismissing it outright. ::Then something wore his likeness so completely there was no meaningful difference,:: he said slowly. ::Bra'tac himself has never once left this world, let alone stood in whatever room received you. Whatever spoke with his voice was not him. But it was, by your account, most of what actually happened that night.::

::...that's not less weird, by the way. That's so much weirder.::

::Indeed,:: Teal'c said, and found he had no better answer to offer than that single word, not yet.

"Old friend." Bra'tac's grip on his forearm was as strong as it had ever been, four hundred years of a life that had outlasted more gods than most Jaffa lived to learn the names of, and for a long moment neither of them let go, standing in the gate room saying nothing at all, because there was too much sitting on the other side of four years to trust to the first thing either of them tried to say to it.

"You look older," Teal'c said finally, which was not remotely what he meant, and Bra'tac's laugh, when it came, was rough with the exact recognition Teal'c had been reaching for.

"I have looked older since before your grandfather's grandfather drew breath, Teal'c. You, on the other hand, look like a man sleeping less than I do, which I did not believe possible for anyone still drawing Tau'ri rations." Bra'tac studied him properly now, the way he studied everything, missing nothing. "Four years. I will admit there were nights I was not certain I would live to see this one."

"Nor was I certain of it, for you. Your channels went quiet for the better part of a year, once. I assumed the worst for most of it."

"The worst very nearly happened. I will spare you the telling of exactly how narrowly it did not, it is not a pleasant one, and I do not intend to spend this reunion on old wounds." Bra'tac's grip finally eased, though he did not step back, not yet. "Rya'c. What do you actually know of him. Truthfully. Not what you hope is true."

Teal'c considered how to answer that honestly, and found the honest answer smaller than he wanted it to be. "Less than a father should know. Apophis permits no contact with that household, and I have had no reliable channel into it in four years. I know that he lives. I know almost nothing else with any real confidence."

Something shifted in Bra'tac's face, old grief giving way to something more careful. "Then let me be the one telling you something, for once, instead of the one asking." He held Teal'c's gaze a moment, weighing how much of it was kind to give all at once. "My own channels reach further into that household than yours currently can. He is well, so far as such reports can ever be trusted. Larger than you would expect. Still furious with you, by every account that has reached me, though there are whispers, more recent ones, that the fury has begun bending into something else. Grief, perhaps. Or simply the ordinary business of growing older."

Teal'c needed a moment. Four years of rumors and silence had not prepared him to hear even this much from a source he trusted.

"Furious is not the worst thing a son can be toward a father who left him behind for reasons the boy was too young to understand at the time," Bra'tac said, quieter now, old grief worn smooth by long handling. "I raised his father to be exactly that stubborn. I cannot fault the boy for having inherited it honestly."

They spoke for a long while like that, standing in the gate room well after the wormhole had disengaged behind them, four years of separated lives narrowing back down into something that still, despite everything, fit together the way it always had: Bra'tac's rebellion, scattered cells across a dozen worlds, more losses than victories most seasons but more victories than anyone four years ago would have believed possible; Teal'c's own strange new life among the Tau'ri, a world that had somehow, against every reasonable expectation, become something closer to home than Chulak had felt like in years. Neither man wasted the reunion on things that could wait. Both understood, without needing to say it aloud, that neither of them could promise there would be a next one.

Eventually they moved to the empty commissary. Bra'tac heard the full account and sat back as if the arithmetic had offended him.

"I knew Drey'auc," he said, quietly. "Before you did, in fact, when she was still a girl training alongside her father's household guard, sharper with a blade than most boys twice her age. I did not think, when you married her, that I was watching a woman walk into a cage. I have thought it a great deal, in the years since."

::oh man, Bra'tac knew her? Like actually knew her, not just knew of her?::

"He did," Teal'c said aloud, answering both of them at once, a habit he had stopped trying to hide from Bra'tac some time ago. Bra'tac's eyebrow rose, the smallest fraction, the only outward sign the old Jaffa ever gave that something had genuinely surprised him.

"You are speaking to someone else. Now. While we sit here. Alone."

"I am."

Bra'tac's gaze moved to the empty air beside Teal'c's shoulder. Four centuries had left him difficult to startle and almost impossible to frighten, but for one second he looked unsteady. He found nothing there. He looked again anyway.

Bra'tac studied him a long moment, a teacher's scrutiny, checking a student's work for a flaw he already half-suspected was there, and then, to Teal'c's genuine surprise, nodded once, slowly, as though a piece of a puzzle he'd been quietly assembling since walking through the gate had just clicked into place.

"This explains the fighting style Va'lar's men reported," Bra'tac said. "I heard whispers through my own channels before your General's briefing reached me. A First Prime who fights like a green recruit for part of an engagement and then, in the same breath, like himself again. I did not know what to make of it. Now I do."

"You are not troubled by it."

"I am many things, Teal'c. Troubled is rarely one of them, this late in a life like mine." Bra'tac's mouth twitched, not quite a smile, close enough to one that Teal'c recognized it instantly. "I have carried a symbiote in this body for four hundred years and called it a partnership when it suited me to. I am hardly positioned to judge you for carrying something stranger, particularly when the stranger thing appears to have kept you alive."

Bra'tac was quiet a moment after that, studying him with the same unhurried attention he had once used to judge whether a much younger Teal'c was ready to hold a staff weapon in earnest. "You have not, in this whole telling, once spoken of him as a burden. Nor as a curiosity, nor as a problem your Tau'ri physicians might one day solve for you."

"He is not a burden." Teal'c held his old teacher's gaze. "He is Sha'reth kanar to me, Bra'tac. I do not say that lightly, and I have said it of very few men in my life. I say it of him without hesitation."

Bra'tac went very still.

"You have known this boy," he said finally, "a matter of months. I have known men forty years and never once found cause to say that word of them."

"I am aware of how it sounds."

"I did not say it sounded wrong." Something crossed the old Jaffa's face, complicated, closer to wonder than to doubt. "I taught you that word decades before you had any use for it, and I have watched you carry it carefully ever since, spending it perhaps twice in a lifetime I have shared the greater part of. I did not expect to hear you spend it a third time, on a stranger who arrived by means neither of us understands, inside a body he did not ask to share. And yet here we are."

"Does that trouble you?"

"No." Bra'tac's answer came without hesitation of his own. "It reminds me why I taught you the word at all. Not to be hoarded. To be recognized, when it is true, regardless of how strange the circumstances of its truth turn out to be."

::I like him. I really like him.::

::He tends to have that effect,:: Teal'c said, and felt something loosen slightly in his chest, a tension he hadn't fully registered carrying until it eased.
