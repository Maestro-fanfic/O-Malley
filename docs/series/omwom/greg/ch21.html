Bra'tac's Warning

They were still in the same corner of the same emptied-out commissary, the same two cooling cups of something neither of them had actually finished drinking sitting between them, when Bra'tac set the last of the personal business aside and let the rest of why he'd actually come take its place.

"Enough of old wounds and old grandsons, for a moment." Bra'tac's voice shifted, the warmth still present underneath but something harder settling over it now, the tone Teal'c remembered from a hundred briefings before battles that had not all gone well. "Tell me what your Tau'ri intelligence has given you of the compound. I will tell you what it has missed."

Teal'c laid it out as he had for Hammond that morning: the fortified perimeter, the household holdings consolidated into a single location for the first time in years, the tunnel network Carter's orbital imagery had flagged but could not fully map from above ground alone.

"The tunnels are real," Bra'tac said, once he'd finished. "Older than your officers likely believe. I have walked them myself, decades past, in service to a master whose name I no longer speak aloud if I can help it. They were dug in the years siege warfare briefly came back into fashion across a dozen worlds, a rival System Lord's doing, long before Apophis ever inherited the compound and everything beneath it. Three primary tunnels, if memory serves, though memory is permitted to have grown imprecise across so many decades. One runs directly beneath the household wing, the most heavily used, and so the most heavily watched. The second serves the garrison's own supply needs, and will be the most heavily defended of the three, soldiers value nothing so highly as their own resupply. The third." He paused, weighing something. "The third was never meant to be found at all. An old evacuation route, dug for the compound's original masters, who trusted no ally enough to let them know every way out existed. I do not know if Apophis himself is even aware of it."

"You believe it still exists."

"I believe stone remembers longer than men do, and I have rarely been wrong to trust a tunnel over a rumor." Bra'tac's finger traced a rough shape in the dust on the tabletop between them, an old habit Teal'c had watched him fall into a thousand times before, a man who thought best with something moving under his hands. "If it still exists, and if Apophis's engineers have not since discovered and sealed it, it offers your team an approach no patrol will be watching for. A path even Hasshak could manage."

"Do not call him that." Teal'c said it plainly, without heat, but without room left in it for argument either. "It never fit him, and I will not sit here and let it stand unanswered, even said in jest, even by you."

Bra'tac's eyebrows rose, genuine surprise this time, not the practiced kind he wore for effect. "You defend him quickly."

"I defend him accurately." Teal'c held his gaze. "I have named O'Neill, Carter, and Daniel Jackson Sha'reth kanar, in the same breath I used for the boy tonight, without qualification, and without a single one of those namings weighed any less carefully than the one before it. And honestly, old friend, it is a name he never earned in the first place. My own perceptions painted him as such, in those first months, before I knew him as anything beyond an unfamiliar face leading an unfamiliar team. The fault was never his to earn his way out of. It was mine, for seeing him through eyes Apophis had spent a lifetime training to see humanity as beneath notice."

Bra'tac studied him a moment longer, and then, slowly, nodded, something like real respect settling into the gesture. "Then I will retire it as well. You have my word on that." He was quiet a beat, weighing something further. "I will be honest with you, in return for the honesty you have just given me. I do not believe I ever thought the word accurate either, not truly, not even the day I first used it. It was habit wearing the shape of judgment, nothing more, the kind of thing a man carries so long out of a life like the one I once served that he stops examining whether it was ever earned at all." He shook his head once, slowly. "I owe him better than habit. I suspect I have owed him that for some time."

"You owe him more than that."

Bra'tac's eyes found his, something sharpening behind the age in them, not offense exactly, but close kin to it. "I am aware of what I owe O'Neill considerably better than you are, Teal'c." A beat, the old, dry warning creeping back into his voice, the register of a teacher correcting a student who has forgotten exactly which of them taught which. "Atu'rel kel'tannen. Roughly: do not teach the grandfather his own staff-work. I did not require the lesson."

"I cannot promise you the tunnel remains passable, in any case," Bra'tac added. "Decades collapse tunnels that centuries sometimes spare. But it is worth confirming before you commit your team to the tunnels everyone expects you to use."

"There is a risk you are not accounting for," Bra'tac said, once the tactical portion of the conversation had run its course. "You are planning this rescue as a soldier. That is correct, and necessary, and I would expect nothing less of you. But you are also the husband and father in question, and a man cannot fully separate those two things no matter how much discipline he brings to the attempt. I have watched good men die because they tried."

"I am aware of the risk."

"Awareness and immunity are not the same thing, old friend." Bra'tac's hand found his shoulder, the same grip that had steadied him through a hundred smaller crises across two decades of knowing each other. "You have someone now who was not there before, watching from inside your own skull. Let him watch for the thing you cannot see in yourself. That may be the most useful thing he does for you in the whole of this."

Cut to the gallery.

A quiet officer's voice spoke into the pause. "The job doesn't make the man stop being a man. That's why somebody watches his back."

Nobody answered. It did not need one.

✳

::I can do that. I promise I can do that.::

"He says he can," Teal'c told Bra'tac, and for the first time since Hammond's briefing that morning, allowed himself something close to real hope.

"Good." Bra'tac rose, joints that had outlasted four centuries protesting only slightly, and clasped Teal'c's forearm again in farewell. "I will return before your team departs, with whatever further intelligence I can gather in the time we have. In the meantime, old friend, eat something. Sleep, if you can manage it. A man who walks into a fight already exhausted has already lost half the fight before it begins."

::he's not wrong. When's the last time you actually slept? Like real sleep, not the meditation thing?::

::Jaffa do not require sleep in the manner humans do,:: Teal'c said, ::though I confess even Kel-no-reem has offered little rest since the briefing.::

::yeah, that tracks. Okay. New rule. You're gonna at least try tonight. Actually try.::

"You have made a friend who worries about you," Bra'tac observed, one eyebrow raised again, watching Teal'c's face carefully enough to catch whatever passed across it in response.

"He has made something of a habit of it."

"Good," Bra'tac said again, and this time he did smile, brief and genuine, before turning toward the gate room and whatever remained of his own preparations. "You have needed one, Teal'c, longer than you would ever have admitted to me directly. I am glad, whatever the manner of his arrival, that one finally found you."

Teal'c watched the old warrior's steady stride carry him away. Nothing about the situation had improved. It no longer felt quite as impossible.

::hey. For what it's worth. I think I like him more than I like most people I've met who are actually alive.::

::He engenders that reaction in most who meet him,:: Teal'c said. ::I have never entirely worked out how he manages it.::

::practice, probably. Four hundred years of it. And insults, which for reasons I do not comprehend produce the exact same result.::

::I can explain that one.::

::I do not follow.::

::Okay, so, human guys, and I mean this as an anthropological observation, not a personal confession: they show affection by being mean to each other, sometimes. Genuinely, unironically mean, in a way a total stranger would swear meant we hated each other, and it means basically the opposite. Calling your best friend an idiot, roasting him in front of everybody for something dumb he did, that's not hostility, that's a hug with extra steps. I watched you two do a whole version of it tonight and didn't even clock it until just now. He used the actual word "grandfather" about himself in the middle of basically dressing you down, and you didn't even blink. Both things happening at once. That's not a contradiction where I'm from. That's basically the whole operating system.::

::That seems needlessly inefficient.::

::Yeah, well, welcome to my species. We also high-five instead of hugging half the time, and once, in gym class, a guy told his best friend "I would die for you" by throwing a dodgeball at his head as hard as he possibly could. It's a whole thing. I don't make the rules. I just have to live inside them.::

::Perhaps,:: Teal'c allowed, and found, turning the thought over, that he did not entirely disagree.
