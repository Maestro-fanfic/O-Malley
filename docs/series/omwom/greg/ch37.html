Chapter 37 of 40

Indeed

Three weeks passed before anything about Teal'c's life resembled ordinary again, Drey'auc and Rya'c settling into quarters on base while the diplomatic and logistical questions of two refugees from another world worked their slow way through channels Teal'c had stopped pretending to understand the full workings of.

In the weeks since that late night in the lab, Greg's presence had become an ordinary fact of team life. Daniel still asked occasional questions about the mechanics, sometimes with more animation than he had shown since the P3X-866 glyph sequence. Carter had mostly stopped asking. She treated Greg as true, the way she treated any other fact about a teammate she trusted.

"For what it's worth," Carter said, one afternoon in the commissary, apropos of nothing in particular, "he still owes me that conversation. Properly. Not just secondhand through you."

::tell her I'd like that. I'd genuinely like that. We should actually do that one of these days.::

"He says he would like that as well," Teal'c relayed, and found himself, still, three weeks on, quietly pleased every time this particular exchange came around again.

::this is good, right? This is a good ending to this part.::

::It is a good ending to this part,:: Teal'c agreed. ::It is not the ending to all of it. I do not believe there will be one, not in the way endings usually mean. Only more of this. More days like today.::

::yeah. I think I'm actually okay with that. I wasn't sure I would be, when this whole thing started. I think I actually am.::

That evening, Teal'c sat with Drey'auc and Rya'c in the small apartment the SGC had arranged off base after the medical, security, and refugee-status interviews were complete. The address existed under a quiet Air Force cover, close enough to the mountain for regular check-ins and ordinary enough not to feel like another cell. It was the first real quiet the three of them had shared since the rescue. Rya'c was teaching himself the rules of a card game Daniel had shown him, badly and cheerfully, while Drey'auc watched with uncomplicated contentment.

"You are doing the thing again," Rya'c said, not looking up from his cards. "The listening-to-someone-who-isn't-here thing."

"I am."

"Does he have opinions about the game?"

::tell him his hand is terrible and he should fold.::

"He says your hand is terrible," Teal'c relayed, "and recommends folding."

Rya'c folded, laughing, the first real laugh Teal'c had heard out of his son since the rescue, and something in Teal'c's chest, still learning how to hold laughter after four years without much practice, ached in a way that had nothing to do with pain at all.

Later, alone, the apartment quiet, Teal'c sat by the window a while, no candle, no need for one, simply watching a sky that held a version of home now on both sides of a Stargate for the first time in four years.

::hey. Whatever happens next. However long this actually lasts, however weird it stays. I'm glad it was you. I don't think I've said that plainly enough, this whole time.::

"Indeed," Teal'c said, and let the single word carry everything four years of discipline had never quite taught him how to say in more of them, and found, for the first time since a stranger's gift arrived uninvited behind his eyes, that one word was, for once, entirely enough.
