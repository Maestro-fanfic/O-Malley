The First Handoff

Teal'c had never once, in his life, handed control of his own body to anyone. Not to Apophis, not to any master he had served before his defection, not even to Junior, whose low murmur he had spent years learning to hold at arm's length precisely so this exact moment would never come. But Va'lar had trained beside him for years before Teal'c ever walked away, had drilled against his exact style so many times that the two of them could have fought each other blind, and every motion Teal'c made now, however sound, was a motion Va'lar's men had already seen, already countered, already built an answer for. He had taken two hits proving it. He was going to take a third fighting the same way a fourth time.

There was one thing in this fight Va'lar's men had no answer prepared for at all, because it wasn't a technique, wasn't a style, wasn't anything a soldier could drill against: someone who didn't know how to fight, occupying a body that did.

There was a second reason, one he would admit only later. Directing a body through a fight left room for nothing but the next block, strike, and half second. Teal'c needed to see the whole ambush rather than merely survive its next instant, and he could not do that while his own hands were still moving.

Teal'c had a half second to decide, staff blasts scorching the ground close enough to feel the heat off them, and he made the decision the way he made every decision that mattered: fast, and without asking himself twice.

::wait, what, what are you...::

Now, Teal'c thought, with everything he had, and let go.

✳

Greg had never operated a body that wasn't, technically, dying at the time. This one was very much alive, six and a half feet of trained muscle responding to input he had no idea how to give, and his first attempt at forward sent them stumbling sideways into a tree hard enough to rattle teeth that weren't, strictly, his to rattle. A staff weapon he didn't remember picking up was somehow already in both hands, heavier than anything he'd ever held, and every instinct he actually possessed screamed at him to drop it and run, an instinct that turned out to be spectacularly wrong for a body built to do neither.

::okay okay okay how do arms work, how do THESE arms work, there's gotta be a, there's gotta be some kind of, oh my god is that a guy, there's a guy, why is there a guy right there...::

The guy right there was one of Va'lar's Jaffa, staff weapon already leveled, and the shot that should have ended things there missed by a margin wide enough to be almost insulting, because Greg, mid-panic, had flinched the entire body sideways a half second before the trigger pull, an utterly untrained, utterly graceless flail that no combat instructor alive would have taught and that happened, by pure accident, to save his life.

✳

It went on like that. Greg swung the staff weapon like a bat because that was the only frame of reference he had for holding a long stick with both hands, connected with exactly nothing on purpose, and twice put Teal'c's body flat on the ground avoiding blows that a single moment of actual training would have parried standing up. It should have gotten them both killed inside of ten seconds. It didn't, because Va'lar's Jaffa, disciplined and drilled and utterly unprepared for an opponent moving like nothing they had ever trained against, kept overcorrecting for attacks that weren't coming and missing the ones that were, thrown off by a fighting style that wasn't a style at all, just a terrified teenager doing whatever kept the pain away for one more second.

::I don't know what I'm doing, I really don't know what I'm doing, why isn't anybody stopping me, somebody should really be stopping me...::

Internally, Teal'c watched, and did the one thing he had bought himself the room to do: he counted. Six Jaffa at the start, one down at the treeline, one more Carter had taken from behind the log, leaving three, two flanking wide and one holding the center, a formation with a gap in it that would close again in seconds if nobody moved through it first. He had no hands to point with, no voice loud enough to carry the instruction the way he needed to. He had only the same channel Greg had been shouting through since the moment he'd arrived.

::there. The gap between the two on your left. Go now, not toward them, past them.::

Greg didn't answer, didn't have time to, but Teal'c felt the borrowed body react anyway, some fraction of the thought landing even through the panic, close enough to right to matter. The opening came from Carter a half second later, a shot from behind the fallen log that took one more of Va'lar's men off his feet, and Greg, already moving on the direction he'd barely registered receiving, threw the body bodily through the gap toward the trees, dragging two more Jaffa's attention with him and buying, by an accident that was not entirely an accident, exactly the chaos O'Neill needed to get Daniel moving toward the gate.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

A precise, mild voice cut in. "Directing a body you can't fully see through a fight you can't fully follow, with lives depending on it, never stops being terrifying."

Underneath him, something stitched together from a dozen borrowed tones added: "good... boy... he did... good."

GLADLY: I don't want that explained.

FURY: Wise.

✳

::give it back, give it back, I don't want this, I don't want to be the one who...::

Teal'c felt the wheel offered back before Greg finished the thought, an instinct as raw and untrained as everything else about him, panic reaching for the one thing it actually knew how to do, hand off the hard part. Teal'c took it without hesitation, and the body that had been flailing a half second ago became, in the space of a single step, exactly what it had always been: economical, precise, a First Prime's discipline reasserting itself all at once. Two more staff blasts, controlled, deliberate, and the line broke, Va'lar's remaining men falling back rather than pressing an advantage that had just gotten considerably more dangerous.

Va'lar himself was already gone, retreated with the others rather than risk being the one still standing when the rest of SG-1 reached the gate. That, too, felt like something Teal'c would need to think about later, when there was time.

There was not, yet, time.

"Go!" O'Neill was already moving, Daniel half-carrying Carter, and Teal'c fell in behind them, staff weapon steady in hands that had, until a few seconds ago, belonged entirely to someone else.

::I'm sorry. I'm so sorry, that was, I don't even know what that was, I probably made it worse, I definitely made it worse...::

"You did not," Teal'c said, quiet enough that only Greg would ever hear it, the gate's blue wash rising ahead of them. "You bought seconds none of us had. That is not nothing."

He did not say what it had cost to watch his own body move through a stranger's fear. That could wait until they were home.
