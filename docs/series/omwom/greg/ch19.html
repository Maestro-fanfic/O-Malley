Chapter 19 of 40

Word From Apophis

General Hammond called the team in on a Tuesday morning that had, up to that point, promised to be entirely unremarkable, and the moment Teal'c saw the briefing room's screen already lit with a tactical map of a sector he knew better than he knew his own quarters, he understood that unremarkable was no longer on offer.

"We picked this up through one of our off-world contacts three days ago," Hammond said, not wasting time on preamble, a habit Teal'c had always respected in him. "Confirmed independently yesterday. Apophis has relocated a significant portion of his household holdings, consolidated them into a single fortified compound rather than the scattered arrangement he's maintained for years."

"Households," Daniel repeated, already working the implication before anyone finished stating it. "As in, hostages. Leverage."

"As in leverage, yes." Hammond's gaze found Teal'c's and held there, not unkindly, but carrying the weight of a man about to say something he knew would land hard. "Including, according to our source, the wife and son of a First Prime who defected some years back."

Teal'c did not move. He had trained his whole life not to move in rooms exactly like this one, and the training held, even now, even here, even with the ground shifting under everything he had spent four years quietly, privately building toward.

::oh no. Oh no, this is because of us, isn't it. This is because of Va'lar.::

It was, Teal'c suspected, exactly that. He said nothing of the suspicion aloud, not yet, but he felt it settle into place the way a missing piece settles when a puzzle turns out to have been assembling itself without his notice.

"Do we know why now?" Carter asked. "Why consolidate now, specifically?"

"Our source wasn't able to confirm motive," Hammond said. "Only the fact of the move itself, and the timing. Roughly two weeks after a patrol engagement on P2X-908."

The room went very quiet.

::it's us. It's actually us. Va'lar must have reported something, right, something about how the fight went, and Apophis got nervous and pulled everybody in closer just in case...::

::I believe you are correct,:: Teal'c told him, silent, internal, the only channel available to him that wouldn't put words in front of five other people not yet meant to hear them. ::Va'lar witnessed a fighting style shift mid-engagement that should not have been possible. A report of that nature, reaching a god already inclined toward paranoia regarding his own First Prime's defection, would explain a move of exactly this kind.::

::this is my fault. This is actually my fault. If I hadn't panicked, if you hadn't had to hand it over...::

::You did not cause this,:: Teal'c said, and meant it fully, though he recognized, even as he said it, how hard a thing is to believe simply because it happens to be true. ::A god's paranoia requires no genuine cause to act upon. He would have found a reason eventually, with or without Va'lar's report. You gave him a reason sooner. That is not the same as giving him a reason he would not otherwise have found.::

✳

"Sir," O'Neill said, breaking the quiet before it could stretch further, "I'm gonna go ahead and guess this isn't just an FYI briefing."

"No, Colonel, it is not." Hammond's attention shifted, briefly, unmistakably, back to Teal'c. "I understand this touches you personally, Teal'c. I want to be straight with you about that before we go any further, and I want you to understand this briefing happens regardless of anything else, because a consolidated Goa'uld holding facility of this size is a strategic concern for this program whether or not your family happens to be inside it."

"I understand, General Hammond." Teal'c did understand. He also understood, in the same breath, that the strategic framing was a courtesy extended to let him keep some portion of his composure intact in front of people he respected, and he was, quietly, grateful for it, in a way he did not yet have the words to offer back.

"That said," Hammond went on, "I won't pretend the personal angle doesn't factor into how fast we're prepared to move on this. Colonel, I want options on my desk by 1800 hours. Full workup: approach, extraction, contingencies. This isn't a green light yet. It's a green light on planning."

"Yes, sir."

Carter pulled up the orbital imagery next, a compound built into the side of a ridge, walls that read as old, expanded on more than once over what looked like decades, defensive positions along the approach that suggested a garrison well beyond what a simple holding facility usually warranted. "Whatever else this is," she said, "it's not undefended. I'm reading emplacements consistent with at least a company-strength Jaffa presence, maybe more once you factor in whatever's inside the walls we can't see from orbit."

"Apophis doesn't do anything halfway," O'Neill said. "Least give him that."

"There is also this." Teal'c leaned forward, tapping a section of the ridge the imagery hadn't fully resolved. "A tunnel network beneath compounds of this design is standard, dating to a period when Apophis favored fortresses built to withstand siege rather than merely defend a perimeter. If it holds true here, there will be an approach beneath the walls that the garrison itself may not fully account for in its patrol patterns."

"You've been inside a compound like this before," Daniel said. It wasn't quite a question.

"Indeed. I have built compounds like this," Teal'c said.

No one asked him to elaborate.

The briefing continued a while longer after that, technical details, contingency branches, Carter and Daniel trading theories about the tunnel network's likely age and construction, and Teal'c absorbed all of it with the part of his attention built for exactly this kind of work, while the rest of him sat somewhere else entirely, in a fluorescent-lit kitchen he had never stood in and a doorway he had walked through four years ago without looking back.

::hey. You still with me?::

::I am here,:: Teal'c said. ::I am simply also, for the moment, several places at once.::

::yeah. Fair. Take your time. I'm not going anywhere.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

AN ENTHUSIAST: “I'm not going anywhere.” That's a good line.

FURY: It's a promise, not a line. Different thing.

THE WATCHER: The difference is whether he stays.

✳

The briefing ended a few minutes later, chairs scraping back, the ordinary business of a team dispersing to prepare for a mission that was not yet, technically, a mission. Teal'c lingered at the table a moment after the others had gone, staring at a tactical map of a place he had never seen from the outside, only ever from within, and found himself, for the first time in four years, genuinely uncertain whether hope or dread was the larger part of what he was feeling.

::can it be both, for you too?::

::Yes,:: Teal'c said, quietly, to no one else in the room. ::I believe it can.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

FURY: ...Huh.

GLADLY: Did he... hear us?

A silence that goes on a beat too long to be nothing.

FURY: No. And I would appreciate it if we did not knock on that door again.

THE WATCHER: What hairline?

FURY: Not helping.
