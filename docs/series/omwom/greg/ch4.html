Chapter 4 of 40

Kel-no-reem, Interrupted

P3X-866 gave up nothing more interesting than a set of ruins Daniel Jackson would be cheerfully cataloguing for the next three weeks, and SG-1 came home through the gate a few hours after local sunset, carrying the plain tiredness of a long, uneventful mission rather than anything sharper. Teal'c filed his report standing, as he always did, gave General Hammond the same handful of clipped, accurate sentences he gave after every mission, and retreated to his quarters the moment he was released.

He needed, more than he had needed anything in a very long time, to be alone with his own mind. Or as alone as that was still going to mean.

His quarters at the SGC held little enough to occupy anyone's attention: a bunk, a footlocker, a single candle set on the small shelf he used for exactly this purpose, and nothing else that spoke to a life beyond the mountain. He had never seen the need to acquire more. A First Prime learned early that possessions were leverage in someone else's hands before they were comfort in his own, and the habit had outlasted the reasons for it by a good many years now.

::okay so this is your room, this is a real room on a real military base, there's a candle, why is there a candle, is that a religious thing, is that a Jaffa thing, oh my god I have so many questions, do you guys have, like, a whole culture, obviously you have a culture, that was a dumb thing to think, sorry, I don't know why I said sorry, you can't even hear me say sorry, can you hear me say sorry...::

Teal'c knelt before the candle regardless, back straight, hands resting on his knees, and began the breathing that had carried him through worse nights than this one. Slow, even, a rhythm laid down so many years ago that his body no longer needed his mind's permission to find it. Kel-no-reem did not require sleep. It required stillness, a deliberate quieting of every current running beneath the surface of a man's thoughts, until there was nothing left but the candle flame and the breath and the self.

The self, tonight, was no longer only his.

::is this the meditation thing, is this Kel-no-reem, I know that word, I actually know that word, I feel bad that the only reason I know it is a fake TV show but I know it, okay, I'll be quiet, I can be quiet, I'm being quiet right now, this is me being quiet...::

It did not work.

✳

Teal'c had expected resistance from without, an enemy's blade, a Goa'uld's will pressed against his own, and had built his entire discipline around meeting exactly that kind of pressure without yielding. He had not built it to meet a boy who wanted, with an intensity that had nothing manipulative in it at all, simply to be heard. The stillness he reached for kept arriving fractured, a held breath with a voice threaded straight through the middle of it, apologizing for existing and then, a heartbeat later, unable to stop existing quite so loudly anyway.

He tried again. Breath in, held, released. The candle flame steadied in front of him, and for the length of perhaps four full breaths together, something close to quiet actually took hold, quiet enough that he let himself believe, briefly, that the discipline was going to hold the way it always had.

::sorry, sorry, I'm trying, I really am, it's just really hard to think about nothing when there's, like, everything, there's SO much everything right now, I don't even know where to start not thinking about it...::

The quiet broke. Teal'c exhaled slowly, deliberately, refusing to let the failure show in his breathing even here, alone, with no one to see it fail.

He opened his eyes.

"You are not being quiet," Teal'c said, aloud, to the empty room, because there was no other way yet to speak to something that lived behind his own eyes rather than in front of them.

::oh my god you can hear me, wait, was that a rhetorical, no, you're actually, hi, hi, I'm Greg, I don't know if you got that from the yelling earlier, it's Greg, Greg Veder, and I am so sorry, I really am trying, it's just, this is a lot, this is genuinely the most a lot has ever a lot-ed at me, and I don't really have an off switch, my mom used to say that, actually she still says it, said it, whatever tense that's supposed to be now that I'm apparently living inside a Stargate person...::

"Teal'c," he said, cutting cleanly through it, the way he might have interrupted a nervous recruit spiraling toward a mistake. "I am called Teal'c."

::Teal'c. Okay. Teal'c. That's, that's a great name, that's a genuinely cool name, I'm not just saying that, although I guess I'd say that either way because you could probably kill me with your bare hands, no offense, that's not a threat thing, that's a fact thing, you have very large hands...::

"Greg Veder." Teal'c said the name slowly, testing its shape the way a man weighs an unfamiliar weapon before deciding whether to trust it in a fight. "You are afraid."

The chatter stopped. Not all at once, but enough that Teal'c felt the shape of the silence change, something in the noise pulling back on itself.

::...yeah. Yeah, I am. Is that, is that weird, I feel like I should be handling this better, I feel like there's a version of me that would be handling this way better, that's, that's kind of my whole thing actually, there's always a version of me handling it better, in my head, and then the real me shows up and...::

"You died," Teal'c said, with a soldier's preference for truth before comfort. "And you did not choose this. Neither did I."

::no. No, I didn't. Either of those.::

"Then we are, for the present, required to endure each other." Teal'c closed his eyes again, sought the breath, found it harder to hold than it had been a few minutes before, not because the boy was still talking, but because he had stopped, and the quiet left behind by that stopping was, in its own way, its own kind of demand. "I ask only this. When I require silence, you will grant it if you are able. When you cannot, you will tell me so, rather than simply failing to provide it."

::...okay. Yeah. I can do that. I mean, I'll probably be bad at it, fair warning, but I can try to do that.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: He's gonna fail at that miserably, isn't he.

A RECOGNIZABLE VOICE: Indeed.

GLADLY: Nope. Not asking.

✳

It was not Kel-no-reem. There was no true stillness, no full quiet, nothing his masters would have recognized as mastery. But the pressure behind his eyes had eased by one degree, and Teal'c had learned not to discard measurable progress merely because it was small.

He blew out the candle eventually, more from habit than necessity, and sat a while longer in the dark that followed, turning the evening over the way he turned over anything unfamiliar before deciding what to make of it. He had spent his whole life being told, by masters and gods alike, that a Jaffa's strength lay in what he refused to show. No one had ever told him what to do with a strength like that once something had moved in behind his eyes and simply refused to leave.

::hey, um. Thanks. For listening. I know that's probably not, like, a big thing for you, but it kind of was for me.::

"Rest, Greg Veder," Teal'c said, quietly, to the dark. "Tomorrow will ask more of both of us than tonight did."

✳

Teal'c did not sleep that night, Jaffa did not require it, but for the first time since the gate had opened onto P3X-866's gray morning, he sat with the second voice in his skull and did not feel, entirely, alone in carrying it.
