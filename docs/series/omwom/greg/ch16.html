Chapter 16 of 40

Noticed

The laugh did not do it by itself. O'Neill had been collecting smaller changes for weeks: Teal'c answering jokes he once ignored, relaxing half an inch during a briefing, changing a gym schedule he had treated as immovable for four years. None belonged in an incident report. Together, they belonged in a conversation.

He caught Teal'c after an uneventful debrief, when the paperwork was signed and the rest of the team had dispersed toward showers and downtime. O'Neill stayed in his chair. Teal'c stayed in his.

Not a debrief anymore. Not really. Just two men and an empty room, and whatever this actually was going to be.

He didn't lead with the laugh. Teal'c had expected him to, had half-rehearsed an answer for it, and got, instead, something he hadn't prepared for at all.

"You remember the commissary. Couple weeks back. I said you were in a good mood, I was suspicious." O'Neill wasn't smiling, not exactly, flat and serious in the way he only got when a conversation actually mattered to him rather than when he was performing not caring. "I wasn't just messing with you, Teal'c. I've had four years of watching your face do exactly one thing, and it's not one thing anymore. Hasn't been for a while. Little bit different every week, if I'm honest, some days I can't even put my finger on what, just, off. And then P6X-334 happens and you laugh, actually laugh, in front of God and Daniel and everybody, and that's not a one-time thing anymore either, buddy, that's a pattern, and I've been sitting on this a lot longer than I probably should have before saying something."

Teal'c had promised himself he would not lie when this conversation came.

"Indeed. Something has changed, O'Neill. I will not insult you by claiming otherwise."

✳

"Is it bad?" O'Neill asked, direct the way he was direct about things that actually scared him, no dressing it up. "Because I've watched a lot of things happen to people on this program, and 'something's changed' is not always a sentence I like hearing out of somebody I trust with my six."

"It is not a danger to this base, nor to this team, nor, I believe, to myself." Teal'c chose the same line between honesty and disclosure he had offered the young Jaffa. "It is not a matter I am prepared to explain fully. I ask that you extend me the same trust you have extended in four years of far less explicable circumstances, and allow me the time to determine, on my own terms, when and to whom the rest becomes something I can share."

O'Neill studied him as he would a report with one page missing. Then his shoulders eased.

"Okay," he said, simply. "For now. But if it ever does become a danger to this base, this team, or you, personally, I need you to actually tell me. Not make me guess from you laughing at a farmer."

"You have my word."

::that was really close.::

::It was.::

::are you gonna tell him? Ever?::

::I do not know,:: Teal'c said, honest with Greg in a way he had just finished being honest, in a different register, with O'Neill. ::I know only that it will not be today, and that the choice, when it comes, will need to be mine to make on my own terms rather than one taken from me by circumstance.::

✳

O'Neill clapped him once on the shoulder on the way past, the same gesture he'd used after P2X-908, easy and unforced, a man choosing to let a matter rest without pretending it was resolved. "For what it's worth," he said, "whatever it is. Good on you for laughing again. It suits you."

He was gone before Teal'c had an answer ready.

::he's a good commanding officer.::

::He is not merely my commanding officer,:: Teal'c said. ::Four years beside a man in circumstances that kill lesser friendships within months makes him something closer to a warrior-brother than a superior. I would have said so before tonight, had anyone asked.::

Teal'c did not repeat the admission. Once said, it remained true.
