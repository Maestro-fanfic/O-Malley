Shield-Brother

Three days is not very long, as these things went. Teal'c had lived through weeks that changed less than the last three had, and yet the base had already folded both of them, quietly, back into its own ordinary rhythm: reports filed, watches stood, a routine mission to a routine planet that produced nothing more dramatic than an argument between Daniel and a village elder over pottery dating techniques. It was, Teal'c thought, walking the corridor toward the commissary on the third such ordinary morning, precisely the kind of quiet a man learned not to trust, not because it was false, but because it never lasted, and some part of him was still waiting for whatever came next.

He had not told O'Neill the word's meaning. He had decided, that first night, that some things asked to be earned rather than simply given, and he had held the decision through the debrief, through Hammond's quiet endorsement in front of the visiting brass, through every ordinary day since, telling himself the man had asked for nothing more than the fact of the word itself, and that anything beyond it was a gift Teal'c reserved the right to withhold a while longer.

He should have known better than to expect that arrangement to hold. Four years of watching O'Neill work should have told him exactly how long "a while longer" was ever going to last, once curiosity actually had its hooks all the way in.

He heard them before he saw them, passing the open door of Daniel's office on his way to the commissary, and stopped, because something in the shape of the two voices inside told him this was not an ordinary Tuesday conversation about pottery.

"...and you're sure that's how it's spelled?" O'Neill's voice, half-apologetic, half-stubborn. "I might be butchering the sounds. I was kind of distracted when I heard it."

"Reasonably sure, given what you've given me to work with." Daniel was already flipping through one of his reference notebooks, the focus he reserved for an unfamiliar phrase taking over completely. "Sha'reth kanar. This has been rattling around in my head since Teal'c said it to my face and wouldn't translate it. Hearing Bra'tac refuse the same thing at the debrief didn't exactly kill the curiosity. I still didn't have enough to work with until now. The root's close to a couple of Goa'uld military terms I've already catalogued. Sha'reth shows up around shared oaths or binding, formal alliance language, the kind Jaffa houses used before Apophis flattened most of that structure into obedience. Kanar's harder. Could be blood. Could be battle. There's an older root under both that I don't have a clean gloss for, something closer to cost, the price paid rather than merely the debt owed." He looked up, pen still moving though his eyes had left the page. "You actually got him to spell it out? Bra'tac wouldn't give the room a hint."

O'Neill's eyes found Teal'c in the doorway before he answered, caught, and something in his face made it plain he hadn't meant for any of this to be overheard, though he didn't look especially sorry about it either.

"Had a hunch it mattered," he said, simple, unembarrassed, the words aimed half at Daniel and half, obviously, at the man he'd just been caught in front of. "Figured it was worth knowing what I was actually being called."

Daniel's expression shifted, working through it, cross-referencing something new against everything he'd already laid out. "Okay. Best translation I can give you, without more context than a spelling somebody half-remembered off a scrap of paper: something like 'brother sworn in blood.' Or, actually," he tapped the page twice, correcting himself in real time the way he always did when a better answer arrived mid-sentence, "'shield-brother' might be the cleaner rendering. If kanar's doing what I think it's doing there, that cost-root, it's not just the guy standing beside you in the line. It's the guy who'll take the blow meant for you and count it a fair trade." He set the pen down entirely now, all the way turned toward O'Neill. "It's not casual phrasing, Jack. That's not a word for convenient company. It's a word for somebody you've already decided is worth bleeding for, and Jaffa don't throw terms like that around lightly, if they use them at all outside actual oath contexts."

"Yeah." O'Neill's voice had gone quiet, all the half-apologetic stubbornness drained out of it, and he glanced back toward the doorway, toward Teal'c, something settling into place on his face that had clearly been sitting unresolved for three days and had just, finally, found somewhere to land. "I got that part on my own."

::Bra'tac taught me the warrior's version of it, long before I had any use for the word itself,:: Teal'c said, silent, meant for Greg alone. ::A First Prime is not measured by the ground he takes. He is measured by whose place he is willing to take, when the staff weapon is already rising and there is no longer time for anything but the choosing. There is no higher honor among Jaffa than to fall so that a shield-brother need not. O'Neill has never once said it in those terms. He has simply lived it, on a mission I still do not speak of, and asked nothing in return for having done so, not even the knowledge that I understood what it cost him to choose it.::

::that's... okay, that's basically identical to something in the Bible. My mom used to say it, and she meant it, even if I never really got there myself. "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends." John, I think. Fifteen-something. I didn't expect a four-hundred-year-old warrior code to land on almost the exact same sentence as a book she used to read to me at bedtime.::

::Perhaps some truths do not require a shared species to arrive at them,:: Teal'c said. ::Only sufficient cause to need them.::

::he went and found out anyway.:: Greg's voice, quieter than usual, something almost reverent under the disbelief. ::He said he didn't need to know, and he went and found out anyway.::

::Indeed,:: Teal'c said, and felt something in his own chest too large for the discipline he usually kept it behind, standing in a doorway, watching a man who had asked for nothing go looking for the truth regardless, simply because it had mattered to him to understand exactly what he'd been given.

He had not meant to be seen yet. He stepped fully into the doorway anyway, because there was no honest version of this moment that involved him continuing to hide in a corridor.

He had barely crossed the threshold when Carter's voice came from behind him, unhurried, already halfway into the room before either Daniel or O'Neill had noticed her arriving. "Is this the conversation I keep hearing about secondhand? Because if it is, I'm not standing in a hallway for the rest of it."

"How did you even..." Daniel started.

"Base this size, everybody already knows everybody else's business by lunch. I heard Sha'reth kanar from two different people before I heard it from either of you." She folded her arms, not unkindly, and looked between the three of them, some of the old scientist's patience in her stance, the kind that had waited out stranger mysteries than this one. "So. Are we doing this, or is this still a boys' club conversation?"

"Shield-brother," Daniel said, before Teal'c had to decide whether to answer for himself. "That's the best I've got. Cost-rooted, oath-bound, not casual. The word for somebody you've already decided is worth bleeding for."

Carter looked at Teal'c for a long moment, something working behind her eyes that had nothing to do with the linguistics of it. "Yeah," she said finally, quiet. "That tracks."

She didn't say anything else right away, and Teal'c had learned, across four years, exactly what that kind of quiet from her usually meant: not that she had nothing left to say, but that she was choosing, carefully, which piece of it was actually hers to say out loud.

"For what it's worth," she said finally, "I already knew what I was to you. I didn't need the word for it. But I'm glad I have it anyway. Words matter. They're not just labels. They're an admission that something's real enough to need one."

"That is, I believe, precisely why my people do not hand them out carelessly," Teal'c said.

She nodded once, satisfied, and let the moment close on its own rather than reaching for anything more to add to it.

::and it goes both ways, right? The shield thing.:: Greg again, careful now, aware of the weight in the room even without eyes of his own to read the faces in it. ::Because I feel like I've only ever seen it be you. Covering everybody else.::

::It goes both ways,:: Teal'c said, and the memories arrived without needing to be searched for, close to the surface the way the truest ones always were: O'Neill's return fire buying the seconds that got them clear of Va'lar's ambush, a body put deliberately between Teal'c and a blast that would otherwise have taken him center mass on a mission neither of them spoke of often, O'Neill choosing, in the half second there was to choose anything at all, to be the one who paid rather than the one who watched. ::I require it far less frequently than he does. That has never once meant it was not offered, or that the cost of offering it was ever weighed and found too high. That is the whole of what the word means, Greg Veder. Not merely a willingness to stand beside. A willingness to be spent, if spending is what standing beside ever actually requires.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

A gruff, oil-rig-weathered voice said, "That's the whole of it. Wish somebody'd handed me that sentence earlier. Would've saved explaining it afterward."

The quiet officer answered, "Some lessons don't arrive early enough to spare anybody. Doesn't make them less true."

"No," the first agreed.

✳

Daniel was still watching him from behind the desk, curious in the open, unguarded way he'd been curious the night Teal'c had told him and Carter everything, together, weeks before any of them had ever heard the name Apophis attached to a compound on a ridge. There was nothing left to offer any of them here that hadn't already been given freely, in one room or another, over one long night or the weeks since, and something in that plain fact settled in Teal'c's chest more comfortably than he'd expected it to.

"You already have the whole of it," Teal'c said, to all three of them now, not just Daniel. "This is merely the one corner of it I had not yet gotten around to sharing myself."

"Fair," Daniel said, grinning, entirely unbothered at having been beaten to a piece of the story by his own translation work. "Guess I found this corner on my own, then. Feels earned."

"It generally does, with you," Carter said, dry, something like real warmth moving under it.

::he really did earn that one.::

::He did,:: Teal'c agreed. ::I find I do not mind, this once, being caught slightly behind a friend's own curiosity. It is, on reflection, an entirely fair trade for four years of being the one who usually keeps everyone else waiting.::

✳

They stayed in Daniel's too-small office while the base continued its ordinary morning outside. The word belonged to all of them now. Teal'c found he preferred it that way.
