Preparing

The task-organized company got two days to prepare for a mission most of its Marines would never be told the personal shape of at all. Turnpike's six knew only that the recovered personnel mattered enough to make General Hammond spend a company on bringing them home. It was enough. Doyle still spent more time on his hair than strictly necessary. Kowalczyk still had an opinion about everything. Radio still hummed through half of every briefing like it was background music for something more interesting happening in his own head. None of it worried Turnpike now. Habits were not failures of discipline if they disappeared the moment the work began.

Day one was the ship. Bra'tac's Tel'tak had carried massed Jaffa before, but never sixty-four Marines trying to disembark in doctrinal order through one narrow ramp without turning the cargo hold into a traffic jam. They rehearsed the load backward from the order of movement: reserve deepest, then assault, support, security, command, reconnaissance nearest the ramp. Equipment was tied to function and placed with its element. Casualty litters remained reachable even with every seat occupied. The four corpsmen walked their routes blindfolded until they could reach the collection point through a hold full of bodies without asking anyone to move. Bra'tac imposed hand signals once the cloak went active and voice traffic became a liability. Doyle complained about the cargo hold's lighting. Turnpike told him enemy transport design was unlikely to protect his complexion.

Day two belonged to the raid. The training bay became a rough objective area: chalk ship, taped tree line, tunnel mouth, release point, blocking positions, holding block. They rehearsed each element separately, then together. Reconnaissance cleared and marked. Security occupied without bunching on the route. Support found firing positions that did not cross the assault's path. The breach team opened three different mock doors and refused a fourth because the hostage marker had been placed inside the blast danger area. The reserve rehearsed four commitment triggers, including total loss of communications. Then they ran withdrawal in doctrinal order until the recovered-personnel markers reached the ship, security came off last, and Captain Mercer could account for every element without a single voice asking who was missing.

On the ninth full run, a mock casualty in the assault element forced support to shift, the reserve to replace a security team, and recon to use the alternate route. The company lost forty-three seconds and no one lost the shape of the operation. Turnpike made them run it again anyway. By the end, Teal'c no longer saw sixty-four Marines performing adjacent tasks. He saw one plan retaining its shape while its parts changed places.

::do the Marines know? About me, I mean.::

::No,:: Teal'c said. ::Nor will they, unless circumstance requires it. Their role does not require the knowledge, and I have learned this year the value of only extending a truth this size to those who have genuine cause to carry it.::

::fair enough. Just wondering. Feels weird, knowing there's a whole team out there prepping for this, and I'm just, like, invisible cargo.::

::You are considerably more than cargo, invisible or otherwise,:: Teal'c said, and felt Greg's answering warmth at that even without words attached to it.

By the second evening, watching the company complete its final withdrawal from the observation deck above the training bay, Teal'c found himself unexpectedly reassured. Whatever else went wrong tomorrow, nobody below was relying on courage to supply a missing piece of the plan. Every piece had an owner, an alternate, and a condition under which it changed.

While the Marines drilled a landing zone that would only ever exist as chalk lines and satellite photographs, Teal'c ran a drill of his own, one he had never once needed before Greg Veder and had no intention of walking into tomorrow without having run at least once, deliberately, under conditions calm enough to actually learn something.

They used the base's empty gymnasium after hours, a familiar room with mats, open floor, and no staff weapons in reach. Teal'c handed control over on his own terms and let Greg practice moving the body: walking a straight line, catching a thrown weight, the ordinary mechanics of a life Greg had never gotten to finish learning in his own.

::this is so much harder than it looks. How do you make walking look like nothing? I feel like I'm operating a crane.::

::You are improving,:: Teal'c said, watching his own hand close around a tossed weight with only the second attempt's worth of hesitation rather than the first's. ::Considerably, in fact, from where we began.::

::yeah, well, "better than falling on my face" is a low bar, but I'll take the win.::

They ran it every evening for two days, an hour at a time, Teal'c calling instructions Greg could feel more than hear, correcting weight distribution and the false confidence of moving too fast before the body had caught up. By the second night, Greg caught the weight on the first try three times running.

The rest of the recovery team watched the final run from the gym door. Carter tracked the changes in gait and posture rather than the catch itself. Daniel saved his questions until Teal'c had control again. Bra'tac watched with the expression he had once reserved for a training yard.

"Okay," O'Neill said when Teal'c had control again. "If you switch in there and have time to tell us, say 'change.' One word. If you don't have time, we know what it looks like now, and we give you room instead of trying to tackle you back to normal."

"Agreed," Teal'c said.

"Greg agree?"

::yeah. Definitely. Also, for the record, I appreciate that the official plan for me looking weird is no longer 'assume Teal'c forgot how legs work.'::

"He agrees," Teal'c said. "With commentary."

"Figured."

::okay but for real, why does this matter? Tactically, I mean. Nobody's gonna hand me control to play catch tomorrow.::

::Because control given under panic, the way it was given the first time, asks the body to learn under the worst possible conditions,:: Teal'c said. ::Control practiced under calm asks it to learn properly, so that panic, should it come again, has something better than chaos to fall back on.::

::...that's annoyingly smart, actually.::

::I did not spend four years training Jaffa recruits by accident,:: Teal'c said, and felt, saying it, the plain satisfaction of watching a lesson land exactly as intended.

✳

The night before the mission, Teal'c laid out his gear the way he always did before something that mattered, methodical, unhurried, each piece checked twice regardless of how many times he'd already checked it that week. Staff weapon. Zat, secondary. The plain, unmarked knife he'd carried since long before SG-1, older than the program itself, older than most things in his current life except himself.

::can I ask you something? And it's okay if you don't want to answer, I just, it's been sitting with me and I don't want to not ask.::

"Ask."

::what happens to me. If you die tomorrow. Like, actually, for real, not being dramatic about it, just. I don't know how this works. Nobody's ever told me how this works.::

Teal'c set down the knife rather than answer while his hands were still working.

"I do not know," he said. "No one has told me either. The entity who placed you here did not see fit to tell you. And I have had no opportunity to interrogate him."

::that's genuinely terrifying, just so you know. Not blaming you. Just, that's a big unknown to be carrying around this whole time.::

"It is." Teal'c resumed his check of the gear. "I will tell you what I can offer, which is less than certainty. I do not intend to die tomorrow. I have not intended to die on any mission that came close to killing me regardless. Intention is not armor. But I have four years of proof that it has been enough so far."

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: Nobody's gonna answer it, are they. The actual question.

FURY: Maybe not. Sometimes you get someone willing to sit in the not-knowing with you.

✳

::okay. Okay, that's, I appreciate you being honest instead of just telling me it'll be fine.::

"I will not lie to you, Greg Veder. Not about this. Not about anything, if I can help it." He looked up from the gear laid out across his bunk, meeting the moment as directly as he could manage when the person he was meeting it with had no face to look at. "I will tell you this, however, and it is not a comfort so much as a fact I have come to believe: whatever tomorrow costs either of us, the four years I spent alone before you arrived were not better than the risk of these last months. I would not trade them back, even knowing what tomorrow might ask."

::...yeah. Same, actually. Even with the not-knowing. Same.::

They sat with that for a while, no further words needed, the ordinary quiet of two people who had run out of things that needed saying and were, for once, simply comfortable letting the silence do the rest of the work.

"There is one further matter," Teal'c said eventually, breaking it himself. "If tomorrow does go badly. If I do not return, or return in a state that leaves me unable to speak for myself. I want you to know that Bra'tac is aware of you, in full. That is one person, at least, who would know to explain what you are to whoever needed telling."

::you'd actually want Hammond to know too? Eventually, I mean.::

"Eventually. I have been considering it since Bra'tac raised the question of who else deserves to know. I have not decided the timing. Tomorrow asks enough of both of us without adding a second reckoning on top of it. But yes. Eventually, I believe I will."

✳

A knock came at the door not long after, Bra'tac, still awake despite the hour, carrying two cups of something dark and bitter that passed, on this base, for the closest approximation of a proper Jaffa evening drink anyone had managed to brew.

"I did not expect you to sleep tonight," Bra'tac said, handing one over and settling into the room's single chair without waiting for an invitation, "so I saw no reason either of us should sit awake alone."

They talked a while of smaller things, old missions, old jokes neither of them had told in years, the easy quiet of two people who had trusted each other with their lives long enough that silence between them never once felt like something that needed filling. It was, Teal'c reflected, exactly the evening he would have wanted, had anyone asked him in advance what the night before a day like tomorrow ought to look like.

"You are ready," Bra'tac said eventually, rising to leave. "Not because nothing could go wrong. Because you have done everything within your power to be ready regardless. That is the only readiness any of us are ever actually given."

"Thank you, old friend. For all of it."

"Do not thank me until we are all standing on the other side of tomorrow," Bra'tac said, though the warmth in his voice gave the gruffness away as performance more than substance. "Then you may thank me as much as you like."

Teal'c finished laying out his gear, methodical to the last piece, and blew out the candle he had, for once, actually lit, sitting a while in the dark that followed the way he had learned to sit in it these past months, not alone in it any longer, whatever tomorrow turned out to cost either of them.

::hey. For what it's worth. I'm glad ROB picked you. Out of everybody he could've dropped me into. I'm glad it was you.::

::And I you, Greg Veder,:: Teal'c said, quietly, meaning every word of it. ::Whatever comes tomorrow.::
