Into Apophis's Domain

Bra'tac's Tel'tak set down in a forest clearing forty minutes before dawn, cloak holding steady the whole descent, close enough to the compound's outer perimeter that the walk from landing site to tree line took minutes rather than hours, the scoured, cultivated ground Apophis's domains always favored close to a stronghold already visible even in the dark, land worked hard enough by conscripted labor that nothing grew there but what served the garrison directly.

The company moved in the order it had loaded. Turnpike's reconnaissance element came off first, Doyle's team through the narrow ramp and into the tree line before anyone else put a boot on alien soil. They cleared the landing zone, marked the ship with an infrared reference only the Tau'ri could see, and pushed toward the release point on two offset routes rather than drawing one line every following element would be forced to reuse.

Mercer and the command cell followed reconnaissance far enough to establish control clear of the ramp. Security came next and divided without stopping. One team disappeared into the trees around the landing zone. Two more moved toward the ground overlooking the tunnel approaches, already choosing blocking positions from which they could isolate the objective without silhouetting themselves against it. Support carried the weight after them: crew-served weapons broken into man-portable loads, breaching kit, extra radios, litters. The assault element and SG-1 came next, then the reserve. Sixty-four Marines cleared one ramp without bunching because every one of them had known the order before the ship left Earth.

Only when Turnpike reported the release point secure did the assault and recovery elements move beyond it. His reconnaissance platoon stayed ahead. Bra'tac walked beside its point team now, reading terrain the way Teal'c had watched him read it a hundred times before, while Turnpike's Marines marked the primary route and quietly began looking for the alternate the briefing had required. Behind them, security owned the space they had passed through, support waited where it could move either inward or back toward the ship, and the reserve remained one bound behind the assault instead of a radio call and a long march away.

Nobody spoke beyond what the mission required. Carter checked her scanner readings every few hundred meters, silent, methodical; Daniel carried a pack heavier than his usual field kit, extra medical supplies nobody had needed to ask him to bring; O'Neill walked point beside Bra'tac with the loose, deceptively casual alertness of a man who had done this exact kind of approach more times than he'd ever bothered counting.

::this is really happening. This is actually really happening.::

::It is,:: Teal'c said. ::I need you steady, Greg Veder. Not silent. Steady. There is a difference, and I will need you able to feel the difference before this day is finished.::

::yeah. Okay. Steady. I can do steady.::

The tunnel entrance, when they found it, sat exactly where Bra'tac's four-hundred-year-old memory had placed it, a collapsed section of old irrigation channel that hadn't been an irrigation channel in generations, disguised well enough that O'Neill walked past it twice before Bra'tac corrected his path with a single raised hand.

"Four hundred years," O'Neill muttered, "and the man still remembers where the secret door is. I hate how good that is."

"Discipline, Colonel O'Neill," Bra'tac said, already lowering himself into the gap. "It is available to anyone willing to pay its price."

✳

The tunnel itself ran narrow and low, forcing even Teal'c into a half-crouch for most of its length, air close and stale with centuries of disuse. Turnpike's lead team went first with shaded lights and Bra'tac at its shoulder. SG-1 and the Marine breach-and-assault team followed. Support brought only what the interior might require; the reserve halted at the release point, exactly where it had rehearsed, while security closed around both ends of the route. Bra'tac's memory held true turn after turn, a testament either to the genuine sharpness of a four-hundred-year-old mind or to how little this fortress had changed, likely both at once.

::how are you not freaking out right now. I'm freaking out and I'm not even the one who has to crawl through this.::

::I have crawled through worse tunnels toward worse ends,:: Teal'c said. ::This one, at least, I crawl through toward something I actually want at the far side of it.::

They emerged, forty minutes later, into a storage cellar beneath what Bra'tac identified, in a whisper, as the compound's secondary garrison quarters. Reconnaissance cleared the cellar and the first two junctions beyond it. One security team came forward to isolate the tunnel exit; support established a position in the cellar; the reserve remained below, ready to move on four different triggers without needing a fresh order. O'Neill brought the assault and recovery elements through only after each report arrived.

Nothing. Silence, and the ordinary distant sounds of a garrison going about a morning that did not yet know anything was wrong.

"So far, so good," O'Neill breathed.

"Indeed," Teal'c said, though he did not yet trust it.

"Daniel, Carter, you're with Bra'tac and Second Team. Get eyes on the holding block. Teal'c, First Team is with us." O'Neill looked to the Marine assault leader, received one nod, and returned it. "Both approaches move on my signal. Reserve stays at the release point unless Mercer commits it."

Carter caught Teal'c's eye before the team split, a brief, steady look that said more than the moment had time for. "We'll get them out," she said, quiet, certain. "All of them."

"Thank you, Major Carter."

"Don't thank me yet." She managed something close to a smile despite the circumstances. "Thank me after."

✳

They split as planned, each recovery group carrying a Marine assault team with it while reconnaissance stayed one junction ahead and security closed the passages behind. Teal'c and O'Neill moved toward a stairwell Bra'tac's memory had marked as leading up into the compound's central courtyard, close enough to the holding block that a coordinated approach from both directions would pin any response between them. Teal'c's heart, if he had allowed himself to name the sensation honestly, was not steady in the way he had asked Greg to be steady. It was racing, four years of discipline doing everything it could to keep that fact off his face and largely succeeding, though he suspected, had anyone looked closely enough, they might have caught the strain of it regardless.

::I can feel that, you know. The racing thing. You don't have to hide it from me, at least.::

::I am aware,:: Teal'c said. ::I am attempting to hide it from myself as much as from you, without success.::

::that's fair. Honestly, kind of comforting? Like, if you're scared too, that means being scared isn't the wrong reaction to have right now.::

::It is not the wrong reaction,:: Teal'c agreed. ::It is, perhaps, the only sane one.::

The stairwell came up short of the courtyard, opening instead into a narrow service corridor that ran parallel to it. Turnpike's point pair cleared the corner. The Jaffa patrol came through an unmarked side door behind them and directly in front of O'Neill, two men walking a route Bra'tac's centuries-old memory had never accounted for at all. Reconnaissance had not missed them. The map had missed the corridor they came from.

O'Neill froze. Teal'c stopped half a second behind him. The day's first real test had arrived early.
