Arrival

The event horizon took Teal'c the way it always did, a rush of cold and light and unmaking that lasted no length of time at all, and delivered him, as it always did, whole.

He stepped through onto the packed dirt of P3X-866 a half pace behind Colonel O'Neill, staff weapon already angled to cover the tree line, the motion as automatic to him now as breathing. Major Carter came through next, then Daniel Jackson, and the Stargate behind them shut itself down into silence, leaving the four of them standing in the gray, close-treed light of an alien morning that looked, for all the wonder Daniel Jackson still insisted on finding in such things, very much like a forest in Minnesota. Mist sat low between the trunks. Somewhere off to the east, birds, or something that filled the same ecological niche birds did here, were making a great deal of noise about the sunrise.

It was in the half second between the wormhole's release and his own next breath that the second mind arrived inside him.

There was no gradual awareness of it, no soft edge to ease the transition. One moment there was only Teal'c, ordered and entire, the way he had trained himself to be ordered and entire even under a symbiote's constant low murmur, a discipline so old it no longer felt like effort. The next, there was noise. Not sound. A presence, vast and formless and already, somehow, talking, a wash of thought too fast and too unshaped to be language yet, fear layered on confusion layered on something that, absurdly, felt very much like delight, all three arriving at once, with no order to them, the way a landslide arrives all at once.

Teal'c did not stumble. He had trained twenty years and more to give an enemy nothing, and whatever this was, until he understood it, he would extend it no more courtesy than he extended a Jaffa patrol. He held his stance, staff weapon steady, eyes on the tree line, and inside the perfect stillness of his own skull, something that had not existed a moment ago began, in a rising and increasingly panicked internal voice that was somehow already fully formed, to lose its mind.

::oh my god oh my god okay that's a real Stargate that is a REAL Stargate I just walked through a REAL Stargate that's not, that's not a set, nobody builds a set that big, holy crap is that O'Neill, that's actually Colonel O'Neill, wait no, hang on, is that...::

Cut to the gallery.

A VOICE: For the record, “you good” is doing a lot of heavy lifting as a check-in. I'd know.

FURY: Nobody asked you.

A VOICE: Never does.

O'Neill glanced back over one shoulder, rifle loose in his hands even as his eyes kept working the tree line. "Teal'c. You good?"

"Indeed," Teal'c said, and the word came out exactly as level as it always did, which took, just then, more of him than four years of Kel-no-reem had ever asked for at once.

::HE TALKED, oh my god he actually talks like that, that's not just a show thing, that's really how he... wait. Wait wait wait. Wormhole X-Treme. This is Wormhole X-Treme. No, hang on, THIS is what Wormhole X-Treme was ripping off, this is the REAL THING, I used to make fun of that show, I used to make fun of the actual guy who says "indeed" in real life, oh my god he actually SAYS it...::

✳

Teal'c did not know what a Wormhole X-Treme was. He filed the phrase away regardless, the way he filed away any unfamiliar term uttered by an enemy or an ally, to be understood later, at a time of his own choosing rather than one forced upon him. For now there was only the fact of the thing itself: a second consciousness, undeniably present, undeniably distinct from his own, undeniably not going anywhere, occupying the same skull he had spent his entire life learning to master.

He had done this before. Not this precisely; no enemy, no ally, no god posing as either, had ever managed precisely this. But the shape of it was not entirely foreign to him. A presence within, demanding acknowledgment, requiring discipline rather than force to hold at bay. He had carried the symbiote, the one O'Neill had taken to calling Junior, a jibe Teal'c tolerated without ever once using himself, since the day of his first prillar, had learned the patient stillness required to keep a second will from ever once dictating his own, a skill honed across years he did not care to dwell on. Whatever this was, it was not that. It did not press for control the way a symbiote occasionally, subtly, tested its boundaries, feeling for weakness the way water feels for a crack in stone. It simply talked. Endlessly, without apparent capacity to stop, a boy's voice, young, frightened beneath the noise in a way that made itself known even through the excitement layered over top of it, the fear and the delight tangled together so thoroughly that Teal'c doubted the boy himself could have told them apart.

::okay okay okay focus, Greg, focus, you're not dead, or you are dead, or, no wait, you're in someone, you're literally IN someone, this is that thing, this is the thing Mr. Gladly, if that was even really Mr. Gladly, the other student, oh no, am I the passenger, is this guy the driver, is this guy going to be able to hear me, hey. Hey! Can you hear me? Big guy? Mister Symbol-on-the-Forehead? Can you...::

✳

"Carter, what've we got?" O'Neill said, already moving off toward the tree line, and Major Carter fell in beside him with her recorder out, reading off some low string of readings Teal'c heard and, for once, did not fully process. Daniel Jackson had drifted a few paces to the left, already crouched over a half-buried stone with a marking on its face, murmuring to himself the way he always did when he'd forgotten anyone else was present. "Sam, come look at this, this glyph sequence isn't Goa'uld standard, I think this might actually be older than..."

"Later, Daniel," O'Neill said, without looking back, carrying the weary patience of a man who had said some version of that sentence more times than he could count and expected to say it many more before he retired.

The ordinary business of an ordinary mission, continuing entirely without him, entirely unaware that anything at all had changed. Teal'c fell into position at the rear of the loose formation, the shape his body had taken on missions like this one so many times before that it required no thought at all, watching the tree line, watching the angles a threat might come from, while a voice that had not existed an hour ago kept talking, underneath everything, without pause.

::seriously, is anyone going to answer me, hello, big guy, I can feel you not answering me, that's a thing I can apparently feel now, is that normal, is that a normal thing that happens, oh my god what if this is normal, what if this happens to people all the time and nobody talks about it because they all signed something...::

Teal'c closed his eyes for the length of a single breath, the smallest concession he would allow himself.

Nothing on his face had moved. Nothing would. He had learned that lesson longer ago and at far greater cost than anything a symbiote or a System Lord had ever taught him directly, learned it in rooms where showing anything at all could be the difference between a man's life and his death, and he was not about to unlearn it now for the sake of a voice that, whatever else it was, had not yet actually threatened him.

Inside, where no one else could see it, Teal'c gathered every year of discipline he had built against Apophis's cruelty, his own grief, and a lifetime spent giving enemies nothing to use. Then he turned all of it toward not screaming back.

He had not yet mastered it. He had only, in that first long moment, decided that he would, and fell into step behind his team, staff weapon steady, face like stone, one voice too many now living behind it.
