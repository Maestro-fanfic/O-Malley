Chapter 8 of 40

Loyalist Jaffa

P2X-908 was not supposed to be anything. A resupply cache, three days old, left by an SG team that had come and gone without incident, buried under a cairn of loose stone half a klick from the gate, and O'Neill's read on the mission going in was retrieval, not reconnaissance, in and out before lunch. The gate had deposited them into a wide, grassy clearing ringed by dense forest, quiet enough that Daniel had spent the walk in speculating cheerfully about whether the cairn itself might turn out to be older than the cache stored beneath it, some previous culture's marker worth a return trip regardless of what the SGC actually needed from the planet. Carter found the cache exactly where the coordinates said it would be, packed crates still sealed, and for a good twenty minutes the whole exercise felt like exactly the kind of quiet, forgettable Tuesday a team could use after the last few weeks.

::okay, this one seems chill, right? Chill planet. Nothing's gonna...::

The patrol came out of the tree line before Greg finished the thought, six Jaffa in formation too tight and too practiced to be anything but real soldiers, and Teal'c knew what they were before he could make out a single face, the armor alone telling him everything the rest of him didn't want to hear. Not a lesser house's colors. Apophis's own mark, worn the way Teal'c himself had once worn it, First Prime's guard, the elite among the elite.

✳

Their leader stepped forward alone, and Teal'c felt something in his own chest go very still and very cold before the man had said a single word, recognition arriving faster than thought, the particular gait, the set of the shoulders. Va'lar. They had trained together, once, two boys handed staff weapons before they were old enough to understand what they were being handed, and Va'lar had stayed exactly where Teal'c had once stood, loyal, decorated, unbothered by any of the things that had eventually driven Teal'c to walk away.

✳

THE SKEPTIC: “The particular gait.” Fourth.

THE SKEPTIC: Mine has columns.

A DRY VOICE FROM THE BACK: Of course it has columns.

✳

"Teal'c." Va'lar said the name the way a man says a word he's been saving. "First Prime. Or you were, before you ran to hide behind the Tau'ri like a coward with no god left to answer to."

Teal'c said nothing. Silence had always cost him less than the alternative.

"Your wife still asks after you," Va'lar went on, closing the distance at an unhurried walk, savoring it, "though less than she once did, I think. And your son. Growing, I'm told, into something you'd hardly recognize. Apophis keeps them close. A kindness, he calls it, though I confess I've never fully understood the word applied to a cage."

::oh god. Oh god, this is real, this isn't a mission-of-the-week thing, this is actually about his family, I can feel it, I can actually feel how bad this is, this isn't funny, none of this is funny...::

Behind Va'lar, the six-man patrol held formation, weapons not yet raised but not lowered either, waiting on a word Teal'c could feel building toward its arrival. Beside him, O'Neill had gone quiet the way he always went quiet before a firefight, all easy sarcasm burned off, hand already drifting toward his weapon. Carter's read on the numbers would already be running, Teal'c knew, the same math he was running himself: six against four, no cover worth the name, forty yards of open ground between here and the gate.

There was no deflection in it this time, no joke reaching for the surface, no trailer-voice narration standing between Greg and what he was feeling. Just the fear, plain and correctly sized for once, matching the danger instead of running wildly past or short of it, and Teal'c, in the one still-functioning corner of his attention not occupied by Va'lar, noted the difference the way he noted any change in a known quantity.

✳

"You have said what you came to say," Teal'c said, and if his voice shook at all, it did not show it, four years of discipline holding the line his heart wasn't holding nearly so well. "Say the rest of it, or step aside."

Va'lar smiled, and it was not a kind expression. "There is no rest of it, Teal'c. Only this."

His hand came up, and the staff weapons behind him rose with it, six trained soldiers against four, the numbers wrong in every direction that mattered, and O'Neill's shout of "Cover!" arrived a half second before the first blast scorched the dirt where Daniel had been standing.

The fight that followed had none of the ragged, improvised chaos of P4X-212. This was disciplined, coordinated, Jaffa who had trained together for years pressing an advantage SG-1 had walked into with no warning and no plan for. Carter went down hard behind a fallen log, favoring one leg. Daniel was pinned flat, no clean angle to return fire from. O'Neill was shouting something about the gate, the gate that was still forty yards and an entire kill zone away, laying down what cover he could while Teal'c held the center, the only one of them standing close enough to Va'lar's line to actually blunt it.

He fought the way he always fought, economical, no wasted motion, dropping one Jaffa with a staff blast at close range and taking the return hit across the ribs without breaking stride. It bought seconds. It didn't buy enough of them. Two more of Va'lar's men were already flanking wide, cutting the angle toward the gate down to nothing, and Carter's leg wasn't going to carry her that distance even if the angle had still been open.

::we're gonna die, we're actually gonna die, this isn't the tribute episode, this isn't ANY episode, nobody's coming to save this...::

Teal'c took a second hit, this one across the shoulder, staggered, and felt, for the first time since a boy's voice had arrived uninvited in his skull, something that was not quite a thought and not quite a decision, but sat exactly between the two.

Daniel, from behind the log he shared with Carter, was shouting something at O'Neill about the treeline to the east, a possible second angle to the gate if they could just buy the seconds to reach it, and O'Neill was shouting back that seconds were exactly the thing in shortest supply. The whole clearing had narrowed, for Teal'c, down to the staff weapon in his hands, the two Jaffa still flanking, and the growing, undeniable certainty that his own hands, however disciplined, were not going to be enough.

He was going to need more than his own two hands were going to give him.
