Staff Infection

Drey'auc stood at the center of the chamber beyond the door, older than the memory Teal'c had carried for four years, sharper-eyed, a woman who had survived a god's suspicion through nothing but her own careful strength, and for one long moment neither of them said anything at all, four years of everything unsaid arriving all at once with nowhere left to put it.

"Teal'c." Her voice broke on his name, the first crack Teal'c had ever seen in a composure he'd once believed unbreakable.

There was no time for the reunion either of them deserved. Alarms were sounding throughout the compound now, real ones, the garrison fully mobilized, and it was into that chaos, staff weapon already raised, that Apophis himself arrived, flanked by a full guard, drawn by the one disturbance in his own domain he would never delegate to a subordinate.

The Marine assault teams reacted as one element. One closed around Drey'auc and Rya'c, putting bodies and armor between the recovered personnel and the door. The other turned outward to meet Apophis's guard. Over the radio, security reported both blocking positions still holding. Support shifted fire deeper into the western corridor. The reserve, already committed, kept the route behind them from closing. The company could not fight Teal'c's battle for him. It could make certain that battle was the only one he had to fight at once.

"Teal'c." Apophis's voice filled the chamber the way a god's voice always did, designed centuries ago to require no volume at all to command a room. "My First Prime. Come to steal back what was always, by rights, mine to keep."

✳

"They were never yours," Teal'c said, staff weapon already leveling to meet him. "They were never anything but leverage to you, and leverage does not love the hand that holds it, whatever you have told yourself across four years of believing otherwise."

Apophis's answer was not words. It was violence, immediate and total. His guard struck with him and met the Marine assault element instead, close fire and disciplined movement tearing the escort away from its master before it could bury Teal'c under numbers. Apophis himself came straight for the one man who had ever successfully walked away from him, and Teal'c met him with everything four years of freedom and discipline had built, staff weapons clashing in a rhythm that had nothing left to prove and everything left to survive.

::I'm here. Whatever you need. Just say it.::

::Stay,:: Teal'c said, breathless, blocking a blow that would have ended a lesser fighter outright. ::That is all I require. Simply stay.::

The fight ran longer than any single exchange Teal'c had survived in years, Apophis's strength undiminished by time, his own body carrying wounds already accumulated across a long, brutal day, and it was in the moment his own strength finally began to fail, staff weapon nearly torn from his grip, that he understood, with total clarity, exactly what Va'lar's swordfight adage had actually been pointing toward all along.

::give it to me. Now. I know what to do.::

::You do not know how to fight a god, Greg Veder.::

::No. But he doesn't know how to fight ME. Nobody in this whole galaxy has ever had to fight ME. That's the whole point. That's always been the whole point.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

A LOW, UNHURRIED VOICE: He's handing the body to the one person Apophis can't read.

THE FLAT VOICE: Because the kid doesn't know what he's doing.

A LOW VOICE: Exactly.

✳

Teal'c let go, one final time, trusting completely a passenger who had spent a whole book learning exactly this moment without either of them realizing that was what the learning had been for, and the body that had, minutes before, fought with a First Prime's precision, began instead to move like nothing Apophis had ever trained against, no pattern, no style, no predictable angle to counter, simply a terrified, determined, wildly improvising presence throwing everything it had at a god who had spent centuries only ever needing to out-think soldiers.

It should not have worked. It worked anyway, the same accident of unpredictability that had saved them all on P2X-908, scaled up now to a target who had never once needed to plan for it, and in the opening it bought, the single unguarded half-second no trained opponent would ever have offered, Teal'c felt the wheel return to him unbidden, Greg's own instinct once again reaching for the thing it actually knew how to do at the critical moment: hand off the finishing blow to the person who could land it.

Teal'c's staff weapon found the gap Greg had bought him, and Apophis, for the first time in all the years of Teal'c's own service and the four years of defiance since, went down.

Not dead. Teal'c felt that certainty settle even as the blow landed, a god's survival instincts already pulling him back from the true killing strike, retreat rather than death, exactly the pattern Bra'tac had warned him to expect. Apophis's remaining guard, already divided from him by the Marine assault teams, broke formation entirely when they saw their god fall, some fleeing, others lowering their weapons, an army built around one man's certainty discovering that the certainty could bleed like anyone else's. But down, and beaten, and driven from his own stronghold by the First Prime he had spent four years hunting and the passenger neither of them had ever seen coming.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

A smooth, amused voice drawled, "That's the problem with self-appointed divinity. Wear the crown long enough and you start believing the crown did the work. It's always the mortal who does the interesting part. I'd know."

The theatrical voice purred, "Oh, I adore him. We should compare notes, darling."

"We have. It got weird."

✳

O'Neill's voice crackled over the radio a moment later, breathless, urgent. "Teal'c, talk to me, what's your status, we heard the whole compound go sideways from here..."

"We are secure," Teal'c said, and found, saying it, that the words carried a weight he had not expected them to. "Drey'auc and Rya'c are with me. Apophis has retreated. We require extraction."

"Copy. Package secure," O'Neill said, changing the entire company's purpose with two words. "Execute withdrawal."

Turnpike answered on the company net. "Recon has the alternate route marked. Assault and recovered personnel follow us. Reserve moves next, support takes the wounded behind it, command follows, security peels on sequence and comes off last. Captain?"

Mercer's reply was immediate. "Execute. Bring them home."

✳

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: THEY DID IT. THEY ACTUALLY DID IT.

AN ENTHUSIAST: HE HIT A GOD WITH WEAPONIZED INCOMPETENCE.

FURY: They got the family out. That's the part I'm counting.

✳

Teal'c turned, breathless, wounded, and found Drey'auc already moving toward him, Rya'c a half-step behind her, uncertain still, but no longer raising a weapon against the man standing in front of him.

"Father," Rya'c said, testing the word for the first time in four years, and Teal'c, staff weapon finally lowered, allowed himself, for the first time since the fight began, to simply feel every part of what the day had actually cost and given him both at once.
