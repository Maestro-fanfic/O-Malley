Chapter 40 of 40

Staff Infection, A Retrospective

The gallery, one last time. The doors behind them, all thirty-nine of them, stand quiet now.

GLADLY: Okay. Big one. You want to do the honors, or should I?

FURY: You've done every honors so far. Go ahead.

GLADLY: (clearing a throat that doesn't exist) In a world where one Winslow sophomore's forum research got him mauled by dogs before he could finish a sentence he'd been building his whole short life toward... a teenager who spent years narrating himself as the hero of somebody else's show woke up as the world's least consensual roommate, inside a man whose entire vocabulary could fit on an index card. Neither of them asked for this. Both of them, thirty-nine chapters later, would tell you they wouldn't trade it back.

FURY: Not bad.

GLADLY: I've had time to workshop the whole-book version.

✳

GLADLY: Can we talk about the waiting room? My scene. Before any of this.

FURY: The scowl.

GLADLY: The scowl! Nobody's brought up the scowl since chapter two, and I think about it constantly.

FURY: You meant that scowl, Gladly. That wasn't a script note. You actually felt something, right before you handed the rest of it off.

GLADLY: I did. Weirdest part of the whole assignment, honestly, getting exactly one line and a face I didn't plan on making, and that being the part that stuck. I said my one line, "well, that didn't turn out like you thought it would," and the kid just froze, and something about watching him freeze made the whole thing feel less like a bit and more like actually catching somebody at the worst second of his life. Which, you know. It was. Then I was gone and somebody else did the real work. But I got my one second, and I meant every word of it.

FURY: That's the job, though. Playful in the delivery, exact in the execution. You held both. First time out, too, no less.

GLADLY: I didn't know if I was doing it right.

FURY: Thirty-nine chapters later, kid's laughing at card games with his dad. You did it right.

✳

GLADLY: Let's do the numbers. Self-control. Started at zero. Ended at... still not great, if we're honest, but he folded a bad hand on request in the last chapter without arguing about it first. That's growth.

FURY: Stick removal: also incomplete. But he laughed. Actually laughed, out loud, in front of witnesses, more than once by the end. First time in four years.

GLADLY: So neither diagnosis fully landed.

FURY: Neither diagnosis was ever supposed to fully land. Gladly said "a lesson in self control," not "total mastery of self control." He said "get the stick out of his butt," not "surgically remove the stick." Read the actual prescription next time.

GLADLY: ...Fair.

✳

GLADLY: Okay, favorite bits. Go.

FURY: The swordfight line. "The most dangerous person in a swordfight was never the swordsman." Paid off twice. Once against a patrol, once against an actual god. That's a callback that earned its keep.

GLADLY: Mine's the card game. Last chapter. "Tell him his hand is terrible and he should fold." No stakes, no danger, just a dad talking to his kid through a stranger's voice, and it's funnier and sadder than half the fight scenes.

FURY: That's the whole show in one line, honestly.

GLADLY: What's yours actually, though? The real one, not the tactic one.

FURY: (a pause) Rya'c folding the weapon down an inch at a time. Nobody wrote that as a big moment. It was the biggest moment in the book.

✳

GLADLY: Should we talk about the tic?

FURY: We should absolutely talk about the tic.

GLADLY: Eight, nine, ten times through the middle stretch, by my count. Twice a chapter at the start. Once every other chapter through the middle. And then...

FURY: Nothing. Last dozen chapters. Clean.

GLADLY: Even the reveal earlier, where I caught you doing it...

FURY: We are still not discussing that.

GLADLY: ...even that stopped happening. Somebody actually learned.

FURY: Second book anybody's written all the way through in this whole sandbox. Wasn't sure it'd happen this fast. Glad it did.

✳

GLADLY: Okay. The bigger question. Was this worth it? All of it? The dogs, the interrogation room, the kid with the staff weapon at the end?

FURY: Ask the guy who folded a bad poker hand and laughed about it a week after nearly dying in a hostage rescue. Ask his son, who called him "father" for the first time in four years and meant it. Ask the passenger who spent his whole life not mattering to anybody and ended up mattering enough to talk down a god's entire garrison from inside somebody else's skull.

GLADLY: That's not really an answer.

FURY: It's the only answer that was ever going to be honest. Nobody in this story got everything fixed. They got further along than they'd have gotten alone. That was always the deal. Read the series bible.

✳

GLADLY: What happens now? To them, I mean. After the last page.

FURY: Same thing that happens to everybody in this sandbox once the door closes on their story. They keep living it. We just don't get to watch anymore.

GLADLY: That's kind of sad.

FURY: It's kind of the point. We're not the main characters, Gladly. Never were. We're just the ones who got to watch somebody else figure out how to be less alone.

✳

GLADLY: One more thing. Before we go.

FURY: What.

GLADLY: Was I any good at this? The riffing. The commentary. All forty chapters of it.

FURY: (a long pause, the kind he doesn't usually give anyone) Yeah, Gladly. You were good at this.

GLADLY: ...Thanks.

FURY: Don't make it weird.

GLADLY: Too late. Already weird. Been weird since chapter two.

A door opens somewhere behind them, quiet, unhurried, the next entry's own beginning waiting on the other side of it. Neither of them walks through it. That's not their story to tell.

Fade. Staff Infection, complete. Thirty-nine chapters, one stubborn stick, one slowly-learning self-control problem, and, somewhere on the other side of a door neither man ever fully closed on the other, two people who turned out to be exactly what the other one had always needed and never once thought to ask for.

Indeed.
