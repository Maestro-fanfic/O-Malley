Chapter 18 of 40

Five

Carter sat up straighter. "This is ridiculous."

"What is?"

"This. All of it. You, going door to door tonight like some kind of one-man crisis outreach, telling each of us separately, alone, in the dark, like we're not all on the same team hearing the same thing." She stood, gathering her jacket off the back of the chair. "Daniel's still up. I guarantee O'Neill hasn't actually made it to sleep either, whatever he told you in that corridor. If we're doing this at nearly two in the morning, we should be doing it together. All of us. Properly. In the same room, for once tonight."

Teal'c found no honest objection. "I did not do this in pieces because I believed pieces were sufficient. I did it because I did not trust myself to tell all of you at once without losing the composure the smaller rooms allowed me."

"Yeah, well." Carter held the door open for him. "Tonight, you don't get the smaller room anymore."

✳

They found Daniel where they had left him, still bent over the same books with a mug gone cold beside them. He saw the two of them together and closed the notebook.

"I take it we're doing something," he said.

"We're doing something," Carter confirmed. "Where's O'Neill actually sleeping tonight? Because I don't believe for one second it's his own quarters."

Daniel considered this with the plain fondness of a man who'd known the answer to that question for four years running. "His office. Same as always, when he can't settle. He'll say he went to bed. He didn't."

They found him ten minutes later with his boots on the desk and the lights dimmed. He took in Carter, Daniel, and Teal'c filling his doorway at nearly two in the morning, then lowered his boots without making the available joke.

"Okay," he said. "This a delegation, or an ambush?"

"Neither," Teal'c said. "I find I no longer wish to say true things to each of you in separate rooms, one at a time, as though the truth diminished by being shared too widely too quickly. It does not. I should have realized that hours ago."

Daniel took the second chair. Carter perched on the corner of the desk. For the first time that night, all four of them occupied the same silence.

"So," O'Neill said finally. "Since we're all here. What's this actually about, Teal'c? Not the facts, I've got the facts. What's it actually about, for you."

Teal'c did not answer from habit.

"There is a teaching among my people, older than the word I gave two of you tonight, though it is the same teaching wearing a different shape. We do not measure a warrior's worth by the ground he holds. We measure it by whose place he is willing to take, when there is no longer time to weigh the cost of taking it. There is no greater love, in the accounting my people use, than a man who lays down what is his to keep, without being asked, for someone he has already decided is worth the keeping. I recited that teaching as doctrine for most of my life, true, unquestioned, and entirely theoretical. I did not feel its actual weight until a boy with no training and no true obligation to any of us threw himself, and this body, into a line of fire that was never his to stand in, simply because standing in it meant the rest of you reached the gate alive." He looked at each of them in turn, deliberately, the way he looked at a room before deciding what was safe to say in it. "That is what this is actually about, O'Neill. Not the fact of him. The shape of what he chose to be, the moment it mattered, without anyone asking it of him."

::you're gonna make me cry and I don't even have my own tear ducts anymore, this is deeply unfair::

O'Neill looked down at his desk. "You know I already did that. For you. Before any of us knew his name."

"I know it," Teal'c said. "I have known it since the moment it happened. I did not have the word for it then either. I merely knew that a man had chosen, in the half second there was to choose anything at all, to be the one who paid rather than the one who watched, and that I would have done the same for him without a second's more thought than he gave it."

"So it's not just a Greg thing," Daniel said. "It's the whole team. It's what's always been underneath all of this, every time one of you took a hit meant for somebody else, every time Carter ran toward the fire instead of away from it. We just never had a word that fit all of it at once."

"We had actions," Carter said, quiet. "We just never sat down and called it anything." She looked at Teal'c, and something in her voice sharpened, gentled, at the same time. "Can I ask him something? Greg, I mean. Directly. If that's something you can actually do."

"He can hear you now, if you speak to him. And I have no wish to filter his answer through my own mouth again tonight. Ask him yourself. He can answer for himself, if he is willing."

Carter addressed the air beside Teal'c's shoulder rather than his face. "Greg. P2X-908. When you threw yourself at those two Jaffa, when you dragged their attention off the rest of us, did you actually understand what you were risking? Or did you just do it?"

After a long pause, Teal'c's posture changed. No one had entered the room, but everyone looked toward the new arrival.

"Honestly?" Teal'c's voice said, and it was still, technically, his own voice, and was not, in any way that mattered, his own at all, cadence wrong, word choice wrong, something younger and rawer sitting where his usual economy usually lived. "I didn't think about it at all. That's kind of the whole point, isn't it? I wasn't calculating odds. I didn't have time to be brave about it on purpose. Teal'c needed room to actually think, and giving him that room was the only thing that felt like it mattered right then, more than whether I got hurt doing it, more than whether I even technically could get hurt doing it. I still don't fully know what would've happened to me if that body had gone down. Wasn't thinking about that either. I think that's supposed to scare me more than it does."

O'Neill's mouth twitched. Carter looked down before Greg could see the smile Teal'c had already felt.

"That's the whole teaching, right there," Teal'c said, his own voice returning as the borrowed cadence receded, quiet, without ceremony. "Not the choosing. The absence of needing to choose at all. A warrior who has to weigh the cost first has already lost something a shield-brother never has to find."

"Tell him," O'Neill started, out of four years of habit, and then visibly caught himself, redirected mid-sentence instead. "You're not background in this story. Not anymore. Wasn't ever gonna be, the second you did that without stopping to think about it first. That's not an accident, kid. That's exactly what the rest of us have been doing to each other for four years running. You just joined the club faster than any of us did."

Daniel leaned forward, elbows on his knees. "For the record, and I want this on the record, because I don't think we've actually said it plainly enough tonight: whatever this is, whatever we end up calling it once I've done the linguistic legwork, it's not a burden this team is carrying. It's the same thing we've always been to each other, finally with enough people in the room to say it out loud. Five of us instead of four. That's not smaller. It's not more complicated in any way that actually matters. It's just five people now willing to pay that same price for each other instead of four."

"Five," Carter repeated. "Yeah. Okay. Five."

✳

Four years of discipline offered Teal'c no useful response to the four people sharing O'Neill's small office. He found he did not miss it.

"Indeed," he said, and for once let the word carry every bit of what it actually meant.

No one moved for a while. When they finally left for beds they might or might not use, nobody said goodnight.
