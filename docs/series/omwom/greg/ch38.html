The Waiting Room, Again

The desert returned before either of them expected it to, sand and heat and a horizon that resolved into absence the way it always had, and Teal'c understood, with a lurch that had nothing to do with any wound he'd taken in the last several weeks, exactly where he was standing.

::wait.:: Greg's voice, and then, a half-second later, not just his voice. Greg himself, standing beside him in the sand, solid, visible, a separate body casting its own separate shadow for the first time since the night this had all started. ::wait, I can see you. I can actually see you.::

"As can I," Teal'c said, staring at a boy's face he had known only from the inside. "Greg Veder. It is stranger seeing you than being you."

"Yeah. No. Same." Greg looked down at his own hands, turning them over slowly, testing them the way a man tests a chair before trusting his full weight to it. "This is incredibly weird, and also, kind of the best thing that's happened to me in months, not gonna lie."

They talked until time stopped mattering. Teal'c had known Greg was sixteen; seeing all that courage housed in a narrow teenage frame made the fact new again. They exchanged gratitude, several awkward apologies, and enough laughter to embarrass both of them had either been inclined to keep count.

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: Okay. This is possibly the single best thing I have ever gotten to produce.

FURY: You said one line. The other actor did the work.

✳

It was Fury's voice, eventually, that interrupted them, warm despite the interruption, unhurried in exactly the way that meant more was coming.

"Not yet," Fury said, when Greg turned toward the sound of it, half-expecting the conversation to be finished. "One more guest. Wait."

"Wait for..."

The horizon answered before Fury did, resolving the way it always resolved in this place, absence becoming presence one careful step at a time, and it was Bra'tac who stepped out of it: not the tired, four-hundred-year-old rebel Teal'c had spent the last several weeks fighting alongside, but something underneath that face, older still, wearing it with an ease that belonged to someone who had chosen it rather than simply aged into it.

"Bra'tac," Teal'c said, and heard, in his own voice, the exact confusion he had carried since the door in Ch20, finally given somewhere honest to land.

"The very same face," the figure said, "though I suspect you already understood that much, some months ago, without either of us saying it plainly. I did not come here to explain myself, Teal'c. I came because I wished to see, with my own eyes, what became of the thing I started."

"What you started," Greg said, quiet now, "was the best thing that's ever happened to me. I know that probably sounds strange. It's still true."

"I am glad of it," Bra'tac's face said, "though I confess, I did not know, the day I first wore this face for the two of you, how far you would actually carry it between you. I have cast a great many roles in a great many stories. Few of them surprise me anymore. You did."

Teal'c found he had a question after all, one he had been carrying since the door itself. "Why this face. Why him, specifically, if only for a moment, before you gave the rest of the work to another."

"Because some jobs require warmth to open the door," Bra'tac's face said, "and some require weight to close it properly. I judged, that first day, that you would need both eventually, though not from the same source at the same time. I believe I was correct."

A silence passed between them, comfortable rather than heavy.

"You will not remember this conversation," the figure added, gently, "not in the way you remember the rest of it. That is simply how this works, and I will not pretend otherwise to either of you. But I wanted, before the account closes, for both of you to have had it regardless. Some things are worth saying even to someone who will not carry the saying of them forward."

"Then say the rest of it," Teal'c said, "while there is still time to hear it."

The figure considered them both a long moment, warmth in old eyes wearing a familiar face. "You gave each other exactly what the other one needed and never once knew how to ask for. That is rarer than either of you likely realizes, and rarer still that it happened by accident rather than by design." A pause, something almost fond in it. "Go on, now. Both of you. There is a book waiting to close, and I find, against my own better professional judgment, that I would rather you were there for the end of it than standing here with me a moment longer than necessary."

It turned to go, and stopped itself before it had gone more than a step, the horizon answering a second time, a second shape resolving out of the same absence that had produced the first.

This one did not wear the face with ease. It was simply his own, aged by four hundred real years rather than chosen and weathered by a life the figure beside him had only borrowed for an afternoon.

::wait,:: Greg said. ::wait, there's two of him...::

"There is one of me," the real Bra'tac said, dry, "and one more polished imitation, whom I understand I have you both to thank for putting to good use." He inclined his head toward the figure beside him. "I do not fully understand how I came to be standing here. I understand only that when a debt of this kind calls, a man answers it, however strange the summons."

The figure inclined its head in turn, something like genuine respect passing between the two faces. "I borrowed nothing I did not already recognize as worth borrowing," it said, and then, without further ceremony, stepped backward into the horizon and was simply gone, leaving the real Bra'tac standing alone in the sand.

Greg found himself, for possibly the first time in this entire ordeal, genuinely speechless.

"Greg Veder." Nothing was borrowed in the real Bra'tac's voice now. "I have lived four hundred years learning to be surprised by very little. You, it seems, are the exception."

"...hi," Greg managed, finally. "I've, um. I've heard a lot about you. Obviously. Through," he gestured vaguely at Teal'c, "the whole shared-body thing."

"As I have heard a great deal about you," Bra'tac said, "though secondhand, through a man who does not waste words on things unworthy of them, which I have come to understand is the highest compliment he is capable of paying anyone." He studied Greg a long moment, warmth working into an expression that had spent four hundred years learning not to show it easily. "Sha'reth kanar. I taught Teal'c that word before you were born, on a world you have never heard of, for reasons that had nothing to do with either of us knowing this day would ever come. I find I am glad, beyond anything I expected to feel about it, that the word found a second home."

Teal'c, watching the two of them, found he had nothing useful left to add, and settled instead for simply standing there, letting the moment be exactly as large as it actually was.

✳

The desert began to fade the way it always had, sand losing its grip on solidity, and Teal'c felt, rather than saw, Greg's separate shape dissolving back into something that was simply presence again, familiar, shared, no longer requiring its own shadow to be real.

::hey,:: Greg said, quieter now, already half something else again. ::for what it's worth. Best insertion job I've ever heard of. Ten out of ten. Would get accidentally merged with a four-hundred-year-old alien warrior again.::

::Considering the alternative was, by your own account, Rockville,:: Teal'c said, ::I have heard that comparison enough times now to understand precisely the compliment it actually is.::

::Yeah. Yeah, exactly.::

The fading paused, just once, one last interruption before the desert finally let them go.

::wait,:: Greg said. ::there's more of you out there, isn't there. Watching. This whole time.::

"Considerably more than either of you were ever told," said a voice neither of them recognized, dry, familiar in a way that took a full second to place, and then, all at once, entirely placed. "Consider this a professional courtesy. Most of the room doesn't get names. I'm making an exception."

A different voice, delighted, warm, entirely too pleased with itself for the moment to fully explain why, spoke up before Greg could answer. "Oh, I love this part. 'A voice.' No name, no face, just doing the heavy lifting from somewhere nobody's looking, and everybody assumes it's simple until it turns out to matter more than the person standing in front of them. That's my favorite kind of story. I have, personally, a great deal of experience being exactly that kind of voice."

FURY: Nobody asked for your resume.

"I wasn't asking permission," the voice said, cheerfully. "I was just being nice about announcing myself first. Most of the time I don't even bother with that part."

Greg's whole face did something complicated. ::wait. Wait, is that...::

"Yeah," the voice said, and there was no mistaking it now, the exact cadence of a man constitutionally incapable of leaving a silence alone. "It's me. Don't make it weird."

::YOU'VE BEEN IN THE GALLERY THIS WHOLE TIME? Oh my god, oh my god, that explains so much, I knew some of those lines had a voice I couldn't place...::

"In my defense," Jack said, "nobody actually told me I wasn't allowed to talk. I found out there was a room, and I assumed the usual rules applied. Turns out the usual rules were 'two guys do all the talking, everybody else sits quietly,' which, and I cannot stress this enough, has never once in my life been a rule I successfully followed."

FURY: For the record, we tried.

GLADLY: We tried so hard.

FURY: He found a loophole in a rule we didn't even know we were enforcing.

"I contain multitudes," Jack said, entirely unrepentant, and somewhere behind him, in the fading dark at the edge of everything, something that might have been laughter, and might have been several somethings, rustled once and went quiet again, patient, waiting its own turn in a room that, it turned out, was considerably more crowded than anybody had let on.

::there are more of you? How many more of you are there??::

"That," said a new voice, considerably more theatrical than the last, dripping with an enthusiasm that felt entirely out of place and somehow completely at home anyway, "darling, is a question for an entirely different story." A pause, something almost wistful threading through it. "Though I will say, before I go, four hundred years of quiet devotion and one perfectly good sacrifice offered up right at the end? Be still my heart. I do love a story that knows exactly where to bleed for maximum effect."

GLADLY: ...I feel like that's a compliment? I genuinely cannot tell if that's a compliment.

FURY: It's a compliment. Trust me, you don't want to hear the alternative review.

"Oh, hush, both of you, I'm being perfectly lovely," the voice said, delighted, and then, as quickly and completely as she'd arrived, she was gone, leaving behind nothing but the faint, oddly pleasant impression of having just been very thoroughly judged by someone with excellent taste and absolutely no restraint. "Ta-ta for now, darlings. Do try to keep the ending half as gorgeous as the buildup deserved."

GLADLY: ...okay, we are absolutely not getting into that one today.

FURY: Nope.

A third voice followed hers, unhurried, cultured, the kind of voice that sounded like it had spent a long career deciding exactly how much of any given truth a room actually needed to hear. "For what it's worth, I've technically already been to this particular sandbox once before, under an earlier management. Different face at the time. Nobody remembers the pilot episode." A soft, knowing chuckle. "Funny business, this. You spend a career playing every angle in the room, and every so often the room turns out to have been playing you first."

GLADLY: Wait, what does that even...

FURY: (a long, weary sigh) Some jokes only land for the people who've read the credits twice. Let it go, Gladly.

::was that...::

::I do not know,:: Teal'c said, honestly, ::and I am beginning to suspect I do not wish to.::
