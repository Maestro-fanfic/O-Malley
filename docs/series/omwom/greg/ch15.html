The Crack

P6X-334 turned out to be nothing more dangerous than a trading outpost, minor, unaffiliated, the kind of world SG-1 visited a dozen times a year without incident, stalls and awnings and the low hum of a market day going on regardless of who'd just walked in through the standing stone. The local quartermaster, an anxious, overworked man named Bel who reminded Greg, with an intensity he couldn't fully explain, of every substitute teacher who'd ever tried too hard to be liked, was in the middle of a genuine crisis: a shipment of grain seed had arrived contaminated, half the settlement's spring planting at risk, and nobody in his own chain of command willing to take responsibility for the decision to destroy it and start over. He'd cornered Daniel first, hoping an off-worlder might carry more weight than his own superiors seemed willing to lend him, and Daniel, deep in unrelated trade terms, had gently redirected him toward the rest of the team without much hope it would help.

::oh my god, I know this guy. Not literally, but I know this guy, this is a guy who needs somebody to just tell him it's okay to make the call...::

Daniel was mid-negotiation over trade terms entirely unrelated to the seed crisis, O'Neill visibly counting the minutes until they could reasonably leave, and it was Greg, unable to help himself, unable to do anything else, who fed Teal'c the words.

::tell him it's not his fault the shipment came bad. Tell him the actual failure was whoever signed off on it before it ever reached him, and that catching it now, before planting, is the only reason this isn't a disaster instead of an inconvenience. He needs somebody bigger than him to say it's not his call to feel guilty about.::

Teal'c said exactly that, in his own voice, his own cadence, no trace of Greg's rambling delivery anywhere in it, and Bel's whole posture changed, a man visibly setting down a weight he'd been carrying since the shipment arrived.

"You have my thanks," Bel said, and then, entirely unprompted, entirely earnest, "You have a very calming presence. Like the old stone wardens carved into our west gate, from before my grandmother's time. They say a look like that alone used to be worth more than a garrison."

::oh my god. Oh my god, "stone wardens," that's, that's literally the Stone Guardians, that's the exact thing from the show, this guy's never even heard of Earth and his own actual culture has the same... okay, wait, that actually tracks, doesn't it, if the show really was based on leaked reports, somebody who wrote it must have gotten a description of an actual world and just, I don't know, translated it into a costume budget...::

There was a pause, the kind that could have gone several directions.

"I do not know what a Wormhole X-Treme is," Teal'c said, entirely deadpan, to a man who had never once heard those words in his life and had absolutely no idea what to do with them, "but I am given to understand it is not what it appears to be."

Bel blinked, visibly recalculating, and decided, with the diplomatic instincts of a man who has learned not to question off-worlders too closely, to simply nod as though that had been a completely normal thing to say.

::YOU DID THE BIT. YOU ACTUALLY DID THE BIT, AND HE HAS NO IDEA WHAT YOU'RE TALKING ABOUT, THAT'S EVEN FUNNIER, I've been trying to get you to do a bit for FIFTEEN CHAPTERS...::

Teal'c laughed before four years of practiced stillness could stop him. It was not a large sound, but it was the first anyone on that team had heard from him.

Daniel turned first, trade terms abandoned, delight and shock fighting across his face. Carter followed with a small smile she did not hide. O'Neill kept watching the horizon. Four years beside the man had taught him when looking directly at a thing would only make it retreat.

Bel, for his part, looked simply pleased with himself, entirely unaware he'd just done anything worth remarking on, already moving on to thank Daniel a second time for the introduction.

Teal'c recovered himself within a second, the laugh already gone, his face already settling back into its ordinary stillness. But a second had been enough. A second, once heard, could not be unheard by the people standing close enough to catch it.

::I'm sorry. I didn't mean to, I wasn't trying to make you...::

"Do not apologize," Teal'c said, quiet enough that it stayed between them. "I have not laughed in four years, Greg Veder. I did not know, until this moment, how much I had missed the sound of it."
