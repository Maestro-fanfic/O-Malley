What Only Greg Sees

The door's control mechanism sat behind a narrow observation slit, and through it, once Carter had fought the panel beside it into sluggish, half-functional life, they could see the one thing none of them had prepared for: not a locking mechanism at all, but a young Jaffa, alone, staff weapon raised, standing between the team and the door's release exactly as though he had been posted there for precisely this contingency.

He could not have been more than sixteen or seventeen, by human reckoning. Teal'c felt the recognition arrive before his conscious mind had finished assembling the pieces that produced it, a shape of the face, a set of the shoulders, unmistakable even filtered through four years of growth he had not been present to watch.

"Rya'c."

The name left him before he had decided to say it, and on the other side of the slit, the boy's whole body went rigid.

"You are not my father," Rya'c said, staff weapon steady despite the tremor Teal'c could see running through his voice. "My father is dead to this house. A coward who abandoned his god and his family both. I have been told this since I was old enough to understand what it meant."

"Rya'c, I am..."

"Do not." The staff weapon didn't waver. "I have heard this trick before. Apophis warned us the Tau'ri would attempt exactly this, a face wearing my father's shape to weaken my resolve. I will not be weakened."

Teal'c felt something close to despair arrive, fast and total, four years of hope collapsing against the one obstacle he had never once prepared himself to face: a son so thoroughly shaped by a god's careful cruelty that the truth itself had become indistinguishable from the lie built to counter it. Direct appeal would not work. He could see that plainly, the same discipline that had carried him through every other crisis in this compound offering him nothing here but the certainty of his own failure.

::wait. Wait, let me, I think I actually know what to say, but you have to trust me, and you have to let me say it in your voice because it needs to come from you, not from me...::

::I do not know what there is left to say.::

::I do. Please. I've spent my whole life on the other side of exactly this. Let me.::

Teal'c, out of options and running out of time, did the hardest thing he had done since a boy's voice first arrived behind his eyes: he let go entirely, not of his body, but of his own certainty about what needed saying, and trusted a passenger's read over his own trained instincts one final time.

"Rya'c." The voice was Teal'c's own, but the words, when they came, were not the words he would have chosen. "I am not going to tell you I am your father and demand you believe it. You have been told too many lies for one more claim to mean anything. I am going to tell you something else instead. Somebody who has never once been able to make his own father proud, no matter how hard he tried, no matter how much he wanted it. Somebody who spent years being told exactly what he was worth by a man who never once bothered to actually look at him."

THE WATCHER: Greg has the wound. Teal'c has the voice Rya'c might believe.

"I do not know," the voice went on, quieter now, "what Apophis has told you your father is. I know only that whoever raised me to say what I am about to say to you has never had the chance to say it to his own son directly, and would give almost anything, right now, in this exact moment, for someone to have said it to him at your age. So I am saying it to you instead, because I can, because you are here, and because it might be the only true thing that reaches you through everything else you have been told."

Rya'c had not lowered the weapon. But something in his face had shifted, the rigid certainty cracking, just slightly, around the edges.

"Whatever he did," Teal'c said, and this part, finally, was entirely his own, "whatever choice cost you a father, I promise you it was never because he stopped thinking of you. Not for one day. Not for one hour of the four years since. If that is a lie, it is the only one either of us has told you today, and I do not believe it is one."

The staff weapon lowered, an inch, then another, Rya'c's whole body trembling now with something that had stopped being certainty a long while ago.

"How would you know," Rya'c said, barely above a whisper, "what he thought. If you are not him."

"Because I have felt exactly what it costs a person to be thought of and not reached," Teal'c said, and let both truths, his own and Greg's, sit together in the words without separating them. "From both directions at once. That is not something Apophis could have taught either of us to fake."

Rya'c said nothing for a long moment, staff weapon held loosely now, more forgotten than lowered, a boy caught between everything he had been taught to believe and something in his own chest that had just recognized truth without his permission to do so.

"My mother," he said finally, voice unsteady, "has never once spoken ill of you. Even when others in this house did, in front of her, expecting her silence to mean agreement. She never agreed. I did not understand, until this moment, why she never once let herself believe what I was told to believe."

"She knew me," Teal'c said, simply. "Longer, and better, than any story Apophis could build to replace what she actually knew."

::tell him it's okay to still be scared. Even if he believes us. Being scared and believing something aren't opposites.::

"It is permitted," Teal'c said, relaying it exactly as it had been offered, "to still be afraid, even now. Belief and fear are not opposites, Rya'c. You are allowed to hold both at once, the way I have held them both since the moment I saw your face through that slit."

✳

Rya'c's hand found the release mechanism, shaking, uncertain, a boy choosing to believe something he had been trained his whole life not to, and the door behind him began, slowly, to open.
