Chapter 14 of 40

Drey'auc and Rya'c

The infirmary released him the next morning, the leg bruised badly but nothing broken, and Teal'c did not return to his quarters. He walked instead to the small, unused briefing room on Level 22 that nobody bothered with after hours, sat, and did something he had not done in the whole of the time since a boy's voice had first arrived behind his eyes.

He opened the door himself.

::hey. You don't have to, you know. After yesterday. I wasn't trying to trade you into it.::

"I know," Teal'c said. "I am not doing this because you gave me something and I owe you a return. I am doing this because I have carried it alone for longer than I can any longer justify, and you are, whatever else is true, the only one who has ever actually been inside the carrying of it with me."

He did not ease into it. He had never been a man who eased into anything that mattered.

"Her name is Drey'auc. My wife." The word sat strangely in his mouth, unused this long. "My son is called Rya'c. He was a boy when I left them, younger than you are now. He will not be a boy any longer."

::how long has it been?::

"Four years, by the reckoning of this world. I do not know how Apophis's domain marks time against it, only that it has not stopped moving without me in it."

::what was she like? Drey'auc. If you don't mind me asking.::

Something in Teal'c's chest, already raw, gave a little further at the question, gently asked, no agenda behind it beyond the wanting to know. "Sharper than I was, in most of the ways that mattered. She saw the rot in Apophis's service before I allowed myself to see it, said as much to me more than once, in whatever privacy two people can find inside a god's household. I did not listen quickly enough. When I finally did, it was already too late to leave together."

::that's not on you. The timing.::

"It is entirely on me, Greg Veder. That is not self-pity. It is simply the fact of it."

Greg did not argue. The empty briefing room held the silence without asking either of them to fill it.

The memory, when it came, came on his own terms this time, offered rather than torn loose: the night of his defection, Drey'auc's face in the doorway, not pleading, never pleading, a woman who had married a First Prime and understood exactly what that meant about the choices available to both of them. Rya'c asleep in the next room, too young to be woken for a goodbye that would have meant nothing to him yet. Teal'c had not said goodbye to his son at all. He had told himself, at the time, that it was a mercy. He had spent four years since increasingly unsure that it had been anything but a coward's math, dressed up as kindness.

✳

::do they know? That you think about them? That you're, I don't know, out here trying to figure out how to...::

"I do not know what they believe of me," Teal'c said, and this, more than anything else he had said, was the part he had never once permitted himself to say aloud. "Apophis will have shaped it to his own purpose. A traitor. A coward. Whatever tale serves him best against them, that is the tale they have been given, and I have had no way, in four years, to offer them a different one."

::the kid we brought back. From the ambush. He said Apophis is tightening up, keeping people closer. That's them, isn't it. That's why it landed the way it landed.::

"Yes."

He did not soften it further than that single word, and Greg, to his credit, did not ask him to.

::okay. So what do we do.::

"I do not yet know," Teal'c said, and the admission cost him something separate from everything else he had already given away in this room, a First Prime's whole training built around always, always knowing what came next. "I know only that I am no longer carrying the not-knowing by myself. That is a different thing than it was yesterday."

✳

Teal'c stayed in the empty briefing room, with no candle and no Kel-no-reem to turn the confession into ritual. One other person now knew. It was not relief, but it was lighter than what he had carried in alone.

::for what it's worth,:: Greg said, quiet, none of his usual rush behind it, ::Rya'c's got a dad who never once stopped trying to get back to him. That's not nothing either. Whatever else Apophis has told him.::

Teal'c did not trust his own voice with an answer to that. He let the silence stand in for it instead, and found, somewhat to his own surprise, that the silence said enough.
