Chapter 10 of 40

What Greg's Actually For

They hadn't meant to take a prisoner. It happened in the last chaotic seconds before the gate, one of Va'lar's Jaffa cut off from the retreat by his own side's collapsing line, and O'Neill's call, fast and unglamorous, had been to zat him rather than leave a wounded eighteen-year-old bleeding out on an alien planet neither of them would ever see again. He'd come through the gate unconscious and left the infirmary two days later still refusing to give his name, which was, General Hammond noted dryly, technically an improvement over refusing to speak at all.

Teal'c volunteered for the interrogation. Nobody else on base spoke to a captured Jaffa quite the way he did, soldier to soldier, the shared grammar of a life neither Daniel's diplomacy nor O'Neill's charm could reach.

It was not going well.

The room was small, gray, deliberately unremarkable, a table bolted to the floor and two chairs, one of which Teal'c had not yet used. He had opened with the standard approach, the one that had worked on a dozen prisoners before this one: his own history laid out plainly, First Prime once, defector now, proof that the path away from Apophis existed and could be survived. It had worked on soldiers who still believed, on some level, that loyalty was a question of conviction. This one didn't so much as blink at it.

"You will not be harmed," Teal'c said, for perhaps the fourth time, standing rather than sitting across the small table, because sitting had gotten him nothing either. "Cooperation earns you consideration. Apophis's reach does not extend into this facility."

The boy, and he was a boy, barely older than Rya'c would be by now, said nothing, jaw set, eyes fixed somewhere over Teal'c's shoulder.

::he's not being stubborn. Well, he is, but that's not the actual thing. Look at his hands.::

Teal'c kept his eyes on the boy's face and registered the observation anyway.

::his hands are doing this whole thing where they keep almost reaching for his collar, right where a family mark would sit if he had one visible, and every time you say "cooperation" he does it again. He's not scared of you. He's scared of what happens to somebody back home if word gets out he talked.::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

AN ENTHUSIAST: He's good at this part.

THE WATCHER: He's good at people. Nobody ever notices because he works so hard to make sure they notice everything else first.

A DRY VOICE FROM THE BACK: And also because he puts real effort into showing the world how little he gets. The Void Cowboy brand does not maintain itself.

GLADLY: I'd put it on the trailer.

✳

Teal'c considered the read for the length of a single breath, weighing a passenger's unverifiable instinct against a lifetime of interrogations conducted the disciplined, procedural way, and found, somewhat to his own surprise, that he trusted it. Not because Greg had been right before, though he had, once, with the sigil. Because it matched something Teal'c himself half-recognized and hadn't let himself look at directly: the particular stillness of a man protecting someone he can't protect from here.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

THE SKEPTIC: Fifth.

AN ENTHUSIAST: I missed it.

THE SKEPTIC: That's why the spreadsheet exists.

✳

He pulled out the chair across from the boy and sat, for the first time, close enough that the gesture itself meant something.

"I had a wife," Teal'c said, and the boy's eyes flicked to him for the first time in the whole interview, startled by the change in register. "A son. Both remain in Apophis's domain. I know precisely what it costs to imagine what happens to them because of a choice I made, alone, in a room very much like this one."

The boy's jaw didn't unclench, not fully, but something in his shoulders dropped half an inch, the give of a man who's been braced for a fight nobody actually offered him.

"I am not asking you to betray your family," Teal'c went on. "I am asking whether there is a version of the truth you can give me that keeps them safe rather than the reverse. Tell me who, and I will tell you what I can and cannot promise honestly, no more than that."

::that's it, that's exactly it, oh my god, you're actually good at this, why didn't you just do this from the start...::

::I did not know, until you told me, that this was the door to try.::

It was the first time Teal'c had answered him mid-scene, silent and internal, no external word given away, and Greg went quiet in a way that had nothing to do with being told to.

The boy talked. Not everything, not at first, halting, careful, testing each admission against whatever consequence he still feared before offering the next one. "My sister," he said eventually, the first real crack in his guard. "She is young. Twelve summers. If word reached Apophis that I had spoken to the Tau'ri, it would not be my safety in question. It would be hers."

"I understand," Teal'c said, and meant it more completely than the boy could possibly have known. "I will do what is within my power to see that word does not reach him."

It was a name, a village, and enough of a thread for Daniel and Carter to start pulling. Then came the detail Teal'c had not prepared for: Apophis had recently consolidated forces and tightened his hold on the outlying households he kept as leverage. Drey'auc and Rya'c were among them, though the boy did not know those names meant anything to the man across the table.

Teal'c did not let it show. He had learned that lesson longer ago than anything else he owned.

✳

"You have done well," Teal'c told the boy, and meant it, before General Hammond's people came to take him somewhere safer than a holding cell. Alone in the corridor afterward, staff weapon absent, no mission clock counting down, no team waiting on a call, Teal'c allowed himself, finally, to feel the thing he'd kept off his face for the better part of an hour.

::hey. You okay?::

::No,:: Teal'c said, which was, itself, a kind of first. ::But I know a door to try now, when I am ready. That is more than I had this morning.::

He stood in the corridor under the fluorescent hum. There was no mission clock counting down toward Drey'auc and Rya'c, no order to give, nothing he could do with the knowledge tonight. Greg stayed with him anyway.
