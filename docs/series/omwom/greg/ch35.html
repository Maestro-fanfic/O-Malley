In Full

He did not summarize. Smaller rooms had taught him what summaries omitted, and he would not make the omission merely because this audience was larger.

"His name is Greg Veder," Teal'c said. "He did not arrive by any means this program has a category for. Shortly before the mission to P4X-212, without warning, without my consent, and without, so far as I can determine, malicious intent, he became present within me. Not possession, in the sense your files will understand that word. A passenger. A second consciousness, entire and separate from my own, sharing this body and this mind without displacing either."

A woman near the far end of the table, insignia Teal'c did not immediately recognize, leaned forward first. "Separate how? We have files on Goa'uld symbiosis. Is this a variant we haven't documented?"

"No. A symbiote takes. Greg Veder has never once taken anything from me that I did not, eventually, choose to give him. The distinction is not semantic. It is the entire distinction."

"Then walk us through it," she said. "From the start. Don't summarize for our benefit. I'd rather have the real shape of it than a clean one."

So he did.

"The first days were disorientation alone," Teal'c said. "A voice in my own skull I could not silence, and did not, at first, believe was real. I considered, more than once, that some enemy of mine had finally succeeded in breaking something in me that four hundred years of discipline had otherwise kept whole. I was wrong. I did not know that yet, and the not-knowing cost me sleep I have never entirely recovered."

He let that land before continuing.

"At P4X-212, he recognized a sigil from an entertainment of his own world, one I would never have placed myself. It led us to a hidden weapons cache and changed an entire tribute-world's read on the fight ahead of it. That find was his. I will not claim otherwise."

"The confidence that followed it was mine to manage, and I managed it poorly. I trusted an unverified guess further than it had earned, passed it along as intelligence, and it nearly cost Major Carter her life in the field that followed. That failure is mine, not his. I have not allowed myself to forget it since."

"At P2X-908, cut off from the gate and outnumbered six to four, I handed him control of this body for the first time, out of necessity rather than trust, because I had no better option left. He had no training whatsoever. He fell more than once in the seconds that followed. But those same seconds bought the difference between all of us walking away from that patrol and none of us doing so. I would not be standing in this room tonight without them."

He neither softened the account nor performed contrition he did not feel. Carter's hand went briefly to her shoulder at the mention of P4X-212. Daniel had heard the account weeks ago and watched the room instead.

A man Teal'c placed as Air Force, three chairs down from Hammond, finally spoke. "You're describing something that had every opportunity to compromise this team's operational integrity, repeatedly, and instead it appears to have improved it. I have to ask the obvious question. Why should this room believe that outcome was earned rather than lucky?"

"You should not believe it on my word alone. Ask Colonel O'Neill whether the intelligence that led us to Apophis's compound was sound. Ask Major Carter whether the raid company's scheme held when the alarm sounded and the map failed. I am not asking this room to trust a stranger's account of a miracle. Evaluate the incidents themselves."

"He is also," Bra'tac said, unprompted, the first he had spoken since the questioning began, "Sha'reth kanar to Teal'c. I will not translate the word for this room. It is not owed to strangers. I offer only that among my people, it is not a term extended carelessly, and Teal'c has extended it to few enough in four hundred years of my own acquaintance with him that I can name every one of them from memory."

The room absorbed that in its own way, some of them clearly filing it as sentiment rather than substance, a few, Teal'c noted, filing it as exactly the kind of substance Bra'tac had intended.

::you okay? This is a lot of people staring at you at once.::

::I am better than I expected, telling this many strangers at once,:: Teal'c said. ::I thought it would cost more.::

::good. Because for what it's worth, I'd do the whole thing again. Every part of it. Even the parts that sucked.::

He told them, finally, about the rescue itself in full: the handoff at the door with Apophis's guard between them and Rya'c, and the moment against Apophis himself when trained precision had failed and something wholly unpredictable had succeeded in its place, an accident of chaos that no plan, his or anyone else's, could have produced alone.

O'Neill did not so much as shift in his seat through any of it, steady in a way that read, to anyone who didn't already know why, as simple command-level composure. Teal'c knew better. It was the stillness of a man who had already sat with this exact information once, alone, and had already decided, days ago, precisely where he stood on it. Daniel and Carter wore the same borrowed calm beside him, three people in a room full of strangers all quietly already knowing the ending of a story the rest of the table was only now being told.

The room did not erupt. It did something Teal'c had not fully prepared for instead: it listened, carefully, professionally, the same measured attention this program gave any anomaly that had proven itself operationally sound rather than operationally dangerous.

THE WATCHER: Before the anomaly. That's the part he refused to leave out.

Hammond spoke before anyone else could claim the silence. "Teal'c. I'm not interested in relitigating a decision that clearly worked out. I am interested in whether this arrangement poses any ongoing risk to this program or its personnel."

"None that I am aware of, General. If anything, the reverse. I would not be alive, and Drey'auc and Rya'c would remain in Apophis's domain, without him."

Hammond held the room's attention a moment longer, weighing it, and nodded once, the same decisive gesture Teal'c had come to associate with the man's actual authority, quieter and more absolute than anything the visiting brass could offer.

"Then as far as I'm concerned, this stays exactly what it's been: an asset to this program, handled with the same discretion we handle every other classified element of it. Colonel O'Neill, get with Teal'c on a compartmented update to the relevant files. Anyone else in this room with concerns can bring them to me directly, not to a rumor mill."

"None of what Teal'c has said today goes into an open report, personal notes, or any file outside that compartment." Hammond's gaze settled on an unnamed face partway down the table, one of the visiting officers who had said nothing through the meeting but had clearly been taking notes of his own. "Is that clear?"

"Yes, sir."

✳

Cut to the gallery.

The quiet officer's voice returned. "Hammond put his own name between Teal'c and the room. That's the job."

The oil-rig voice answered, "And told everybody where to bring the argument."

✳

O'Neill caught him in the corridor afterward, the same corridor, Teal'c noted, where so much of this had already played out between them.

"How's it feel?" O'Neill asked, no preamble, the same directness he'd brought to the locked office the night everything had actually started. "Being on the actual record now. Not just between the two of us."

"Lighter than I expected," Teal'c admitted. "I did not know what the secrecy cost until it ended."

"Yeah. That tends to be how secrets work. Heavier held than told, every time, and nobody ever believes it till after." O'Neill leaned against the wall, some of the formal debrief posture finally sliding off him now that the room behind them was empty. "Hammond handled that about as clean as I've ever seen him handle anything. Wasn't sure even he'd go that far out on a limb in front of visiting brass."

"Nor was I."

::he already knew, and he still sat there and let them ask you like it was news. That's, that's actually kind of impressive, holding a secret that well.::

::He has had a great deal of practice holding things,:: Teal'c said. ::It is, I am coming to understand, simply part of what the word brother has always meant to him.::

"For what it's worth," O'Neill added, already turning to go, "I'm glad it worked out the way it did. Both halves of it. The rescue, and whatever this turns out to be long-term. You've earned some good news, Teal'c. Try to let yourself actually enjoy it."

He was gone before Teal'c had an answer ready, which was, Teal'c was beginning to understand, simply how the man operated, leaving the last word unspoken and somehow still entirely landed.
