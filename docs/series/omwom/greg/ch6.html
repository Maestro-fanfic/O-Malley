Chapter 6 of 40

False Confidence

The collectors arrived at midday, three Jaffa in the service of a minor Goa'uld none of them had a name for yet, escorting a hovering supply sled and moving with the particular unhurried confidence of men who had never once been refused. Their armor bore a mark Teal'c did recognize, a lesser house, one of a dozen such houses that rose and fell in Apophis's shadow without ever quite mattering enough to be remembered by name, which told him less about their strength than about their arrogance. Lesser houses overcompensated. He had served long enough to know that much without needing to see it proven.

SG-1 watched from the tree line, close enough to act, far enough, O'Neill hoped, to avoid having to. The village had gone quiet around them in a way that felt rehearsed, doors shut, animals penned, the whole square arranged like a stage set for a performance everyone present had given more than once before.

::okay, okay, I've got this, I actually know this one, in the episode the collectors always back off if you damage the tribute crate in front of them, because it's supposed to be, like, a sacred insult thing, an honor thing, they can't be seen accepting a spoiled tribute, so if we just, like, break one of the jars before they load it, they'll leave, that's literally the whole plan from the show, it works every single time...::

✳

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: Oh, I remember this plan! This is a good plan!

FURY: It's not a good plan.

GLADLY: It worked in the show.

✳

Teal'c did not immediately dismiss it. The previous night's mark had been real, useful, earned him something he had not expected to feel about the boy's borrowed knowledge. That success sat close enough behind him that it made the next voice easier to hear than it should have been.

"O'Neill." He kept his own voice low, level, the way he delivered every piece of intelligence he had ever passed his commanding officer. "There may be a custom regarding damaged tribute. Spoiled goods refused rather than accepted."

O'Neill's eyebrow went up, but four years of Teal'c never once passing along something worthless bought the idea more weight than it had earned. "You sure about that?"

Teal'c hesitated, the length of that hesitation the only honest answer he had. He was not sure. He was relying on a source he did not fully trust, delivered by a voice he had only just begun to.

::yeah, no, I mean, pretty sure, I mean it worked in the show, and shows are usually based on something real, right, that's like, the whole thing you said, THIS is what the show was based on, so if the show got that detail, it probably came from somewhere real, right?::

It was, Teal'c would think later, exactly the reasoning that got soldiers killed. Not malice. Not even carelessness, precisely. Just a chain of if-thens built on hope instead of verification, each link plausible enough on its own that the whole chain felt sturdier than any single piece of it actually was.

"I believe it worth attempting," Teal'c said.

Carter moved before he'd finished the sentence, close enough to the sled to put a hand on the nearest jar, and cracked it hard enough against the sled's edge to shatter it in full view of the collecting party.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

PEANUT GALLERY: I could cite an entire page of reasons why this is a terrible idea.

FURY: In the show, nobody bleeds.

✳

The Jaffa did not retreat.

The Jaffa raised weapons, all three at once, staff blasts scorching the dirt a body's width from where Carter had been standing a half second before Teal'c's own shout put her flat behind cover, and what followed was not a negotiation, it was a fight neither side had planned to have this early, chaotic and close, villagers scattering into the doorways they'd left cracked open for exactly this, O'Neill's return fire buying just enough seconds for the team to break contact and pull back into the trees rather than the clean, bloodless retreat the borrowed plan had promised.

It lasted perhaps ninety seconds. Teal'c put himself between Carter and the nearest line of fire twice and took a glancing staff blast across one shoulder plate that would leave a bruise he had no intention of mentioning. By the time they broke for the trees, one Jaffa was down, the other two were falling back toward the sled, and SG-1 was running hard for the gate.

::oh my god oh my god I'm so sorry, I didn't, I really thought, I was so sure, I was SO sure, oh my god is Carter okay, please tell me Carter's okay...::

"Major Carter is unharmed," Teal'c said, once they'd put real distance behind them, once the gate address was keyed and the wormhole's blue wash lit the trees around them. His voice, to anyone listening, gave away exactly as much as it always did. Nothing.

O'Neill clapped him once on the good shoulder on the way past, still running hot on adrenaline, too wired to fully process the near miss yet, and said something about buying the intel desk a drink for the bum steer on "no visible military presence," and then they were all through the gate and it was, as far as the rest of SG-1 was concerned, simply another mission that had gone sideways the way missions sometimes did.

Internally was a different account entirely.

"You were certain," he said, and there was an edge in it now he did not try to soften, "of something you had no true grounds to be certain of. A story built for entertainment, and you presented it as knowledge."

::I know. I know, okay, I know, you don't have to, I already feel like garbage about it, you don't have to make it worse...::

"I am not attempting to make it worse. I am telling you that Major Carter was nearly killed because I permitted a guess to be spoken as though it were fact, and I permitted it because the last time you offered something, it proved true, and I allowed that single success to purchase more trust than it had earned." He stepped through the event horizon a half pace behind O'Neill, the cold rush of it no distraction at all from what he was still saying, even mid-transit. "That failure is mine as much as yours. But I require you to understand the difference, in the future, between what you have seen and what you merely believe."

::...yeah. Yeah, okay. I get it. I'm sorry. I really am.::

He said nothing else on the walk through the SGC's gate room. He did not trust the next words to come out measured.
