Chapter 7 of 40

Previously, On...

The gallery, between doors. Six chapters down. Nobody in the story knows this room exists.

FURY: You want to do the honors, or should I.

GLADLY: Oh, me, please, I've been practicing.

A throat clears, somewhere that doesn't have a throat.

GLADLY: In a world where one Winslow sophomore's forum research got him mauled by dogs before he could finish his sentence... a teenager who spent his whole life narrating himself as the hero of somebody else's show wakes up as the world's least consensual roommate, inside a man whose entire vocabulary could fit on an index card.

FURY: That's not bad.

GLADLY: I workshopped it. Anyway. Our hero discovers that meditation doesn't work on grief, self-control isn't a setting you can just switch to, and, this is the part I really love, that the show he used to make fun of at lunch was based on something so much bigger than the show ever let on, and now he's living in the parts they cut for time.

FURY: He also almost got a decorated Air Force officer killed on his very first real assignment.

GLADLY: ...I was getting to that part.

FURY: Sure you were.

GLADLY: Look, the mark on the post was real. He wasn't wrong about everything.

FURY: He was wrong about the part that mattered. There's a difference between a detail and a plan, Gladly, and he built a plan out of a rerun.

GLADLY: In fairness, so would I have. That episode had a real emotional arc.

FURY: It had a laugh track.

GLADLY: Can we talk about the meditation scene? I love the meditation scene.

FURY: The one where it doesn't work?

GLADLY: That's the one! Four thousand years of discipline, and one anxious teenager just walks right through it like it's a screen door. I don't know why that's funny to me. It shouldn't be funny. It's a little bit funny.

FURY: It's funny because it's true. You can't meditate your way out of not being alone anymore. Doesn't matter how good you are at it.

GLADLY: ...Okay, now you've made it sad.

FURY: It was always a little sad. You just like the version where it's only funny.

A pause. Somewhere in the back of the gallery, one of the anonymous watchers, present since the very first door, leans forward.

AN ANONYMOUS WATCHER: Has anyone else noticed the narrator keeps doing that thing?

GLADLY: What thing?

THE WATCHER: "The particular something of a man who." "The specific something of somebody who." It's happened, by my count, in every single chapter so far. Twice a chapter, like clockwork.

FURY: (a long beat) Huh.

GLADLY: Oh, now that you say it, it is everywhere. "The specific weary patience of a man who." "The particular unhurried confidence of men who." It's not even hidden. It's just sitting there.

FURY: This is only the second one of these anybody's actually written all the way through. Give it time.

GLADLY: Should we... do something about it?

FURY: We watch. That's the job. Somebody get the beverage.

Somewhere, off to the side, a machine hums to life and dispenses something labeled, unhelpfully, PLOT-RELEVANT BEVERAGE. Nobody drinks it. Nobody ever does.

GLADLY: So. Should we recap the theme, or just let it sit there being obvious?

FURY: Do the theme.

GLADLY: Right. So. Two people who are each exactly what the other one's missing, and neither of them asked for the delivery. One needs to learn there's a version of caring that isn't a performance. The other needs to learn that being seen isn't the same thing as being erased. Ancient stuff. Very "physician, heal thyself." Except the physician has a staff weapon and the patient is fifteen years overdue for a permission slip.

FURY: He's sixteen.

GLADLY: Right, sixteen, sorry, I still think of him as fifteen, that's on me.

FURY: (dry) Genuinely reassuring, coming from his teacher.

GLADLY: I meant it the good way!

FURY: There isn't a good way.

Another pause, shorter this time.

GLADLY: Anyway. Next up: a man who's spent decades pretending a wound isn't still bleeding, a boy who's about to find out exactly how deep that wound goes, and somewhere out there, a god who used to own both of them in different ways and hasn't finished being petty about losing either one.

FURY: That's a good line.

GLADLY: I workshopped that one too.

THE WATCHER: Before you go. The false-confidence chapter. The one where the kid nearly gets the blonde one killed.

FURY: Carter.

THE WATCHER: Carter. That one bothered me more than the others.

GLADLY: It bothered all of us.

THE WATCHER: No, I mean it bothered me differently. He wasn't lying. He believed what he was saying. That's worse than lying, somehow. A liar knows he's wrong.

FURY: (quiet) Yeah. That's the one that's gonna cost him longest, I think. Not the dying. The believing.

A pause, no jokes reaching for it this time.

GLADLY: Are we doing the recap or the group therapy session?

FURY: Little of both, apparently.

Fade. Somewhere, a door that wasn't there a moment ago opens onto the next part of the story, and neither of them follows it in. That's not their job either.
