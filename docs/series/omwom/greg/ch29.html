Chapter 29 of 40

Complications

There was no time for a plan. There was only the half second in which the two Jaffa noticed motion in a corridor that should have been empty, and the half second after that, in which O'Neill's hand found his weapon a fraction slower than the moment demanded, still recovering from the shock of a route Bra'tac's memory hadn't accounted for.

Teal'c moved first, closing the distance before either patrol member could raise an alarm, and the fight that followed was fast, brutal, and almost entirely silent, four years of discipline built for exactly this, no room for anything but precision. It was over in seconds. It was not, however, over cleanly.

::oh no. Oh no, one of them got a hand on something, did he hit an alarm, was that an alarm...::

He had. A small, handheld signaling device, crushed under Teal'c's boot a half second too late, its purpose already served, a soft tone somewhere in the compound's depths already announcing that something, however small, had gone wrong in a corridor that was supposed to be quiet.

"That's not good," O'Neill said, understating it, calm the way a man gets when he's decided panicking would not help and is choosing not to indulge it. He keyed the company net once. "Compromise, compromise. Continue assault. Security isolate. Reserve stand by. Carter, timeline just got shorter."

A VOICE: For what it's worth, "radio Carter, tell her the timeline just got shorter" is exactly the kind of thing I'd say. Calm on the outside, screaming internally. Good line.

GLADLY: ...you say that like you'd know.

A VOICE: I do.

✳

They moved fast now, the careful stealth of the plan's first phase abandoned in favor of speed, Teal'c leading them through corridors he half-remembered from a life he'd spent years trying not to think about, muscle memory surfacing in ways that unsettled him more than the alarm itself had. This had been, once, a place he might have walked as its master's servant rather than its enemy. The knowledge sat uneasily alongside the urgency, two truths that didn't fit together and had to be carried regardless.

::you okay? You've gone quiet in a way I don't love.::

::I am navigating a building I once helped enforce,:: Teal'c said. ::I am finding that harder than I expected to hold alongside everything else this day requires of me.::

::okay. That's fair. I got you, though. Whatever that's worth right now.::

::It is worth more than you realize.::

They reached the holding block's outer approach to find Carter, Daniel, Bra'tac, and their assault team pinned down, a larger response force than the alarm alone should have produced converging from two directions at once.

The company plan responded before any one person could improvise a new one. The inner security team took the eastern column at its blocking position and reported contact. Support shifted one weapons team forward to hold the western passage without firing across the assault element. When the eastern team reported more Jaffa behind the first rank than it could contain, Mercer committed the reserve on the trigger Turnpike had written beside its name. It moved from the release point in under a minute, one squad reinforcing security while another replaced two wounded Marines and kept the route to the tunnel open.

Turnpike's voice came across the net, tight and level. "Objective isolated. Both routes contested. Reserve committed east. Assault still has a lane. Keep moving."

That was the difference between backup and a formation with work already assigned. Nobody had to decide, under fire, which frightened group of armed men should run toward the noise. It was Bra'tac, in the middle of it, who voiced the thought Teal'c had been circling since the alarm first sounded.

"This is not merely a response to one triggered alarm," Bra'tac called over the exchange of staff fire. "This garrison was already at heightened readiness. Someone expected us."

Cut to the gallery.

A low, unhurried voice approved the split. "Good tactics is knowing which half of the problem to solve first."

A flatter voice answered, "You did simple math slowly and made it sound like wisdom."

"Slow and correct beats fast and dead."

"You still talk too much."

FURY: There it is.

GLADLY: Wait, what? What's "it"?

FURY: Apophis wasn't just being paranoid, moving the household. He set a trap in case the paranoia paid off. Question is whether he actually knows who's inside it yet.

✳

The fight that followed cost them time they could not spare, and cost Carter a grazing hit across one arm that she shrugged off with a grimace and kept fighting through. Daniel, no soldier by training but four years of exactly this kind of crisis behind him regardless, dragged a wounded Marine behind cover while the assault element's corpsman moved to meet them instead of being summoned from somewhere outside the building. Support suppressed the western passage. Security held the east. The assault kept moving because the other elements were doing their own jobs instead of collapsing toward the loudest gunfire. In the middle of it, Teal'c felt the second control transfer arrive not as panic this time, but as something closer to a decision made jointly, without either of them needing to say it aloud first.

::let me help. For real this time. Not the flailing thing. I've been paying attention, I've watched you fight enough times now, I think I actually...::

::I know,:: Teal'c said, and meant it as the highest compliment he had available to give in the middle of a firefight. ::Take it. I will guide where I can.::

The transfer bore no resemblance to the chaos of P2X-908. Greg still moved with hesitation, but months of watching and feeling Teal'c's instincts through the connection had become something almost like competence. Not skill. Close enough to matter.

They broke through the pincer together, Greg's unfamiliar-but-improving hands and Teal'c's guidance running in parallel rather than in the desperate, disconnected chaos of the first handoff, and reached the holding block's actual entrance with the whole team still standing, breathing hard, alive.

But the door ahead of them was sealed, reinforced, nothing like the schematic Bra'tac's memory had promised, thick alloy plating that had not existed the last time anyone Bra'tac trusted had walked this corridor. The Marine breacher and Carter reached it together. One checked the frame and hinges while the other ran a scanner across the plating. The breacher marked three possible charge points, looked through the observation slit, and crossed all three out.

"We can open it," he said. "We can't guarantee the people behind it survive the opening."

"And not quickly," Carter added after a second scan. "Not with what we can place safely."

"There is a control mechanism," Bra'tac said, already moving toward a narrow observation slit set into the wall beside the door itself, the one detail his memory had gotten exactly right even as the door's construction had changed around it. "Whoever holds this position controls the release."

Somewhere beyond it, Teal'c could feel, with a certainty that had nothing to do with tactics and everything to do with four years of hope finally close enough to touch, that Drey'auc and Rya'c were waiting on the other side of a door that pure force alone was not going to open in time.
