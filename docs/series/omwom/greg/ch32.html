Aftermath

They did not run for the ship as one frightened mass. The withdrawal happened in the order Turnpike had put on the wall two days earlier.

Reconnaissance led the recovered personnel and assault element through an alternate service passage it had marked on the way in, bypassing the western corridor where support was still trading fire. Drey'auc and Rya'c moved at the center, a Marine on either side, Teal'c close enough to touch them and still unable to believe the distance was finally shrinking instead of growing. The reserve came behind the assault, intact enough to answer a new threat without stripping another element of its job. Support followed with Carter and the other wounded, litters already unfolded, the assault corpsman handing casualties into a chain that had existed before anyone was hurt. Mercer and the command cell moved ahead of the security element, still controlling the withdrawal rather than becoming its last problem.

At the release point, the reserve became the moving rear guard while the inner security teams broke contact by sequence. One displaced while the other covered. Then the second moved. Nobody abandoned a passage merely because the people they had come for were already clear of it. The last security team left the tunnel mouth only after Mercer had reports from every element and Turnpike's reconnaissance Marines had confirmed the route from release point to landing zone remained open.

The company reached the Tel'tak with the garrison regrouped and furious behind it, but never able to close its hand around the whole force at once. Reconnaissance guided the column to the ramp. Turnpike handed the route to the landing-zone security leader, sent his platoon aboard with the recovered personnel, then remained just inside the hatch to track the rest of the withdrawal. Assault loaded Drey'auc and Rya'c first. The reserve boarded next. Support passed the wounded directly to the shipboard corpsmen, followed by Mercer and the command cell. The landing-zone security team came last, collapsing its positions inward by pairs until the final Marine crossed the ramp and called clear.

Mercer stood inside the hatch counting element reports while O'Neill counted people and Turnpike checked each element off the net. All three reached the same answer.

"All elements, all recovered personnel," Mercer said.

Turnpike looked back once at the empty clearing. "Then close it."

Before Bra'tac raised the ramp, Mercer turned to him. The captain's salute was not required by rank. The Medal of Honor made it something older and more personal than rank, and the shared ground between them made it unnecessary to explain.

"You brought them out again, Gunny."

Turnpike returned the salute. "Company brought them out, sir. I just made sure everybody knew his job."

The ramp closed. Bra'tac was already at the controls. The short cloaked hop back to the gate's clearing took minutes instead of the hour a march would have cost them, Carter favoring her wounded arm while the corpsman worked, O'Neill counting heads one final time before allowing himself to relax. Drey'auc and Rya'c came through the wormhole into a world neither of them had ever seen, blinking against fluorescent light after a lifetime under a single sun, and the SGC's gate room erupted into the controlled chaos of a rescue that had actually, against real odds, succeeded.

Hammond met them personally, which he did not do for every mission, and the look on his face when he took in the two extra people standing amid his returning team told Teal'c, more clearly than any words could have, exactly how unlikely he'd believed this outcome to be.

"Debrief in an hour," Hammond said, quiet, and then, breaking his own protocol entirely, added, "Take the hour first, Teal'c. Whatever this needs to be, it can wait that long."

::that's really decent of him.::

::It is,:: Teal'c agreed. ::I did not always give this program enough credit for the decency it is capable of.::

✳

The infirmary took the worst of the physical damage in hand, Carter's arm and a dozen smaller wounds across the rest of them. Drey'auc and Rya'c went through the same medical isolation and contamination screening as anyone brought through the gate, with Janet Fraiser conducting the examinations herself and a security officer waiting outside rather than in the room. The first debrief, held on Hammond's original one-hour schedule, was deliberately narrow: casualties, enemy disposition, what Apophis had done after he fell, and anything that might matter before the next team went through a gate. The full review could wait. Intelligence interviews could wait until the two people being interviewed had eaten, slept, and understood that medical care was not conditional on cooperation.

It was in the infirmary afterward, gear finally shed, that Teal'c found himself alone with Drey'auc for the first real conversation either of them had been able to have since the fight ended.

"You look older," she said, and there was no accusation in it, only the plain observation of a woman taking honest stock of four years neither of them had gotten to spend together.

"As do you." He studied her face, still sharp, still exactly the woman who had seen the rot in Apophis's service before he'd allowed himself to. "I am sorry. For the manner of my leaving. For every year since that I could not undo it."

"You are also," Drey'auc said, something almost like amusement threading through the words, "carrying someone else in your head. Rya'c told me what happened at the door. What was said."

Teal'c did not deny it. He had promised himself, walking into this compound, that whatever else the day cost him, he would not build his family's return to him on a foundation of concealment.

"His name is Greg Veder," Teal'c said. "He arrived some months ago, in a manner I still do not fully understand the mechanics of. He is, in the truest sense I have, the reason any of us are standing in this room today."

::hi. Um. Hi, Drey'auc. This is really weird for me too, for the record.::

Drey'auc's expression, when Teal'c relayed the words, shifted into something he had not seen on her face in longer than four years, real, unguarded warmth. "Then he has my gratitude, whatever the nature of his presence. Tell him that directly, if you are able."

::she said that? For real?::

::She did.::

::...tell her thank you. Tell her I'm really glad I got to help bring her home.::

Drey'auc listened to the relayed message with the same careful attention she gave most things, and something in her posture eased further, a tension Teal'c suspected she had been carrying since long before today, perhaps since the moment she first understood what marrying a First Prime would eventually cost her.

"I have spent four years teaching myself not to hope," she said. "It was easier that way. Hope, in Apophis's household, was rarely rewarded, and often punished simply for existing. I find I do not yet know how to stop, now that hoping has actually paid off."

"Neither, I confess, do I," Teal'c admitted. "I have spent these same four years assuming this day, if it came at all, would come too late to matter. I did not plan for what comes after having been wrong about that."

"Then we will learn together," Drey'auc said, "as we should have been permitted to do from the start."

Rya'c found him separately, later that evening, in the corridor outside the guest quarters Hammond had arranged, still visibly uncertain how to hold himself around a father he'd spent years being taught to hate.

"I do not fully understand what happened today," Rya'c said. "The voice. What you said. Some of it did not sound like you at all."

"Some of it was not entirely me," Teal'c said, and told him the truth: a passenger, a boy roughly his own age, carried inside him since a stranger's gift arrived months before. Rya'c listened with his mother's care and did not flinch.

"I am being called to account for certain discrepancies related to this subject," Teal'c added, once he judged his son had had room enough to sit with the larger truth first. "I will ask if you and your mother would be allowed to attend, should you wish it."

"He said," Rya'c said finally, "that he had never made his own father proud. That was true. Actually true, not merely useful."

"It was."

"Then I am glad," Rya'c said, "whatever else this turns out to mean, that you were not alone carrying whatever burdens I imagine you carry. I would not wish that on anyone. Least of all you."

It was not the reunion Teal'c had imagined. It was uncertain, imperfect, and theirs.

✳

He found Hammond in his office not long after, the general still at his desk despite the hour, paperwork from a day that had asked more of him than most days did.

"General." Teal'c did not sit, though Hammond gestured toward the chair across from him. "Before we speak further, I have a request. Drey'auc and Rya'c have asked to be present for whatever accounting this conversation requires. I would ask your permission."

Hammond studied him a moment, then set his pen down entirely. "Of course. Bring whoever you feel you need, to say this fully." A beat, something almost gentle working into the words. "I meant what I told you before the mission, Teal'c. This is a conversation. Not an interrogation. You get to decide who belongs in the room for it, not me."

Something in that landed harder than Teal'c had expected it to. "Then I would ask one thing further. The rest of my team already carries this truth alongside me. I would have them present as well, if you are willing."

"I was rather hoping you'd ask," Hammond said, and for the first time since the request had been made, his face eased. "Forty-eight hours. My office. Bring all of them."
