Debrief

The formal after-action review took place two days after the rescue. The immediate operational debrief had already captured casualties, enemy disposition, and the company's withdrawal. This was the longer accounting: how a task-organized Marine raid company, SG-1, and a four-hundred-year-old rebel Jaffa had entered a System Lord's stronghold, recovered two prisoners, and driven Apophis from the field. Drey'auc and Rya'c sat beside Teal'c at the table, with the rest of SG-1 and Bra'tac close enough that the arrangement required no explanation.

O'Neill did most of the talking, characteristically undersold, the tactical details laid out clean and precise despite how little of the actual mission had gone according to any plan they'd walked in with. Carter filled in the technical gaps. Bra'tac, present by special dispensation, corrected the tunnel network's final layout for the official record, dry about the discrepancy between his own decades-old memory and the compound's current construction.

It was the colonel, the same one who'd argued for the diplomatic option, who finally asked the question everyone in the room had clearly been circling.

"Colonel O'Neill's report mentions a, quote, 'significant shift in combat pattern' partway through the engagement with the loyalist patrol. Twice, in fact, once on P2X-908 and again during the extraction itself. Can someone explain that for the record?"

"This is not information you are cleared for, Colonel." Hammond didn't so much as look up from the folder in front of him. "Also, I was under the impression I'd had you escorted off my base."

"Reinstated this morning, sir. Pending review." The colonel said it without a trace of embarrassment, a man long since numb to being the least popular voice in any given room. "The escort was for the meeting. Not the program."

"Then feel free to see yourself out." Hammond still hadn't looked up from the folder, and did not need to, to register the sound of a chair pushing back, footsteps crossing the room, and a door closing behind them, exactly the way a suggestion behaves when everyone in the room already knows it was actually an order.

The room went quiet. Teal'c felt every eye that mattered settle on him, and understood, with total clarity, that this was the moment, chosen for him rather than by him, that the choice he'd been circling since Bra'tac first raised it would finally have to be made.

"I can," Teal'c said. "Though the explanation will require more of your patience than a standard debrief typically allows."
