The Question Neither Asks

It came up almost by accident, the second night of downtime, Teal'c seated in his quarters with the candle unlit for once, no Kel-no-reem attempted, just the ordinary quiet of a man with nowhere he needed to be. He'd spent the better part of an hour simply sitting with it, an unfamiliar luxury, no mission to prepare for, no report to file, and had almost let himself believe the whole day might pass without either of them reaching for anything heavier than the previous afternoon's argument about a fictional sheriff's marksmanship.

::hey, so, can I ask you something? About, um. About your family?::

He felt the question arrive the way he'd learned to feel all of Greg's questions now, a small pressure behind his own thoughts before the words themselves resolved, and something in him, old and well-practiced, began closing the door before he'd even decided to.

"Another time," Teal'c said, and the words weren't unkind, only final.

::yeah. Okay. Another time.::

Neither of them said anything else for a while. The silence sat between them, not unfriendly, just occupied, the shape of a door two people both know is there and have both agreed, for now, not to open.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

GLADLY: That's not a real answer, though. "Another time" isn't an answer.

FURY: It's an answer. Just not the one you wanted.

GLADLY: He's sitting with it this time. No candle. No meditation.

FURY: Don't make him perform the progress.

✳

He read one of the few Earth novels he had found worth returning to. The words moved past without landing; his attention kept circling the question he had refused. Greg had gone quiet in the way that meant he was busy not saying something, a restraint that cost him more effort than chatter ever did.

::you can ask me back, you know. If you wanted to. I wouldn't hold the not-answering against you the way you're probably expecting me to.::

::I am aware,:: Teal'c said. ::I am choosing the moment regardless.::

::why?::

::Because a question asked carelessly deserves a careless answer, and neither of us, I think, would be well served by that tonight.::

Greg didn't have a reply ready for that one, which was, in itself, its own small answer.

It was Teal'c who almost asked, an hour later, into a silence that had gone comfortable again without either of them noticing the shift.

"Your own family," he said, careful, testing the shape of the question the way he tested most things before trusting them. "You have mentioned a mother. Rarely, and never at length. And a father, mentioned not at all."

He felt Greg go very still, a stillness with none of the ease theirs had built up over the last two days, something older and more guarded underneath it. Teal'c had felt fragments of this before, in passing, unbidden, a flinch here, a flash of something raw during an unrelated conversation there, never enough to form a full picture, only enough to know a shape was missing from it, the way a room can feel wrong before you've counted what's not in it.

::yeah. There's, uh. There's not a lot to say.::

Teal'c heard the lie as clearly as Greg must have heard his refusal an hour before. He did not press on ground he had declined to cross himself.

"Then we will leave it," Teal'c said, "for another time."

::...yeah. Thanks.::

✳

Nothing more passed between them that night. But something had shifted regardless, a mutual recognition neither one said aloud: two doors, closed for now by mutual, unspoken agreement, both of them still standing, both of them, for the first time, acknowledged rather than simply avoided. That was not nothing. Teal'c, increasingly, was coming to suspect that very little about this particular arrangement was ever going to be nothing.

✳

Cut to the gallery.

THE SKEPTIC: Seventh.

AN ENTHUSIAST: I didn't even hear it that time.

✳

Teal'c blew out the candle he'd never lit and sat a while longer in the dark that followed anyway, out of habit more than need. Somewhere behind his own eyes, a boy who talked constantly about everything else in the universe was, for once, saying nothing at all, and Teal'c found he did not mind sitting in that silence together, not tonight. There would be another time. He was beginning, cautiously, to believe that.

Before sleep came, he thought of the door he had kept shut for four years and the one Greg guarded just as carefully. Neither had opened. Both had finally been acknowledged.
