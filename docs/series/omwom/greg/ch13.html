Chapter 13 of 40

Leak

The mission itself was almost nothing, a routine perimeter check on P9X-441, a rocky, wind-scoured world with no population to speak of and no reason for SG-1 to be there beyond confirming a naquadah reading Carter's scanner had flagged from orbit. It went wrong only in the smallest possible way, an unstable ledge giving out underfoot on the climb down, a rockslide that caught Teal'c's leg and pinned him hard enough that pulling free took real time, real effort, and enough pain that his usual walls, the ones that had held against gods and torture both, found themselves briefly, uselessly occupied elsewhere.

The climb down had been unremarkable right up until it wasn't, loose scree underfoot the whole way, Carter calling the naquadah reading closer with every step, Daniel picking his way along a few yards off complaining good-naturedly about his knees. Nobody had flagged the ledge as a risk. Nobody flags the ledge, until it is one.

It was into that gap that the memory came through.

Not Greg's voice, not his usual rambling narration. Something rawer, unfiltered, arriving whole and uninvited the way sensation arrived, the way pain arrived: a kitchen, fluorescent-lit, the bulb overhead gone an ugly yellow that should have been replaced years ago. A boy at a table, younger than the one Teal'c knew, maybe twelve, holding a report card he already knew wasn't good enough before anyone else had seen it. A man's voice, flat, already disappointed before the conversation had properly started, tired and contemptuous, a man who had stopped expecting anything and was simply confirming it once more for the record. You know what your problem is? You want everybody to like you so bad you never once try to be somebody worth liking.

Teal'c, pinned under a slab of stone he could not yet shift, felt the words land as if they had been said to him.

::oh god. Oh god, no, not that one, not that one, I didn't mean for you to...::

"I am here," Teal'c said, aloud, low enough that only Carter, working the stone above him, might have caught the strangeness in his voice and not the words. "I am not going anywhere."

::I didn't want you to see that. I really didn't want you to see that one.::

The memory did not fully recede, not the way sensation usually did once its cause had passed. It sat instead, lodged somewhere behind Teal'c's own thoughts, a kitchen he had never stood in, a father's voice he had never heard, and he understood, with a clarity that had nothing gentle in it, that this was not a story Greg had chosen to tell him. It was a wound that had simply, finally, torn open under enough pressure to stop being containable.

GLADLY: ...That's a real one.

FURY: That's the difference between a bit and a scar. The bit keeps people from seeing where it hurts.

✳

Carter got the stone shifted a moment later, real time and pain reasserting themselves, Daniel and O'Neill both hauling on the same lever arm she'd wedged under it, and the rest of the extraction happened the ordinary way, hands and shoulders and O'Neill barking coordinates at the gate team. Teal'c said nothing about any of it, walked out under his own power despite what the leg clearly cost him, refused the infirmary's insistence twice before finally submitting to it a third time, mostly to end the argument rather than out of any conviction he needed it.

::you okay?::

::That is my question to ask you,:: Teal'c said, and meant it entirely.

::yeah. I mean. Now you know. That's, that's the actual thing, that's not the version I tell people, that's just, that's what actually happened, more or less, a hundred more or less like it.::

Teal'c lay with his leg elevated and considered the kitchen he had never seen until today. He would not set it aside merely because examining it hurt.

"He was wrong," Teal'c said finally.

::yeah, well. Doesn't really matter if he was wrong. He was there every day. Being wrong every day.::

::how old were you? In the memory.::

::Twelve, give or take. Report cards were a whole production in my house. Still are, I guess, if my mom still bothers to make me look at hers.::

::And your mother. In that room. Where was she.::

::Kitchen doorway, mostly. Not saying anything. She never really said anything, when he got going. I used to think that meant she agreed with him. Took me a long time to figure out it probably just meant she was scared of him too, in her own way.::

Teal'c considered a mother's silence that might have been agreement or fear, perhaps indistinguishable even to the woman standing in the doorway.

✳

Teal'c could not undo twelve years in a kitchen he had never entered. He could refuse to look away from them. Greg felt him make that choice and stay.
