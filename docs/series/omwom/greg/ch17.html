Chapter 17 of 40

Sha'reth Kanar

Two days passed after the conversation in the empty debrief room before Teal'c reached a decision. He used the second to make certain the first day's conclusion would survive a night's reflection. It did.

He found O'Neill that evening, alone in his office with paperwork he was clearly not reading. A warrior does not ask his brother for trust while withholding the one fact that explains everything else.

"Colonel O'Neill. I require a word, and I require it to remain between us until I say otherwise."

O'Neill looked up, read something in his face that made the paperwork a lower priority than it had been a second earlier, and set it aside without comment. "Yeah. Okay. Door's shut. Go."

"Two nights ago, in the debrief room, I told you something had changed. That was not the whole truth, only enough of it to ask for your trust." Teal'c remained standing and gave O'Neill the rest without softening it.

"A passenger arrived within me some months ago, by a means I still do not fully understand. He has shared this body since before P4X-212.

"On the first mission after his arrival, he saw a mark on a support post I would have walked past without a second look. He knew it from a stranger's television program, and I did not trust it. A children's program is no source of intelligence. I was wrong to doubt him as long as I did. We searched beneath that post on his insistence and found a cache of Goa'uld-forged weapons, hidden by villagers already planning to fight, not merely endure, the tribute collectors due the next day. Without him, we would have made camp that night believing ourselves among simple farmers. That find was his. I will not claim otherwise.

"The same program cost us nearly as much as it gave. He was certain, once, of a plan drawn from an episode he remembered, certain enough that I let his certainty stand in for knowledge I had not verified myself. Major Carter was nearly killed because of that. I want to be plain with you, O'Neill. He offered a guess dressed as fact. I let it stand as one. That is my failure as much as his, and I have not allowed myself to forget it since.

"On P2X-908, we were ambushed by a First Prime's guard I had once trained beside, six against our four, and every technique my hands knew, they had already countered. I had never surrendered control of this body to anyone, not even to Junior in all the years he has ridden with me. I surrendered it that day. He held that body through the worst of it, untrained, graceless, nothing a combat instructor would call skill, and he bought this team the seconds it needed to reach the gate. I would not be standing in this office tonight without him. That is not something I say lightly, and I will not be saying it twice.

"In ways I am still working through myself, that day changed more about me than I had fully accounted for, until I had cause, two nights past, to say as much aloud without yet telling you why."

O'Neill listened with both hands flat on the desk and the paperwork forgotten beneath them. He did not look away or reach for a joke.

"The post," he said, once Teal'c had reached the end of it. "I remember standing there thinking, huh, look at that, Teal'c's getting sentimental about fence posts. Real growth for the guy." Dry, not quite a laugh. "Four years of you never once being wrong bought a total stranger's rerun the same trust I'd have given your own gut, and I signed off on it without blinking twice. Good outcome. I'm gonna go ahead and not think too hard about how many other calls that's true of and I just never noticed."

He was quiet longer before the next part, and when he spoke again his voice had dropped, gone lower and more careful than a moment before. "And the jar. That's the one that's gonna sit with me a while." A beat, the humor gone out of him entirely for it. "Carter almost died over a rerun. That's a joke, somewhere, and it is not one I find funny." He held Teal'c's eyes through this part too. "You telling me that straight, that lands like a gut punch, and I'm not gonna sit here and pretend it didn't. But you also just told me you own half that call, and I know you well enough to know you're not saying it to make yourself feel better about it. You're saying it because it's true. So I'm not gonna let you carry it by yourself either." Something worked in his jaw, once, and eased. "That's not forgiveness, Teal'c. I don't have the standing to hand that out, that's hers if she wants to give it. It's not blame either. Not from me. Not tonight. That's just what family does."

"And P2X-908." O'Neill exhaled, short, through his nose, something that wasn't quite a laugh. "I watched you go down twice and get back up fighting like you'd forgotten everything I've ever seen you do in a fight, and figured, hey, guy's having a rough day, it happens." A beat, drier. "Turns out I was more right than I knew, and for entirely the wrong reason. Never once thought," he started, and then stopped himself, visibly, on the edge of a word he hadn't used once all night. "Never once thought there was somebody else in the driver's seat. A kid who'd never held a weapon in his life, apparently out-improvising half the recruits I've trained, and I owe him this whole team making it back through that gate."

"A kid," O'Neill said finally, quieter now, nothing left in him to work through, all of it already spent on the specifics. "A kid from another universe, dead in his own, been riding shotgun in your head this whole time. And after everything you just told me, 'half the wins on this team's board' sounds like an undersell."

"That is an accurate accounting, yes."

"And you're telling me now because."

"Because you asked me, two nights ago, to extend you the same trust I have asked of you across four years, and I discovered I had no interest in offering you half of it." Teal'c held his gaze. "You are not merely the officer I follow into a gate address, O'Neill. In the tongue of my people, there is a word for what you have become to me. Sha'reth kanar. I will not translate it for you. Some words are not improved by translation. I will tell you only that I have used it of very few men, and that I did not use it lightly tonight."

O'Neill looked at the word as though it were an unread number on Carter's scanner. His mouth opened. He stopped the question himself.

"Buddy." O'Neill's voice, when it came, had lost every trace of the careful lightness he usually wrapped around things that actually rattled him. "I don't know what that word means, and I'm not gonna pretend I do." A beat, the wanting still sitting plainly behind his eyes, costing him something to leave it there unanswered. "But I know how you said it, and that's enough for me tonight."

::I like him.::

::As do I,:: Teal'c said. ::More than I have generally permitted myself to say.::

He did not go to his quarters. The decision was made; the late hour offered no reason to leave it half-finished.

He found Daniel and Carter exactly where he expected to, in the lab, heads bent together over some project neither of them had needed to still be working on at this hour, the restlessness of two people who'd rather stay occupied than go home to an empty quiet.

"I require a few minutes of your time. Both of you. Now, if you are willing, not tomorrow."

Carter looked up first, read something in his face that made her set her work down without being asked. "That sounds serious."

"It is. It has also waited longer than it should have, which is the only reason I am asking it of you tonight rather than a better hour that would only mean waiting longer still."

Daniel closed his own folder, already moving to clear a space at the table. "Okay. You have our full attention."

Teal'c told them. Not the abbreviated account, and not, this time, without interruption.

He stayed on his feet, the same as he had an hour ago in O'Neill's office. Some habits from a life long before this one didn't need reconsidering just because the room, and the audience, had changed.

O'Neill appeared in the lab's open doorway and stopped. Four years made him recognizable without Teal'c turning to look. He did not enter. By the time Teal'c reached the second incident, the doorway was empty again.

"On P4X-212, the mission with the tribute collectors, he saw the mark on the repaired post. The one I nearly walked past without a second look."

Carter's head came up before he'd finished. "That's why you asked me to scan the base of it. Out of nowhere, no explanation. I remember thinking that was strange, you'd never asked me for something like that with nothing behind it before."

"I did not yet trust what I had been told enough to explain it. I trusted it enough to ask you to look anyway."

"That cache is the reason we didn't spend that night thinking we were camped among simple farmers," Carter said, working it through out loud. "I always wondered why you suddenly cared about a repaired fence post."

"You were not the only one wondering, that night."

Daniel had gone quiet, watching him instead of the table now.

"There was a second incident, the same mission, the next day. He was certain of a plan drawn from an episode of the same program. Certain enough that I let his certainty stand in for verification I should have done myself."

Carter went still, differently than before. "The collectors who didn't back off. I thought that was bad intel from the desk."

"It was not the desk's failure. It was a boy's memory of a television show, and my own choice to trust it as though it were more than that." Teal'c held her gaze and did not look away from it. "You were nearly killed because of that choice. I am not telling you this to be forgiven for it. I am telling you because you are owed the truth of what nearly cost you your life, and whose failure put you in front of it. His. And mine, for letting it stand unquestioned."

Carter was quiet a moment. "Okay," she said finally, her voice gone thin but not shaking. "Thank you for telling me that plainly instead of rounding the edges off it."

"I do not know how to tell you anything any other way."

The ghost of a smile crossed her face and was gone as quickly as it arrived.

"And P2X-908," Daniel said, before Teal'c could continue, the pieces already assembling themselves behind his eyes. "You went sideways into a tree. I remember thinking that was the strangest thing I'd ever seen you do, and there wasn't time to ask about it before we were all just trying to survive the next ten seconds."

"That was him. Not a technique, and not an accident in the sense you mean it. I surrendered control of this body to him entirely, for the worst of that engagement, the first time in my life I had ever done so with anyone."

Carter's eyebrows went up. "You let go. Completely."

"Va'lar's men had already read every motion my own hands knew how to make. An opponent who fights like nothing anyone has trained against cannot be countered by a plan built to counter me. He held that body through the worst of it, untrained, graceless, nothing a combat instructor would call skill, and he bought this team the seconds it needed to reach the gate."

"That's..." Daniel searched for the word. "That's not nothing, Teal'c. That's most of the engagement, actually, now that I'm thinking through the timeline."

"I would not be standing in this room tonight without him. That is not something I say lightly, and I do not intend to say it twice."

The questions didn't stop there. Daniel leaned forward, both elbows on the table, already reaching for the next one. "The mechanism, when he arrived. Do you have any sense of it at all? Because if this is some kind of crossover we haven't catalogued yet, that matters for a lot more than just you."

"I do not. Whatever brought him here bore no resemblance to any technology I have encountered, Goa'uld, Asgard, or otherwise. He does not know either. He has considered the question since his arrival and reached no answer he trusts."

"Okay." Daniel sat back, already turning the problem over the way he turned over an unfamiliar inscription, cataloguing rather than solving. "That's its own kind of answer, I guess."

Carter's earlier questions had concerned what happened, when, and to whom. Her voice changed now, taking on the care it always did when Jolinar entered the room. "Has it ever felt like a threat? Not to him. To you. Your own sense of where you end."

"No." He did not have to search for the answer. "There were moments early on, before either of us understood the shape of the arrangement, when the boundary felt less certain than I would have liked. That has not been true for a very long time."

"Good." Her shoulders dropped. "I know a little about what it's like, carrying someone else. It doesn't go away just because you'd rather it did."

"I am aware," Teal'c said, gently, "of exactly why you asked first."

She held his gaze a moment, then nodded, once, and let the rest of her questions come easier after that.

"For what it's worth," Carter said, when the questions finally ran their course, "I'd like to meet him properly sometime. If that's something that's even possible."

::tell her I'd like that. I'd genuinely like that.::

"He says he would like that as well," Teal'c relayed.

One thing remained.

"Sha'reth kanar," Teal'c said. "The same word I gave O'Neill an hour ago. I am not going to translate it for either of you, any more than I did for him, and I am not going to dress that refusal up as anything more than what it actually is: mine to keep, for now. What I will tell you plainly, because I do not want either of you spending even one night wondering about it, is that the word applies to you too. Both of you. Exactly as much as it applies to him."

Carter's careful steadiness finally deserted her. "Teal'c..."

"You do not need to answer it tonight," Teal'c said, before she had to find the rest of that sentence. "I did not tell you to require a response. I told you because it was true, and because I had already decided nothing true was going unsaid in this room tonight."

She nodded, once, and did not trust herself with more than that yet.

Daniel looked as though Teal'c had placed an untranslated tablet in his hands. "You're not going to translate that for me either, are you."

"I am not."

"Can I ask why?"

"You may ask," Teal'c said, "and I may, in time, answer. Not tonight."

Disappointment and delight argued across Daniel's face. "I'm going to look into it. Goa'uld linguistic roots, warrior-culture honorifics, whatever cross-reference the SGC's databases actually have. You know that, right? I'm not just going to let this sit."

"I did not expect you to," Teal'c said. He was already curious what Daniel would find.

"Honestly," Daniel said, quieter now, "I think we're stronger for this. Going forward. Whatever comes next, we're not carrying it around half-informed anymore."

"I believe so as well," Teal'c said. "It is the reason I did not wait."

✳

Daniel and Carter returned to their work. Teal'c reached the corridor's first junction before admitting he had no intention of going to his quarters while the night remained unfinished.

He circled back roughly an hour later, telling himself it was only to confirm Carter had actually gone home rather than moved her own exhaustion into some other room, and found Daniel's office door still open, the light still on, O'Neill already leaning in the doorway with his arms crossed, watching the scene inside with an expression caught somewhere between fondness and real concern.

Daniel had not, as far as Teal'c could tell, moved from his desk since the lab. Four books lay open around him in an order that answered to no filing system Teal'c could identify. Two monitors ran queries against databases whose names meant nothing to him. A legal pad sat forgotten under one elbow, its handwriting degrading from careful to nearly illegible somewhere around the third page.

"He's cross-referencing dead Jaffa dialects," O'Neill said, without turning around, voice pitched low enough not to break Daniel's concentration. "Been at it since the two of you split up. Walked past twenty minutes ago, figured he'd wind down eventually. Walked past again just now. He has not wound down."

"Sha'reth," Daniel muttered, mostly to himself, not looking up, one finger tracing a line of text across the nearest monitor. "The root's older than standard Goa'uld administrative vocabulary, it doesn't scan the same way at all, and kanar, kanar shows up in at least two Jaffa oral tradition fragments I already know of. If I can just place the compound construction..."

"Daniel Jackson."

That got him to look up, finally, blinking like a man surfacing from somewhere much deeper than a desk. "Teal'c. Hey. This is, this is actually incredible, do you have any idea how rare it is to get a live speaker of a term this old? Most of what we have on Jaffa linguistics is secondhand at best, reconstructed off fragments, and you just handed me the genuine article."

"I did not hand you a translation," Teal'c said. "I would ask you not to mistake the two."

"I know, I know, you already said not tonight, I heard you the first time." Daniel didn't look remotely deterred by the correction. "I'm not asking you to give it to me. This is just what I do, Teal'c. You can't hand a linguist an unsolved word and expect him to simply sit with that and not touch it."

Teal'c watched him work and let go of a concern he had not admitted carrying. "I did not expect you to," he said. "I would have been more concerned had you managed it."

O'Neill, still in the doorway, made a sound that was almost a laugh. "Yeah, buddy, he's not sleeping tonight. Neither's Carter, probably, wherever she actually is right now. This team doesn't really do 'process it tomorrow.'"

"No," Teal'c agreed, looking at the lit office and the two men still in it. "It does not."

Eventually, because even Daniel Jackson's endurance had a floor somewhere, they left him to it, the light still burning behind them as they went. In the corridor, O'Neill fell into step beside him rather than peeling off toward his own quarters.

"Get some sleep, buddy," O'Neill said anyway, like there was any real chance of that for either of them tonight, and clapped him once on the shoulder before finally splitting off down the corridor.

Teal'c did not go to his quarters. Wherever she actually is, O'Neill had said. He found Carter back in the lab, alone in the glow of one monitor, an old case file open but unread.

She looked up when he came in and did not ask how he'd known where to look. "Couldn't sleep either, huh."

"No," Teal'c said. "Nor did I intend to try yet. I did not want to leave tonight with you sitting alone in the dark, wondering."

"Wondering what?"

"Whatever it is you are wondering. I did not presume to know which part of it. Only that some part of it was likely still unfinished, and unfinished was not a state I intended to leave any of you in before this night was through."

Her composure slipped. She gestured at the chair across from her.

He took it.

They sat without speaking. For a while, that was enough.
