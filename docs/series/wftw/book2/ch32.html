Chapter 32

Steve came by to review security logistics three weeks before the sedan, routine given his continued informal involvement in our protection, and partway through I got genuinely surprised by a dry wit I hadn't fully clocked in him before.

"Your perimeter's solid," he said, reviewing Natasha's notes at the kitchen table. "Better than most professional installations I've reviewed, honestly. Whoever set this up knew what they were doing."

"That was Natasha, mostly."

"Figured. Nobody adds redundant sightlines to a garden shed by accident." Something dry crossed his face. "Once reviewed a supposedly secure facility with a full blind spot behind the vending machine. Three years, nobody caught it. Haven't fully trusted vending machines since."

Cent, listening from the counter, laughed despite herself. "That's a specific trauma."

"I carry several. Most much more dramatic. That one's just the dinner-party version." He set the folder down, something more serious settling in, though the dry undercurrent stayed close. "Want to ask you something, since I'm here. Been turning it over since this whole thing started."

"Go ahead," I said.

"Fury's operation with the analyst. The interrogation, the deal for cooperation. I signed off on it, technically. Want you to know I didn't do that lightly." His jaw was tight. "Spent a long time drawing lines about what I'll authorize, even against people who've done real harm. Coercion sits uncomfortably close to some of those lines, however clean the process was legally."

"You're allowed to feel conflicted about that."

"I know. Doesn't make it smaller." He looked at his own hands a second. "Fought a war where the other side did far worse than anything we've done here, and I still think about where the line sits, every time I have to help draw it again. I'd rather stay uncomfortable about it than get too comfortable. The day that stops bothering me is the day I've got a real problem."

"That's an admirable way to hold it."

"Exhausting, mostly, if I'm honest. Admirable's the version that looks good from outside." Wry again. "Peggy used to tell me I'd overthink my way into a stress ulcer someday. Think about that more than I probably should, this many decades later."

"You still miss her."

"Every day. Different shape now. But yeah." Plain fact, no self-pity. "Grief's a lot like your Aerie, actually, from what you've described. You don't get rid of it. Just find somewhere to put it down for a while, and go visit when you need to."

I turned that over. "That's a good way to think about it."

"Learned it from a therapist. Not yours, a different one. I've had my own list to work through." A small real smile. "Anyway. Perimeter's solid. Vending machine's not involved. You're about as secure as anyone in your position can reasonably be."

"Thank you, Steve. For all of it."

"Anytime." He paused at the door, something more genuine in his face than the professional visit had carried. "For what it's worth, I like coming here. Not just the work. Feels like an actual home, in a life that doesn't always leave room for those. Don't say that to many people."

He left before I could answer that properly.
