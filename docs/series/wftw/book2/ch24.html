Chapter 24

I spent a Saturday morning sketching business logistics on a legal pad, ten days before the sedan — an idea that had been quietly forming since that first call with Cory finally taking real shape.

"What if this actually became something real," I said to Willow, on our regular Saturday call, turning my notebook so the camera caught it. "Not just research. An actual operation. Delivering things to orbit, professionally."

Willow's eyes went wide. "Cent, that's — that's huge. Reshaping-an-entire-industry huge."

"I know. Feels enormous just writing it down. But the more I think about it, the more it feels like exactly what this ability might've always been for. Not just family emergencies. Something mine. Something built."

"What would you actually need?"

"Cory's suit, once we've tested it properly. Some way to handle bigger payloads without crushing myself. A lot of very careful orbital mechanics I'm still working through." I flipped to another page, rough platform sketches. "Keep thinking about staging areas. Building up momentum gradually instead of trying one impossible jump."

"This is brilliant, Cent." Her excitement was infectious. "Have you told your parents the full scope of this?"

"Bits and pieces. Don't think I've said the whole 'reshape the aerospace industry' part out loud, even to myself, until just now."

"How do you think they'll take it?"

I thought about it. "Dad's gonna worry. A lot, probably, at first. But underneath it I think he'll be proud. He's spent so many years just surviving with this ability, using it defensively. Watching me build something with it — that might mean more than either of us fully knows yet."

"And your mom?"

"Mom's gonna want every safety question answered before she lets herself get excited. Once she's satisfied, though, I think she'll be thrilled."

I told my parents the fuller scope that evening.

"You want to start an actual business," Dad said, slow, working through it. "Delivering payloads to orbit. Using your ability, professionally, at scale."

"Eventually. Not tomorrow. Lot more research and testing before that's viable. But yeah. That's genuinely what I'm thinking."

He was quiet a long moment, something complicated working through his face — worry, clearly, but underneath it something that looked a lot like pride.

"That's remarkable, Cent," he said, finally. "I spent so many years just surviving with this ability. Protecting us with it. Watching you think this far past that — I don't have the words for how proud that makes me."

"You're not gonna tell me it's too dangerous?"

"I'm sure I'll have plenty of concerns once we get into the details." He managed a real smile despite the worry underneath. "But no. I'm not telling you not to dream this big. You've earned the right to chase something this remarkable."

Mom nodded beside him, though her face already had the focused look of someone cataloguing every safety question she meant to work through.

"We'll want real detail," she said. "Real protocols, properly considered. But before any of that — I think this is remarkable, Cent. Proud of you for even imagining it."

✳

Something warm settled in my chest — the whole thing finally given room to exist, not just a private daydream anymore.

"I'm gonna need a name," I said, struck by the practical reality of it. "For the business. If this is actually happening."

"Any ideas?" Dad asked.

I thought about it. "Something about reaching outward. Beyond what anyone expected." A beat. "Apex, maybe. Apex Orbital."

"I like that," Mom said. "Sounds exactly like the kind of thing our daughter would build."

I stayed up with it a long while after they'd gone to bed.
