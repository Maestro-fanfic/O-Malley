Chapter 25

We held an actual family dinner the following weekend, one week before the sedan — Joe home again, Willow and Xander jumping in for the evening, Faith arriving with an actual loaf of her own bread, proud and a little nervous about it.

"This is wonderful," Faith said, setting it down among the rest of the spread, watching for reactions.

"It's incredible, Faith," I told her, meaning it. "You should be proud."

"I am, actually. Feels good, having something to bring, instead of showing up empty-handed."

We settled around the table, bigger than our usual three.

"Tell them about Apex Orbital," Dad said, proud in the prompt.

I laid it out. Watched the excitement build around the table.

"That's remarkable, Cent," Xander said. "I've spent four years just finding empty worlds for the wonder of it. You're talking about reshaping an industry."

"Had a good teacher. Watching you build a whole life around patient exploration taught me something about what's possible if you just follow the curiosity."

"That's moving, Cent," Willow said.

Joe squeezed my hand under the table. "Told you it's brilliant. Don't think she fully believes me yet."

"I believe you," I said, leaning into him. "Still getting used to the size of it."

Conversation ran easy — Faith's cooking, Willow and Xander's ongoing research, Joe's coursework. Sitting there, I was struck by how much this family had grown this year.

"I want to say something," Dad said, once the meal settled. "Been thinking about it a lot, watching all of you."

The table quieted.

"Twenty years ago I built my whole life around isolation. Kept people at a distance so they wouldn't get hurt because of me." He looked around the table. "Watching this — all of you, choosing to show up for each other — this is what I always secretly hoped my life could become, even when I didn't believe it was possible."

"You built something remarkable, Davy," Willow said, quiet.

"We built it together," Mom said, reaching for his hand. "None of us built any of it alone."

Looking around at every face — my parents, Joe, Willow, Xander, Faith — something certain settled in my chest.

"I love all of you," I said. "However Apex Orbital turns out, I want you to know how much this, right here, means."

"We love you too," Faith said, and the table murmured agreement.

Dinner ran long, nobody in a hurry. By the time everyone said their goodbyes — Joe's kiss at the gate, Willow and Xander's hugs, Faith's proud look at her empty bread basket — I stood on the porch with my parents, watching the dark settle in.

"Good week," Dad said.

"Good week," Mom agreed, leaning into him.

"The best," I said, meaning it.
