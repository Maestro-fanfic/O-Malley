Chapter 22

Bruce mentioned a colleague, two and a half weeks before the sedan — an independent researcher named Cory Matoska who'd spent years on lightweight pressure-suit tech that never found commercial backing.

"His work's remarkable," Bruce said, on one of our now-regular calls. "Well ahead of anything commercially available. Think you two would have a lot to talk about."

"What kind of pressure suit?"

"Mechanical counter-pressure, not gas-pressurized. Far more practical for real mobility, in theory, though the design's got real problems nobody's solved. Mainly, getting into the thing at all, given how form-fitting it has to be."

Something old stirred at the edge of my attention. "I'd love to talk to him. Could you connect us?"

"I'll send an introduction. Fair warning — bit of a hermit, professionally. Brilliant, but he's had a rough time getting proper backing. I think he'd appreciate real interest."

Bruce made the introduction that week — a message first, since Cory was on his own side of things entirely, then an actual conversation over the relay once the two of us had traded enough back-and-forth to want to just talk instead of type. The relay couldn't carry video, not for something this casual, so it was voice only and whatever he could describe well enough for me to sketch out myself after. Talking to Cory Matoska for the first time, I liked him immediately — focused, a little awkward, the kind of enthusiasm that comes from years of being quietly brilliant with nothing to show for it.

"Bruce says you're interested in pressure suit mechanics," he said, no real preamble.

"I am. I've got some unusual capabilities that might be relevant to some of your design problems, actually."

He raised an eyebrow, or so I imagined — all I actually had was his voice. "Unusual how?"

I explained the broad strokes, the parts Bruce had already cleared me to share. Heard him go quiet the good kind of quiet, the kind that means someone's actually turning something over instead of just waiting for their turn to talk.

"That's — okay, that's a lot to sit with," he said finally, half a laugh in it. "I don't even know what the useful question is yet. Just know there's probably one in there somewhere."

"Same, honestly. Wanted to talk to somebody who actually understands suit design before I started guessing at what mattered."

"I'd love to show you what I'm stuck on. Mainly getting into the thing at all, given how form-fitting it has to be. See what you make of it, now that I've got a better sense of what I'm actually working with."

"I'd love that."

We talked over an hour, working through the details of his design, and I found myself genuinely fascinated by problems I'd never had reason to think about before.

I mentioned it to Dad that evening.

"You want to test wearing an experimental spacesuit," Dad said, carefully neutral — working hard not to veto it on the spot.

"Eventually. Not tomorrow. But the concept's fascinating, Dad. If it worked, if I could actually survive vacuum properly protected, that opens up things I hadn't let myself think about."

"That's a lot more ambitious than anything we've discussed."

"I know. Not asking permission to try it tomorrow. Just telling you it's something I want to explore, eventually, with real safety in place."

He thought about it. "Appreciate you telling me directly instead of pursuing it quietly. That matters. We'll talk properly once you've got more worked out."

"Deal."

I called Cory again a few days later, and over the following weeks found real pleasure in a purely intellectual collaboration — no urgency, no crisis, just curiosity about what his engineering and my ability could build together.

"I think we could build something groundbreaking," Cory told me, near the end of one call. "Don't say that lightly. Watched a lot of promising research go nowhere for lack of backing."

"I'd like that." I turned it over, excited. "Sounds like exactly the kind of project worth chasing, once things settle a bit."

"Whenever you're ready. I'm not going anywhere."

I hung up with the whole possibility warm in my chest, already imagining the work ahead.
