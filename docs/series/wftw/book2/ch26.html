Chapter 26

Xander found Willow in the Magic Box's back room five days before the sedan, both of them lingering after everyone else had gone home, still riding the high of the Rice family's dinner table.

"Okay, I need to say something, and it's gonna sound self-pitying for exactly four seconds before it becomes a real point, so bear with me," Xander said, dropping into the chair across from her. "Cent's building an actual space company. With her jump ability. I've spent four years finding sloths."

"You found a lot more than sloths. You built the entire reason any of us have friends on three different Earths."

"I know, I know, getting to the real point, that was just the warm-up act." He waved a hand. "My actual point is I watched a seventeen-year-old lay out orbital mechanics tonight like it was a book report, and some petty part of my brain went 'well what have YOU done with unlimited access to infinite worlds, Harris,' and the honest answer is a staggering personal collection of nature documentaries nobody asked for."

"That's not nothing, though. You built the bridge everyone else is now walking across. Cent's business doesn't happen without four years of you proving jump-travel could be gentle and curious instead of just tactical."

"Okay, when you put it that way it sounds almost impressive."

"It is impressive. You're just bad at applying the word to yourself." Willow bumped his shoulder. "You do this. Someone does something big and shiny and you immediately demote your own stuff to 'sloth guy.' I've watched you do it for years."

"'Sloth guy' is a title I have earned and will defend."

"It's also not the whole of what you are, and you know it." She softened. "You built the wonder part. Cent's building the next chapter of it. Those aren't competing. That's just sequels."

"Sequels are usually worse than the original, statistically."

"Empire Strikes Back."

"...Okay, fair. Withdrawing the argument." He grinned, some of the mock self-pity draining out. "I do love this life, though. All of it. Even the part where I apparently still do this exact bit every time someone reminds me multiverses are a lot."

"I do too." Willow went quiet a beat. "Does it ever actually scare you, though? Underneath the jokes? How big it's gotten. More people to love means more people who could get hurt."

Xander dropped the deflection. "Yeah. Genuinely, yeah. But I keep thinking about what Davy said tonight — that connection's not about making the risk go away, it's about not carrying it solo. That's the actual trade. More love, more exposure, but a lot more hands helping hold it up when it gets heavy."

"I like that framing."

"Learned it from watching a guy nearly get himself killed twice this year specifically by finally letting people in instead of running. Strong argument for the method, honestly, near-death parts included."

They sat a while before Xander leaned over and kissed her, and Willow melted into it the way she still did after years of exactly this.

"I love you," he said.

"I love you too," she said, and they were still tangled together when Buffy let herself in from patrol, crossbow slung over one shoulder, hair doing something feral from the wind.

"Oh good, you're doing the thing," Buffy said, dropping into the third chair. "Please tell me it's the fun kissing thing and not the sad philosophical-crisis thing. Long night. Vamp dust is in my everything."

"Bit of both," Willow admitted.

"Ugh, the combo platter. My favorite." Buffy stole the last of the tea from Willow's abandoned mug. "What's the crisis about this time? Don't tell me. Xander's having Feelings about Cent's rocket company."

"How did you—"

"Because you've had this face since the Convergence, where somebody younger than you does something impressive and you go into a whole spiral about sloths." Buffy grinned, fond. "For the record, you're allowed to just be proud of a teenager without turning it into a referendum on your own life choices. I do it constantly. It's very freeing."

"You do not do that constantly."

"I absolutely do. I just do it while also staking things, so it looks more productive." She stretched, wincing at something that had clearly gotten her good earlier. "Honestly, though? I think about it too. How big this whole thing's gotten. Used to be just us, some vamps, a library. Now it's this. Multiple Earths. Actual gods. A teenager running a space company." She shook her head. "Younger me would've had zero chill about any of this. Current me's just tired and grateful, which I guess is its own kind of growth."

"That's weirdly profound, for someone still picking dust out of her hair."

"I contain multitudes," Buffy said, deadpan, and the three of them dissolved into the kind of laughter that only comes from people who've earned the right to sit in a dusty back room at midnight and just be glad everyone they love is still breathing.
