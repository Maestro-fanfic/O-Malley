Chapter 28

I turned forty-three the week before the sedan, and spent most of the actual birthday quietly taking stock.

Not a crisis. Just the useful kind of reflection a milestone sometimes earns, if you let it. Davy and Cent had planned a small dinner, and I spent the morning before it alone in my office, between clients, turning my own life over the way I usually reserved for other people's.

I'd built something real here, I decided. Not just a marriage, not just a daughter, though both mattered more than almost anything. A career too — mine, built slowly over two decades from a young therapist still finding her voice into someone whose clients trusted her because she'd learned the hard way what real crisis costs and what real recovery actually requires.

I thought about my own mother — widowed young, raising me mostly alone, building a full life anyway instead of just enduring the loss. Took me until well into adulthood to appreciate how remarkable that actually was.

I thought about the woman I'd been at twenty-three, meeting Davy for the first time, unprepared for how thoroughly it would reshape my life. I hadn't chosen an extraordinary life the way he'd been forced into his by the ability. I'd chosen him, specifically, and everything else followed from that.

Did I regret it? I turned the question over the way I'd have turned it over for a client.

No. Not once. Not even the hardest years — the early uncertainty, the fear that never fully left, even now.

I called my own mother that afternoon.

"Forty-three," Mom said, warm. "How's it feel?"

"Good. Keep thinking about you, actually. About everything you built after Dad died, without ever making me feel like a burden alongside the grief."

"You were never a burden, Millicent. You were the reason I kept building anything at all."

"I think I understand that properly now, in a way I don't think I did a decade ago." I thought about my own life. "I think I've spent my whole career trying to give people what you gave me, without quite realizing that's what I was doing."

"That's a beautiful thing to realize, dear." Real warmth in her voice. "I'm proud of you. Not just the marriage, the family. The woman you've become, independent of any of that. You'd have built something remarkable regardless of who you married. Davy's lucky you chose him."

Davy found me on the porch that evening, before the dinner.

"Good birthday so far?"

"Really good. Spent most of it thinking about my own life, actually. Properly. Not just in relation to yours or Cent's." I tried to explain it. "I've spent so many years being the steady one, the professional one — I don't think I've given myself enough credit for the life I built alongside all that."

"You should give yourself more credit. Genuinely. Don't think I've said that clearly enough, across twenty years." He took my hand. "You didn't just support my life. You built your own, right alongside it."

"I appreciate you saying that." I leaned into him.
