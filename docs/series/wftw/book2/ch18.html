Chapter 18

Clara found me in the hangar on an ordinary evening, doing minor maintenance on the Maule mostly to keep my hands busy while my head circled half-formed worry about Home's rebuilding.

"You've been quiet all week," she said, settling onto the workbench, shoulder against mine. "Good quiet or worried quiet?"

"Bit of both." I set down the wrench. "Thinking about Home a lot. About Mom's history there. How much weight she still carries, even now, even with things better."

"Heavy thing to carry alongside her." Her hand found mine. "You don't have to carry it alone, though."

"I know. Still learning to believe it, some days." I managed a tired smile. "You're good at this. Reminding me."

"Had practice. You're not exactly subtle when something's bothering you." She bumped my shoulder. "For the record, I like being the person you let in on the heavy stuff."

I looked at her. "I love you. Don't think I say that enough outside the easy moments."

"I love you too." She leaned in, kissed me. "You say it plenty. I just like hearing it during the harder ones too."

We sat a while, her head finding my shoulder.

"Can I ask you something," she said, eventually. "About your mom. You think she'll ever fully stop carrying that weight? Or is it just always there?"

"Always there, I think. But I don't think that's a bad thing. It's proof of how much she survived. Doesn't mean she can't be happy alongside it."

"That's a mature way to think about it."

"Learned it watching her. She's gotten good this year at holding both at once." I found her hand again. "That's what I want for us too. Whatever we end up carrying, I want us still happy alongside it."

"I want that too," Clara said, and kissed me again, longer.

We finished the Maule's maintenance, her steady presence beside me doing more good than any amount of solitary worry could've managed.

"Thank you," I told her, walking back toward the house, hand in hand. "For always showing up for the harder stuff."

"You'd do the same. You already have." She squeezed my hand. "That's the whole point, isn't it. Building something where showing up isn't even a question."

"Yeah," I said. "I think that's exactly right."
