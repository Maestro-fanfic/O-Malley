Chapter 5

I found a new place by accident, six weeks before HYDRA — jumped somewhere I'd never aimed for, one of the rare surprises this ability still throws after twenty-odd years.

I'd meant to jump to the Aerie, wanted the quiet after a long week, and landed instead somewhere I didn't recognize. A coastline. Cliffs dropping straight into water clear enough to see real depth from the edge. Seabirds wheeling overhead in numbers that meant a colony nesting close by.

I stood there a minute, working out what happened. Some quirk in the jump. Maybe my own head pulling me somewhere new instead of where I'd meant to go. It happens, rarely, and I'd learned a long time ago to treat it as a gift instead of a mistake.

I spent the rest of the afternoon just exploring. Seabirds nesting on the cliff face, more of them than I'd ever seen in one place, the whole afternoon full of their noise. Found a narrow path down to a cove, sheltered from the wind, water calm.

I called Millie that evening.

"You found somewhere new? After twenty years?" Real surprise in her voice.

"By accident. Some kind of subconscious redirect. Haven't had one of those in years." I described it — the coastline, the birds, the cove. "Want to take you and Cent out there this weekend, if you're both free."

"We're free."

We went Saturday, all three of us. Watching Cent's face at the cliff's edge, I remembered why I fell for this ability in the first place, decades back, before any of the danger piled on top of it.

"This is incredible," Cent said. "How'd you even find this?"

"Pure accident. Sometimes the best ones are." I settled beside her. "Used to find places like this all the time, when I was younger. Before responsibility crowded it out."

"I want more days like this," Cent said. "Not instead of everything else. Alongside it."

"I think that's right," Millie said, joining us at the edge.

We spent the whole weekend on it. Catalogued seabird species Willow would want notes on. Found a tide pool ecosystem in the cove that held Cent's attention most of an afternoon.

"I want to name it," Cent said, our last evening, the sunset doing something impossible to the coastline. "Feels wrong calling it 'the accidental cliff place.'"

"What would you call it?"

She thought about it, watching the birds settle for the night. "Something about the accident. About how the best things happen when you're not even trying."

"Serendipity Cliffs," I said, half joking.

"That's actually perfect." She grinned. "I like that a lot."

We jumped home that evening.

"Same time next month?" Cent asked — a phrase that had, over the past year, turned into something like a family motto. "Serendipity Cliffs, I mean. Not just Home."

"Same time next month," I said.
