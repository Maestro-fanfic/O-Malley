Chapter 34

Joe found me in the workshop on his own, without Cent, three weeks before the sedan, and something in how carefully he knocked told me this wasn't casual.

"Got a minute?" he asked. "Cent's inside with your wife. Wanted to catch you alone, if that's okay."

"Sure." I set down what I was working on.

"Everything okay?"

"Yeah. No. I don't know." He hovered near the doorway, then sat on the old stool across from my workbench, hands working together in his lap. "Been wanting to say something for a while. Kept chickening out. Figured today was as good as any before I lost my nerve again."

I waited.

"You know some of it, probably. That there was a whole — thing. Between me and Cent. A while back." His jaw worked. "Don't know exactly what she's told you. Don't want to assume, don't want to make her relive it filling in gaps for me. So I'm gonna tell you myself, if that's okay."

"Okay."

"I cheated on her. Not an ambiguous, gray-area thing. Clear, stupid, selfish, at a party, with someone I didn't even particularly care about, for reasons I still can't fully explain to myself." He said it plain, no flinching, though his hands didn't stop moving. "She found out. Obviously. Want you to know, sitting here, I understand exactly what that almost cost me. Not just her. Everything."

"Tell me."

"Almost lost the only person who's ever made me want to be better than my own worst instincts. Not exaggeration. Mean that literally." His voice cracked slightly, and he pushed through it. "Grew up in a house where nobody modeled real accountability. My dad's version of an apology was buying my mom flowers and never saying the words. Didn't have a template for what I was supposed to do once I'd broken something that mattered that much."

"What'd you do instead?"

"Wrote about nine versions of an apology. Deleted most of them because they were really just me trying to explain myself out of the consequences, which I eventually figured out wasn't an apology at all. Just a longer way of asking to be let off the hook." A small, humorless laugh. "Finally wrote one that didn't ask for anything. Didn't defend anything. Told her the truth, told her I didn't expect a response, meant it. Walked away after saying it because I believed, right then, I didn't deserve one."

Something shifted in my own chest, old memory of my own worst mistakes surfacing alongside real, growing respect for the kid sitting across from me.

"That took real courage," I said. "The not-expecting-anything part specifically. Most people apologizing still want something back. Forgiveness. Relief. Sounds like you actually understood you weren't owed any of it."

"Didn't feel courageous. Felt like the worst version of myself, walking away from that alley not knowing if I'd just said the last thing to her I'd ever get to say." He met my eyes. "I think about that almost-cost almost every day, still. Not to punish myself forever — done the work, with an actual therapist, to make sure it's not just guilt for guilt's sake. But I never want to forget how close I came to losing something I still don't fully understand why I got a second chance at."

"Why are you telling me this? Now."

Joe thought about it seriously. "Because you're her dad. Because somewhere under everything polite you've been to me since we got back together, there's gotta be a part of you wondering if I'm actually trustworthy, or if you're just watching your daughter make the same mistake twice out of love. Wanted you to hear it from me. Not the polished version. The real one."

I was quiet a long moment.

"I appreciate you telling me," I said, finally. "Genuinely. Won't pretend it doesn't matter, hearing it — I'd be lying if I said some part of me hadn't wondered exactly that. But I've watched you with her this year. Watched how careful you are with her trust now, how you don't just assume it's fully rebuilt because time's passed. That matters more to me than the original mistake, honestly. I've got my own list of things I'm not proud of. What matters is what you build after."

"That's — thank you." Something visibly eased in him, tension he'd clearly been carrying since he knocked. "Wasn't sure how this was gonna go."

"For what it's worth, the fact you were scared to tell me and told me anyway says something on its own."

"Learned that from your family, actually. This whole tell-people-the-hard-true-thing philosophy. Cent talks about it a lot. Figured I owed you the same honesty."

We sat together a while, something settled between us now.
