Chapter 29

Rick asked me to come look at the Maule's engine with him one Sunday, and watching him work I got struck by how much more there was to him than the quiet, steady presence I'd mostly known.

"You're really good at this," I said, watching his hands move through the engine, easy and confident.

"Had to learn. Family never had money for someone else to fix things." Plain fact, no weight to it, wiping grease off with a rag that had seen better days. "Figured out young that if I wanted things to work, I needed to learn how myself."

"That's a lot of responsibility for a kid."

"Maybe. Didn't feel like responsibility at the time. Felt like the thing that needed doing." He thought about it more. "That's actually where I get the most satisfaction, honestly. Fixing things properly. Understanding something well enough to make it work better."

"Is that why you're so quiet in the group? You mostly just watch, then say something sharp right when it matters."

Rick thought about that seriously. "Probably. Never been great with a lot of fast words — Joey's got that covered for the whole group. But I notice things. I like understanding a situation before I say anything about it."

"I like that about you. Feels rare."

"Thanks." Something surprised crossed his face, like it landed somewhere real. "Not everyone appreciates it. People read quiet as boring, or slow. Neither's quite right."

"I don't think you're either of those."

We worked another hour, Rick explaining each part as he went. I got fascinated by how carefully his mind worked — every angle considered before he committed to anything, the same patience that apparently made him such a ruthless board game opponent once he'd picked a strategy.

"Can I ask you something," I said, as we wiped the grease off our hands. "What do you actually want? For your own future. I know what Charlie wants, what Marie and Joey want. Never heard you talk about it."

Rick thought about it properly. "Want to build things, honestly. Actual things, not just fixing what's broken, though I like that too. Been teaching myself engineering, mostly from books. Home's got real need for people who understand infrastructure, with everything still being rebuilt. Think that's where I want to end up. Somewhere I can help build something lasting."

"That's a genuinely beautiful ambition."

"Think so too. Don't talk about it much, though. Feels exposed, saying it out loud." A small real smile. "You're easy to talk to. Don't know if I've said that before, but you are."

"Thank you. Means a lot, coming from somebody who chooses his words carefully."

I mentioned the afternoon to Charlie later that week.

"Rick's remarkable," Charlie said, fond. "Been that way since we were kids. Doesn't say much, but when he does, it's worth hearing. Half of why our whole group works is because he's steady even when the rest of us are being loud about everything."

"I understand that now, properly. Spent an afternoon on the plane with him, learned more in those few hours than months of group hangouts."

"That's Rick. Worth the patience."
