Chapter 30

Tony found me in the workshop the night before Cory's first suit test, four days before the sedan, ostensibly checking on equipment he'd loaned Cory for the fitting. Looking for an excuse to talk, I suspected.

"You've got a good kid," he said, no preamble, examining a piece of climbing gear with more attention than it warranted. "The whole watching-her-build-something-real thing. Not nothing. Most parents get scared when a kid starts reaching that big. You just signed off on it. Practically day one."

"Scared plenty, honestly. Just didn't want the fear to be the thing she remembered."

"Yeah." Something careful crossed his face, the usual quick energy dialed way down. "My old man wasn't big on that particular parenting philosophy. Howard had opinions. Loud ones. Mostly about what I should be building, not what I actually wanted to."

"Didn't know that."

"Not something I lead with." He set the gear down, looked at me directly. "He wasn't cruel, exactly. Busy. Important. Building the future, capital letters, and I was supposed to fall in line behind whatever he'd already decided Stark Industries needed to become. Took most of my adult life to figure out the difference between inheriting a legacy and choosing one."

"Sounds like a hard thing to untangle."

"It was. Still is, some days." A small, wry smile, more vulnerable than his usual performance. "Watching you with Cent — actually asking her what she wants instead of deciding for her — that's not small, Davy. You don't know how rare that is, coming from someone who's spent an embarrassing amount on therapy trying to become the opposite of his own father."

I thought about that. "For what it's worth, I don't think I'd have gotten here without my own hard lessons first. Wasn't always this good at it. Spent years thinking the safest thing I could do for anyone I loved was disappear before I could disappoint them."

"Yeah, I've read the file. Worse dad, comparatively." Something warm crossed his face. "Think that's actually why this has gotten to me more than I expected. Watching your family do the thing mine never quite managed — building something better than what got handed down, instead of just repeating it with better tech."

"You're doing that too. In your own way."

"Trying to." He picked the gear back up, needing something for his hands. "Got a kid of my own now. Little older than a baby. Terrifies me more than anything in my professional life ever has."

"Never fully goes away, for what it's worth. You just get more practice carrying it."

"Comforting and not comforting, in exactly the ratio I've come to expect from parenting advice." A real laugh. "Wanted to say it out loud to somebody who'd get it. Watching you build something with Cent that's actually about her — that's the thing I'm trying to figure out how to do myself. Good to know it's possible."

"You'll get there. Nobody gets it right consistently. Just more right than wrong, eventually, if you keep trying."

"More right than wrong. I can work with that. Lower bar than 'live up to Howard Stark,' which is genuinely the only one I grew up handed." He set the gear down properly this time. "Thanks, Davy."

"Anytime."

He left a while later. I sat in the workshop alone for a long time after, turning it over — how much weight a man who looked like nothing but easy confidence actually carried underneath it.
