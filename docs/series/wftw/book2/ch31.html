Chapter 31

Thor asked me to explain "meme" three separate times over one dinner, and by the third attempt I'd started questioning my own understanding of it.

"So this image," he said, holding up my phone with the careful grip of a man handling something possibly fragile, "of the small dog wearing the tiny hat — this is humorous specifically because the dog appears to experience an emotion it cannot possibly be experiencing."

"Basically, yeah."

"And its popularity stems from thousands of others taking this same image and applying their own words to it." He considered this with real scholarly attention. "Not unlike certain oral traditions of Asgard, wherein a legendary framework gets retold with local embellishments across generations."

"I — guess? Never thought of it that way."

"I find it more comprehensible, thinking of it that way." He handed my phone back with the care of a man returning a sacred artifact. "Midgard's humor operates on principles I am only beginning to map. I confess it took me the better part of two years to understand that 'yeet' was not, in fact, a formal greeting."

"Who told you it was a formal greeting?"

"Your friend Xander. I remain uncertain whether he was engaging in what you call a 'prank,' or genuinely believed it himself." Real puzzlement, even now. "I greeted several SHIELD agents this way for roughly three weeks before Director Fury took me aside and explained, with remarkable patience, that this had not gone unnoticed."

I nearly choked on my food. Dad was outright laughing.

"Fury actually pulled you aside for that?"

"He did. Sparing me further embarrassment, I believe, though his delivery was drier than the concern itself." Genuine fondness at the memory. "I have since learned to verify Xander's instructions through at least one additional source before formal settings."

"Good policy," Mom said, still smiling.

"A necessary one." Thor considered his plate. "In Xander's defense, he has taught me a great many genuinely useful things, alongside the occasional formal-greeting sabotage. The sloth remains one of my favorite creatures across all the realms I've visited, purely from his enthusiasm describing it."

"You've never actually seen one in person?"

"Not yet, though Xander has promised to arrange it, given advance notice and what he calls 'proper sloth-viewing conditions.'" Hopeful. "I am told they move with remarkable unhurriedness. After everything this year has demanded, I find myself rather looking forward to observing a creature so entirely committed to taking its time."

"Could use a little more sloth energy myself, honestly," I said.

"A wise philosophy. I shall consider adopting it formally, once the current threat's addressed." Thor raised his glass. "To sloths. And to Midgardian humor, which I intend to keep studying, however slowly my comprehension progresses."

We all raised our glasses, and I laughed properly for the first time in days.
