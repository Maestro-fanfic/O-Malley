Chapter 19

Joe came by Thursday evening, four weeks before the sedan, home for the weekend from Stanford, easy in the way we'd settled into once things found their footing again after everything the year before. Some part of me still checked, quiet and automatic, whether "easy" actually meant easy or just meant practiced.

"You look tired," he said, on the porch step beside me, shoulder touching mine.

"Been running my parents' whole discovery thing through my head all week. Dad's expanded awareness. Mom's resonance. My own structural thing. There's this whole layer to the ability none of us understood before."

"That's very you. Find something remarkable, immediately want every angle of it." He took my hand. "How's the understanding part going?"

"Slowly. Which is its own kind of frustrating." I let him take my hand, didn't lean in yet — old habit, running its quiet check before I let myself relax into anything. "How's Stanford?"

"Good. Physics is kicking my ass in the good way. Got a research position starting next semester." He squeezed my hand. "Missed you. More than I let myself admit most weeks."

"Missed you too." I studied his face before I said it — the old reflex, reading for anything off, anything performed. Found nothing, same as usual lately. Leaned in anyway. "We're doing okay, aren't we. Distance and everything."

"We're doing okay," he agreed, relief in it. "Took a lot of work getting back here. I don't take it for granted."

"Good. Because I still notice myself checking, sometimes. Not constantly. Sometimes. I don't love that about myself, but I'd rather tell you it's still there than pretend it isn't."

"I'd rather know. Always." He didn't flinch, didn't get defensive. "I'd be more worried if you didn't check at all. Feels like the kind of thing that should take longer than a year to stop doing."

I thought about that year — the hard conversations, the accountability he'd had to sit with, the work I'd had to do trusting again. I still remembered the actual apology word for word, the way you remember things that land hard enough to carve in — him chewing his lip raw before he got it out, tears he clearly hadn't planned on, saying he didn't need a response and didn't expect one, then walking off saying he didn't deserve one either. I told him, weeks later, that he did. Deserve one. That was its own hard conversation. Harder than staying angry would've been. Some days the anger felt safer.

"I love you," I said, finally, and it still cost something small to say out loud. "More than I let myself admit most days."

"I love you too," he said, and kissed me, and I let myself sink into it properly this time, the checking finally quiet for a minute.

We sat a while.

"Can I tell you something weird? Been turning it over since we started this research."

"Always."

✳

"I think there's another layer to it. Past the structural thing. Something about having something to actually push against mid-jump, instead of just moving myself through space." I tried to put words to it. "Got a flicker of it once, years ago, testing velocity stuff in the desert. Never chased it down. Been thinking about it again, watching Mom and Dad make their own finds."

"Sounds like exactly the thing you should chase, instead of letting it sit."

"Maybe. Feels like a project for later. Lot going on right now, between school and Home."

"Fair. But don't forget it. Sounds like your kind of rabbit hole." He grinned. "I'll hold you to it. Once things settle."

"Deal," I said, and meant it.

We spent the rest of the evening in easy company. Watching him head home later, I felt grateful for a relationship rebuilt into something more solid than it had ever been. I don't think the checking ever fully goes away. Some quiet part of me still runs its own small audit, most days — faster now, easier to answer, but never quite gone. I've made a kind of peace with that. Trust rebuilt doesn't retire the vigilance. It just means the vigilance usually comes back with a good answer now.
