Chapter 23

Joe came back for another weekend two weeks before the sedan. I met him at the gate, excited to show him the progress on the structural-perception thing.

"You look happy," he said, pulling me into a hug before either of us said much. "Good happy. Research glow."

"Really good happy. Dad and Mom made their own finds too, and Bruce introduced me to a researcher, Cory, who's got a spacesuit design that might actually work with my ability in ways nobody's really considered." Words came faster than I usually let myself talk. "I know it's a lot. I'll let you get a word in eventually."

"I love watching you like this," Joe said, walking with me toward the house, hand in mine. "Completely absorbed in something because it fascinates you. Reminds me why I fell for you in the first place."

Some part of me still automatically flagged lines like that — old habit, checking whether it was real or the kind of thing he used to say right before doing something that made the saying feel cheap after. Found nothing this time except him meaning it. Let it land.

"Even with everything complicated in between?"

"Especially with that. We came back to us because it was actually worth coming back to. That matters more, I think, than if we'd never hit a rough patch." He squeezed my hand. "Feels earned this time. Not assumed."

I turned that over. "I've been trying to hold that same idea about my parents' whole thing too. That the hard parts don't undo what they eventually built. Just part of the honest shape of it."

"Exactly that."

We spent the evening on the porch, going through my notes, Joe asking questions that pushed my thinking further than I'd managed alone — his physics background giving him an angle nobody else in my life offered.

"You mentioned momentum transfer too," he said, flipping back a page. "The thing you tested in the desert years ago. Thought more about it?"

"A little. Still mostly a stray thought. Keep meaning to chase it, always something else first."

"You should, eventually. Sounds like it could matter, if you developed it." He looked at the notes. "Imagine transferring velocity to something you're holding, not just yourself. Opens up things neither of us has probably considered yet."

"I know. Keep telling myself 'eventually.'"

"Fair. Just don't let it disappear. Sounds like your kind of project."

We spent the rest of the weekend easy — dinner with my parents, a lazy Saturday at one of Dad's quiet spots. Watching him get ready to head back Sunday evening, I felt grateful for how steady this had gotten, nothing like our earlier, rougher attempts at the same relationship.

"Back in three weeks," he said, at the gate, one last kiss. "Call me during the week, though. I like hearing about the research as it happens, not just the full download every third weekend."

"I will. Promise."

"Love you," he said, simple.

"Love you too."

I went back inside, sat at my desk with the notes still spread out, turned back to the momentum-transfer question. Tucked it away again, same as I'd been tucking it away for years.

Someday, I thought. I'd actually dig into that one properly someday.
