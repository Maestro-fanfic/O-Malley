Chapter 33

Joe showed up for his visit with an actual PowerPoint presentation, projected onto our living room wall with the specific, slightly unhinged enthusiasm he reserved for exactly two subjects: orbital mechanics, and, apparently, me.

"I need you to understand this isn't a joke," he said, clicking to a slide titled Reasons Cent Rice Is Objectively The Most Interesting Person At Stanford, A Data-Driven Analysis. "I built actual graphs. There's a bibliography."

"Joe. This is deeply unnecessary."

"It is extremely necessary. My roommate bet me twenty dollars I couldn't make a rigorous academic case for why I'm dating you specifically. I have never once turned down a bet involving both academic rigor and twenty dollars." He clicked again — an actual scatter plot, axes labeled "Danger Tolerance" and "Genuine Scientific Curiosity," one data point marked CENT sitting alone in the far upper-right corner. "You're a statistical outlier. I have the math."

"You're ridiculous."

"I contain multitudes, most of them nerdy." He closed the laptop, dropped onto the couch beside me. "Collected the twenty dollars, for the record. Marcus owed it the second he saw slide four. Didn't even make it to the bibliography."

I laughed properly, the unguarded kind Joe seemed to specialize in earning out of me.

"How's the actual coursework, underneath the bit-committing?"

"Brutal. Genuinely brutal. Taking an orbital dynamics seminar that's convinced me I know nothing, which, given what you're currently attempting with actual physical teleportation into orbit, should probably bother me more than it does." He stretched. "Also picked up rock climbing. Bad at it. Spectacularly. Fell off an intermediate route in front of considerable witnesses last week."

"Why keep doing it, if you're bad at it?"

"Because being bad at something new is a good reminder that being good at things takes actual work. I think I'd gotten a little too comfortable only doing stuff I was already good at." More serious than his usual delivery. "Also, unrelated, my therapist thinks it's good for me. Something about physical vulnerability being practice ground for the emotional kind. Mostly I just think it's funny watching myself fall off walls."

"Your therapist sounds smart."

"Terrifyingly perceptive. Stopped trying to be clever with her. Waste of both our time." He glanced at me, teeth catching briefly at his lower lip the way they still did when something real was coming. "Speaking of which — want you to know I'm still doing the work. On the trust stuff. Don't bring it up because I don't want it to feel like I'm asking for credit every five minutes. But don't want you thinking it's just done. Handled. I check in with myself on it constantly."

"I know. I can tell, mostly." I reached for his hand. "For what it's worth, I'm still doing my own version too. Still catch myself checking sometimes, even now. Not saying that to make you feel bad. Just — didn't want you thinking the trust rebuilt itself automatically because we got back together. Still work, both ends."

"Good. That's good." He squeezed my hand, visibly relieved. "Okay, heavy moment concluded. Want to see slide seven? Pie chart. Very proud of the pie chart."

"Show me the pie chart, Joe."

He did, and I spent the next hour laughing at increasingly absurd data visualizations charting exactly why he found me worth crossing a reality's worth of complication for. Solid didn't mean fully healed, though. I still check, some days more than others. Doesn't fully retire, no matter how many good years pile on top. Just gets quieter. Easier to sit with. Some days that even feels like enough.
