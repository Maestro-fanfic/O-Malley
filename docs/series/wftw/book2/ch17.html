Chapter 17

I spent a Saturday with the full Five at Charlie's ranch, six weeks before the sedan, and finally clocked something I'd been missing on earlier visits.

"You two are together, right?" I asked Marie, quiet, while Charlie and Clara worked on the old hangar door together, moving with the kind of coordination that doesn't come from just being friends.

"Officially? On-and-off the past year. Pretty solidly on right now." Marie watched them, fond. "Took them embarrassingly long to admit it. Rick and Joey and I had a betting pool going on when they'd stop pretending it was just friendship."

"How long?"

"Years, unfortunately. Painful to watch after a while." She grinned. "Worth it now, though. They're good together."

Joey found me watching. "Disgustingly cute, right? Took them long enough."

"They really are."

"Steal that. I've been saying it for months, nobody appreciates my wit."

"Appreciated. Occasionally."

"I'll take occasionally." He looked at the two of them a second, something fond under the usual bit. "Honestly, though — watching them finally get it right feels good. Proof the complicated stuff can work out."

Rick joined us, quiet as always. "They earned it. Watching them hold each other up through everything Home's put us through — that's real, not just teenage romance."

Clara pulled me aside later, easy warmth in it.

"Charlie talks about you a lot," she said, on the hangar's worn bench. "Your family. He's fond of all of you, past just interesting-crossover-friend territory."

"We're pretty fond of him too. All of you, honestly."

"I'm glad." She looked at me, curious. "What's your family actually like, day to day?"

I spent an easy hour on it — Dad's hard road learning to accept help, Mom's work, our own digging into the ability's depths — and noticed, talking to her, how naturally the two families had settled into each other, entirely apart from any romance.

"You've got a remarkable family," Clara said, once I'd finished.

"Thank you. Yours too. I like how you all actually show up for each other, even with everything Home's asked of you."

"We try. Might be the whole secret. Just choosing, over and over, to show up."

I mentioned the afternoon to Mom over dinner.

"Charlie and Clara are actually together. Have been a while, it sounds like." I turned it over. "Nice, seeing that. Makes the whole friend group feel more solid."

"That's wonderful," Mom said. "Glad you're building real connections with all of them, not just Charlie."

"Yeah. Me too."
