Chapter 55

Sunnydale settled back into its own rhythm in the weeks following the crew's return, senior year resuming its ordinary demands even against the backdrop of everything that had changed, and Xander noticed he was, more than once, caught in the strange, grounding whiplash of algebra homework sitting alongside memories of a newborn cosmic entity's first words.

"You're doing the thing again," Willow said, catching him staring at his textbook without actually reading it, lunch table noise washing pleasantly around them.

"Which thing."

"The thing where your brain's clearly out in that field even though your body's very much stuck here doing polynomial factoring."

"Guilty." He managed a rueful smile. "Feels weird, doesn't it? Going back to worrying about test grades after everything."

"A little. Mostly it feels nice, though. Ordinary's underrated. I don't think I fully appreciated that before this year." Willow nudged his tray back toward him, pointed reminder to actually eat. "Besides, we're not really gone. Just — not there every single day. Big difference."

Faith arrived mid-conversation, dropping into her usual seat with the easy comfort of someone thoroughly settled into exactly the family this table represented. "Patrol was quiet last night, for the record. Actual quiet, not ominous-mural quiet. Feels like we've earned a boring stretch, honestly, given the last few months."

"Don't jinx it," Buffy warned, arriving a beat behind her, sliding in beside Xander with the easy, established closeness that had become simply how they existed together.

"I contain multitudes of jinx-resistance," Faith said, unbothered, stealing a fry off Xander's tray on principle. "Sunnydale's had its share of drama this year. I think it's allowed a quiet stretch too."

The table gave way to its familiar, comfortable noise, and Xander caught himself, watching the four of them together, feeling something he hadn't quite let himself name until just now — sincere gratitude, not just for the astonishing parts of the past few months, but for this too, the ordinary, precious rhythm of lunch and homework and easy company that had somehow, remarkably, remained fully intact underneath everything else.

"Good stretch," he said, mostly to himself, though Buffy caught it and smiled.

"Yeah," she agreed, leaning into him. "Really good stretch."
