Chapter 127

The man — Voss, according to the identification T'Challa's people eventually pulled from the compound's own records — offered little resistance once Thor and Natasha secured him properly, his calculating composure finally cracking into something far more petty and small once his methods lost the shield of clinical detachment.

"You don't understand what I was trying to build," he insisted, as Fury's people prepared to transport him for formal custody. "A reliable method. Something that didn't depend on the unpredictable luck of finding honestly devoted people in the right circumstances. I was going to help more families than your slow, steady approach ever could."

"You were going to hurt more beings," Millie said, cold with a fury Charlie had rarely witnessed from her. "There's no version of what you built that was ever going to end in anything but exactly this — pain, forced into a shape it was never meant to hold."

Voss had no answer for that, and Fury's people led him away without further ceremony, the compound's cold, clinical horror finally, fully left behind.

The freed presence, meanwhile, remained jagged and wary despite Dawn's unhurried reassurance, trust clearly going to require a good deal more than a single afternoon's careful welcome.

"This is going to take time," Wanda said, quiet, settling into the same even-tempered work she'd once brought to Nadiya's own dearly bought trust. "Real time. Possibly a great deal longer than any previous case."

"We have it," Millie said, immediate and certain. "However long this takes, we're not leaving until it's really, safely settled."

They stayed three full weeks at the compound, slowly transforming its cold, clinical structures into something much warmer — Steve and Sam again taking up practical work, tearing down reinforced walls and replacing sterile rooms with the kind of genuine, welcoming space Voss's twisted methodology had never allowed to exist. Bruce and Shuri worked carefully to understand and, where possible, gently correct whatever damage the forced process had done to the entity's own developing sense of self, while Wanda and Dawn together maintained the slow, calm work of rebuilding trust one careful day at a time.

By the third week's end, the jagged flickering had settled, gradually, into something significantly steadier, still marked by its difficult beginning but no longer defined entirely by it.

i do not know if i can ever fully trust the way your other children seem to, the presence admitted, quiet, to Dawn, during one of their now-daily conversations. what was done to me in my earliest days — i do not think that simply disappears, however steady you are with me.

it may not disappear entirely, Dawn said, gentle, honest rather than falsely reassuring. but it does not have to remain the whole shape of your story either. i promise you that, from someone who has learned, far, how much room a life actually has for both old pain and true, growing peace.

The presence considered that a long moment, something in its wary flickering settling, just slightly, further toward steady warmth.

i would like to try believing that, it said, finally. even if trying is difficult, some days.

Trelane, who had said almost nothing since the compound's raid, finally spoke, quiet, from where he'd been keeping careful distance from the presence's healing. I confess I spent a good portion of these three weeks wanting very badly to have done something considerably less patient to the man who built this place. Old habits are hard to break, it seems, even when the impulse arrives dressed as righteousness instead of temper.

"But you didn't," Millie said, gentle, watching him.

No. I didn't. I found, rather to my own surprise, that I wanted this family's good opinion more than I wanted the old satisfaction. That's new, for me. I'm not entirely certain what to do with it yet, beyond simply noticing it happened.

"Trying's enough," Millie said, warm, stepping forward at last. "It's always been enough, every single time. We'll be right here, for however long trying takes."
