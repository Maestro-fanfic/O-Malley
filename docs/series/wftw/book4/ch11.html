Chapter 11 of 131

Chapter 11

Millie found Ashgrove alone on the porch a little before midnight, the Councilor still awake, still working through a tablet's worth of notes by lamplight while the rest of the house had finally gone quiet.

"You should be resting," Millie said, settling into the chair beside her without waiting for an invitation.

"So should you." Ashgrove didn't look up right away. "I find I do my best thinking after everyone else has stopped needing me to perform confidence for them."

"That's a very specific kind of tired to recognize in somebody else." Millie let the silence sit a moment, the old professional instinct surfacing before she'd consciously decided to use it. "You've been carrying this alone for eight months. Suspecting, not knowing, not able to tell anyone because you couldn't prove it and didn't want to risk being wrong. That's not just diplomatic caution. That's isolation."

Ashgrove's hands stilled on the tablet.

"You're doing the thing," she said, something almost wry under the words. "The reading-people thing. I'd heard you trained in some manner of counsel, back before you settled here permanently."

"Occupational hazard. I don't always manage to turn it off." Millie didn't apologize for it. "You've spent eight months being the only person in Home who knew this was coming, unable to tell my family, unable to fully act, just watching and waiting and hoping you'd guessed right about the timing. That's not a comfortable position for anyone. It's an especially uncomfortable one for somebody who clearly cares more than her job technically requires."

"Is that an accusation?"

"It's an observation. You drove case, alone, into a house full of people you had every reason to expect might be furious with you, because you decided being honest mattered more than being safe." Millie met her eyes. "That's not the profile of somebody who's here to manage us. That's the profile of somebody who's actually trying to protect us, and has been for longer than any of us realized to thank her for it."

Ashgrove was quiet for a long moment, something in her carefully composed bearing finally, briefly, cracking.

"I don't know if it's going to be enough," she admitted, quieter than Millie had heard her yet. "Whatever I argue, whatever precedent Giles finds, whatever your daughter's numbers actually prove — I don't fully control this outcome. I've spent forty years believing I understood the shape of my own authority within Home, and this week I've discovered how much of that understanding was assumption rather than actual knowledge."

"That's a hard thing to sit with."

"It is." Ashgrove set the tablet down finally. "May I ask you something in return? Not as Councilor to family. Just as one tired person to another."

"Go ahead."

"Are you angry with me? Entirely. Not the diplomatic answer. The plain one."

Millie considered the question honestly, the way she considered most things that actually mattered.

"I was, this morning," she said. "I think some part of me still is, a little. Eight months is a long time to have carried a warning I never got to hear. But I've spent this whole day watching you argue our case to people with actual power to hurt us, at unmistakable cost to your own standing in Home, and I don't think anger's actually the useful thing to be feeling anymore. I think gratitude is, even if it's complicated gratitude. You could have stayed quiet indefinitely. You didn't."

"That's more grace than I expected."

"I didn't say it was easy grace. I said it was the useful kind." Millie stood, offering a hand to help Ashgrove up from the chair. "Come inside. Get some actual sleep. Whatever comes tomorrow, you're not facing it as well rested as you'll need to be if you keep sitting out here doing this to yourself all night."

Ashgrove took the offered hand, something like genuine relief crossing her face for the first time since she'd arrived.

"Thank you," she said. "Not just for the invitation. For the reading. I don't believe anyone's actually seen me that clearly in some while."

"Occupational hazard," Millie said again, gentler this time, and led her back inside.
