Chapter 23

The light didn't fully settle into anything nameable that first night, and by morning the household had reorganized itself around a single, obvious, entirely unprecedented question: what do you actually do, practically, day to day, with something that's still deciding what it is.

"It's not going anywhere," Wanda reported, over a breakfast nobody had much appetite for despite the hour. "I checked twice before I let myself sleep. It's eased into something like a resting state, still present, still gently aware of us, but not actively reaching the way it was yesterday. I think it's processing. The way an infant sleeps most of the day, even while its brain is doing enormous amounts of work underneath."

"So it needs care," Millie said, already thinking practically, the old instinct surfacing fast. "The way anything new needs care."

"I believe so, yes. I don't yet know what that care actually looks like for something like this. I don't think anyone does."

Ashgrove, who'd spent most of the night in tense contact with Home through channels Charlie didn't fully understand, looked up from her tablet with an expression caught somewhere between exhaustion and plain astonishment.

"Home has no protocol for this," she said. "None. I've now confirmed that directly, from the highest level I have access to. Nobody has ever reported anything like it. I don't say that to frighten anyone further. I say it because it means the protocol, whatever it turns out to be, gets built here, by the people actually present for this, rather than handed down from somewhere with more experience than us."

"That's either terrifying or the best news we've had in weeks," Tony said. "Possibly both. I contain multitudes on this one."

"It means we get to decide who this becomes, at least partly," Wanda said, quiet, something like unmistakable weight settling into the words. "Not control. I don't think control's the right frame, and I don't think it would work regardless. But influence. Presence. The same way any child's raised partly by who chooses to show up for them."

Fury hadn't said much up to that point. When he finally spoke, old instinct was working through the actual operational shape of the problem.

"That raises a question I don't love asking, but somebody's got to," he said. "Who else finds out about this, and when, and how do we control that rollout before it controls us. Because I guarantee you, whatever Stark's satellites picked up on the intervention work is nothing compared to what's about to start showing up on every serious monitoring system on this planet, the second this thing's presence stabilizes into something detectable at scale."

The room's easy warmth cooled slightly at that, the practical weight of the question landing where the wonder of the night before had briefly let everyone forget it existed.

"He's right," Natasha said. "Governments. Other agencies. Possibly other realities entirely, if the Keepers themselves are watching this closely. Something this significant doesn't stay a family secret for long, no matter how careful anyone is."

"Then we get ahead of it," Steve said. "Same as we should've done with the intervention work from the start, if we're honest. Controlled disclosure, on our terms, to people we trust, rather than another satellite finding it first and setting the terms for us."

"Easier said than managed," M'Baku said, blunt as always. "But not wrong. Wakanda's got plenty of experience controlling exactly this kind of rollout. I'd be glad to advise, if it's wanted."

"It's wanted," Millie said, without hesitation. "Whatever this becomes, I don't want us caught flat-footed by our own success a second time. We've done that once already this month. I'd rather not make it a habit."

The room sank into planning after that, the wonder of the night before folding into the practical, unglamorous work of figuring out how to protect something remarkable in a world that hadn't yet decided whether to be amazed by it or afraid of it — one more impossible task added to a household that had, by now, gotten remarkably practiced at exactly this kind of work.
