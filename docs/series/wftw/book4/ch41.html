Chapter 41

Evening settled over the homestead with the last of the guests departing in careful, staggered waves, Natasha and T'Challa's security planning holding steady right through to the final handshake, and by the time full dark arrived, the property had quieted back into something like its familiar shape — smaller, warmer, just the family and the roster who'd become, over the past month, something closer to family themselves.

They gathered on the porch and the surrounding steps, plates from Thor's insisted-upon feast still scattered nearby, the light above the open ground folded into its now-familiar evening rhythm, and Fury, standing near the rail with a look Charlie had learned to recognize as him working through something significant, finally spoke.

"I've had confirmation from three separate agencies now," he said. "All standing down from any containment posture. The story's already spreading exactly the way we hoped — truth first, wonder attached, fear far harder to attach to something this many credible witnesses have already vouched for personally." For once, he let the smile show, honest and unguarded. "I've run a lot of operations in my life. Not many where the actual goal was just making sure the world got to be amazed by something good. I'd forgotten how much better that feels than the usual work."

"You should let yourself feel it more often," Sam said, from nearby. "Good work exists. Doesn't have to always be crisis management."

"Duly noted," Fury said, dry, though something plain sat underneath it.

Ashgrove, seated near Millie, received a message on her tablet partway through the conversation and went very still reading it, enough that the whole porch's conversation gradually quieted around her.

"Home's Council has reached a preliminary position," she said, finally, looking up. "Not final — that will take longer, proper process, proper deliberation. But preliminary, and I think you'll want to hear it tonight rather than wait."

"Tell us," Millie said, steady despite the sudden tension in her voice.

"The Council recognizes this family's intervention work as consistent with, rather than contrary to, the mission's actual intent. The manner of achievement — direct, unsanctioned, deeply human in its methods — is being treated not as a violation, but as evidence that the mission's authors may have underestimated exactly what unmistakable stewardship could look like when pursued by people who simply refused to wait." Ashgrove's voice caught slightly. "There is no punishment forthcoming. There is, instead, a formal request — not demand, request — that this family continue their work with Home's full support and resources, rather than in quiet isolation."

The porch went very still, and then, all at once, erupted.
