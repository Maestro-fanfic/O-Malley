Chapter 53

Dawn asked about Buffy specifically on a quiet Thursday evening, three months into her life, the question arriving with a curiosity that felt, to Wanda, subtly different from her usual searching interest in the world.

"She wants to know more about the Slayer," Wanda reported, finding Millie in the kitchen. "Not generally — specifically. Buffy. She's asked twice this week, and both times there's been something underneath the question I can't quite name. Not urgency exactly. Something closer to recognition, like she's reaching toward something she half-remembers rather than something entirely new."

Millie considered that, filing it away with the same careful attention she gave every one of Dawn's small, wondrous developments. "Maybe she just liked her, from the disclosure day. People don't need a bigger reason than that."

"Maybe," Wanda agreed, though something in her voice suggested she wasn't fully convinced.

Out in the field, when Wanda relayed the question properly, Dawn's answer arrived easy and a little wistful, more contemplative than her usual bright curiosity.

i don't know why i think of her so often, Dawn admitted. i only met her the one day, and only briefly. but something about her feels important to me, in a way i don't yet have words for. like a thread i haven't finished following. is that strange?

"Nothing about you is strange," Wanda said, gentle. "You're allowed to feel drawn to people for reasons you don't fully understand yet. That's not unusual, even for beings who've had far longer than three months to work out their own feelings."

i would like to see her again, when it's possible. i don't need it to be soon. i think i just wanted you to know it mattered to me.

"I'll let her know," Wanda promised. "I think she'd be glad to hear it, honestly. I don't think anyone who's met you forgets the feeling easily."

Millie, hearing the report later that evening, felt something quietly, unnamably moved by it — no clear reason to feel that way, nothing concrete to point to, just the exact instinct of a mother recognizing, without being able to explain how, that something important was quietly, gently taking root.

"Strange, sweet thing to be curious about," she said, half to herself.

"Maybe," Davy said, beside her, watching the distant light pulse soft and thoughtful above the field. "Or maybe some connections just know themselves before anyone else does. Wouldn't be the strangest thing we've learned this year."
