Chapter 32

Faith spent the afternoon on perimeter duty alongside Clint, mostly because Natasha had asked and mostly because sitting still with everything else going on had stopped being an option for her days ago.

"You're better at this than I expected," Clint said, watching her read the treeline with the same wary competence she brought to a Sunnydale graveyard. "Different monsters, same instincts, I'm guessing."

"Something like that." Faith kept her eyes on the distant vehicle, still parked, still watching. "Feels weird, being on this side of a stakeout instead of the one usually getting stalked by something undead."

"Feels weird being on this side of a lot of things lately, if it helps. I signed up for archery and ended up guarding a newborn cosmic entity from a government surveillance van. Wasn't in the brochure."

That got an unmistakable laugh out of her, some of the day's tension easing. "Yeah. Nobody warned me about this either, back when Xander first asked if I wanted to see something cool on an alternate Earth."

"Regret it?"

"Not even a little." She glanced toward the house, toward the warm light still hovering steady above the open ground, visible even from this distance. "Feels like exactly the kind of thing worth standing guard for, honestly. First time in a while I've felt like the thing I'm protecting is something purely good, not just something less bad than the alternative."

Clint considered that, something thoughtful in his own expression. "That's a good feeling to hold onto. Doesn't come around as often as you'd think, in this line of work."

"Yeah. Figured I'd enjoy it while it lasts." Faith shifted her stance, scanning the treeline once more, steady and unhurried. "Whatever happens with that van, whatever happens at the disclosure thing — I'm glad I got to be here for this part. Just wanted to say that out loud, I guess, before things get more complicated."

"Noted," Clint said, something warm in it. "For what it's worth, glad you're here too. Good to have somebody on this fence who actually knows how to read a treeline properly."

They settled back into comfortable, watchful quiet, the afternoon light gold and steady, the distant vehicle still and unhurried, and the warm presence above the open ground pulsing on, gentle and unafraid, entirely unbothered by anything currently watching it from a careful distance.

32.5. The Ancient One: What She Saw

Strange found the entry that evening, back in the Sanctum's archive for a handful of hours between shifts at the homestead, searching for anything in the Ancient One's old records that might touch on nascent cosmic consciousness — and found instead something he hadn't been looking for at all.

It was dated years before he'd ever taken up the mantle, recorded in her own careful hand in a private ledger he'd only ever skimmed before, filed under a heading that simply read: a vision, unasked for, worth keeping regardless.

He read it twice before he trusted himself to believe it.

She had seen an open field, she'd written. Warm light, gathered soft and slow like dawn arriving out of sequence. A gathered crowd — some familiar to her power's long sight, most not, faces she didn't yet have names for, standing together in a configuration that read, even from the strange remove of vision, as something like family, however improbably assembled. A woman with her hand pressed to her heart. A young man reaching for someone's hand without quite deciding to. A god, unashamed, with tears standing in his eyes.

i do not know what this is, she had written. i do not know when it comes, or through what door, or by what unlikely gathering of the guarded and the powerful and the ordinary. i know only that when i touched this vision, i felt no danger in it at all. only welcome. only the particular, unmistakable warmth of something new choosing, freely, to be glad of the world it was born into.

whoever eventually stands in that field — protect it, when the time comes. not because i have seen it require protecting. because i have seen how rare and how precious it is, and rare, precious things deserve protecting on principle, whether or not the danger ever actually arrives.

i will not live to see this day myself. i find, strangely, that i am at peace with that. it is enough to have been allowed to glimpse it once, from a distance, and to know that whatever comes for this world eventually, this — this warmth, this welcome — comes too.

Strange lingered over the entry a long while, the Sanctum quiet around him, and caught himself, for the first time in longer than he could easily remember, truly moved to something close to tears.

He thought of the open ground at the homestead. The gathered crowd. Millie's hand pressed to her heart. Xander reaching for Faith's hand without deciding to. Thor, unashamed, tears standing in his eyes.

she saw this, he thought. years before any of it happened, she saw exactly this, and she wasn't afraid of it. she was glad.

He copied the entry carefully into his own working notes, and beneath it, added a single line of his own before closing the book.

she was right to be glad. i have seen it now myself. it is every bit as wonderful as she wrote.
