Chapter 70

The response arrived in early spring, nearly four months after Dawn's message went out, and it came not through Fury's careful channel but directly, the same way the Keepers themselves had first arrived — pressure shifting slowly in the open air above the field, gathering into something the household had, by now, learned to recognize immediately.

But this presence felt different from the Keepers' vast, ancient weight — younger, brighter, carrying an energy that read, even before it fully arrived, as something closer to eager than solemn.

hello, a new voice said, arriving in every mind present with a great deal more flourish than the Keepers had ever employed, something theatrical and delighted threading through the greeting. i am told i have a sister. i confess i did not expect that news, in all my substantial years of expecting rather a lot of things. but here i am. i do hope i am not terribly late. i understand punctuality is valued, among your kind, though i've never quite mastered the habit myself.

Dawn's light flared, bright and startled and unmistakably delighted. you came.

of course i came. one does not simply ignore the discovery of a sibling, particularly not one so recently and so remarkably emerged. i have heard rather a lot about you, actually, in the time since your message reached me — genuine inquiry on my part, i confess, once i understood what i was dealing with. A pause, something warmer beneath the theatrical energy. i am glad you exist. i did not expect to feel that so plainly, so quickly. but there it is.

Millie watched the exchange with something caught between wonder and careful maternal wariness, old instinct assessing the new presence even as her heart, plainly, was already softening toward it.

"You're welcome here," she said, stepping forward. "Whoever you are, whatever you've been through. Dawn's hoped for this for months now."

how marvelous. a whole assembled welcoming committee, and not a single one of you looking especially terrified, which i confess is something of a novel experience for me. The presence seemed to consider the gathered crowd properly for the first time, something delighted and faintly wicked threading through the observation. gods, kings, a scientist who appears to be quietly assessing whether i can be contained by conventional engineering — you needn't bother, dear, i checked several centuries ago and the answer remains no — and an entire field positively humming with the particular energy of people who've already survived one improbable cosmic ordeal this year and are bracing, i suspect, for a second.

Fury, arms crossed, studied the presence with the particular skepticism of a man who'd read every scrap of the fragmentary Starfleet report twice. "Before this goes any further — I want it understood, plainly, that we don't take kindly to being toyed with. Whatever games you used to play."

Something shifted in the presence, fast and sharp, an old reflex surfacing before it could be caught — do not presume to instruct me on what i will or will not — — and then, just as quickly, it stopped itself, the sentence left hanging, unfinished, the theatrical brightness dimming into something considerably more careful.

forgive me. old habit. i am told my temper used to arrive rather faster than my judgment did, and apparently it has not entirely forgotten the shortcut. A pause, genuinely rueful this time. i am not that anymore. i am trying very hard not to be. but i thought it more honest to let you see the old reflex once, rather than pretend it never existed at all.

"Are we bracing correctly," Tony asked, dry, though something in his posture had gone slightly more alert.

only slightly. i've been assured, at no small amount of length and by parties whose judgment i've learned to trust, that theatrics are not the same thing as trouble, and that i am rather better at telling the two apart these days than i once was. i intend to prove it. mostly by being delightful instead of destructive, which i'm reliably informed is the preferred arrangement.

"That's reassuring," Fury said, still visibly unconvinced.

it should be. i've had rather a long time to practice the distinction. The theatrical brightness softened, just slightly, something more sincere surfacing beneath it. and you are the family who taught her that gladness is worth sharing freely, the presence added, warmth threading now through what had been pure performance a moment before. i have been rather a long time learning that lesson myself, through much harder means than she seems to have needed. i find i am glad she had gentler teachers than i did. i do intend to remain entertaining regardless. reform, in my experience, need not mean becoming dull.

"You can learn it here too," Wanda offered. "If you want to stay a while. Get to know her properly."

There was a pause, and when the presence spoke again, something in its tone had shifted into true, unguarded hope, the theatrical flourish still very much present underneath it.

i believe, it said, slowly, delight and sincerity tangled together in equal, unmistakable measure, i would like that very much indeed. do warn me, though, if i overstay my welcome. i'm told i have historically required the warning rather bluntly stated.
