Chapter 65

Wanda relayed Giles's theory to Dawn that evening, careful and measured, the same way she'd relayed every significant piece of news since the very beginning — plainly, without softening it, trusting Dawn to meet it on her own terms.

There was a pause, longer than usual, the particular quiet of someone processing something that had entirely, completely surprised them.

what? Dawn said, finally, warmth and astonishment tangled together in the single word. i have a brother?

Wanda couldn't quite help the smile that broke across her face. "Maybe. It's not confirmed. Giles found an old report, decades old, describing something that sounds a lot like the Keepers dealing with a previous child. We don't know for certain it's the same beings."

but it might be. Dawn's light pulsed, quick and searching, something new and delighted moving through it. i did not expect that. i had wondered where i came from. i had not wondered if i came from somewhere with someone else in it too.

"How does it feel? Finding that out?"

strange. good-strange, i think. i have had this whole family, freely given, since my very first night. i had not imagined there might also be a family i was simply born into, the way you all were born into yours. A pause, thoughtful. do you think he would like me? if it is true?

"I don't know," Wanda admitted, honest as always. "I don't know anything about him beyond a decades-old report describing some pretty significant mischief and a fairly dramatic parental intervention. But for what it's worth — if he's anything like you at all, I'd guess yes."

i would like to meet him. eventually. carefully, the way i met the keepers. Dawn's light warmed further, something bright and hopeful spreading through it. i did not know, until just now, that i might have been missing something. i find i am glad to have something new to hope for.

Millie, listening from a few steps away, felt her own chest tighten with something easy and complicated — pride, and protectiveness, and the same, novel vertigo of realizing her family had just, potentially, gotten larger in a direction none of them could have predicted.

"Whatever you find," she said, stepping closer, "however this turns out — you know this family doesn't shrink to make room for another one. It just gets bigger."

i know, Dawn said, certain and warm. i think that might be my favorite thing about existing, so far. that there always seems to be room for more.

Xander hadn't said much, but he spoke now, something wry and affectionate in his voice. "For the record, if this kid turns out to be even half as dramatic as that old report makes him sound, I feel like we should probably prepare accordingly. Family reunions with reformed near-omnipotent troublemakers seem like exactly the kind of thing that goes sideways if nobody plans ahead."

"Noted," Fury said, dry. "Adding it to the list."

"Also," Faith added, deadpan, "gonna need somebody to clarify what 'juvenile' means on a scale where the guy can apparently rearrange whole planets for fun. Because if we're doing a family reunion, I want to know up front whether I'm buying a onesie or building a bunker."

"Both," Tony said, without missing a beat. "Get both. I'll expense it."

i do not think i require a onesie, Dawn put in, warm amusement threading through the words, clearly delighted to have found something to tease them back about. though i confess i am curious what one is.

"Don't ask Tony to explain it," Willow said. "You'll regret it."

"I would give an excellent explanation," Tony said, wounded.

"You would give a forty-minute explanation involving at least three unnecessary diagrams."

"That's not a genuine rebuttal, that's just a description of my strengths."

The household folded into easy, hopeful speculation after that, Dawn's quiet wonder at the thought of a brother spreading warm through the whole gathered evening, one more strange and delightful possibility folded into a year that had, by now, made a habit of exceeding everyone's expectations.
