Chapter 73

The Giles-Trelane correspondence became its own small, delightful fixture of the household over the following weeks, phone calls and in-person visits alike settling into a rhythm built substantially on literary sparring that left most of the rest of the family cheerfully lost.

"He quoted Prospero at me yesterday," Giles reported, to Xander, with the particular weary satisfaction of a man who'd found an opponent worthy of his effort. "Entirely unprompted, in the middle of describing an incident involving a rather unfortunate encounter with a Klingon vessel. 'We are such stuff as dreams are made on,' delivered with a straight face, immediately followed by a joke about torpedo trajectories."

"Did you have a comeback?"

"Naturally. One does not survive a Watcher's education without a reasonably deep well of Shakespeare to draw from." Giles allowed himself a small, satisfied smile. "I responded with Puck. 'Lord, what fools these mortals be.' He laughed for a full minute. I believe I've found the one being in this household capable of properly appreciating my particular sense of humor."

Trelane, arriving mid-conversation with his usual theatrical flourish, caught the tail end of it with evident delight. He speaks of me fondly. I'm touched. Truly. Do go on.

"I was merely noting your fondness for quoting a man who's been dead for centuries."

A man who understood theater a great deal better than most of your current century manages, if I'm honest. We have quite a lot in common, he and I. Both of us rather partial to a well-timed dramatic reveal.

"You're doing it right now," Faith observed, from across the room.

Naturally. Consistency is a virtue.

Dawn, watching the exchange with obvious warmth, leaned toward Wanda. "I think this might be my favorite thing he's found here. Not just family. An actual proper sparring partner."

"Everyone deserves one of those," Wanda agreed, smiling. "Glad he found his in Giles, of all people."

"Honestly," Xander said, "kind of glad too. Giles needed somebody who could keep up with him. Rest of us mostly just nod along and hope we're following."

The banter rolled on through the evening, Shakespeare and starship anecdotes tangling together into something that had become, remarkably quickly, one of the household's most reliable sources of easy delight — one more small, unexpected gift folded into a year that had, by now, made something of a habit of them.
