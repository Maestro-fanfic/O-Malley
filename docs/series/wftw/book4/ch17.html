Chapter 17

The signal changed shape on the ninth night, and this time it was Wanda who caught it first, sitting up straight in the middle of an otherwise ordinary dinner with an expression that emptied the table's conversation in half a second.

"It's reaching," she said, already moving toward the workshop, everyone trailing after her. "Not at us specifically. Just — reaching. The way you reach out before you've learned to talk yet, testing whether anything's there to reach back."

The monitor's readout had shifted, the slow accumulation Shuri had modeled now interspersed with sharp, searching pulses, structure folding into structure in a rhythm that looked, even to Charlie's untrained eye, less like data and more like an attempt.

"I need Wong," Wanda said, already pulling out her phone. "This is language-adjacent now, or trying to be, and he's spent more time than almost anyone alive studying how consciousness first learns to reach across a barrier it doesn't yet understand."

Wong arrived through a portal far less dramatic than Charlie expected, given everything Xander had once described about the man, and studied the readout with the unhurried focus of someone who'd seen stranger things and refused to be rushed by the urgency in the room around him.

"This is early," he said finally. "Very early. Whatever's forming hasn't found its own shape yet, let alone a shape it could use to speak to you specifically. But Wanda's right that it's reaching. That's meaningful. Things that are only growing don't reach. Reaching implies awareness, even unformed awareness, of something outside itself worth reaching toward."

"Can we reach back," Millie asked.

"Carefully. If at all." Wong considered the readout a long moment more. "I would not attempt direct contact yet. Not because it's dangerous, necessarily — I honestly don't know that it is. Because premature contact with something still forming its own sense of self can shape that self in ways none of us would be able to predict or undo. Patience isn't caution for its own sake here. It's the only responsible option."

Thor, listening from the doorway, finally spoke, something in his bearing shifting toward a decision he'd clearly been turning over for a while.

"I would like to call Valkyrie," he said. "Not for combat — I don't believe combat is remotely the shape of what we're facing. But she has stood closer to true cosmic uncertainty than nearly anyone I know, and survived it with her judgment intact. I would value her counsel on how a people prepares itself for something this large, this unprecedented, without either panicking or growing complacent."

"Call her," Fury said, without hesitation. "At this point I'm inclined to call anyone with relevant experience. We're well past the stage where extra caution costs us anything."

Valkyrie arrived the following afternoon, unbothered by the short notice, took in the assembled roster with a single long look, and let out a low whistle.

"Thor wasn't kidding about the scale of this," she said, dropping her bag by the door and immediately scanning the room for something to drink, settling for black coffee with visible disappointment. "Alright. Somebody catch me up on the cosmic newborn situation, and skip the parts where you all try to make it sound more dignified than it is. I've dealt with gods, I've dealt with watching an entire civilization actually end around me. I can handle a fussy baby, metaphorical or otherwise."

Something in the flat, practiced ease of that last line made Thor go quiet for a moment, and Charlie noticed it — the way Valkyrie said "watched a civilization end" the exact same way she'd say anything else, no weight added, no weight allowed to show. Nobody pushed on it.

"That's the spirit," Sam said, from the couch. "Mostly we've been running on 'stay calm and hope for the best,' if you want the honest summary."

"Works for me. I've built entire strategies on considerably less than that." Valkyrie surveyed the monitor readout with plain interest, then glanced sideways at Thor. "You didn't tell me it glows. That's a nice touch. Better than the last cosmic emergency you dragged me into, and that one had a genuinely excellent soundtrack, so it had a high bar to clear."

"I appreciate you noticing the improvement."

"I notice everything. It's why I'm still alive." She said it lightly, but something under it landed heavier than the joke intended, and she moved past it fast, the way Charlie was already learning was just how she handled anything that got too close to the actual weight of her history. "So what do we actually do while we wait for the universe to finish thinking."

"Mostly," Millie said, something almost like an honest smile finding her for the first time in days, "we keep doing exactly what we've been doing. Ordinary life, astonishing circumstances, side by side, for as long as it takes."

"I can work with that," Valkyrie said, and reached for the coffee pot like she'd already decided to make herself at home, though Charlie caught her eyes lingering a beat too long on the assembled, tightly-knit family before she looked away.

17.5. Chapter 17.5

Millie found Valkyrie on the porch a little after midnight, a bottle of something considerably stronger than the household's usual stock produced from somewhere Millie hadn't bothered asking about, the two of them the only ones still awake in a house that had finally, exhaustedly settled.

"You're not sleeping either," Millie said, settling into the chair beside her without waiting for an invitation.

"Don't really, much. Old habit." Valkyrie didn't offer more than that, and Millie didn't push, the old professional instinct recognizing exactly how much space to leave open. "You're doing the thing, by the way. The reading-people thing. Thor warned me about it."

"Occupational hazard. I don't always manage to turn it off."

"Yeah, well. Feel free to point it somewhere else tonight. I'm not really in the mood to be a case study." It came out sharper than she'd clearly intended, and she winced, faintly, at her own tone. "Sorry. Long few centuries. Don't take it personally."

"I won't." Millie let the silence sit a while, comfortable rather than pointed. "For what it's worth, I wasn't reading you for a diagnosis. Just noticed you flinched a little, back in the kitchen, when Thor mentioned the last cosmic emergency he dragged you into. Figured that wasn't really about the mission."

Valkyrie was quiet a long moment, turning the bottle in her hands.

"Lost my whole unit once," she said, finally, flat, the same practiced ease she used for everything that actually mattered. "Valkyries. All of us, sworn to the same throne, same duty, same everything. Got slaughtered by somebody who should've been on our side, and I was the only one who made it out. Spent a long time after that deciding the smartest move was never trusting anything that asked me to care again."

"That's a hard thing to carry alone."

"Wasn't alone, technically. Had a whole planet's worth of garbage and cheap liquor to keep me company." Valkyrie managed something close to a real, rueful laugh. "Took a stubborn Asgardian showing up and refusing to take no for an answer to shake me out of it. Rude of him, honestly. I had a perfectly good exile going."

Millie smiled at that, warm despite the weight underneath it. "Sounds like exactly what you needed, though. Whether you wanted it or not."

"Yeah." Valkyrie considered the field beyond the porch, Dawn's light pulsing soft and steady in the distance. "That's actually part of why I said yes to coming out here, if I'm honest. Watching something new get to start out loved instead of having to claw its way back to trusting anyone — feels like exactly the kind of thing worth showing up for. Even if it's not really my usual line of work."

"You'd be welcome to make it your line of work, if you wanted. This family's gotten pretty good at finding room for people who've earned a second kind of family."

Valkyrie glanced at her, something unguarded flickering across her face before the old easy deflection slid back into place. "I'll think about it. Don't go putting me on the payroll yet." She raised the bottle slightly, an old habit of a toast. "To showing up anyway. Rude Asgardians included."

"To showing up anyway," Millie agreed. Neither of them moved for a while after that, the night settling quiet and companionable around them, one more piece of hard-won trust finding its own slow way toward home.
