Chapter 13

"You called Jane," Thor said, when Bruce mentioned it almost in passing over breakfast, something in his voice shifting into a careful, hopeful neutrality that fooled nobody at the table.

"I called Jane," Bruce confirmed. "The resonance signature's honestly strange, Thor. Not strange like alien-technology strange, which I could probably muddle through eventually. Strange like it doesn't behave according to any physics I fully understand, and Jane's spent years working on exactly that category of problem. I wasn't going to not call the best astrophysicist I know because it might be awkward."

"I didn't say it would be awkward."

"You said it with your whole face."

Jane arrived that afternoon, travel-worn and visibly curious despite the short notice, and had her laptop open before she'd even finished the introductions, scanning the monitor's readout with the hungry focus of a scientist who'd just been handed a truly new kind of problem.

"Okay, this is incredible," she said, mostly to herself, tracing the spike pattern from the previous night. "This isn't gravitational. It's not electromagnetic, not in any conventional sense. It's — Bruce, this almost reads like the signature's carrying information, not just energy. Structured, not random."

"That's what I thought too. I didn't want to say it out loud until somebody else confirmed I wasn't reaching."

"You're not reaching." Jane looked up, taking in the room properly for the first time — the whiteboard, the assembled roster, the particular tension of a house mid-crisis. "Somebody want to catch me up properly? Thor's version on the flight over was mostly 'a family did something remarkable and now powerful people are deciding whether to reward or punish them for it,' which, no offense, Thor, is not a briefing."

"It was an accurate summary."

"It was three sentences."

"Accurate three sentences."

Marie walked her through the actual data while Jane listened with the particular stillness of someone filing away every detail for later use, occasionally interrupting with a question sharp enough that Marie had to actually stop and think before answering.

"This is honestly one of the best-documented first-contact-adjacent signal sets I've ever seen," Jane said finally. "I'd kill for a chance to actually study this properly, under better circumstances. Under these circumstances, I'd settle for helping you people not lose everything you've built before you get the chance to have this fight on fair terms."

*

T'Challa's call to Wakanda that evening was brief, and the response arrived far faster than protocol usually allowed, which told Charlie something about how seriously this was already being taken before M'Baku ever set foot through the gate.

He arrived imposing in a way that had nothing to do with size, though he had that too, taking in the assembled roster with an expression that gave away exactly nothing until he'd finished his own careful assessment of the room.

"So," he said, finally, to T'Challa, in a tone that carried the worn-in weight of an old argument neither of them needed to fully restate. "Wakanda involves itself in another outside power's business."

"Wakanda involves itself in a family's business, at their invitation, because the alternative to us being here is them facing this judgment with no advocate who's ever successfully argued this exact case before."

"You mean our case. Vibranium, and every empire that ever decided they were owed it." M'Baku's gaze swept the room, landing eventually on Millie. His voice dropped, sudden and grave. "Cross my king on this, and I will personally see to it that—" He let the silence hang a beat too long, watching Millie's face carefully. "—that he never lets me hear the end of it, because he will insist I stop threatening his allies within the first five minutes of meeting them. I'm kidding. Mostly. I don't say this to diminish your situation. I say it because I want you to understand exactly what kind of ally you have in my king. He argues from experience, not sentiment. That should reassure you more than it worries you."

"Noted," Millie said, carefully.

"Good." M'Baku dropped into an empty chair with the unhurried confidence of a man entirely comfortable being the most skeptical person in any room. "Now. Tell me everything, and don't soften it for my benefit. If I'm advising my king on whether this fight's worth Wakanda's name attached to it, I need the actual shape of the danger, not the version built to make me feel generous."

Watching from across the room, Fury cracked something close to a plain smile — the first genuine one all week.

"I like him," he said, to nobody in particular.

"Most people eventually do," T'Challa said. "It generally takes a good deal longer than an afternoon, however."

"I contain multitudes," M'Baku said, entirely unbothered, already reaching for the nearest data printout with the same hungry seriousness Jane had brought to the monitor readout an hour earlier, and the room, already crowded with gods and scientists and kings, made room for two more voices without much trouble at all.
