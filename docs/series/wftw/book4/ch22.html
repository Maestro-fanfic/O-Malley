Chapter 22

The signal changed again on a Tuesday, which felt to Charlie like exactly the kind of ordinary day the universe had no business picking for something this large, and this time there was no mistaking what was happening.

"It's not reaching anymore," Wanda said, already on her feet, already moving toward the door and the strip of open ground beyond it where the resonance always read strongest. "It's arriving. Something about to actually be present, not just gesturing toward presence."

The whole household followed her out, gathering at a careful distance Wong had specified days earlier, close enough to witness, far enough to not crowd whatever was about to happen.

Wanda stood alone at the center of the open ground, hands loose at her sides, the particular stillness of someone who'd spent days preparing for a moment she still couldn't fully predict.

The air didn't shimmer so much as it seemed to hold its breath, a pressure building that had nothing to do with weather, and Charlie noticed he was reaching for Clara's hand without quite deciding to, the whole gathered crowd going instinctively, reverently quiet.

Light gathered first — not harsh, not the violent crack of Thor's usual entrances, but something closer to dawn arriving out of sequence, soft and slow and entirely unhurried, pooling in the open ground until it took, gradually, the barest suggestion of shape.

"Oh," Millie said, quiet, one hand pressed to her mouth.

It wasn't a person, not exactly — more like the memory of a person, still deciding on its own edges, light and warmth folding into something that might, eventually, become recognizable as a face, as hands, as a shape the human eye could properly hold onto. It turned, slow and curious, taking in the gathered crowd with what could only be described as wonder, the same wide, searching quality Charlie remembered from watching his own baby cousin discover, for the first time, that the world contained more than just her own hands.

Wanda took a single careful step forward, and the light turned toward her immediately, fully, the way a plant turns toward the only source of warmth it's ever known.

"Hello," Wanda said, quiet, steady, her voice carrying easily across the hushed open ground. "You're not in danger. Nobody here means you any harm. We've been waiting for you, actually. Watching, and waiting, and hoping."

The light pulsed, gentle, searching, and for a moment nothing happened at all, the whole gathered crowd holding its breath together.

Then, slowly, carefully, the light reached out — not toward Wanda specifically this time, but outward, toward all of them, toward the house, the barn, the whole assembled, remarkable crowd of gods and scientists and family standing together in the open evening air — and Charlie felt something wash over him that he didn't have proper words for yet, easy and curious and achingly, impossibly gentle, the strange sensation of being seen, truly seen, by something experiencing the act of seeing for the very first time.

"It's not afraid," Wanda said, wonder plain in her voice now, all her careful preparation giving way to something closer to awe. "It's curious. It's — I think it's happy. I think this is the happiest thing I've ever felt from anything, anywhere."

Thor, beside Charlie, had gone very still, tears standing unashamed in his eyes.

"I have witnessed the birth of stars," he said, quiet, reverent. "I have never witnessed anything quite like this."

The light pulsed once more, gentle and slow, and somewhere in the gathered, hushed crowd, Cent began, very quietly, to cry — not from fear, not from grief, but from the sheer, overwhelming joy of watching something entirely new choose, in its very first moments of being, to look at all of them with nothing but wonder.
