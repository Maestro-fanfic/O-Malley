Chapter 108

The Keepers' next visit arrived in early winter, four months after Nadiya's naming, and this time Charlie noticed, watching the familiar pressure gather in the field, that Dawn's own light had gone quietly, unusually still beside him — as though she, too, sensed this visit carried more weight than the recent, casual check-ins had.

we have withheld something from you, the presence said, without preamble, none of its usual calm warmth softening the admission. not the existence of other designated earths — you have long understood that much, having walked two of them yourselves. what we have withheld is the scale.

"Tell us," Millie said, steady despite the sudden tension gathering in the field.

you have treated your three cases as rare. precious, and worth the no small amount of effort you gave them, but rare — three notable instances, found through careful searching, across three years. A pause, weighted with old, careful consideration. they are not rare. they are simply the only three your detection network has been capable of finding.

The gathered household went very still.

"You mean there are more," Fury said, slowly. "A lot more."

we believe the property that makes emergence possible is not unique to any single earth, nor to any small handful of them. it appears to be a quality of the type itself — something about earth, broadly, across however many versions exist, that responds to a threshold properly crossed. your own detection framework can only see what happens on earths you already have some means of reaching. the true number, across every designated earth bearing this quality, is very likely a good deal of. we do not have an exact count. we suspect even we do not have the full picture.

Dawn's light flared, something between wonder and profound unease moving through it. there are others like me. many others. right now.

very likely, yes. some supported, as you were. most, we suspect, far less fortunate — alone, frightened, with no family prepared the way yours was, no initiative built to help them through it.

Millie felt the full weight of the revelation settle over her, old protective instinct warring with something larger, more urgent. "Why tell us now? Why not from the beginning, once we understood other Earths existed at all?"

because the true scale would have been unbearable to carry, set against only three years of dearly bought capacity to help. we believed — we still believe — that revealing the full weight of it too early would have overwhelmed rather than empowered you. Something almost apologetic threaded through the vast, careful words. we may have been wrong to wait this long regardless. that is part of why we tell you now, rather than continuing to wait for a moment that felt more comfortable.

Ashgrove, processing the scope of it with visible effort, finally spoke. "How many Designated Earths are we actually able to reach? Realistically. With the resources this Initiative currently has."

a small fraction of the total, at present. wong's own capacity is substantial, but not infinite, and the coordination required across realities this distinct from one another has rarely been attempted at any scale larger than the individual cases you have already undertaken.

Charlie looked around the gathered household — three years of painstaking wisdom, an entire Initiative built from steady, careful love — and felt something enormous and urgent settle into shape alongside the fear.

"Then we figure out how to reach more," he said. "However hard it is. However much coordination it takes. If there's a true number out there, even a fraction of it, I don't think we get to just stay comfortable with three cases, knowing that."

Millie reached for Davy's hand, old resolve steadying into something new and a good deal larger.

"No," she agreed. "I don't think we do either."
