Chapter 129

Faith and Buffy both came this time, the three of them landing in a world of high alpine meadows, snow-capped peaks ringing the horizon in every direction under a sky so clear it looked unreal.

"Okay, this one's stunning," Faith said, turning slowly to take it all in. "Genuinely stunning. Different kind of stunning than the lake trip."

"Every trip's got its own kind, apparently," Buffy said. "I've stopped being surprised by how many kinds there are."

They spent the first day working through the meadow, cataloguing wildflowers in colors bright enough to rival anything from previous trips, and found the actual wildlife on the second morning — a herd of long-haired, goat-like creatures grazing at the meadow's edge, utterly unbothered as all three of them walked right up into the middle of the herd without a single animal so much as looking up from the grass.

"They're kind of majestic," Faith said, really charmed, reaching out to run a hand through one's shaggy coat while it kept right on grazing. "Didn't expect majestic. Expected weird, mostly."

"Most of it's weird and majestic at the same time, honestly," Xander said. "That's kind of the whole theme." He glanced at the nearest kid, small and curious, nosing at his sleeve without a trace of the herd's usual wariness. "Also, hey, baby one's friendly. Alan's gonna cry actual tears over this."

They spent the rest of the trip in easy, appreciative quiet, and by the third evening had fell into the kind of comfortable rhythm that came only from real trust between people who'd been through notably more together than any of them had expected when this whole strange life started, one new addition to the zoo's collection curled up contentedly in the makeshift carrier Willow had insisted they start bringing along for exactly this kind of trip.

"This is a good one," Buffy said, watching the last light fade off the peaks. "After everything with Fury, with the report, with all of it — this is exactly the reset I needed."

"Same," Faith agreed. "Feels good, having this be normal now. Having somewhere to just be quiet together."

"Yeah," Xander said, meaning it fully. "Feels like the actual point of all this, honestly. Not just the animals. This part too."

They made it home with goat photos, wildflower samples, and a fresh reminder for all three of them why this strange, complicated life was worth every bit of trouble that came bundled with it.

Willow was waiting up, curled in the doorway with a blanket around her shoulders. "Well?"

"Best one in a while," Buffy confirmed. "Mountains, goats, genuine peace. Ten out of ten."

"I'm so glad," Willow said, and pulled them both in before either had even finished setting their bags down.

Buffy caught up with Faith on patrol that same week, the two of them falling into step together along the cemetery's back row, comfortable in the kind of quiet that came from years of trusting someone at your back.

"Can I say something," Buffy said, eventually, "and you tell me if I'm wrong, and we never bring it up again if I am?"

Faith's steps didn't change, but something in her shoulders went carefully still. "Sure."

"That lake trip. You watched me and Xander for a second longer than you needed to, and it wasn't jealous. It was something else." Buffy kept her eyes forward, giving Faith room not to have to look at her while she answered. "I'm not saying that to put you on the spot. I'm saying it because if I'm right, I don't want you sitting on it alone, wondering if it's even safe to say out loud. It is. Whatever you end up deciding to do with it."

Faith was quiet a long moment, a stake turning over slowly in her hand. "You're not wrong."

"Okay."

"That's it? Just 'okay'?"

"For now, yeah. You don't owe me the rest of it tonight. I just wanted you to know I noticed, and that noticing isn't the same thing as it being weird." Buffy bumped her shoulder against Faith's, easy, familiar. "Take whatever time you need. We're not going anywhere."

Faith let out a breath she hadn't quite realized she'd been holding. "Yeah. Okay. Might take me a minute."

"Take your minute. I've got nowhere else to be."

They finished the patrol in comfortable quiet after that, nothing else needing saying just yet, but something had shifted regardless — a door left open rather than closed, waiting for whenever Faith was actually ready to walk through it.

129.5. Chapter 129.5

Faith waited until the gear was unpacked and the evening had settled into its ordinary quiet before she said anything, which in itself told Xander something was coming — Faith rarely waited for a good moment when a blunt one would do just as well, and the fact that she'd clearly picked this one meant she'd been carrying it a while.

"So," she said, dropping onto the couch across from where the three of them had settled, "I've been thinking about something for a few weeks now, and I'd rather just say it than keep circling it."

"Okay," Buffy said, carefully neutral, the way she got when she could tell something mattered before knowing what it was.

"You three. This thing you've got." Faith gestured vaguely between them, not unkindly. "I've been watching it for a while now. Longer than you probably realized. The way you are with each other. It's not subtle, for the record, in case anybody was under the impression it was subtle."

"We know it's not subtle," Willow said, a little wary.

"I'm not bringing it up to be weird about it. I'm bringing it up because I've been sitting with something since that lake trip, and every week since it's just gotten harder to keep sitting on it instead of saying it out loud." Faith held Xander's eyes for a second, then Buffy's, then Willow's, steady despite the vulnerability underneath it. "I want in. Not — I'm not saying that to be dramatic, or because I think I'm owed anything. I'm saying it because I've thought about it a long time now, and I'd rather be honest and get told no than keep sitting on it and wondering."

The room went quiet for a moment, nobody rushing to fill it.

"Can I ask why now?" Xander said, gently, not a rejection, just genuine curiosity.

"Honestly? The hammer thing helped. Figuring out I was worth something on my own terms, not just compared to somebody else." Faith shrugged, though the gesture cost her more than she let show. "And that lake trip, weeks back. Watching you three just be easy with each other, the way you are, and realizing I wanted that. Not instead of anything. Just — also. If that's something you'd even want. I've caught myself watching it happen more times than I probably should've since then, honestly."

"I noticed you noticing," Buffy admitted. "Wasn't sure what it meant, exactly. Didn't want to assume."

"I've thought about it too," Willow said, surprising herself a little with how easily it came out. "Not, like, actively planning anything. Just — noticed. That you fit, somehow. In a way that made sense before I ever tried to name it."

"Same," Buffy said, quiet. "I didn't want to say anything, because I didn't want to make it weird if it was just me reading into something."

"It wasn't just you," Xander said, something easing in his own chest at finally admitting it out loud. "I've thought about it too. Didn't know how to bring it up, though, given everything else you've had going on lately."

Faith let out a breath she'd clearly been holding since she sat down. "Okay. That's — truly more of a yes than I let myself expect, walking in here."

"It's not a yes to anything specific yet," Buffy said, careful but warm. "It's a yes to actually talking about it properly. Figuring out what it'd even look like, together, instead of just deciding it in one conversation on a couch."

"I can work with that. I wasn't expecting one conversation to solve it anyway." Faith managed a real smile, some of the tension finally leaving her shoulders. "Just needed to say it. Sitting on it for weeks was getting old."

"For what it's worth," Willow said, "I'm glad you said it. All of it. The wanting-in part and the honest-about-why part both."

"Yeah," Xander agreed. "Me too."

They talked well into the night after that — careful, unhurried, nobody rushing toward an answer bigger than the conversation could actually hold in one sitting, and by the time Faith finally headed home, something had shifted in the room that none of them fully had words for yet, but all of them recognized as real.
