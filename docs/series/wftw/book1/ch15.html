Chapter 15

Cordelia found out about the sloth secondhand, which she considered a personal failure, since she generally prided herself on knowing everything worth knowing at Sunnydale High a full four hours before anyone else did.

"There's an actual car-sized sloth. At the zoo. Right now." She cornered Willow at her locker, practically vibrating at having been scooped on a story this good. "Someone said Xander brought it home himself. Right onto the football field, security and everything."

"He tranq'd it and everything. It's real. It's got its own enclosure now, they broke ground on it within the week."

"That is not better, Willow, that is somehow worse. Why does Xander Harris keep bringing home bigger and bigger animals every time he disappears for the weekend?"

"I think it's just what was there. It's not like he's escalating on purpose."

"It feels like escalating on purpose. Last month it was birds. Now it's an actual sedated prehistoric sloth in my zoo. What's next month, a dinosaur?"

"Statistically unlikely, dinosaurs have been gone a lot longer than ground sloths, but I can't actually rule it out."

Cordelia stared at her. "That was supposed to be a joke."

"I know. I just got excited about the taxonomy." Willow shrugged, unbothered. "It happens."

"Separate topic," Cordelia said, regrouping. "Is it true Faith's been staying at Buffy's?"

"For a while now, yeah."

"Since when does Buffy's mom just adopt people?"

"Since Joyce doesn't really take no for an answer once she's decided to help somebody, and Faith needed somewhere to stay." Willow closed her locker. "It's not really a whole story. She just kind of lives there now."

"Everything in this friend group is 'not really a whole story' until it turns out to be an enormous story two weeks later," Cordelia said. "I've learned not to trust the phrase."

"Fair, honestly."

Faith arrived at that exact moment, sliding into the space beside Willow like she'd been listening from one row of lockers over the whole time, which she probably had.

"Somebody talking about me?" Faith asked.

"Cordelia wanted to know about the living situation."

"It's not a whole story," Faith said, flat, daring anyone at the table to push further. "Needed a place. Joyce had one. Not everything's gotta come with a plot."

"Everything about you people is gossip-worthy," Cordelia said. "That's sort of the problem."

"Then find better gossip," Faith said, "because you're not getting mine."

The bell rang before the conversation could go anywhere else, and the hallway filled with the ordinary noise of a school day continuing to happen — lockers slamming, somebody's radio playing too loud two hallways over, the general low hum of four hundred teenagers pretending they'd done the reading. Faith fell into step beside Willow toward the cafeteria, in no visible hurry despite the bell, and had to steer them both around a senior couple stretched hand-to-hand across the width of the hall, walking slow enough to block traffic on principle, followed immediately by a second couple doing the four-legged-beast thing — his arm locked around her waist from behind, her hands over his, both of them steering like a single ungainly organism straight down the center of the hallway.

"Every single day," Faith muttered, once they'd cleared both of them. "That's the third one this week. And Denise and Marcus have been doing their goodbye thing by the trophy case again, which means we've got maybe ninety more seconds before the bell rings on them too and everybody in a four-locker radius has to watch the whole production."

"I think it's sweet."

"It's an obstruction. There's a difference. Also I've heard their whole 'no, I love you more' bit through a closed door. Twice. It does not improve with repetition."

"She's gonna tell everybody about the sloth by lunch," Faith said. "You know that, right?"

"Oh, absolutely. I give it forty minutes, tops."

"You could've asked her not to."

"Wouldn't have mattered. Also, honestly, it's not really a secret. Xander tells everyone anyway. I think he wants people to know he actually caught it."

"He definitely wants people to know he caught it. He's been rehearsing the story since it happened. I caught him practicing the two-dart part in the mirror this morning."

"He does not practice in the mirror."

"I watched him do it, Red. Full gestures and everything. Really committed to the part where his hands were shaking on the second reload."

They reached the cafeteria to find Xander already holding court at their usual table, one hand gesturing wide to indicate some dimension of the sloth story that apparently required physical demonstration, Jesse leaning in with rapt attention despite having presumably heard some version of this already that morning.

"—and it just went down slow, like watching a building deflate," Xander was saying, "which honestly, after two days of convincing myself I actually trusted that dosage number, felt like the longest ten minutes of my entire life."

"Told you," Faith murmured to Willow, and the two of them slid into the remaining seats just in time to catch the part of the story where the second dart came up, which Xander delivered with the reverence most people reserved for actual combat.
