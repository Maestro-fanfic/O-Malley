Chapter 31

The universe, all three of them decided within the first hour, had apparently taken Xander's manifesting seriously and then decided to be funny about it, twice over, by dropping company into the joke too.

It was shade, technically. Dense rainforest canopy, green in every direction, humidity thick enough to chew, and absolutely, categorically, the rainforest with the ants.

"Okay," Xander said, standing very still the second he recognized the terrain. "Okay, I know exactly what this is, and I would like to formally request a redo."

"What is it," Buffy asked, already wary.

"Supercolony. Ants. Miles of them, moving in columns. Learned this the hard way, solo, more than once."

"How do we avoid it?" Willow asked, notebook already out despite the warning.

"Very, very carefully. Check the ground before every single step. I mean it."

They checked the ground. They checked it religiously, in fact, for a solid day and a half, right up until the trouble found them anyway on day two, in the form of a column none of them saw until Buffy was already three steps into the middle of it.

"Nope," Buffy said, already moving, "nope, nope, NOPE—"

"RUN," Xander confirmed, unnecessarily, since everyone was already running.

They ran together, not fast so much as steady and purposeful, aimed at the nearest water Xander remembered from his initial scan, and reached the shallow stream in a scrambling, undignified rush, all three of them going in without slowing down to check the depth.

"I have ants in my BOOT," Willow announced, mid-splash, her scientific composure completely, finally failing her. "There are ants INSIDE my BOOT."

"We all have ants somewhere," Buffy said, sitting hip-deep in the stream, checking a handful of bites along one ankle. "This is a group ant experience now."

"I didn't consent to a group ant experience."

"None of us did," Xander said. "That's kind of the whole theme of today."

They sat there a solid ten minutes, cursing insects collectively, loud enough to startle a bird out of a nearby tree, before finally dragging themselves out to assess the damage, Xander pulling Willow into a brief, fully unhelpful hug that just got both of them wetter, and pressing a kiss to Buffy's temple for good measure once she'd finished counting her own bites.

"Okay," Willow said, once she'd emptied her boot with visible ceremony. "For the record, I've read papers about supercolonies. Reading about it and personally being invaded by one are very different experiences."

"Welcome to fieldwork," Xander said. "It's rarely as dignified as the papers make it sound."

"I hate that you're right about that."

They spent the rest of the trip walking with the deliberate, ground-scanning caution of three people who had, collectively, finally learned something, and got their actual reward on the final afternoon — a bird none of them had seen before, brilliant blue-green, perched low enough for a genuinely good photo, entirely unbothered by their presence the way nearly everything out here seemed to be.

"Worth it," Buffy declared, watching Willow get the shot. "Barely. But worth it."

"I'm still mad about the ants," Xander said. "For the record. Officially, collectively mad, on behalf of all three of us." He held a hand out slowly toward the branch, and the bird hopped onto his fingers without any visible concern at all, weighing next to nothing. "Also, hey, small and cooperative. Alan's getting a new arrival."

"Seconded," Willow said.

"Seconded," Buffy agreed, and the bird, completely unaware of the diplomatic consensus reached in its honor, simply kept perching, unbothered, while all three of them counted the trip a qualified success by the time the jump pulled them home — ant bites all around, one honestly beautiful photo, and a newly shared, thoroughly earned respect for watching where they put their feet.
