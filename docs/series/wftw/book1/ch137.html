Chapter 137

Buffy came along this time, both of them wanting a genuine break before the convergence of visitors currently scheduled to descend on the household over the following weeks.

The world was highland moor, purple heather stretching in every direction under a sky heavy with slow-moving clouds, wind carrying a chill that hinted at snow not far off.

"Okay, this one's moody," Buffy said, pulling her jacket tighter. "Very moody-moor energy."

"Very moody-moor energy that's apparently also home to something interesting," Xander said, spotting movement in the heather. "Look."

Small, hare-like creatures bolted at their approach, quick and skittish in a way that made both of them laugh at how thoroughly they'd startled the local wildlife just by existing.

"Okay, they're fast," Buffy said, watching one vanish into the heather. "Genuinely fast."

"Everything out here seems built for speed. Guess the wind explains that."

They spent the trip working the moor's edges, cataloguing what wildlife they could get close enough to properly document, and by the third evening had eased into the easy, appreciative quiet that came from a trip asking nothing complicated of either of them.

"This is good," Buffy said, watching the last light fade over the heather. "After everything coming up — Fury, the sorcerers, whoever's coming from Cent's family's people at Home — this is exactly the reset I needed."

"Yeah. Same, honestly. Feels like these quiet ones matter even more right now."

"They do." She looked over at him. "You ready for all of it? Everyone converging at once?"

"Getting there. Feels less overwhelming every time I actually sit with it instead of just reacting to the next new arrival."

"Good strategy. Seems to keep working for you."

"Yeah," Xander said, meaning it fully. "Yeah, I think it actually does."

They made it home with hare photos and windburned cheeks, carrying exactly the grounding they needed before the really enormous few weeks ahead.

Willow kissed Buffy hello, then Xander, already tugging them both toward the kitchen. "Come tell me while I heat up food, I haven't eaten since you left."

"Quiet moor, fast hares, zero complications," Buffy said, letting herself be towed along. "Exactly what we needed."

"I'm glad," Willow said, already pulling plates down.

137.5. Ilsa: Preparing to Cross

Ilsa chose her team carefully, weighing capability against the strict resource limits Osei had made clear weren't merely bureaucratic caution but genuine, hard-won necessity, spending two full days reviewing personnel files in the small, cramped office that served as her department's headquarters, coffee going cold beside her more than once as she worked.

She chose Petra first, a field biologist with considerable experience assessing unfamiliar biomes quickly and safely — useful, given how little anyone actually knew about current conditions on the tame side beyond Miriam's brief, urgent report, and useful too for the calm Petra brought to unfamiliar environments, a steadiness Ilsa had personally witnessed twice before on significantly smaller assignments.

Her second choice took longer. She eventually settled on Dov, young for the assignment but sharp, with a gift for languages and cultural assessment that Ilsa suspected would matter more than any of them currently appreciated, given how little Home's current generation actually understood about the tame side's present-day culture — decades of disconnection having left even their most current records badly out of date.

"Three of us," Petra said, reviewing the assignment brief with visible unease, papers spread across the small conference table where the team had gathered for their first formal briefing. "Against whatever's actually happening over there."

"Three of us, plus Miriam, plus whatever local allies she's cultivated," Ilsa corrected. "We're not going in blind, and we're not going in alone. Just carefully."

"Carefully. Right." Petra didn't sound fully reassured, flipping through the brief's pages a second time as if additional information might materialize on a closer look. "Do we know anything more about the scale of this instability?"

"Only what Miriam's reported, and her own instruments are old, decades out of proper calibration. We'll know more once we're actually there." Ilsa checked the small kit they'd been allotted — instruments, medical supplies, nothing that would draw undue attention on a world she understood to be far more paranoid about strangers than Home's own currently was. "I want us focused on assessment first. Understanding the shape of this before we make any recommendations back to the Council."

"And if it's worse than we think?"

"Then we report that too, honestly, and let Home decide what additional resources we actually need to commit." Ilsa met Petra's eyes, holding the look until she was certain the point had properly landed. "We are not going to repeat old mistakes. If this requires more than three people, we ask for more people. Pride cost us everything once. I won't let it cost us again, not on my watch."

Dov, quiet through most of this, finally spoke, turning his own copy of the brief over in his hands. "Do you think Miriam's family will actually trust us? Given how long she's been disconnected from the mission?"

"I don't know," Ilsa admitted. "I know she chose transparency this time, when she easily could have chosen silence again. That has to count for something."

"I hope so. I'd like this to go well. Not just for the mission. For her, too. It sounds like she's spent a long time carrying this alone."

"That's a generous thought," Ilsa said, something in her expression softening slightly. "Hold onto it. We'll need generous thoughts, probably, more than we'll need instruments, before this is finished."

Ilsa closed the kit, the weight of the assignment settling properly now that preparation had given way to the actual reality of what came next. "We leave within the week. Get what rest you can. Once we're through, I doubt any of us will get much of it for a while."

Petra hesitated at the door on her way out. "Ilsa. For what it's worth — I trust your read on this. If you say careful is the right approach, I believe you."

"Thank you. I'll try to earn that, the whole way through."

"You already have. That's why I said yes to this in the first place."

The three of them dispersed to their final preparations after that, each carrying, in their own way, the weight of representing a civilization that had already lost almost everything once, now venturing back out into a wider, stranger reality than any of them had ever expected to actually witness firsthand — a small, careful team, stepping forward on behalf of people who could not afford, this time, for anything to go wrong.
