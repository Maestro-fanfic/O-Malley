Chapter 49

Giles found the reference four days later, buried in a text he'd nearly given up checking, and called Faith to the Magic Box the moment he'd confirmed it wasn't a false match.

"Cambion summoning," he said, sliding the open book across the counter before she'd even finished sitting down. "The symbol style matches a specific regional variant, used historically to call forth entities that are neither fully demon nor fully human. Dangerous. Not catastrophic, typically, but dangerous."

Faith stared at the page. "Neither fully demon nor fully human. That's a great category. Really specific. Really not vague at all."

"I didn't invent the terminology."

"I'm not blaming you, I'm just saying whoever did invent it really phoned in the naming convention."

Faith leaned over the page, studying the illustration beside the text — a symbol close enough to the mural's central mark that her stomach dropped a little just recognizing it. "That's it. That's the same shape."

"I believe so, yes. Which gives us significantly more to work with than we had yesterday." Giles removed his glasses, cleaning them slowly. "Cambion summonings require significant preparation — the mural itself, the accumulating marks, all of it consistent with someone building toward a specific ritual date, likely tied to a particular lunar or calendar alignment."

"So we've got time. Maybe."

"Maybe. I'd like to narrow the timeline further before we assume anything comfortable." Giles flipped forward several pages. "The text suggests these rituals traditionally align with specific astronomical events. If I can determine which one applies here, we may have an actual date to work against, rather than simply waiting for the mural to finish itself."

"What do we do once we've got a date?"

"That depends heavily on what we're dealing with. Some cambion entities are relatively benign once summoned, bound loosely to their summoner's will. Others are considerably less cooperative." Giles met her eyes. "I won't pretend I know which version we're facing yet. I'd rather tell you honestly that I don't know than offer false comfort."

"Appreciate the honesty. Genuinely." Faith looked back at the symbol, turning it over in her memory against everything she'd already catalogued from the mural. "So next step's finding the date."

"Next step is finding the date, yes. I'll need a few more days with the text. In the meantime, I'd like you to keep a closer eye on the mural than you have been — not engaging, simply observing, but documenting anything that suggests the timeline's accelerating."

"Already planned on it." Faith straightened up, notebook already coming out of her jacket. "This is actually starting to feel like something. Not just a hunch anymore. An actual thing with an actual name."

"It's very much an actual thing," Giles agreed. "And I want you to know — finding this reference took far longer than I'd have liked, largely because the sketch you provided was detailed enough that I kept second-guessing my own less precise memory of the relevant texts. Your work made this discovery possible, not merely convenient."

"You're just saying that."

"I generally don't say things I don't mean, Faith. You've likely noticed that about me by now."

She had, actually, which was part of what made the compliment land harder than it might have from anyone else in her life up to this point. "Yeah. Noticed."

"Good." Giles closed the book carefully, marking the page. "Go home. Get some rest. Tomorrow we start narrowing down a date, and I suspect neither of us is going to enjoy how close it might turn out to be."
