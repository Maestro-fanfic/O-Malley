Chapter 52

Giles called with the date on a Wednesday evening, and Faith knew from the first word of his voice on the phone that she wasn't going to like the answer.

"Nine days," he said, without preamble. "The lunar alignment the text describes falls in nine days. I've checked it against three separate sources now. I'm confident in the timing."

"Nine days." Faith sat down heavily on the porch steps, phone cord stretched taut behind her. "That's not a lot of time."

"It's more than we had yesterday, when we didn't have a date at all. I'd rather have nine days of preparation than none." Giles' voice carried a steadiness he was clearly working hard to maintain. "I want you here tomorrow, and every day until then. We have notably more to figure out than just the date."

"Like what."

"Like what we're actually walking into. Whether this is a solo practitioner or something larger. Whether the entity, once summoned, can be stopped, contained, or must simply be endured until it chooses to leave on its own. I don't have those answers yet, and I won't pretend otherwise."

"But we've got nine days to find them."

"We've got nine days to find as many of them as possible, yes." A pause. "Faith, I want to say something, and I want you to actually hear it rather than deflecting the way you usually do when things get sincere."

She braced herself out of habit. "Okay."

"You found this. Weeks ago, before anyone else had any idea something was building. If you hadn't been walking that block every single night, hadn't noticed a pattern most people would have dismissed as nothing, we would have had considerably less warning than nine days. Possibly none at all." His voice was steady, certain. "Whatever happens over the next nine days, I want you to understand that the time we have is entirely because of you."

Faith didn't say anything for a moment, throat tighter than she wanted to examine too closely.

"Thanks," she managed finally. "For saying that. And for not making it weird by, like, giving me a whole speech about it."

"I believe I just gave you a whole speech about it."

"Yeah, well. A short one. I'll allow it."

"Generous of you." Something in his voice eased slightly, the tension of the day's discovery giving way, briefly, to something warmer. "Get some rest tonight if you can. Tomorrow we start working in earnest, and I suspect neither of us is sleeping particularly well between now and the ninth day regardless of how much preparation we manage."

"Speak for yourself. I sleep great during a crisis. It's the boring Tuesdays that keep me up."

"That's a deeply concerning thing to be true about you."

"Yeah, well, add it to the list." Faith let the joke fade before adding, quieter, "But we'll be ready. Whatever this is. We'll be ready."

"I believe we will," Giles said. "Goodnight, Faith."

"Night."

She sat on the porch steps a while longer after hanging up, counting nine days forward on her fingers like that might make the number feel less immediate, and found, underneath the fear that had settled in her chest the moment Giles said the word nine, something that felt almost like readiness — not confidence exactly, but the steady certainty that comes from having faced enough impossible things by now to know that impossible didn't automatically mean unbeatable.
