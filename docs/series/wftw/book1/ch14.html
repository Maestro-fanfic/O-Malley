Chapter 14

By the next afternoon, once Alan's crew had come and gone and the yard had finally stopped being a small-scale zoological event, there was actually time to sit down and do the thing properly — Xander considerably more sunburned than he'd left three days earlier, grinning like a man who'd been sitting on good news with nobody but a very calm, very unconscious sloth to tell it to.

"Okay, NOW show me the actual photos," Willow said, materializing at his side before he'd even finished stretching out a cramped shoulder. "Yesterday was all sedation logistics and Alan yelling measurements at a clipboard. I want the field notes. Properly."

"Patience was never your strong suit." Xander dug the camera out of his bag with the reverence of a man handling something sacred anyway. "Fine. Witnessed standing up, like it deserves."

Willow's whole face changed the second the first photo came up. "Show me. Show me all of them."

He pulled up the four photos on the little screen, and Willow made a sound that wasn't quite a word, more of a general noise of scientific joy, as she scrolled through them twice.

"This is huge. Xander, this is genuinely huge. Look at the claw length relative to the forelimb, that's — okay, I need the actual measurements, please tell me you wrote down actual measurements and not just 'big.'"

"Half a notebook page. Stride length, height against a sapling, log damage radius, the works." He handed it over like a man passing off a trophy. "Took two full days of just sitting near it before I trusted a dosage number enough to actually pull the trigger. It looked at me the whole time like it couldn't quite be bothered to care."

"That's so validating," Faith said with a grin, dropping into the conversation from wherever she'd been lurking near the fridge. "Even the prehistoric wildlife's unimpressed by you."

"I choose to interpret it as the sloth respecting my personal space."

"Sure. Very respectful. Very mutual respect between man and ten-thousand-year-extinct megafauna."

"Wait, so you actually caught it?" Jesse asked, appearing out of nowhere the second the word 'sloth' hit the air, the way he always did — clearly hearing the full version for the first time. "Nobody told me you caught it. I heard 'sloth' and 'Xander's yard' secondhand and just assumed disaster."

"Not just tried," Xander said. "Two tranq darts, and it's getting the biggest enclosure the zoo's ever built once Alan's crew finishes it — should be done within the week, knowing him. I want it clearly on record that I thought about NOT trying it, given it's the size of a car and I have exactly one working spine, and did it anyway."

"Show-off."

"Realist who got lucky on the dosage. There's a difference, and the difference is whether or not my ribcage survived the weekend."

Willow was still scrolling through the notebook page, lips moving slightly as she did some quick math. "If these measurements are even close to accurate, this isn't just a big sloth. This might be bigger than any ground sloth species we have fossil records for. Like, actually new-to-science bigger."

"New to science, old to that world," Xander said. "It's been chilling out there for ten thousand years with nothing to bother it. Probably the most well-adjusted creature in either universe."

"I want to see it," Willow said, already mentally packing a field kit.

"Absolutely not," Xander and Buffy said, at the exact same time, from opposite sides of the kitchen, Buffy crossing over to loop an arm around Willow's shoulders as a kind of physical veto, the gesture equal parts affectionate and immovable.

"I wasn't actually going to ask to go."

"You were about thirty seconds from asking to go."

"I was going to ease into it," Willow admitted. "Build a case first."

"There's no case. It's an empty world with zero cell service and apparently at least one thing bigger than the sloth moving around in the dark, because something walked past my campsite the second night and I never did find out what."

The kitchen went briefly quiet at that.

"You didn't mention the mystery night thing," Faith said.

"I'm mentioning it now. It was probably nothing. Probably some other big, unbothered herbivore doing its own thing at three in the morning." Xander shrugged, though the shrug came a half-second slower than his usual ones. "Anyway. Good weekend. Great weekend, honestly. Bag's got the usual haul too — grabbed a few ground-nesting birds on the way back through the grassland, figured the zoo people would want something they can actually put on display without needing a bigger enclosure budget than the entire city has."

"You're an entire pipeline," Jesse said with a laugh. "A one-man wildlife pipeline. I bet you could smuggle anything through customs at this point, given the practice."

"I don't go through customs, Jesse, I materialize behind the football field."

"Even better. No paper trail. You're basically a criminal mastermind who happens to only steal animals nobody's ever heard of."

"I contain multitudes," Xander said, fully serious, already heading for the stairs with his notebook and his bag, clearly intent on sleeping for approximately fourteen hours before doing anything else productive with his life.

"Wait, what kind of birds," Willow called after him.

"Ask me tomorrow. I've been awake since a sun that isn't the same sun as this one and I would like eight of my fourteen hours before anybody asks me anything with the word 'species' in it."

Nobody argued with that, mostly because it seemed, on reflection, like an entirely reasonable request from a man who'd just spent three days alone with a car-sized sloth and something unidentified in the dark.

14.5. Leaving Town

Angel found Giles in the library the following evening, patrol notes spread across the table between them same as most evenings lately, the quiet, practiced rhythm of two people who'd worked together long enough not to need much conversation to do it well.

"I'm leaving Sunnydale," he said, without preamble, because there wasn't really a graceful way to lead into it and he'd never been much good at pretending otherwise.

Giles set down his pen. "I see. May I ask why?"

"Not because of anything here. Nothing's wrong." Angel shifted, restless in the way he got when a feeling needed more room than his usual stillness allowed. "It's just time. I've been in one place longer than I let myself stay most years, and staying too long in comfortable stops mattering to me if it stops meaning something. There's still a lot of ground I haven't covered. People I haven't found yet who need finding."

"The atonement," Giles said, not a question.

"The atonement. Doesn't stop just because Sunnydale's been good to me." A pause, something almost rueful crossing his face. "Might be part of the problem, honestly. It's been good to me. Easy to forget why I started walking in the first place when a place starts feeling like it could be home."

Giles studied him a long moment, the kind of look that weighed more than it said outright. "You've been really useful here. Backup Faith and Buffy could rely on. I confess I'll be sorry to lose that."

"They don't need me the way they used to. Faith's got a whole house behind her now. Buffy's got Xander and Willow and whatever this whole strange, extended family's turned into. I was useful when things were thinner around here. They're not thin anymore." Angel's mouth curved, brief and genuine. "That's a good problem. Best kind, honestly."

"When do you leave?"

"Tomorrow, I think. No sense drawing it out." He rose, restless energy finally finding somewhere to go, and crossed toward the stacks, pausing at the door long enough to glance back. "Tell Faith and Buffy for me? I'm not much for goodbyes. I'll say it badly if I try to say it in person, and I'd rather they remember something better than that."

"I'll tell them," Giles said, and meant it, watching him go quietly, understanding better than most the pull of unfinished penance and the cost of trying to talk someone out of it. "Angel. Wherever the road takes you — I hope you find what you're looking for."

"Yeah," Angel said, already half into the shadows of the stacks, voice carrying back soft. "Me too."

Faith took it about as well as Giles expected, which was to say she didn't say much of anything for a full day afterward, and threw herself into patrol with a focus that had less to do with vampires and notably more to do with not sitting still long enough to feel the shape of one more person leaving. Buffy took it better, or at least louder, a long, indignant phone-tag session with Giles that ended in grudging acceptance rather than actual peace with it. Neither of them saw him off. He'd asked Giles to make sure of that too, and Giles, understanding exactly why, made sure.
