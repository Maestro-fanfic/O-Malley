Chapter 150

The first tear opened over the Sunnydale water tower at 2 a.m., a jagged seam of white light that hung in the air like something had reached in and simply pulled reality apart at the stitching, static crackling audibly across every radio and television within a half-mile radius, and by the time Willow's instruments screamed loud enough to wake the whole household, two more had already opened across town.

"Okay!" Xander shouted, already pulling on boots, adrenaline making his hands clumsy on the laces. "Okay, that's — that's happening, that's actually happening now, we're doing this now, apparently, no warm-up, just straight into it—"

'This is fine,' he thought, not believing a single word of it. 'This is a completely normal Tuesday night activity. Lace the boots. Don't think about the tear in reality currently visible from your bedroom window. Lace the boots, Harris, you can panic properly once your shoes are on, there's a whole system to this.'

"You're narrating," Faith said, already moving past him toward the door, hammer in hand, weirdly steady given everything.

"I narrate when I'm terrified, this is a documented pattern, you've all seen it—"

"Then narrate faster and walk at the same time."

They converged on the epicenter site within twenty minutes — a stretch of woods behind the old drive-in, unremarkable until you accounted for every reading from every instrument all pointing to this exact patch of ground — headlights cutting through predawn dark as three separate vehicles arrived within moments of each other, everyone spilling out already moving, already working, months of preparation collapsing into pure, focused motion. They found the seam already visible even from a distance, a vertical tear in the air itself, edges rippling like heat distortion except far more wrong, a low, subsonic hum pressing against Xander's teeth the closer they got.

"That's the one," Ilsa confirmed, instruments already out, voice tight but controlled. "That's the epicenter tear. If we can reinforce this one, the others should stabilize in response."

"Should," Strange repeated.

"Should," Ilsa agreed. "I won't pretend to certainty we don't actually have."

"Comforting," Xander muttered.

"I told you honesty wouldn't always feel good," Giles said, arriving with an armful of materials for Strange's wards.

They set up quickly, positions falling into the rehearsed pattern — Faith at the center with the counter-working, Strange and Ilsa flanking with sorcery and gate-tech respectively, Thor and Fury holding the perimeter against whatever might come through before containment took hold, Xander positioned at Giles' insistence directly behind Faith, "for reasons I confess I can't fully articulate, beyond a persistent instinct that your presence matters here." Cent took up a position beside Petra, helping monitor the secondary readings with a steadiness that surprised even her, given everything, while Dov worked the perimeter's edge alongside Thor, translating shouted instructions between Ilsa's technical readings and Giles' half-remembered Watcher terminology fast enough that nobody lost a critical second to confusion.

"Really specific instructions, Giles."

"I did warn you my confidence in this plan wasn't absolute."

The tear pulsed, once, hard enough that all of them staggered, and something on the other side of it — vast, formless, more felt than seen — pressed against the opening with a weight that made Xander's teeth ache.

"Now!" Ilsa shouted.

Faith began the working, voice steady despite everything, hammer held ready, and Strange's wards flared to life around her in patterns of gold light, Ilsa's gate technology humming an answering frequency beneath it all.

"I can feel it pushing," Faith gritted out, hand tightening on the hammer's grip. "It's not like the ritual. It's bigger. So much bigger."

"Hold," Thor called, from the perimeter, voice carrying easy authority even now. "You are holding it. Trust that."

"Easy for you to say, you're not the one—"

"I absolutely am the one, I have held much larger things than this, and I promise you it is fully a matter of belief as much as strength."

"That's not helpful right now, Thor!"

"It was meant to be encouraging!"

"Bad time for encouragement, really bad time—"

"Then WHEN is a good time, if not during the actual crisis it's meant to address?"

"NEVER, Thor, that's when, or, or afterward, at the earliest, over drinks, when I'm not personally holding back the apocalypse with my ARMS—"

"You're not holding it with your arms, you're holding it with the hammer—"

"That is NOT the point I'm making right now—"

"I merely wished to be precise, given the circumstances—"

"Your precision is NOT what I need from you at this exact moment, your precision can WAIT—"

"Noted!" Thor called back, sounding, despite everything, faintly wounded. "I shall save my precision for a more convenient occasion!"

"THANK you!"

"You are most welcome!"

Even Ilsa, mid-crisis, monitoring readings that could still end everything, spared half a second to glance sideways at the exchange with an expression that suggested she genuinely could not decide whether to be impressed or alarmed.

"Is this normal?" Petra asked her, quietly, from the perimeter.

"I have no idea," Ilsa said. "I've stopped asking that question about this town completely."

Xander, despite the terror, felt something in his chest that was almost laughter, the sheer, absurd humanity of an actual Norse god getting scolded mid-crisis by a teenager holding back the end of reality with a magic hammer.

The seam widened, briefly, dangerously, and Ilsa's gate mechanism screamed with strain.

'Okay, this is the part where I die,' Xander thought, with the strange, detached clarity that apparently arrived right before actual disaster, some unhelpful corner of his brain already drafting an obituary that would definitely mention the sloth. 'Died the way he lived: narrating, mildly unprepared, standing near a hole in reality he had no actual business standing near. Good use of a Tuesday.'

"We're losing containment!" Petra shouted, from her position monitoring the readings. "Fifteen seconds, maybe less!"

"Xander!" Giles shouted, sudden and urgent. "Your hand. On Faith's shoulder. Now, whatever instinct told me to place you there—"

Xander didn't question it, reaching out and gripping Faith's shoulder the same way he'd have gripped anyone's right before a jump, and the moment his hand made contact, something shifted — the ability that had carried him across a hundred worlds suddenly, inexplicably, threading itself into the working, adding its own strange resonance to the mix.

The seam stopped widening.

Then, slowly, agonizingly, it began to close.

"That's it!" Ilsa shouted, disbelief and triumph tangled together in her voice. "Whatever that was, keep doing it!"

"I don't know what I'm doing!" Xander shouted back, hand still locked on Faith's shoulder.

"Then don't stop not knowing!" Faith gritted out, and despite everything, despite the terror and the strain and the vast, formless pressure still pushing from the other side, Xander found himself laughing, short and disbelieving, the sheer absurdity of the instruction landing perfectly even now.

The seam closed fully thirty seconds later, the light collapsing in on itself with a sound like a held breath finally released, and the silence that followed felt, for a long moment, more overwhelming than the crisis itself had been.
