Chapter 53

The jungle river carried him half a mile downstream before he managed to get his footing back, which Xander decided afterward was probably the single fastest introduction any world had ever given him.

He'd materialized on what looked like solid bank, taken exactly two steps, and discovered the bank was significantly less solid than advertised, the current taking his legs out from under him before he'd even finished registering the word river.

"Okay!" he shouted, to nobody, mouth filling with water on the second syllable. "Okay, that's — that's a fast river, that's an actually fast river!"

He got to a low-hanging branch on the third grab, hauled himself up onto a muddy bank far further downstream than his actual camp gear, and lay there for a solid minute just coughing up river water and reassessing every decision that had led to this exact moment.

'Pack's still on my back,' he thought, patting himself down with hands that wouldn't quite stop shaking. 'That's something. That's genuinely something. Lost nothing, technically, just my dignity and about half a mile of dry ground.' The relief of the pack still being there did more to steady him than the actual rest did.

He spent the remainder of the first day drying out gear and nursing a healthy new respect for river currents, making camp well clear of the bank this time, and by the second morning had mostly recovered enough to actually start exploring the jungle proper instead of just recovering from his own poor first impression of it.

The butterflies found him before he found anything else worth noting — not one or two, but dozens, wings iridescent blue in a way that seemed to shift color depending on the angle he caught them at, settling on every surface including, eventually, his own arm when he held still long enough.

"Okay," he said, quietly, not wanting to startle them off. "Okay, this is really beautiful. This makes up for the river a little."

He spent an hour just sitting there, arm outstretched, more butterflies landing than he could easily count, and got photos that he suspected, even without Willow's expert opinion, were going to be some of the best he'd ever brought back.

'She's gonna frame one of these,' he thought, watching the light catch a dozen wings at once. 'I'd bet actual money on it.' It felt like a fair trade, in the end — a bad start, a good middle, and by the time the jump pulled him home, a genuine sense that the good had outweighed the bad by a comfortable margin.

He arrived home soaked through again, this time by the jump itself rather than any river, and found Faith at the kitchen table surrounded by books that hadn't been there three days ago, dark circles under her eyes that told their own story before she said a word.

"Rough few days?" he asked, dropping his gear by the door.

"Define rough." Faith didn't look up from the page she was on. "Nine-day countdown to a possible demon summoning kind of rough. That rough."

"...Right. That rough." Xander pulled up a chair across from her, setting his own concerns about rivers and butterflies quietly aside. "You want company, or is this a solo-suffering kind of research binge?"

"Company's fine. Just don't expect scintillating conversation. I've read the same paragraph about cambion binding rituals four times and none of it's sticking."

"I can't help with the research, but I can sit here and be a supportive presence while you glare at old books."

"That's honestly worth something." Faith finally looked up, managing a tired smile. "How was your trip? Anything good?"

"River almost ate me, then a few hundred butterflies decided I was furniture. Genuinely great trip, all things considered."

"That's a hell of a range for one weekend."

"Welcome to my life," Xander said, and settled in beside her, quietly present while she went back to her books.
