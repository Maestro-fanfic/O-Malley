Chapter 67

Cordelia cornered Jesse at his locker with the determination of someone who'd finally run out of patience waiting for information to come to her.

"Okay, what's going on with Xander and Willow," she said, without preamble, arms crossed like she was owed an explanation by simple virtue of existing. "They've been weird for like two weeks. Whispering. Disappearing together. It's very suspicious, and frankly I deserve to know things before the rest of the school does."

"I actually don't know," Jesse admitted. "I've asked. They keep saying wren measurements."

"Nobody's that into wrens, Jesse. Even you're not that stupid."

"Thanks. I think. That's what I said! Nobody believed me when I said it was suspicious, and now you're saying it's suspicious, so I feel very vindicated right now."

"Don't get used to being right. It's not a look that suits you."

"It's not a dating thing, everybody already knows that," Cordelia said, waving it off like old news. "So what's the actual thing? Because 'wren measurements' isn't a thing people whisper about."

Jesse considered this with genuine seriousness. "Honestly? No idea. It's got a group project energy to it, though. Something they're working on together that they don't want the rest of us knowing about yet."

"How would you know what actual crushes look like?"

"I have extensive personal experience with unrequited ones. I'm basically an expert."

"That's not a flex, Jesse, that's genuinely one of the saddest sentences anyone's said to me all week, and I sat through Harmony's breakup story yesterday."

"I didn't say it was a flex. I said it was expertise. Two different things."

"It's one thing. It's called being pathetic."

"Ouch. Fair, but ouch."

Faith arrived partway through, catching the tail end of the conversation with visible amusement. "You two conspiracy theorists still working the wren angle?"

"It's not a conspiracy theory if it's clearly true," Cordelia said. "Do you know what they're up to?"

"Nope. And before you ask, no, I haven't pushed, because everybody's allowed secrets until they're ready to share them." Faith leaned against the lockers. "Also because I've got my own stuff going on that I'm not exactly broadcasting either, so I'm not really in a position to judge anybody else's secret-keeping."

"You have secrets too?" Cordelia looked personally offended by this development, like the universe had specifically inconvenienced her. "This whole friend group is apparently just secrets wearing normal clothes. It's exhausting, honestly, being surrounded by this much mystery when I have actual things I want to know."

"Welcome to Sunnydale," Faith said. "Everybody's got something. You get used to it."

"I don't want to get used to it, I want to know things. There's a difference, and it's called having priorities."

"That's your character flaw, not my problem."

Cordelia opened her mouth to argue, closed it, and settled for a look of pure, wordless disdain instead, directed impartially at both of them. "Fine. I'll figure it out eventually. I always do, and when I do, neither of you gets to act surprised."

"Good luck with that," Jesse said. "I've been trying for two weeks and all I've got is wren measurements."

"That's because you're bad at this. I'm good at this."

"You're not that much better at this than me."

"I'm far better at this than you, Jesse, and the fact that you'd even argue the point tells me exactly how much better."

The two of them fell into an easy, familiar bickering rhythm as they headed toward class, Faith trailing behind with a small, private smile, really enjoying the sight of ordinary friend-group nonsense continuing exactly as it always had, unbothered by anything bigger sitting quietly underneath it.

67.5. Chapter 67.5

Buffy and Willow both came this time, the three of them landing in a world of open plains broken by scattered rock outcroppings, wind steady and warm across grass that came up past their knees.

"Okay, this one's calm," Buffy said, scanning the horizon. "Suspiciously calm, given our track record lately."

"Don't jinx it," Willow warned, already crouching over a cluster of unfamiliar wildflowers.

The herd showed up by the second day, animals that read as bison-adjacent but leaner, moving across the plain in a loose formation that shifted and reformed with an almost choreographed grace.

"They're beautiful," Willow said, getting her photos carefully. "Look at that coordination. That's not random, that's actual social structure."

"You're gonna be writing about herd dynamics for a week, aren't you."

"Absolutely. No question."

They spent the rest of the trip working the plain's edges, cataloguing burrowing creatures that popped up at the sound of footsteps and vanished just as fast, and by the third evening had settled into the easy, comfortable rhythm that came from a trip asking nothing difficult of anyone.

"This is a good one," Buffy said, watching the sunset stretch orange across the grass, leaning into Xander while Willow's hand found his. "After everything with the mural, with Faith's whole thing — this is exactly the kind of quiet I didn't know I needed."

"Yeah," Willow agreed. "Same, honestly."

They made it home with herd photos, wildflower samples, and the sense that the trip had given all three of them exactly what they needed, without anyone having had to ask for it directly.

Faith was on the porch steps when they got back, looking considerably more rested herself after her own stretch of quiet. "You three look disgustingly relaxed."

"Bison. Wildflowers. Zero drama," Buffy said, dropping down next to her.

"I'm almost jealous. Almost."

"You should come with us sometime. Not just Xander solo — the whole group thing. I think you'd like it."

"Maybe," Faith said, something considering in her expression. "Maybe I will."
