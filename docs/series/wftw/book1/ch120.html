Chapter 120

Natasha arrived on a Thursday, exactly the "sometime this week" Thor had relayed, her entrance carrying none of the chaos Tony's had. SHIELD had spent the week since turning his one-off, ninety-percent-confidence rig into something considerably more controlled — a proper crossing point, manned and monitored, built out in the same storage unit outside the city — and Natasha came through it on foot, alone, whatever vehicle she'd used left well out of earshot on the other side. She made her presence known by simply appearing in the Summers' kitchen doorway while Joyce was making coffee, duffel bag over one shoulder, an expression that suggested she'd already cataloged three exits and two potential threats before saying a single word.

Joyce, to her considerable credit, only jumped slightly, coffee pot steady in her hand despite the surprise. "You must be Natasha."

"I must be. Sorry for the entrance. Force of habit." Natasha's eyes did a quick, assessing sweep of the kitchen, the house, presumably the whole situation, before settling into something closer to relaxed, shoulders dropping a half-inch that Xander wouldn't have caught if he hadn't already spent weeks learning to read Thor's own subtle tells. "Where's Thor?"

"Living room. Wrestling's on."

"Of course it is." Natasha allowed herself something almost like a smile, setting her bag down by the door, keeping her hands free out of old habit. "That tracks. Tony give you all any warning before he descended, or did he just show up?"

"No warning at all," Joyce confirmed. "Just arrived, drove straight through to nearly one in the morning at my kitchen table, and I made enough coffee to float a boat while your friend explained, at some length, why the universe apparently owes all of you an apology. Very polite about it, though. Left the kitchen cleaner than he found it, which I genuinely wasn't expecting."

"That's Steve's influence, not his. Tony wouldn't think to wipe a counter if his life depended on it." Natasha shook her head, something fond under the exasperation. "Glad he got his fix in early. Means I get to actually do this properly instead of racing him to it."

She found Thor exactly where Joyce said, boots still up on the coffee table, and the two of them fell into an easy, familiar rhythm that told Xander, watching from the doorway, more about their actual friendship than eleven days of worried silence ever could have — the kind of comfort that only came from years of trusting someone enough to actually let your guard down around them.

"You look unharmed," Natasha said, dropping onto the couch arm without ceremony. "Annoyingly unharmed, actually, given how worried you made everyone."

"I am unharmed. Genuinely." Thor met her eyes, sincere despite the teasing edge in her voice. "I apologize for the silence. It was not my intention to worry anyone."

"I know. Doesn't make the eleven days less annoying." She studied him a moment longer, something in her posture easing further, the last of the tension she'd walked in with finally draining out now that she'd confirmed with her own eyes what she'd apparently already half-believed from his voice on the phone. "So. Long story?"

"Extremely long story."

"Give me the short version first. I'll get the rest later. I always do."

Thor obliged, working through the hammer, the search, the fracture, Xander's own connection to it all, and Natasha listened with the same careful, assessing attention she'd brought to the whole room initially, occasionally glancing at Xander or Willow like she was filing away details for later, expression giving away almost nothing except the very slight tightening around her eyes whenever the scope of the situation expanded beyond what she'd apparently been braced for.

"So there's a whole multiversal instability situation," she said, once he finished, "caused by us, apparently, and it's been quietly reshaping reality for years without anyone noticing."

"That is, broadly, the current theory, yes."

"Great. Wonderful. Exactly the kind of thing I love hearing about on a Thursday." Natasha rubbed her temples, though there was real humor underneath the exasperation. "Does Fury know?"

"Some of it. Tony has what I gave him and Steve that first night, which Steve made rather clear would be reaching Fury regardless of my own preferences on the timing. But that was before Giles and I had worked through the mural, the counter-rite, or how any of it actually connects back to the ability itself. What Fury has is the rough shape of it. Not the full picture."

"He's gonna want the full picture. Sooner rather than later, given the scale you're describing. Trust me on this one, Thor. The longer this sits half-explained, the worse the conversation gets when he finally has all of it."

"I agree. That's rather why I wanted you to have it properly, before anyone tries assembling a report out of half-finished pieces."

"Fair. Just don't wait too long. I've had that conversation with him before, on your behalf, more than once. It doesn't get easier with practice."

Natasha considered the room again — Xander, Willow, Giles' books stacked on the coffee table, a half-eaten plate of Thor's nachos still sitting forgotten on the counter, the whole strange, domestic scene of a god settled comfortably into small-town life. "This is a truly bizarre way to spend a Thursday."

"Welcome to Sunnydale," Xander said. "We've got a whole thing going on. You get used to it."

"I'm gonna need considerably more context before I get used to anything," Natasha said, though something in her tone suggested she was already, despite herself, finding the whole situation more interesting than alarming, the professional wariness gradually giving way to genuine curiosity. "Start from the beginning. All of it. I've got time."

"You've got time?" Xander repeated, a little disbelieving. "Don't you have, like, an actual job? Missions, or whatever they're called?"

"I've got a few days before anyone comes looking for me specifically. Fury's good about giving me room to actually investigate something properly instead of rushing through it." She settled more fully into the couch cushions, the last of her guarded posture finally easing into something almost comfortable. "Also, honestly? This is more interesting than most of what's currently on my desk."

"Buckle in," Willow said, grinning, already pulling out her notebook, determined not to miss a single detail. "This one's got layers."
