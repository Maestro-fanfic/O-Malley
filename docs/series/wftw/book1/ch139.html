Chapter 139

Faith waited until the house had gone fully quiet, well past midnight on a Tuesday, before she let herself actually reach for it, Buffy's door closed down the hall, Joyce's light long since off, just the two of them — her and the hammer, propped in the corner where it had lived for months now — in the hush that the house only ever got this late, no footsteps, no TV noise, just the faint tick of the water heater and the sound of her own breathing filling the room.

She'd asked Buffy earlier that evening if it'd be weird, borrowing some alone time with it, and Buffy had just shrugged and said it's not really mine to gatekeep, F, go for it — which had somehow made this easier and harder in equal measure. The hammer sat exactly where it always sat, propped against the closet door, ordinary in every way except the one that mattered, streetlight filtering thin through the blinds and catching along its worn handle.

"Okay," she said, to the hammer, which didn't respond, because it was a hammer. "It's been a while. Figured I owed you another shot, given everything."

She thought about Thor's words, turning them over one more time before committing. Not permanent. A pause, waiting for something to catch up to itself. She'd spent months since then quietly becoming someone she trusted more than the girl who'd first reached for this thing and found it immovable — steadier, more settled, less defined by the gap between herself and Buffy than she used to be. She thought about the ritual, the countdown, the nine days that had taught her what it actually felt like to carry real weight for someone else's sake and not just her own survival. She thought about Joyce's kitchen table, and patrol nights that had stopped feeling like proving grounds and started feeling like just, simply, home.

She reached out and closed her hand around the handle.

It lifted.

Not effortlessly, not the way Buffy had described it, but it lifted — a real, genuine weight finally yielding to her grip, shifting off the shelf and into her hand like it had simply been waiting for her to actually be ready to receive it. Her arm dipped slightly with the actual weight of it before she adjusted, catching the balance properly, the metal cool against her palm despite the room's ordinary temperature.

Faith stood there a long moment, just holding it, feeling something settle in her chest that she didn't immediately have words for. Not triumph exactly. More like relief — a question finally getting an honest answer instead of just more waiting.

"Huh," she said, out loud, testing the weight of it properly now. "Huh, okay. There it is."

She didn't feel radically different. That was the strange part — no flash of light, no cosmic recognition, just an ordinary hammer sitting in her hand, considerably lighter than it looked, exactly the way Buffy had once described it. She turned it over once, catching the worn grain of the handle in the streetlight, and found herself, unexpectedly, laughing — short, disbelieving, the sound echoing a little too loud in the sleeping house.

'Guess Thor was right,' she thought, setting it back down carefully, not quite ready to actually take it anywhere yet, just needing to know it was possible. 'Wasn't ever about being good enough. Just about catching up to myself.' It felt like an important thing to have learned, even without fully understanding the mechanism behind it.

She stayed cross-legged on her bedroom floor a while longer in the near-dark, the hammer resting beside her, letting the whole strange, quiet victory settle properly before she trusted herself to move on to whatever came next.

She called Buffy that night, needing to tell someone, and found herself smiling the whole time she explained it, pacing her room with the phone pressed to her ear.

"You lifted it," Buffy said, delighted. "Faith, that's huge."

"Yeah. Feels huge. Doesn't feel as huge as I thought it would, though, if that makes sense. More just — quiet. Settled."

"That tracks, actually. Good things settling instead of exploding feels like progress, honestly."

"Yeah," Faith agreed. "Yeah, I think it does."

"You gonna tell Thor?"

"Eventually. Wanna keep it to myself a little longer first. Feels like mine to hold onto for a minute before I hand it over to somebody else's reaction."

"That's fair. Take your time."

She told him three days later, finding him alone on Joyce's porch in the same spot they'd talked once before, and simply held out her hand, palm up, waiting.

Thor looked at her a long moment, understanding immediately without needing the words spelled out. "You lifted it."

"Yeah."

"How does it feel?"

Faith considered the question properly before answering, the way he always seemed to consider things before speaking. "Quiet. Settled. Not the fireworks I expected, honestly. More like something that was already true finally getting to be true out loud."

Thor's face broke into something honestly, deeply pleased. "That is, in my experience, exactly how it should feel. The loud version is usually a warning sign, not a victory."

"Good to know. Wish somebody'd told me that going in."

"I am telling you now." He studied her a moment longer, something proud and a little emotional crossing his features despite his usual composure. "I am glad, Faith. Genuinely. I told you it was never permanent. I did not expect to be quite this pleased at being proven right so soon."

"Yeah, well. Don't get used to it. Might take me another year to prove you right about the next thing."

"I look forward to it regardless," Thor said, and meant it, and Faith found herself, sitting there on the familiar porch steps, feeling more like herself than she had in longer than she could easily measure.

She didn't tell the rest of the group right away, wanting to keep the discovery just hers a little longer first, but she found herself, over the following days, standing a little straighter, carrying herself with a little less of the old, quiet weight she'd been hauling around since the first failed attempt — not because anything external had changed, but because something internal finally had, and that, she was slowly coming to realize, had always been the actual point.
