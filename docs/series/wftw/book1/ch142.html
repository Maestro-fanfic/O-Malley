Chapter 142

Dr. Elena Vasquez had been asking to come along for the better part of a year, ever since she'd taken over as lead biologist on the Mayor's conservation team and heard, secondhand, about Dr. Webb's single legendary trip years earlier — a story that had apparently achieved something close to department folklore by the time she inherited it. It took considerably longer than she expected for the logistics to actually line up — background checks, careful briefings on what not to touch, a genuinely uncomfortable conversation about liability that Alan Finch had insisted on before signing off.

"I still can't quite believe this is happening," Elena said, adjusting a field kit that looked far more professional than anything Xander usually brought along — proper sample vials, a portable soil analysis kit, a camera with lenses he couldn't begin to identify by function. "Four years of secondhand specimens and photographs, and now I finally get to see one of these places myself."

"Try to temper expectations a little," Xander warned, watching her check and recheck her equipment, clearly having rehearsed this exact moment many times over. "It's not always postcard material."

"I've seen your reports. I know exactly what I'm signing up for."

"Grab on whenever you're ready. Might take a second to feel normal, first time."

She gripped his shoulder with the same careful precision she brought to everything, and the pull took them both, the world folding away and reforming into something much greener than the Magic Box's back room had been a moment before.

The world they landed in turned out to be tropical highland, cloud forest thick with epiphytes and orchids in colors neither of them had names for, mist clinging low between the trees even at midday, the air heavy with a mineral, growing-things smell that Elena breathed in like it was the first real breath she'd taken in years.

"Okay, this is incredible," she said, already crouching to examine the nearest cluster of moss with visible reverence. "The biodiversity here is — Xander, do you understand what this represents? An entire ecosystem, completely undisturbed, going back who knows how many thousands of years without a single invasive species disrupting anything."

"I mean, kind of, yeah. I've been saying that for years."

"I know. I believed you. It's different actually being here." She pulled out her sampling kit, hands steady despite the obvious excitement running through her. "I want to start with soil composition. If I can establish a baseline here, it gives us something to actually compare against the specimens you've brought back before."

They spent the first day working a careful grid pattern through the nearest stretch of forest, Elena documenting everything with a thoroughness that made Xander's own four years of amateur note-taking look almost embarrassingly casual by comparison — soil pH at measured intervals, canopy density estimated with an actual instrument rather than a guess, photographs taken from three separate angles for every specimen worth cataloguing at all.

"This is going to take notably longer to properly document than your usual trips, I imagine," she said, midway through the second hour, not looking up from a particularly promising cluster of fungi.

"Yeah, that tracks with what I figured. You do you. I'll just keep an eye out for anything trying to eat us."

The orchids found them properly on the second day — an entire hillside thick with blooms in shades of violet and deep red, growing from the bark of ancient, moss-heavy trees in numbers dense enough to turn the whole slope into something that looked, from a distance, almost deliberately arranged.

"I need a minute — I need to actually absorb this," Elena admitted, really overwhelmed, camera hanging forgotten in one hand. "I've studied orchid taxonomy for fifteen years. I have never seen anything like this concentration, this diversity, in one single location."

"Take your time. Not going anywhere."

She spent the better part of three hours on that single hillside, cataloguing specimens with a patience Xander found himself truly admiring, cross-referencing bloom patterns against reference photos she'd brought specifically for this purpose, occasionally making small, delighted sounds that reminded him, more than once, of Willow's own reaction to a particularly good find.

By the third day, the two of them had settled into an easy, efficient rhythm — Elena documenting, Xander watching the tree line and handling anything requiring more physical effort than careful observation, both of them working the kind of unhurried, thorough pace that four years of solo trips had never quite allowed him to practice.

"This is the best work I've done in my career," Elena admitted, on the final evening, reviewing pages of careful notation by the light of their small camp fire. "Genuinely. Nothing else comes close."

"Glad it was worth the wait."

"More than worth it. I want to come again. Properly, if the Mayor's willing to arrange it regularly."

"I'll talk to him. Feels like something that should probably happen more, honestly. You got way more out of three days than I ever manage on my own."

"Different skill sets," Elena said. "You're better at surviving it. I'm better at understanding it. Feels like a reasonable division of labor."

"Very reasonable," Xander agreed, and meant it, already looking forward to what proper scientific documentation might reveal about worlds he'd been visiting for years without ever quite appreciating, until now, exactly how much he'd been missing beneath the surface of his own casual observations.

They made it home with samples and data that would keep Elena's team busy for months, both of them satisfied that the trip had finally brought real scientific rigor to a job that had, until now, mostly relied on one determined teenager's careful but amateur observation.

Willow met them at the door, eyeing Elena's field kit with poorly concealed envy. "Okay, I need to know everything, and also where do I get a soil analysis kit like that."

"Incredible," Elena said, still visibly a little dazed. "I have never seen anything like it. I want to go back immediately."

"Welcome to the club," Xander said, grinning.
