Chapter 37

Willow's disaster-prediction model existed, despite her repeated denials, and Giles found the first draft of it spread across the research table on a Tuesday afternoon, color-coded in a system that had grown notably more elaborate since the last time he'd checked in on her progress.

"This is remarkably thorough," he said, examining what appeared to be a chart cross-referencing biome type against injury severity. "I thought you weren't building a disaster-prediction model."

"I'm not. This is a hazard-correlation chart. Completely different thing."

"I see. And the practical distinction between those two things is?"

"One of them sounds like I'm treating Xander's life as a science experiment, and the other one sounds responsible." Willow tapped the chart. "Also, for the record, it's actually working. Desert and rainforest biomes correlate with the highest incident rates. Grassland and coastal are consistently the calmest. If the pattern holds, I might actually be able to warn him what to prep for before he even leaves, based on nothing but which supplies he happens to grab that morning."

"That would be remarkably useful, assuming the correlation holds beyond a handful of data points."

"I know it's not a huge sample size yet. But it's not nothing either, and every trip adds more data." Willow looked up at him. "I just — I want to actually help. Not just take notes after the fact. If there's a real pattern here, even a partial one, that's something I can hand him before he leaves instead of just cataloging what happened once he's already back."

Giles considered the chart a moment longer. "I admire the ambition. I'd only remind you, gently, that some risk in his work may never fully resolve into a predictable pattern, however carefully you track it."

"I know. I'm not trying to make it risk-free. I just want it to be a little less blind every time." Willow flipped to a fresh page, already sketching out the next iteration of the chart. "Also, Faith's mural thing has me thinking about patterns generally. If she can spot something building over weeks just from watching close enough, I feel like I should be able to do something similar with actual data instead of instinct."

"They're not completely different skills," Giles said. "Instinct built on careful observation over time tends to look very similar to a well-constructed model, in my experience. You may simply be arriving at the same kind of understanding from a different direction."

Willow considered that, clearly pleased by the comparison. "I like that. Instinct and data, same destination, different roads."

"Something like that, yes."

"I'm putting that on the chart somewhere. Feels like it deserves a place on the chart."

"I wouldn't dare argue with your filing system," Giles said with a small smile, and left her to it, the chart growing another color-coded column before he'd even made it to the door.
