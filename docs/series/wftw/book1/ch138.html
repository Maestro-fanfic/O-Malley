Chapter 138

Faith and Willow both came this time, all three of them wanting one more genuine break before the household's schedule filled entirely with visiting dignitaries from three separate realities.

The world was cypress swamp, moss hanging thick from ancient trees, water dark and still except where something occasionally broke the surface and vanished again before any of them could get a clear look.

"Okay, this one's atmospheric," Faith said, peering into the shadowed water. "Very haunted-swamp energy."

"Very haunted-swamp energy that's apparently home to something genuinely interesting," Willow said, spotting movement along the nearest bank. "Look."

Turtles, dozens of them, sunning themselves on half-submerged logs, shells patterned in shades of green and brown that made them nearly invisible against the water until they moved.

"Those are incredible," Willow said, already reaching for her camera. "I need every single shell pattern documented."

"You've got time," Faith said, grinning. "They don't look like they're going anywhere fast."

They spent the trip working the swamp's edges carefully, mindful of the dark water and whatever occasionally broke its surface, and by the third evening had settled into the easy, appreciative rhythm that came from a trip asking nothing complicated of any of them.

"This is exactly what I needed," Faith said, watching fireflies begin to flicker among the cypress trees as evening settled in, leaning into Willow's side without quite meaning to. "After everything coming up. Feels good, having one more quiet one before it all starts."

"Yeah," Willow agreed, letting her stay there, an arm coming around Faith's shoulders easily. "Same, honestly. Feels like we've all earned it."

"You've definitely earned it," Xander said. "Both of you. Feels like everyone's been carrying something extra lately, one way or another."

"We all have," Faith said. "That's kind of the theme of this whole year, honestly."

They made it home with turtle photos, firefly-lit memories, and a fresh reminder for all three of them why the ordinary parts of this strange life mattered every bit as much as the extraordinary ones currently converging on them.

Buffy kissed Faith hello, then Willow, then stood back to look them both over. "Firefly light looks good on you two. Good one?"

"Zero complications," Faith confirmed, still a little pleased and a little shy about the greeting even now. "Turtles, fireflies. Exactly what we needed before everything else lands."

"Okay, wait." Willow held up a hand, something calculating crossing her face, the old notebook-instinct surfacing even now. "That's three in a row. Xander's hills, the heather one, now this. Three trips, right before the biggest week any of us have ever had, and every single one of them just happened to be exactly, precisely the kind of calm we actually needed. That's not a coincidence pattern. That's a pattern pattern."

"You think the jump's doing it on purpose," Xander said slowly. "Like the drawn-together thing, but smaller. Quieter."

"I don't know what I think yet. I think I want to start actually tracking it instead of just noticing it after the fact and feeling weird about it for five minutes." Willow was already reaching for the notebook that lived permanently in her bag at this point. "Could still be nothing. Three data points isn't a trend, Giles would be the first to tell me that. But it's not nothing, either."

"Add it to the list," Buffy said, fond, no doubt at all in her voice that Willow would. "Glad you got it, regardless of who or what's responsible," she added to Faith, already steering them both toward the kitchen before Willow could disappear into the notebook entirely.

138.5. Chapter 138.5

Alan took two weeks to actually assemble what he wanted for the seal trip, which surprised Xander a little, given how casually Willow had floated it back in the doorway that day.

"You said someday," Alan pointed out, reviewing a checklist that already ran to a second page. "Someday arrived. I've had a marine vet on standby for a week now, and she's not cheap, so someday's happening before my budget notices."

"Fair. I did say someday." Xander glanced over the list — sedation protocol adapted from real-world seal rescue work, a padded transport sling instead of a crate, wet towels and a cooler of seawater to keep the animal's skin from drying out on the trip back. "This is different from the usual setup."

"Everything about a marine mammal is different from the usual setup. You can't just tranq it and haul it in a cage — skin dries out, they overheat on land faster than you'd think, and if you get the dose even slightly wrong it can affect their breathing in the water before it ever affects anything else. Dr. Okonkwo's very specific about the margins." Alan tapped the second page. "Which is why she's coming in person this time instead of just phoning in numbers."

Willow had brought her original sketches from the cliff-colony trip, spot pattern annotated in three different colors of ink, and spent most of the planning call walking Dr. Okonkwo through exactly where on the beach the colony hauled out and how steep the path down actually was.

"They're skittish about the water side, not the land side," Willow said. "Nothing came at them from the cliffs the whole time we were there. If anything's going to spook them, it's someone approaching from the surf."

"Good to know. We'll come in from the rocks, then, not the shoreline." Dr. Okonkwo made a note of her own, brisk and economical. "I want eyes on the colony a full day before anyone commits to a shot, confirm which individual we're actually after."

They went as a full group this time — Xander, Buffy, and Willow, plus Dr. Okonkwo and two of Alan's most experienced handlers, the largest crew any single trip had ever assembled outside the mastodon and sabertooth runs.

The chalk cliffs looked exactly the way Willow remembered them, gulls wheeling overhead in the same loud, indifferent numbers, and the colony was still there on the narrow beach below, spotted seal hauled out among a dozen plainer gray ones, easy to pick out even from the clifftop.

"That's her," Willow said, checking her old sketches against the animal below. "Same pattern. I'd know those spots anywhere."

They spent the first day exactly as promised, simply watching — Dr. Okonkwo tracking the colony's rhythm, when they fed, when they hauled out to rest, how close a human could get before the whole group startled back toward the water.

"She's a loner within the group," Dr. Okonkwo observed, on the second morning, watching the spotted seal settle a deliberate distance from the others. "Which actually helps us. Less risk of spooking the whole colony if we take her cleanly."

The shot itself went almost anticlimactically well, once the actual attempt came — a single dart from the rocks above, the seal barely reacting before the sedation took hold, no panicked scramble for the water the way Dr. Okonkwo had warned might happen if the dose ran even slightly high.

"Vitals are steady," she called, already moving down the path with two of Alan's handlers close behind her, the padded sling unrolling between them. "Get the sling under her before she's fully out, we want her weight distributed evenly the second she stops holding it herself."

It took all six of them working the narrow path to get the sling secured and the animal moved, careful of the loose chalk underfoot, Willow keeping a running commentary of measurements the whole way that Dr. Okonkwo actually seemed grateful for rather than annoyed by.

"Good work," Dr. Okonkwo said, once the seal was settled in the cooler, wet towels arranged and her breathing checked one more time. "That's about as clean as a first attempt on a species nobody's ever handled gets."

"We had good data going in," Xander said. "Willow's spot pattern sketches basically told you where to look before you even got here."

"They did. I'll want copies for the actual paper, if she's willing."

"Extremely willing," Willow confirmed, already looking a little like she might cry about it.

The pull took all five of them and one very confused, very sedated seal home together, and Alan's team had a proper aquatic holding tank ready and waiting by the time they came through, cool water and everything, the transition handled with a speed that left Dr. Okonkwo visibly impressed despite herself.

Faith opened the door, took one look at the whole group — soaked, chalk-dusted, trailing two of Alan's handlers still hauling gear — and did a slow, deliberate double take. "Do I even want to ask?"

"Really good one," Buffy confirmed. "We have a seal now. An actual, real, new-to-science seal. Alan's got a whole tank for her."

"Okay, that's — we just have seals now? That's a thing we do?"

"Apparently," Xander said, grinning despite the exhaustion. "Willow called it back in the doorway weeks ago. Guess someday finally showed up."
