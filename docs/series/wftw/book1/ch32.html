Chapter 32

"The ants got you again," Alan Finch said, not quite managing to hide his amusement as Xander recounted the trip over the newest addition's paperwork.

"The ants got me again. I want it on record that I saw it coming, tried to prevent it, and lost anyway. This is not a story about carelessness. This is a story about an actually well-organized enemy."

"I believe you. I've heard worse origin stories for a bird acquisition, honestly." Alan peered into the crate at the blue-green bird, currently regarding both of them with the same unbothered patience as everything else that came through this pipeline. "This is a stunning specimen, by the way. Genuinely one of the nicer ones you've brought back."

"She helped make the ant thing worth it. Least she could do."

"Does she have a name yet?"

"Not officially. Willow's got some Latin-sounding placeholder in her notebook, but that's more of a filing system than a name."

"Might I suggest something," Alan said, already reaching for his own clipboard, clearly taking naming privileges more seriously than the moment strictly called for. "I've always thought this species — well, this genus, at minimum — deserved something a bit more dignified than a filing number."

"Go for it. You've earned naming rights on at least a few of these by now."

Mayor Wilkins wandered by partway through the debate over names, drawn by the same internal radar that always seemed to find him wherever new arrivals were being processed, and peered into the crate with open delight.

"Oh, isn't she lovely," he said with a warm smile. "Alan, make sure she gets a perch with good sun exposure. I remember reading these species prefer it."

"Already planned for, sir."

"Wonderful." He turned to Xander, the same paternal warmth as always sitting slightly strange against everything else Xander knew he was capable of. "Ant troubles again, I hear?"

"Word travels fast."

"Alan mentioned it. I do hope you're taking better precautions these days."

"I check the ground now. Genuinely. It's a whole ritual."

"Good man." Mayor Wilkins clapped him once on the shoulder. "Council meeting again. The things I do for this town." He gave the bird one last admiring look, checked his watch, and sighed like a man with somewhere notably less pleasant to be, humming something under his breath that sounded suspiciously cheerful for someone walking toward a budget meeting.

"He really does love this stuff," Xander said, once he was out of earshot.

"Completely genuine," Alan agreed. "It's honestly one of the more grounding things about working for him. Most days I have absolutely no idea what else is happening in this town, but I know he means every word about the birds."

"Small mercies."

"The smallest, and somehow still the ones that matter most." Alan finished his notes and closed the clipboard with a satisfied nod. "I'll get her settled properly. Go home, Xander. You look like you fought a war with insects and lost most of the battles."

"I fought a war with insects and won the war, technically. Just lost a few individual skirmishes."

"Sure," Alan said with a grin. "Tell yourself that."

32.5. Chapter 32.5

It was Faith who found it, which felt right in a way she couldn't have explained to anyone, stepping out onto the Summers' back porch before dawn on a morning she couldn't sleep through and nearly tripping over something that had absolutely not been there when she'd come in from patrol six hours earlier.

It was planted upright in the flowerbed, head-down, like someone had driven it into the soil on purpose and then simply left — a hammer, heavy-headed, handle sticking straight up and worn in a way that didn't match its otherwise pristine condition, sitting there in Joyce Summers' pansies as though it had always belonged among them.

"Okay," Faith said, out loud, to nobody. "That's new."

She didn't touch it right away. Four months in this house had taught her that things which showed up overnight in Sunnydale usually earned a second look before a first touch, and something about the stillness of it — the weight of it, radiating off the metal even without her hand anywhere near the grip — made the back of her neck prickle in a way she'd learned not to argue with.

"Buffy," she called, not loud, just enough to carry through the screen door. "You up? You're gonna wanna see this."

Buffy appeared a minute later in sleep shorts and one of Xander's old shirts, hair a mess, blinking against the early light, and stopped dead at the top of the porch steps.

"Is that in Mom's pansies?"

"Dead center. Very inconsiderate of it."

"Where did it even—" Buffy crouched down at the edge of the flowerbed, studying it without touching. "There's no footprints. No drag marks. It's just — there."

"Yeah, I noticed that too. Kinda my whole reason for waking you up at an unholy hour."

Buffy reached for it slowly, the way you'd reach for something that might bite, and closed her hand around the grip mostly just to test the weight of it, the way you pick up anything unfamiliar sitting somewhere it shouldn't be — and found it lighter than it looked, comfortable in her palm in a way that made her glance down at it a second time, some part of her brain flagging the sensation as worth noting even though nothing about it seemed obviously wrong.

"Huh," she said. "That's — huh."

"What?"

"Nothing. It's just lighter than I expected." She lifted it clean out of the dirt with almost no effort at all, turned it over once, testing the balance, and held it out toward Faith without further thought. "Here. Tell me I'm not imagining that."

Faith took it the same casual way, mirroring the same easy motion — and it didn't move.

Not stuck, not caught on anything — she could feel her own grip tight around the handle, could feel herself actually pulling — but the hammer simply stayed exactly where Buffy's hand had left it, planted in midair for a fraction of a second before gravity won and it dropped straight back into the flowerbed, refusing every ounce of effort Faith put into it as cleanly as if her hands weren't there at all.

"Okay, that's not lighter than expected," Faith said, forcing a laugh, trying again with both hands and getting the exact same nothing for her effort. "That's just heavy. Really heavy. How is a random garden hammer doing this."

"You're not even lifting it," Buffy said, watching. "Faith, your hands aren't even moving."

"I'm aware, B, thank you, I have the same eyes you do." Faith let go, flexing her fingers, something flickering across her face too fast to properly name before she smoothed it back into her usual easy nonchalance. "Whatever. Weird garden ornament. Not everything's gotta mean something."

Buffy picked it back up — easily, the same effortless lightness as before — and stood there a moment just looking at it, something uneasy and curious both sitting behind her eyes.

"I'm calling Giles," she said finally.

"Good plan. This feels like a Giles problem."

Neither of them touched breakfast before Giles arrived, and neither of them mentioned, in the time it took him to get there, the strange easy lightness of something that had apparently refused, just as easily, to move for somebody else.
