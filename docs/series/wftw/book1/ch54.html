Chapter 54

Buffy showed up at the Magic Box on day three of the countdown without being asked, dropped a stack of her own patrol notes on the table, and didn't wait for permission before pulling up a chair.

"I'm not asking to help," she said, before Faith could object. "I'm telling you I'm helping. Six days left and Giles can't cross-reference everything alone."

"I wasn't gonna tell you no."

"Good. Because I wasn't really giving you the option." Buffy flipped open the nearest book, squinting at text that clearly wasn't in English. "Okay, I have no idea what any of this says, but I can at least hold books open while you two do the actual reading."

"That's more helpful than it sounds," Giles said, not looking up from his own stack. "Genuinely. An extra set of hands sorting relevant material from irrelevant saves considerable time."

Willow arrived twenty minutes later with her own notebook, the biome chart set firmly aside for the week, and Xander trailed in not long after that, having apparently decided his jump recovery could wait until the more pressing crisis got handled first.

"I can't read Latin," he announced, dropping into a chair, "or whatever half this stuff is written in, but I can fetch things, make coffee, and provide moral support. Consider me on standby."

"Moral support's underrated," Faith said. "I'll take it."

They worked through the afternoon in a rhythm that had become familiar to all of them over the past several years — Giles and Faith doing the actual translation work, Buffy and Willow sorting and cross-referencing, Xander running coffee and snacks and the occasional truly useful question that came from having no expertise to bias his instincts.

"What happens if we can't stop it in time?" he asked, somewhere around the third hour, the question landing heavier than the room's easy working noise had prepared anyone for.

Giles set down his pen. "Then we face whatever comes through, together, with whatever we've managed to learn by then. I won't pretend that's an ideal contingency. But it is, at minimum, a plan, rather than simply hoping the ritual fails on its own."

"That's not super reassuring."

"No," Giles admitted. "But it's honest, and I'd rather be honestly unreassuring than falsely comforting, given what's actually at stake."

Faith looked around the table — Buffy squinting at a passage she clearly couldn't parse but refused to give up on, Willow cross-referencing three books at once with the same focused intensity she brought to everything, Xander hovering with coffee refills nobody had actually asked for but everyone was quietly grateful for anyway.

"We're gonna be ready," she said, mostly to herself, though the whole table heard it. "Whatever this actually turns out to be. We've got six days and four people who won't quit until we've squeezed every answer out of these books that exists to find."

"Five people," Buffy corrected. "You're forgetting to count yourself."

"I wasn't forgetting. I just don't usually put myself first on the list."

"Start now," Buffy said. "You found this. You're the reason we've got six days instead of zero. Put yourself first on the list, at least this once."

Faith didn't have a comeback for that, which by now had become less rare than it used to be, and she went back to her book instead, the quiet warmth of the moment settling into the working noise of the room without anyone needing to comment on it further.
