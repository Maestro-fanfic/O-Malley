Chapter 66

Willow's family tree theory had felt so promising for exactly four days, which was about how long it took her to hit the wall that made the whole approach collapse.

"It doesn't work," she told Xander, spreading printouts across her bedroom floor with visible frustration. "I traced back what I could of your family — not everything, records get thin past a certain point, but I got back a good five, six generations on a couple branches. There's no pattern. No repeating name, no single line it clusters around. Nothing."

"So it's not hereditary."

"That's the thing, I don't think that's right either." Willow rubbed her eyes, clearly exhausted from more late nights than she'd probably admit to. "I found a reference — buried, weird phrasing, the kind of thing that reads like someone was trying not to say something outright — that suggests this isn't about one family line at all. It might go back further than any family tree could ever trace. Like, before-recorded-history further."

"How much further are we talking?"

"Possibly all the way back. Like, before humans really spread out across the world at all. Which would mean it's not in your family specifically. It could be in basically anyone's family, depending on which ancient branch they descend from, and there'd be no way to trace it forward through normal genealogy because it's not really a bloodline thing anymore. It's older than bloodlines as we'd even understand them." She frowned at her own notes, tapping a particular line twice. "There's one thing that doesn't fully fit, though. Some of the dating in the source doesn't line up cleanly, like the text itself isn't sure when things actually happened relative to each other. I keep assuming that's just old, sloppy scholarship. Probably is. Still bugs me a little."

Xander was quiet for a long moment. "So this could just show up in anybody. Random. No warning, no family history, nothing."

"That's what it's looking like. Which means whoever else might have this — if anybody currently does — probably has no more idea why than you did before it happened to you."

"That's a lot bigger than I was expecting this to get."

"Yeah," Willow admitted. "Me too, honestly. I wanted a tidy answer. A specific relative, a name, something I could hand you like a solved mystery. Instead I got 'possibly older than agriculture,' which is not exactly the tidy bow I was hoping to wrap this up with."

"Are you disappointed?"

"A little. Mostly I'm just — recalibrating. The question got bigger, not smaller. That's allowed to happen in real research, even if it's annoying when it does." She managed a tired smile. "I'm not gonna stop looking. I just have to look somewhere different now."

"Where different?"

"I don't know yet. Maybe Giles has something in the Watcher archives that touches on it — if this really does go back that far, there's a chance it crosses paths with something in Slayer history, given how old that goes too. I haven't asked him yet. Wanted to have more than a hunch before I did."

"You've got more than a hunch now. You've got a honestly huge, slightly terrifying open question."

"When you put it that way, it sounds a lot more impressive than it feels sitting here on my bedroom floor at midnight." Willow gathered the printouts together, squaring the edges with more care than the moment strictly required. "I think it's time to actually ask Giles. Properly. Tomorrow."

"I'm coming with you."

"I was hoping you would."
