Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen showed Xander Harris standing behind Sunnydale High’s football field inside a square marked by traffic cones.

“In a world where most teenagers spend the weekend sleeping late, avoiding homework, and making regrettable social decisions, one teenager has a standing appointment with an uncontrollable interdimensional wilderness commute.”

The cones vanished. So did Sunnydale.

“Same time next weekend.”

This is Working for the Weekend: Bridge Over Troubled Water.

Watch Xander get pulled once a week into a random, uninhabited version of Earth for three subjective days, with no control over the destination, no guarantee of ever returning, and a backpack whose inventory suggests several previous weekends ended with highly specific new rules.

Water purifier. Machete. Collapsible fish tank. Tranquilizer pistol. Bear mace. Air horn. Portable shed.

“That isn’t camping gear,” the narrator said. “That’s a patch history.”

Follow him across untouched grasslands, forests, deserts, and coastlines while he catalogs extinct animals, retrieves impossible specimens, and learns that “random world every week” is less a superpower than an extremely aggressive continuing-education program in field biology.

Watch a giant ground sloth arrive behind the football field and become several adults’ problem with no advance notice.

Meet Buffy and Willow, who eventually join the jumps, turning Xander’s solitary exile into a three-person relationship, expedition team, and recurring reminder that the phrase “family camping trip” can include prehistoric megafauna.

Meet Joyce, whose contribution to interdimensional exploration consists of hugs, food, spare rooms, and the firm conviction that every apocalypse is easier to manage after everybody has eaten.

Meet Faith in a highway motel after Diana’s death, where Joyce offers her a room without a clipboard, Council terms, or a demand for gratitude.

The narrator paused on the doorway.

“The story contains multiversal collapse, gods, portals, alien technology, and a hammer. One of its most important acts is still a woman asking a frightened girl whether she wants somewhere safer to sleep. We’re not making that smaller.”

Then watch the crossover radius expand.

Meet Davy, Millie, and Cent Rice on the one wildside world Xander unexpectedly reaches twice. Meet Charlie and the fixed wooden gate. Meet Miriam and the civilization called Home. Meet Thor, Natasha, Fury, Tony, Steve, Bruce, Strange, Wong, and enough different Earths that the word “home” requires capitalization rules.

The door in Colorado is literal wood with hinges and a padlock. The emergency Gate is a luminous tear in reality. Tony’s relay piggybacks on residual dimensional signatures. Xander’s weekly pull does whatever it wants.

“Please consult the travel appendix,” the narrator said. “No, not that door. The other door.”

Watch Tony Stark respond to a cross-reality communication problem by building a device that rides the wake of someone else’s portal, then scale it up enough to drive a minivan through.

Ninety percent confidence.

“The other ten percent was apparently ‘we become a continuity error.’”

Then face the Convergence: realities destabilizing, Home reconnecting, the whole expanding family discovering the wildside mechanism is part of something much larger, older, and less accidental than a weird weekend curse.

Miriam’s team crosses. Xander carries more people through his personal jump than he has ever tried. Strange helps keep existence from becoming an optional setting. Everyone survives, then immediately schedules another ordinary trip because this series refuses to let cosmic scale take the ordinary heart away from it.

## The Bridge Over Troubled Water Drinking Game

Take a drink every time Xander returns with an animal no local veterinarian was trained to treat.

Take two whenever somebody says “long story” and is forced to provide the long version before dawn.

Drink whenever a new world requires clarification about which side is tame, wild, Home, or MCU.

Take a sip whenever Joyce asks whether it was a good trip.

Finish your drink when Tony drives a minivan between realities on ninety-percent confidence.

Do not drink every time a chapter number contains a decimal. The interstitial POV structure is deliberate; your hospitalization would not be.

## Starring

Xander Harris as Wildside Gig Economy Employee of the Month.

Buffy and Willow as Two Girls, One Random Dimensional Pull.

Joyce Summers as Bed, Breakfast, and Apocalypse Management.

Faith as No Clipboard, No Council, One Spare Room.

Davy, Millie, and Cent as The Other Teleporting Family You Meet While Teleporting.

Charlie as The Boy Behind the Correct Wooden Door.

Tony Stark as Interdimensional Minivan Engineer.

Thor as Residual Signature Donor.

Doctor Strange as Continuity Repair Wizard.

And Xander’s backpack as Mary Poppins’ Tactical Field Kit.

Alternate titles appeared:

Into the Wildside-Verse.

The Weekend Has No Cancellation Policy.

One Hundred Fifty-Four Chapters and a Very Full Kitchen.

The narrator looked at the title already printed on the manuscript.

“Simon and Garfunkel did the naming work for us.”

The card appeared.

# BRIDGE OVER TROUBLED WATER

Directed by: a weekly schedule with no regard for emergencies.

Written by: every crossover door somebody forgot to close.

Field support by: a backpack containing the exact consequence of last week’s mistake.

Special thanks to: Joyce’s front door, which stayed open even when reality did not.

The last screen showed Xander, Buffy, and Willow in an uncomplicated meadow after the crisis.

“Good one?” Joyce asked when they returned.

“Really good one.”

The narrator let the ordinary answer stand.

“Same time next weekend.”
