Chapter 149

The working came together over the following days with the frantic, focused energy of people racing a clock none of them could actually see, only feel, ticking down somewhere just out of view, sleep becoming increasingly optional for everyone involved, the whole household running on coffee and adrenaline and a determination that came from truly understanding what was actually at stake.

Strange handled the sorcery, weaving protective wards through diagrams that looked, to Xander's untrained eye, like nothing so much as an enormously complicated piece of abstract art, gold light tracing patterns across the shop's back room late into the night, the Cloak occasionally shifting to catch and redirect stray sparks before they could scorch anything nearby. Ilsa and Petra worked the gate technology, adapting Home's careful, controlled crossing mechanisms into something that could, hopefully, function as reinforcement rather than passage, their brass instruments spread across an entire table, components disassembled and reassembled a dozen different ways as they tested configuration after configuration. Fury coordinated logistics with quiet, efficient competence — someone who'd clearly run notably more dangerous operations than this one, on paper at least, arranging perimeter security and evacuation contingencies with the same calm he'd have applied to any operation, regardless of scale.

Buffy went to get it herself, given she was the one who'd actually proven she could carry it without strain, jogging upstairs to her own room where it had leaned against the closet door for months now, faithful and unremarkable as a coat rack right up until it mattered.

Buffy carried it home herself that evening, and it sat in her room overnight, propped against her closet door like an oddly serious houseguest, before she brought it over to Faith the next morning.

"Feels weird, handing this off," Buffy admitted, setting it down on Faith's bed. "Like I should say something meaningful. I don't actually have anything meaningful to say. Mostly just — good luck. You've got this."

"That's meaningful enough," Faith said, and took it up, the weight settling into her grip like it had simply been waiting for an actual destination all along.

Faith trained with the hammer every evening after that, Thor beside her, walking her through weight and balance and the discipline of wielding something that responded to more than muscle alone, the two of them working late into the dark behind the Summers house, the hammer's arcs catching moonlight with each controlled swing.

"You're doing well," Thor told her, on the fourth evening, watching her complete another controlled swing, sweat beading at her temples despite the cool night air. "Better than I expected, given how little time we've had to prepare properly."

"Feels like I've been preparing for this longer than a week, honestly. Just didn't know what for."

"Perhaps you have been. The hammer judged you ready some time before this crisis ever demanded proof of it."

Faith considered that, turning it over, resting the hammer's head against the ground for a moment's breather. "Guess that tracks. Feels like everything's been building toward something, this whole year. Just didn't expect it to be this."

"Few of us ever expect the actual shape our preparation was building toward," Thor said. "That rarely makes the preparation any less real, once the moment finally arrives."

"You nervous? For me, I mean. Not for yourself."

Thor considered the question with real seriousness before answering. "Yes. Considerably. But nervousness and confidence are not opposites, Faith. I am nervous because this matters. I am confident because I have watched you become exactly the person capable of meeting it."

"That's a genuinely good answer. Wasn't expecting a honestly good answer."

"I contain multitudes," Thor said, and Faith actually laughed despite the exhaustion, the sound cutting welcome through the tense evening air.

Xander found his own role considerably less clear than everyone else's — no sorcery, no gate technology, no ancient weapon judging his worthiness, just an ability that had started all of this without ever fully explaining itself, and he found himself, more than once during those frantic days, hovering at the edges of conversations he didn't fully understand, feeling useful mostly in the smallest, most unglamorous ways — fetching supplies, keeping everyone fed alongside Joyce, listening when Faith needed to talk through her nerves without anyone actually solving anything.

"You're the variable nobody can quite account for," Giles told him, during one of their planning sessions, not unkindly, setting down the book he'd been cross-referencing for the past hour. "Your ability predates the Convergence, connects to it, yet doesn't behave according to any of the systems the rest of us understand. That may prove more valuable than any of us currently realize, or it may prove entirely incidental. I actually don't know which."

"Reassuring."

"I did warn you honesty wouldn't always be comfortable." Giles managed a tired, genuine smile. "I want you to hear this part clearly, Xander — whatever role you end up playing in this, the fact that you exist at all, that your ability manifested the way it did, may itself be part of why any of us are equipped to face this at all. Don't discount your own significance simply because it doesn't fit neatly into anyone else's framework."

"I'll try to remember that when everything's actually happening."

"Do. I suspect you'll need the reminder."

Cent found him on the porch that same night, both of them taking a rare, quiet break from the frenetic preparation happening inside, and sat beside him without saying anything for a long moment, just letting the silence hold whatever needed holding.

"You okay?" she asked finally.

"Getting there. Feels weird, watching everyone else have a clear job while I'm just kind of... around."

"Being around counts for more than you think. Trust me. I've watched my parents handle enough crises to know the person keeping everyone fed and steady matters just as much as the person wielding the actual weapon."

"Yeah. Guess I know that too, somewhere. Just hard to remember in the moment."

"That's what I'm here for, then. Reminding you." She bumped his shoulder gently. "Whatever happens, you already did the hardest part. You found this. You brought everyone together who's actually capable of fixing it. That's not nothing, Xander. That's the whole reason any of us are even here."

The preparations continued, day into night into day again, everyone working toward a deadline that grew steadily, uncomfortably closer, until finally, on the seventh morning, Ilsa's instruments registered a spike significantly sharper than anything they'd tracked before, an alarm chirping insistently from her equipment loud enough to bring the whole household running.

"It's accelerating faster than the model predicted," she said, voice tight, reading figures that made even Strange's composed expression falter slightly. "We don't have weeks anymore. We might not even have days."

The room went very still, the theoretical crisis they'd been racing toward suddenly, undeniably, about to become real.
