Chapter 50

Willow had a family obligation and Buffy was covering a double patrol shift, which left the canyon to Xander alone that week.

The canyon walls rose on either side of him almost immediately, deep red rock striped with pale bands that spoke of more time than Xander could properly wrap his head around, and he spent his first few minutes just craning his neck up at them rather than paying attention to anything at ground level.

"Okay, this is honestly beautiful," he admitted, to nobody, since nobody was around to appreciate the observation with him. "Willow's gonna be so mad she can't see this in person."

He followed the canyon floor for most of the first day, a dry riverbed underfoot that suggested water ran through here at least occasionally, and found the paintings on a sheltered rock face late in the afternoon — handprints, mostly, dozens of them overlapping in ochre and red, alongside simpler shapes that might have been animals if he squinted.

He stood in front of them for a long time, camera forgotten in his hand.

'Somebody made these,' he thought, the realization landing slower than it probably should have. 'Not recently. Not this world's current empty version. Somebody was here once, and now they're not, and this is all that's left of them.' It was a different kind of discovery than the animals — quieter, heavier, the first real evidence he'd found on any of these worlds that people had existed here before whatever had emptied them out.

He photographed the paintings carefully, respectfully, more mindful of his own presence than he usually bothered to be around wildlife, and made careful notes about position and condition for reasons that had less to do with Willow's biome chart and more to do with a feeling he didn't entirely have words for yet.

The rest of the trip passed quietly under that same weight, canyon wrens calling from ledges too high to photograph well, the dry riverbed eventually widening into a shallow pool that gave him water enough to restock without much effort, everything about the place carrying a stillness that felt less like emptiness and more like aftermath.

He rode the pull home with paintings photographed from every angle he could manage, water restocked, and a question sitting in his chest that four years of collecting animals had never quite prepared him to ask out loud.

"You're doing the quiet thing again," Buffy said, the second he walked through the door, though gentler this time, like she'd learned something from the volcano trip about how to ask.

"There were paintings. Handprints, mostly. People, once, on a world I've always just thought of as empty." He set his pack down slower than usual. "I don't know why it's hitting me this hard. I've known these worlds used to have people, theoretically. Seeing actual proof just made it real in a way I wasn't ready for."

"What do you think happened to them?"

"I don't know. I don't know if I want to know, honestly. Some part of me would rather just imagine they left somewhere better instead of finding out otherwise." He managed a small, tired smile. "Willow's gonna want the coordinates. I'll give them to her. I just — I don't know if I want her turning this one into just another data point yet. Feels like it deserves more than a chart entry."

"I think she'd understand that, if you told her." Buffy crossed the room and wrapped her arms around him, quiet and steady, not saying anything else for a long moment, just holding on.

"Yeah. Probably. I'll figure out how to say it." He looked at Buffy properly for the first time since walking in, forehead dropping to rest against hers. "Thanks for asking. For noticing there was something to ask about."

"Always," Buffy said. "That's kind of the whole point of this, right? Somebody's gotta be here when you get back, ready to actually listen instead of just wanting the highlight reel."

"Yeah," Xander said. "That's exactly the point."

50.5. What the Paintings Meant

He hadn't meant to see it. He'd only borrowed her laptop to check a jump schedule, and the document was already open, minimized but not closed, from whatever she'd been working on before dinner pulled her away — the canyon photos arranged in a careful grid down one side, and beside them, notes in Willow's familiar, cramped handwriting-style typing, clearly written for no audience but herself.

These aren't random, the note read. Look at the placement. Every panel's got a clear sightline from the canyon floor — nothing tucked away, nothing hidden. Whoever made these wanted them seen. Not just by whoever was there at the time. By whoever came after.

I don't think this is a message. I think it's an invitation. People don't paint like this — this deliberately, this visibly — if they think nobody's ever coming back. You paint like this if some part of you believes somebody eventually will, and you want them to know you were here. That you mattered. That you're glad, on some level, they finally showed up to see it.

I haven't shown Xander this yet. I don't think he wants it turned into a data point, and he's right not to want that. But I keep thinking — maybe they didn't just vanish. Maybe they went somewhere. Somewhere else in whatever this whole tangled mess of worlds actually is. And this wasn't an ending. It was a goodbye, from people who figured somebody like him might eventually wander through and want to know they'd been real.

I could be wrong. I don't have proof, just a feeling and some pretty compelling placement geometry. But I don't think he found a grave out there. I think he found a letter. It just took four years and the right person walking through that canyon before anybody was actually there to read it.

I'll tell him when he's ready to hear it as a letter and not a chart entry. Not before.

Xander stared at the laptop a long moment, something in his chest loosening that had been tight since the canyon, and something else utterly — warmer, quieter — settling in right behind it, the specific ache of finding out someone had already understood him this completely without ever once needing to be asked.

He closed the document gently, the way you'd close a door on a room you weren't supposed to be in yet, and found her in the kitchen twenty minutes later, and didn't say anything about what he'd seen — not that night, not for another few days, waiting instead for a quiet moment that felt like it had actually been earned.

"I think I'm ready to talk about the paintings properly," he said, eventually, once the moment arrived on its own.

Willow looked up from her notebook, something careful and hopeful crossing her face. "Yeah? Okay. I'd like that."

"I think you already know what I'm gonna say, though. Some part of me thinks you got there before I did."

Something flickered across her expression — caught, not quite guilty, more like someone realizing a private thought had found its way into the open anyway. "Maybe. I've been sitting with some theories."

"Tell me anyway. I want to hear you say it."

So she did, careful and unhurried, the same words Xander had already read once in secret landing on him a second time with no less weight for the repetition, and by the time she finished, he'd crossed the kitchen and pulled her into a proper hug, holding on much longer than the moment strictly required.

"Thanks," he said, finally. "For not turning it into just a data point. And for understanding, before I even asked you to."

"Wouldn't dream of doing either," she said, warm against his shoulder. "Some things deserve to stay a letter, not a chart entry."
