Chapter 60

Joyce's list got its strangest entry yet the following Sunday: my daughter and her sister-in-everything-but-blood successfully contained a half-demon summoning using a ritual they learned from a library book, and I made lasagna afterward because I didn't know what else to do with my hands.

"You wrote all that?" Buffy asked, reading over her shoulder.

"I needed somewhere to put the feelings. The list seemed appropriate."

"That's the longest entry you've ever written."

"It was the longest week I've ever had," Joyce said, passing the lasagna down the table. "I'm allowed extra words for it."

Faith, significantly more rested than she'd looked all week, reached for the garlic bread with something closer to her usual energy. "For the record, Giles wants the mural studied properly starting tomorrow. Says whatever's contained in there is truly rare and he'd like to understand it better before deciding what to actually do with it long-term."

"Define 'do with it,'" Xander said, reaching over to take Buffy's hand under the table, the small gesture more about needing the contact himself than anything performative. "Because I'm hoping that doesn't mean it lives in the Magic Box basement indefinitely."

Buffy squeezed back, leaning her head briefly against his shoulder before straightening back up for dinner, neither of them making a thing of it.

"Don't know yet. That's tomorrow's problem. Tonight I'm just eating lasagna and not thinking about summoning circles for at least a few hours."

"Solid plan. I fully endorse that plan."

Willow, notebook uncharacteristically absent from the table for once, looked genuinely relaxed for the first time in over a week. "I already started writing up what I observed. Not for the chart — this needs its own separate file. This was honestly new information, magically speaking."

"Everything's data to you," Faith said, with real fondness rather than the usual teasing edge.

"It's how I process things. Also, purely as an aside, the light patterns during the containment were actually fascinating from a purely observational standpoint, completely separate from how terrified I was the whole time."

"Multitasking," Xander said. "Truly impressive multitasking, being terrified and taking mental notes simultaneously."

"Turns out I've got a talent for that too," Willow said with a grin, and the table laughed harder at that than the joke probably deserved, the kind of laughter that came easier after a week this heavy finally lifted.

Jesse, who'd shown up for dinner on the strength of having heard "there was a demon thing" and refusing to be left out of the debrief, leaned in with genuine curiosity. "Okay, but the actual scary part — what did it feel like? The pressure thing, right before it got contained?"

Faith considered the question seriously, more seriously than she usually gave anything at this table. "Like something enormous was leaning against a door I was holding shut with just my voice. That's the closest I can describe it. Scariest ten minutes of my whole life, and I've fought actual vampires hand to hand."

"That's really terrifying."

"It was. I'm allowed to say that now that it's over and everybody's okay."

"You're allowed to say it either way," Buffy said. "Before, during, after. Doesn't matter when. It was scary. That's just true."

"Yeah," Faith agreed. "It really was."

The table settled into its usual comfortable noise after that, the heaviest week in recent memory finally allowed to loosen its grip, Joyce refilling glasses, Xander already angling the conversation toward his next jump in three days like normal life had simply been waiting patiently for its turn to resume, and for the first time in over a week, nobody at that table had anything more urgent to think about than whether there was enough lasagna left for seconds.

60.5. Chapter 60.5

Xander went alone this time, the whole household still quietly recovering from the ritual, everyone needing a few days of ordinary distance from anything resembling a crisis before jumping back into anything else.

The world was wetland, reeds taller than his head stretching in every direction, water underfoot in every step no matter how carefully he picked his route.

"Okay," he said, already soaked to the knees. "Wet one. Very wet. Noted."

He spent the first day working through the reeds slowly, cataloguing insects in colors bright enough to seem almost artificial, and found the birds on the second morning — long-legged waders moving through the shallows with careful, deliberate steps, hunting something beneath the water's surface with a patience that put his own four years of practice to shame.

'Nice change of pace,' he thought, wading in close enough to get a proper look instead of just working the zoom. 'No crisis. No countdown. Just birds and reeds and getting soaked, same as any regular trip.' One of them let him get close enough to actually crouch beside it, entirely unbothered, more interested in the water than in him, and he held very still until it stepped near enough that scooping it up took no more effort than reaching out a hand. It felt good, settling back into the simple, uncomplicated rhythm of the actual job, after weeks of something so much bigger sitting over everything.

The rest of the trip passed quietly, wetland stretching endless and calm in every direction, and by the time the jump pulled him home, he had bird photos, waterlogged boots, one genuinely delighted new arrival for Alan's collection, and nothing more demanding behind him than patience and good aim with a camera.

Willow kissed him hello despite the mud clinging to his boots, then held him at arm's length, studying his face the way she'd been doing all week. "So?"

"Quiet. Wet, but quiet. Exactly what I needed."

"I think we all needed something like that, honestly," Willow said, and Xander found himself agreeing completely, both of them just standing there a moment in the doorway, in no hurry to go be anywhere else yet.
