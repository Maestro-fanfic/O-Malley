Chapter 59

The mural was glowing faintly by the time they arrived, the accumulated symbols catching moonlight in a way that felt less like reflection and more like the wall itself had started producing its own light from somewhere underneath the paint.

"Positions," Giles said, quiet but firm, and the four of them moved to the cardinal points around the wall without further discussion, weeks of rehearsal taking over where nerves might otherwise have slowed them down.

"I'm at north," Xander said, mostly to hear his own voice do something other than shake. "Still north. Claimed it fair and square three weeks ago, still holding it now, under actual pressure, exactly like I said I would."

"Nobody's contesting your corner, Xander."

"I know. I'm just narrating. It's helping. Let me narrate."

Faith stood at the center, counter-rite text held steady despite her hands not being completely steady themselves, and looked around the circle one last time before beginning — Buffy at the west, solid and ready; Xander at the north, visibly pale but standing firm; Giles at the south, expression carved into the same careful calm he brought to everything that actually mattered.

"Whatever happens," she said, to all of them, "thank you. For being here. For believing this was real weeks before anybody had proof."

"Always," Buffy said. "Now do the thing, before I lose my nerve standing here being sentimental about it."

"Same, honestly," Xander added. "The sentimental window's closing fast over here."

Faith almost laughed, the tension breaking just enough to let her actually start.

The incantation felt different from rehearsal immediately — the words carrying real weight this time, the air around the mural thickening with something Faith could feel against her skin like pressure before a storm. Halfway through, the wall's glow intensified sharply, symbols flaring bright enough to cast hard shadows across all four of their faces.

"That's new," Xander said, through gritted teeth, gripping his corner of the wall like it might try to leave without him. "That's a new light situation. Nobody rehearsed the light situation."

"Hold," Giles called out, voice tight but controlled. "Whatever you feel, hold your positions."

"Holding! Very much holding! Just also narrating the holding, for my own benefit!"

The pressure built, and built, and Faith kept speaking through it, words she'd rehearsed a dozen times now feeling like they were being pulled out of her rather than simply spoken. Somewhere to her left she could hear Xander muttering a steady stream of half-panicked commentary under his breath — "okay, okay, that's a lot of pressure, that's a lot of pressure for a Tuesday" — and found, absurdly, that the sound of it actually helped, something familiar and human to hold onto in the middle of something that felt anything but.

At the ritual's peak the mural cracked open — not physically, but in some other sense, a seam of pure white light splitting down its center, something on the other side of it pressing against the opening like it could feel the barrier weakening.

Xander didn't say anything. His hand went white-knuckled against the stone, jaw locked, every scrap of narration finally failing him at the one moment that actually deserved it.

"It's working," Willow shouted, from outside the circle where she'd been monitoring with her own notes clutched tight. "The containment's forming, I can see it, keep going!"

Faith pushed through the final lines, voice cracking on the last word, and the white light snapped inward instead of outward, the pressure collapsing all at once into something considerably calmer — contained, present, but no longer straining against the edges of whatever cage the counter-rite had built around it.

Silence, for a long moment. Nobody moved.

"Is it over?" Xander asked finally, voice still shaking, hand still locked against his corner of the wall like it hadn't gotten the memo yet.

"I believe so," Giles said, though he didn't release his own position yet, watching the mural carefully for any sign of renewed pressure. "The containment appears to be holding."

"Can I let go of the wall now, or is this a trick question."

"You may let go of the wall, Xander."

"Thank god." He peeled his hand off the stone like it had personally betrayed him, flexing his fingers with visible relief. "For the record, that was the longest ten minutes of my entire life, and I say that as a man who has been chased by bees."

They stayed in position another five full minutes before Giles finally gave the word, and Faith's legs nearly gave out the moment she stepped back from the center, exhaustion crashing over her all at once now that the adrenaline had nowhere left to go.

Buffy caught her before she fully went down, both of them ending up in an undignified heap against the pavement, laughing and shaking in roughly equal measure.

"We did it," Faith said, into Buffy's shoulder, voice muffled and disbelieving. "We actually did it."

"We actually did it," Buffy confirmed. "You did it. This was yours from the start, and you saw it through to the end."

"We all did it. Team effort. Very team effort." Faith pulled back enough to look at the others — Xander still shaking out his hand like the stone had actually wronged him; Giles carefully examining the now-dormant mural with something like scholarly satisfaction underneath his obvious relief; Willow already scribbling notes about the containment's exact appearance, because apparently no crisis was too large to also be data.

"Whatever's actually in there," Giles said, straightening up from his examination, "it's contained. Properly, thoroughly contained. I'd like to study it further, at length, but not tonight. Tonight, I think we've all earned notably less academic pursuits."

"I vote food," Xander said. "A lot of food. And also, permanently, no more claiming corners of anything ever again, even for bragging rights."

"Duly noted," Giles said, and something in his exhausted, relieved expression suggested he found the narration really fond rather than exasperating now that it was safely over.

They made their way back to the cars slowly, exhausted in the loose, hollowed-out way that only comes after real fear has finally, fully passed, and found Joyce waiting exactly where she'd promised to be — close enough to see them coming, far enough to have stayed safely out of the way, already moving to meet them the moment she saw Faith walking upright and whole.
