Chapter 30

The sandwich debate had, over the course of several weeks, evolved from a running joke into something closer to an actual institution, complete with its own history, its own casualties, and its own increasingly elaborate rules of engagement.

"New evidence," Jesse announced, sliding into the booth with a printout he'd clearly gone to some effort to produce, immediately shoving Xander further into the corner with one shoulder to make room. "I looked it up. Actual dictionary definition of sandwich."

"You did research," Faith said with a grin. "For the bit. I respect the commitment."

"It says 'two or more slices of bread with a filling between them.' Key word, slices. Plural, flat. A hot dog bun is not slices."

"It's a sliced roll," Willow pointed out. "Technically."

"Don't help him."

"I'm not helping him, I'm being accurate. There's a difference."

"There's really not, not right now, not when I'm losing."

"You're not losing," Buffy said, leaning back against Xander's shoulder without really thinking about it, the way she'd started doing whenever they shared a booth. "There's no scoring system. Nobody's losing."

"I'm losing," Jesse said. "I can feel it happening."

Xander, who'd been quietly eating fries through most of this, one arm settled loosely around Buffy without breaking his focus on the argument, finally looked up. "Can I propose a third category? Not sandwich, not not-sandwich. Just its own thing. Hot dog. Singular classification. Ends the debate, everybody wins."

The table went silent for a moment, considering this with more seriousness than the suggestion probably deserved.

"That's actually not terrible," Faith said.

"It's a cop-out," Jesse said, flicking a fry across the table that bounced off Xander's forehead. "It's an answer designed to make everybody comfortable instead of actually resolving anything."

"Rude," Xander said, unbothered, eating the fry anyway. "Also, most good answers are cop-outs. I contain multitudes of diplomatic solutions nobody asked for."

"I liked it better when you were just tired from getting attacked by a lizard."

"The lizard didn't attack me. The lizard and I had a respectful, distant relationship."

"Every animal you meet has a respectful, distant relationship with you, according to you."

"Because it's true. I'm very good at respecting personal space. Ask the sloth."

"The sloth didn't have a choice, Xander, it's a sloth."

"Consent's complicated in the animal kingdom. I do my best."

"You'd know all about complicated consent conversations, wouldn't you," Jesse said, waggling his eyebrows in Buffy's general direction, which earned him a swift kick under the table from Willow.

"Do NOT," Willow said.

"I said nothing incriminating!"

"You implied plenty. Xander's face is doing a thing."

"My face isn't doing a thing," Xander said, his face very much doing a thing.

Willow snorted into her drink, hard enough that Faith had to thump her on the back, and the whole table dissolved into the kind of loose, easy laughter that came from nothing in certain being especially funny except that they were all tired and it was Friday and nobody had anywhere urgent to be for the next few hours. Buffy tipped her head back against Xander's shoulder, laughing too, and he pressed a quick, unthinking kiss to the top of her hair without breaking stride in the conversation, the gesture so automatic by now that nobody at the table even bothered commenting on it anymore.

"Okay," Jesse said, once he'd recovered, still grinning. "New rule. No more animal consent jokes at the sandwich table. Different table for that."

"There's no different table, Jesse, it's the same table."

"Then no more animal consent jokes, period. I'm instituting a rule."

"You don't get to institute rules," Faith said. "You lost the sandwich debate. Rule-making privileges revoked."

"That's not how any of this works."

"It's exactly how this works," Faith said with a grin. "Welcome to the table."

"I hate it here," Jesse said, without any real conviction, already reaching to steal a fry back off Xander's plate in retaliation. He caught sight of Cordelia passing the booth a moment later, sitting up a little straighter on reflex, same as always. "Okay, unrelated — think today's the day she finally notices me?"

"No," the entire table said, in near-perfect unison, without even looking up.

"Rude, but fair. Statistically consistent with every other day."
