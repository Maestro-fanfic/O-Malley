Chapter 33

The vampire nest turned out to be smaller than expected and considerably more argumentative, which Buffy hadn't realized was possible until she was standing in the middle of it listening to two vampires debate feeding order instead of actually attacking.

"You went first last time," the taller one said.

"That's not even true, I let you go first because you whined about it."

"I did not whine, I made a reasonable case."

"Can we focus," Buffy said, staking the taller one mid-sentence before he could finish his reasonable case. "This is a fight. You're supposed to be fighting."

The second vampire looked really offended on his friend's behalf, which lasted right up until Buffy staked him too, and the whole thing was over inside two minutes, significantly less dramatic than the argument leading up to it.

"That's it?" Faith asked, arriving a beat too late to actually help, stake still in hand. "I heard yelling. I was expecting more."

"They were arguing about who got to bite the mannequin they apparently found in an alley."

"A mannequin."

"A mannequin. I don't think either of them had actually killed anyone. I think they just found something vaguely person-shaped and got excited about it."

Faith snorted. "That's genuinely the saddest vampire story I've heard all year."

"It's up there."

They cleared the rest of the block without incident, the ordinary rhythm of a patrol night settling in once the mannequin situation resolved itself, and ended up sitting on a low wall near the cemetery entrance for a few minutes afterward, the kind of comfortable silence that came after a fight that hadn't actually needed much effort.

"How's the symbol thing going?" Buffy asked, eventually.

"Slow. Giles thinks it's some kind of territory claim. Still don't know whose." Faith turned her stake over in her hands, not quite fidgeting, more just keeping her hands busy. "It's weird, having something that's actually mine to work on instead of just tagging along on whatever you and Giles already figured out."

"You're not tagging along. You never have been."

"Feels different this time. Like I found it, not like I got looped in after the fact." Faith shrugged, a little self-conscious about the admission. "I don't know. Feels good, is all. Wanted to say that."

"I'm glad," Buffy said with a smile. "You should have more of those. Things that are actually yours."

"Don't get sappy on me, B."

"Too late. I already did it."

"Fine. One sentence. I'll allow it." Faith stood, stretching out a shoulder that had taken a glancing hit earlier in the fight. "C'mon. Still got the west side to cover before either of us calls it a night."

"After you."

"Age before beauty," Faith said with a grin, already walking.

"We're the same age."

"Details."

33.5. Chapter 33.5

Giles examined the hammer for the better part of an hour, still planted in the Summers' flowerbed exactly where it had appeared, crouched at the edge of the dirt with the particular stillness he brought to anything he suspected might matter far more than it first appeared to.

"No footprints," he confirmed, echoing Buffy's own observation from earlier. "No disturbance to the soil beyond the hammer's own placement. Nothing dragged, nothing dropped from height — the flowers immediately surrounding it aren't even crushed, which they very much should be if this had simply fallen."

"So it didn't fall. It just — arrived," Buffy said.

"That would be my working assumption, yes, unsettling as it is." Giles reached out, hesitated, then touched the haft carefully with one finger, testing nothing in specific beyond his own nerve. "The head is wrong for a modern reproduction — too heavy at the balance point, the engraving worn in patterns that don't match any decorative style I can immediately place. I believe I may have seen something like it before, though it will take me some time to confirm where."

"You gonna take it? Study it properly?"

"I'd rather not move it more than necessary, given how little we understand about why it's here or what conditions brought it. I'll want to research first, examine second." Giles straightened up, brushing dirt from his knees, glancing between the two girls with real gravity. "In the meantime, I would ask that neither of you attempt to lift it again until I've had a chance to understand what we're actually dealing with."

"Kind of already did that part," Faith admitted. "This morning. Before I called Giles."

"And?"

"Buffy picked it up like it was nothing. I couldn't budge it with both hands and everything I had."

Giles went very still at that, something crossing his face that neither girl had quite seen from him before — not alarm exactly, more the look of a man quietly filing away a detail he suspected he'd need later.

"That's..." he started, then stopped himself. "I'd like some time to turn that information over before I say anything more definitive. Buffy — would you mind lifting it once more, for my own observation?"

Buffy did, easily, the same effortless lightness as before, and Giles watched the whole motion with the intensity of someone memorizing it for later recall.

"Extraordinary," he said, quietly, mostly to himself. "Set it back down, if you would. Precisely where it stood."

She did, and Giles spent a long moment simply looking at it, planted once more in the pansies like it had never moved at all.

"I'll be in touch," he said finally. "This may take some time to properly understand. I'd ask you both to be patient, and to say nothing of it beyond this porch until I have more to offer than speculation."

"Yeah," Buffy said, still looking at the hammer with something unresolved behind her eyes. "Take your time. It's not exactly going anywhere."

She thought about it, on and off, for the rest of the day, the strange easy lightness of something that had apparently refused, just as easily, to move for somebody else.

Faith didn't think about it at all, or told herself she didn't, and if some small, old, familiar feeling sat a little heavier in her chest than usual that evening, she didn't examine it closely enough to give it a name.
