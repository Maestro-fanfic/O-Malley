Chapter 70

Buffy and Willow were both still deep in the aftermath of the mural discovery, poring over texts with Giles late into most nights, which left the forest to Xander alone that week.

The forest this time was old in a way Xander could feel before he could articulate why — trees wider than cars, canopy so dense it turned midday into permanent dusk on the ground below, moss thick enough to swallow his boots up to the ankle in places.

"Okay, this one's got atmosphere," he said, voice dropping instinctively to match the hush of the place. "This one feels like it's got opinions about visitors."

He moved carefully through the first day, more out of respect for the sheer age of everything around him than any specific danger he'd identified, cataloguing fungi in colors he hadn't known existed and a woodpecker-adjacent bird whose call echoed strangely off trunks wide enough to swallow sound.

The owls found him on the second night, or rather he found them, a whole family perched in the hollow of an ancient trunk, eyes catching his flashlight beam with the flat, unblinking patience of creatures that had never once needed to fear anything walking upright on two legs.

"Hey," he said, quietly, not wanting to disturb them. "Didn't mean to interrupt. Carry on."

They didn't carry on, exactly, but they didn't flee either, watching him with mild interest until he'd gotten his photos and backed away, leaving them to whatever ancient owl business he'd briefly interrupted.

'Willow's gonna want measurements on the trees too,' he thought, running a hand along bark rough enough to catch on his palm. 'Not just the animals this time. This forest's a specimen all its own.' It felt like an important addition to make, the kind of thing that had started mattering more to him lately than it used to — not just cataloguing the strange and dangerous, but truly documenting what these worlds actually were, patiently, thoroughly, for reasons that went beyond simple collection now.

The rest of the trip passed in that same reverent quiet, the forest never quite explaining itself but never threatening him either, and by the time the jump pulled him home he had photos of trees that might well predate agriculture back on his own world, and a lingering, unshakeable sense of having stood somewhere honestly ancient.

He found the house quieter than usual when he got back, Willow and Giles' research materials still spread across the kitchen table from the night before, the weight of everything they'd found together sitting almost visibly in the air.

"Big trees," he said, setting his camera down gently among the scattered books. "Genuinely ancient-feeling place. Kind of matches the mood around here lately, honestly."

Willow looked up, managing a tired but real smile. "That's either a coincidence or the universe having a sense of humor about timing."

"I'm choosing to believe it's the universe. Feels more fun that way."

"Fair enough." She reached for his camera, already curious despite everything else weighing on her. "Show me the owls. I need something good today."

"You've got it," Xander said, and settled in beside her, pressing a kiss to her temple before pulling up the photos, the two of them finding a small, quiet pocket of ordinary comfort in the middle of everything else that had gotten so much bigger than either of them expected.

70.5. Chapter 70.5

Dr. Sanjay Okafor, who'd inherited Marcus Webb's old position after his retirement, made his first and, as it turned out, only trip that autumn — a single, carefully arranged expedition that took nearly as much bureaucratic maneuvering as Webb's original had, the practice apparently not having gotten any easier to arrange in the intervening years.

"I heard about Webb's trip when I interviewed for this position," Sanjay admitted, adjusting his own gear with visible nervousness. "Never imagined I'd actually get the chance myself."

The world was coastal scrubland, and Sanjay spent the full three days in careful, meticulous documentation, filling notebooks with the same rigorous attention Webb had once brought to his own single trip.

"This is remarkable work you've been doing," Sanjay told Xander, on the final evening, reviewing his own notes with visible satisfaction. "I understand now why Webb spoke of it so highly, all those years later."

"Glad it lived up to the reputation."

"More than lived up to it." Sanjay managed a tired, genuine smile. "I don't know when logistics will allow another trip like this. But I'm deeply grateful for this one."

It would, in fact, be nearly two years before another researcher made the trip, the practice remaining rare and precious rather than routine — a fact that would only change once Elena Vasquez, considerably more insistent than either of her predecessors, took over the position and simply refused to let the opportunity lapse into occasional legend the way it had twice before.
