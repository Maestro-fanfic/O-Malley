Chapter 19

The Bronze on a Friday night ran at a volume that made actual conversation a negotiation, which suited Xander fine, since most of what he wanted to say to Buffy across their little table didn't strictly require words. It was the same booth they always ended up in without ever quite deciding to — back corner, good sightline to the door, close enough to the speakers that nobody bothered eavesdropping — and Jesse had taken to calling it theirs with enough consistency that other regulars had really started leaving it open most Fridays.

"You're staring," Buffy said, not looking up from her soda.

"I'm appreciating. There's a difference." He reached over and laced his fingers through hers on the table, thumb tracing idle circles against her knuckles.

She smiled at that, leaning across the small table to press a quick kiss to his cheek before settling back into her seat, still holding his hand, feet already hooked over his ankle under the table the way they ended up most nights without either of them tracking when it happened. "Smooth."

"Years of dedicated practice."

Faith arrived a few minutes later with Jesse in tow, mid-argument about something that had started three blocks away and showed no signs of resolving.

"Tell her," Jesse said, dropping into the booth, "that a hot dog is not, under any definition, a sandwich."

"We already had this fight," Willow said.

"We're having it again. New audience." Jesse turned to Buffy. "Back me up."

"I've never had a strong opinion on this, and I'm starting to think that was the right call."

"Coward," Faith said with a shrug, stealing Xander's fries.

Jesse's attention had already drifted across the room toward Cordelia, holding court near the bar with her usual crowd, tracking her with the unsubtle, hopeful focus he'd been deploying at her, unsuccessfully, since roughly the ninth grade. "Okay, actual important question — is three years of working up to actually saying something a normal amount of working up to something, or is that its own diagnosis."

"It's a little concerning, Jesse," Willow said.

"Noted. Persisting anyway." He still hadn't moved, still watching from the safety of the booth. "She'll come around. I contain hidden depths. She just hasn't discovered them yet."

"Unrelated," Xander said, "and considerably more important — anybody want to hear the full, unedited version of the hillside incident? Because I've been workshopping it, and I think I finally nailed the pacing."

"We've heard it four times," Willow said.

"You've heard the short version four times. This is the director's cut."

"Absolutely not," Faith said. "I lived through hearing about it in real time. I don't need the theatrical release too."

"Your loss. It's got a great third act."

The rest of the night dissolved into the ordinary noise of people who'd long since stopped being surprised by each other — the sandwich debate resurfacing twice more, Jesse trying and failing to get Xander to commit to a firm date for the sloth rematch, Willow quietly sketching what she insisted was just a rough anatomical guess at ground sloth proportions and definitely not something she planned to submit anywhere official.

Faith caught Buffy's eye at one point, tipping her head toward the door.

"Walk and talk?" she asked. "Getting a little loud in here."

They ended up on the curb outside, the Bronze's noise dulled to a bassline through the walls, the night cool enough to actually feel good after the crowd inside.

"You good?" Buffy asked. "You've seemed a little quiet tonight."

"Yeah, I'm good. Just thinking." Faith leaned back against the brick. "Two months in this town and I've seen extinct birds, a demon with a jewelry fetish, and a hillside that apparently tried to kill Xander. I keep waiting for it to stop being weird and it just doesn't."

"Welcome to Sunnydale. It's always gonna be a little weird."

"I'm not complaining. Just noticing." Faith looked up at the sky, mostly obscured by the Bronze's sign and streetlight glare. "Beats the alternative, anyway."

"What alternative?"

"Wherever I would've ended up if I hadn't landed here." Faith said it light, the way she said most things that were actually a little heavier than they sounded. "This is better. Just saying it out loud so it's officially on record."

"Noted," Buffy said, bumping her shoulder against Faith's. "Glad you landed here too, by the way."

"Yeah, yeah. Don't get sappy on me, B, we're supposed to be having fun."

"I'm allowed one sappy sentence a week. I'm using it now."

"Fine. One sentence. Don't make it a habit."

They stood there another minute, comfortable in the quiet, before the muffled thump of the Bronze's music pulled them both back toward the door, Jesse's voice already audible through the wall, still apparently litigating the sandwich question to anyone who'd listen.
