Chapter 96

Willow's excitement about Xander's new landing technique lasted about a day before it evolved into something significantly bigger, the way most of her excitements eventually did.

She found him at his locker the way she always did before first period, squeezing in against his side while he dug through the mess of notebooks and loose papers, one arm looping around his waist without either of them registering it as a decision anymore, and stayed there, half-blocking the run of lockers behind them, entirely unbothered by the stream of students detouring around the two of them to get to class.

"Okay, I need to ask something, and I need you to not think I'm being weird about it," she said, catching Xander at his locker before first period.

"You're always a little weird about things. I've made my peace with it." He didn't look up from the locker, arm settling around her shoulders in the same automatic way, the two of them folding into each other in the middle of the hallway the way they'd apparently stopped noticing they did.

"Fair. Anyway — Cent's parents. They've been doing this for decades, right? Actual decades of data, actual understanding of how the ability works, actual documented technique instead of just trial and error."

"That's basically what I said too, yeah."

"Do you think they'd talk to me? Not about anything personal, obviously, I know there's a lot they probably can't or won't share. Just — the mechanics. How it actually works, physically, whatever they're comfortable explaining."

Xander considered that, weighing it seriously rather than brushing it off. "I can ask. Can't promise anything, given everything they've been through. But I think Millie especially might actually enjoy it. She seemed like the kind of person who's spent a long time not having anybody to nerd out with about this stuff either."

"I would be so respectful. I would bring actual prepared questions. I would not be weird about it, or at least, I would contain the weirdness to acceptable levels."

"That's the most reasonable promise I've ever heard you make."

"I'm capable of reasonable! Occasionally!"

He laughed, genuinely, and found himself already composing the request in his head — careful, respectful, giving them every opening to say no without it costing anything between them. "I'll ask this weekend. No promises, but I think it's worth asking."

"Thank you. Genuinely. This could be — Xander, this could be actual groundbreaking stuff, if they're willing to share even a fraction of what they must know."

"I know. That's part of why I want to ask carefully. Feels like a big thing to request from people who've spent their whole lives being careful about exactly this kind of exposure."

Willow sobered slightly at that, catching the weight of it. "You're right. I don't want to push if it's not okay."

"I don't think you're pushing. I think you're asking, which is different, and I think they'll appreciate the distinction if it comes to that." He closed his locker. "Worst case, they say no, and we're exactly where we already were. Best case, you get your groundbreaking stuff and probably a new friend to nerd out with."

"I would like the best case, please."

"I'll do what I can," Xander said, and meant it. The bell rang, and neither of them moved right away, still tangled together against the lockers while the hallway emptied out around them, the specific reluctance of two people who apparently found forty-five minutes apart a really difficult prospect. "I gotta go, I gotta go," he said, not going, kissing her once, then again when the first one didn't seem to take.

"You have Simmons for first period, you're literally three doors down."

"Three doors is a lot of doors, Willow."

"Go," she said, laughing, giving him one more shove toward his classroom door, and he went, backward, still looking at her until he nearly walked into a freshman, already looking forward to the conversation despite the size of what he was about to ask.

96.5. Cent: "Your friend wants to study us?"

Xander asked it careful, the way he'd started asking most things about my family lately, like he was still testing how much weight the ground could take before it gave.

"Not study like a lab rat. Study like — she's smart, truly smart, and she's spent months chasing half-answers about how this works. Your mom's the first real answer she's ever had access to."

"And you trust her."

"Completely. She's careful with things that matter. I've watched her be careful with plenty."

I turned that over, standing there at the fence with him. Mom would decide for herself regardless of what I thought, but he'd asked me first, which counted for something.

"Ask her," I said. "Mom, I mean. Not me. It's her call, and she'll actually consider it instead of just reacting."

"That's what I figured. Just wanted your read first."

"My read's that your friend sounds like somebody I'd probably like. Anybody who chases a mystery that hard usually turns out interesting."

"She is extremely interesting. Occasionally exhausting. Mostly interesting."

I laughed at that, actually laughed, the kind that surprised me a little given how careful I'd been keeping myself about all of this. "Sounds like most of the good people I know."

"Takes one to know one."

"Rude. Accurate. Rude."

Mom said yes before he left that trip, with conditions, same as she does everything, and the actual meeting happened his next time through. I wasn't there for it — didn't need to be, this one wasn't mine to sit in on — but I got the full report that evening at the fence line, Xander practically vibrating about how well it had gone.

"She's amazing," he said. "Your mom. Willow's amazing. It was a honestly great two hours."

"Told you she'd be worth it."

"You called it. I'm formally acknowledging you called it."

"Get it in writing next time. I like proof."

"Noted. I'll bring a notarized statement to the next visit."

I walked back to the house smiling, something easy sitting in my chest that hadn't been there a few months ago, back when all I had was a boot print and a hope I hadn't let myself trust yet.

96.7. The Mastodon Problem

"Okay, so, bigger problem than the wolf," Xander said, dropping into the kitchen chair across from Willow's spread of notes, visibly bursting to deliver a very large realization. "The mastodon. We're doing this. I just need everybody to understand it's not gonna be quick."

"Or mammoth. We still don't know which." Willow didn't look up from her calculations. "Also, what kind of bigger problem?"

"The kind where I did actual math with Alan this morning, not just vibes, and the honest answer is: nothing we currently own can safely move something that size. Not the trailer, not the enclosure budget, none of it. We'd need custom equipment, a specialized large-mammal vet, probably a crane, and about four times what the sloth cost, which was already apparently a stretch." Xander sat down heavily. "So it's happening. It's just happening on Alan's timeline, not mine, and his timeline currently says weeks, not days."

"Weeks," Willow repeated, weighing it. "I can live with weeks. Weeks is a lot better than never."

"Millie's already reaching out to a couple of people her dad used to work with, actual large-animal specialists, since apparently four years of amateur enthusiasm doesn't quite cover 'multi-ton Ice Age herbivore.' Alan's drafting a bigger enclosure plan in parallel so we're not starting from scratch once the transport side's solved." He managed a tired grin. "Turns out there's a size where 'bring it home' stops being an adventure and starts being an actual engineering project."

Willow considered that, tapping her pen against the table. "I can work with an engineering project. Honestly, knowing it's happening, even slowly, is a lot better than the alternative. And it doesn't slow down the other one."

"The other one," Xander agreed, with slightly less enthusiasm than the sentence probably deserved. "Right. The sabertooth. Which you're still absolutely set on, once the mastodon's actually handled."

"Especially the sabertooth," Willow confirmed, utterly unrepentant. "One thing at a time, though. Mastodon first, since it's already in motion. Some things are worth doing right, even if 'right' takes longer."

"That's gonna be on my tombstone. 'He tried to do it right, eventually, on a schedule Alan controlled.'"

"Better than 'he winged it and got eaten.'"

"Fair. Genuinely fair."
