Chapter 25

Cordelia cornered Willow at her locker for what had become, by unofficial count, the fourth time this month, which Willow had started to suspect meant she'd been unofficially promoted to Cordelia's primary source on all things weird, whether she wanted the job or not.

"Okay, deer with forward horns," Cordelia said, without preamble. "Real, or are you people exaggerating again to make your weekends sound more interesting than they are?"

"Real. I was there, I've got actual measurements. The curl pattern's really unusual."

"And no injuries this time? Shocking. I was starting to think Xander was contractually required to bleed at least once a trip."

"First injury-free one in about a month, yeah."

"I've been keeping track," Cordelia said, like she was confessing to something mildly criminal. "Not because I care what you people get up to. Because it's truly the only interesting thing at this school that isn't prom committee drama or the debate team's ongoing personality crisis, and my standards, unlike some people's, remain high."

"That's fair. It has been a busy month."

"Sloth, bees, an emu incident, a hillside with apparent personal vendetta issues, and now peaceful forward-horn deer. Honestly? Feels like a step down. Where's the chaos, Rosenberg. I was promised chaos."

"I don't control the wildlife's dramatic pacing."

"Clearly somebody should. I want to see the actual notebook at some point. Purely so I can judge your handwriting properly."

"I can't show you the actual notebook."

"Obviously you can. You just don't want to."

"Because it's got Faith's handwriting in the margins making fun of half my entries, and some of it's honestly funny, but a lot of it's aimed at me specifically."

"Now I definitely want to see it."

"That tracks, unfortunately."

Faith arrived mid-conversation, sliding in with a tray she hadn't finished eating from yet. "Somebody talking about my margin notes?"

"Cordelia wants to see the notebook."

"Absolutely not. My best material's in there. I'm not giving away the good stuff for free." Faith stole a fry off Cordelia's tray without asking, which drew a flat, unimpressed look.

"Do that again and I stab you with this fork," Cordelia said, completely conversational, not even glancing up.

"Noted. Threat received." Faith didn't look remotely deterred. "Also, unrelated, I need a second opinion. Cordelia. Hot dog. Sandwich or not sandwich."

Cordelia stared at her, weary, like someone who'd already decided this conversation was beneath her but couldn't quite resist answering anyway. "It's a hot dog. Obviously not a sandwich. I genuinely can't believe this is a debate that's lasted long enough to require my input."

"See, thank you," Jesse said, arriving in time to catch the tail end of it, dropping his tray with enough force to make his soda slosh. "Finally, a voice of reason at this table."

"I've been saying this for weeks," Willow said. "Nobody listens to me either."

"You don't count, Red, you agree with him." Faith gestured at Jesse. "I need actual outside perspective."

"I don't want to be involved in this," Cordelia said, already backing half a step away, though she made no actual move to leave. "I answered one question because it was an easy point to score, not because I'm interested in your ongoing personality disorder of a debate."

"Too late," Faith said with a grin. "You're in it now."

"I am extremely not in it."

"You've checked on the notebook four times this month, Cordelia," Willow pointed out.

"That's research. There's a difference between research and being 'in it.'"

"Sure there is," Faith said, unconvinced.

Cordelia opened her mouth, found she didn't have a rebuttal sharp enough to satisfy her own standards, and settled instead for a look that suggested the whole table had personally disappointed her. "Whatever. If any of you tell people I willingly sat here for this conversation, I'll deny it under oath."

"Wouldn't dream of it," Faith said, totally unconvincingly.

Jesse, meanwhile, had already moved on to needling Xander's empty seat like the man himself was present to defend it. "Where is our fearless leader, by the way? Still recovering from the emu incident emotionally, or is he just late."

"Emotionally recovering," Willow said. "He's very sensitive about the whole thing."

"He should be. Man got out-maneuvered by a bird with the wingspan of a Buick." Jesse shook his head with theatrical solemnity. "I'm gonna bring it up at his funeral. 'Here lies Xander. Bested by poultry.'"

"Emus aren't poultry."

"They're bird-shaped and rude. Close enough."

The lunch table settled into its usual noise after that — the sandwich debate reopening in full, Willow's notebook getting passed exactly one page deep before Faith snatched it back, Jesse narrating the whole exchange like a sports commentator, Cordelia lingering notably longer than her stated indifference should have technically allowed — ordinary, loud, and entirely unconcerned with anything more dangerous than an argument about hot dogs.
