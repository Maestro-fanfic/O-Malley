Chapter 126

Willow came along this time, both of them landing in a world of chalk cliffs overlooking a churning gray sea, gulls wheeling overhead in numbers loud enough to hear from well before they crested the final rise.

"Okay, this one's dramatic," Willow said, peering over the cliff edge with appropriate caution. "Very dramatic coastline energy."

"Very dramatic coastline energy that's apparently also a bird highway," Xander said, watching the wheeling flocks. "This is a lot of gulls."

They spent the first day documenting the cliff colony, careful of loose chalk underfoot, and found something genuinely new on the second morning — a species of seal hauled out on a narrow beach below, spotted in a pattern unlike anything either of them had catalogued before.

"Those spots are incredible," Willow said, already sketching furiously. "I need every angle. Every single angle."

"You've got time. They don't look like they're going anywhere fast."

They spent hours documenting the colony, careful of the steep, unstable path down to the beach, and by the third evening had filled nearly every page of Willow's current notebook with careful, excited observation.

"This might truly be one of my favorites," Willow said, looking over her notes with visible satisfaction. "The spot pattern alone is gonna keep me busy for weeks."

"Glad it's a good one. You've earned a good one, honestly, with everything else you've had going on lately."

"Same to you. Feels like we've both been running pretty hard lately." Willow leaned her head against his shoulder, notebook finally set aside for the evening.

"Yeah. This helps, though. Always does." Xander pressed a kiss to her hair, and they sat like that a while, chalk dust and salt air and the easy, uncomplicated comfort of just being together with nothing urgent demanding either of their attention.

They made it home with seal photos and chalk dust in every crease of their clothes, carrying exactly the grounding both of them had needed.

Buffy greeted Willow with a kiss hello before turning to do the same to Xander. "You look like you've been living in a chalk quarry. Good one?"

"Spotted seals, dramatic cliffs, honestly excellent data," Willow confirmed, once she'd caught her breath from the kiss. "I'm thrilled."

"I can tell," Buffy said, grinning at the state of her.

"Same colony's probably good for an actual capture attempt someday," Willow added, almost as an afterthought, flipping back through her notes. "Not this trip. This trip was for us. But that spot pattern's actually new-to-science — Alan's gonna want one properly documented in person eventually, not just sketched from a cliff."

"Someday," Xander agreed. "Today was for chalk dust and no urgency. I'm not ruining that by planning logistics in the doorway."

127. Cent: "Fury wants a report?"

Xander told me about the phone call the next time he made it back to the fence line. I could hear it was weighing on him before he even got the words out.

"Natasha called her boss. Fury. Wants a full written report on the whole fracture thing."

That landed. "That's... a lot bigger than a private theory now."

"Yeah. That's what I keep thinking too."

"You worried?"

"A little. Mostly just — this was our thing, you know? Small. Manageable. Now it's apparently a government-agency thing too."

I understood that better than he probably realized. "Welcome to my whole childhood."

"Fair point. Guess I'm getting a crash course."

"Dad's gonna want to know everything, once I tell him. He's got a lot of opinions about what happens when agencies start taking interest in things like this."

"Good opinions or bad ones?"

"Earned ones. Not the same thing as good, necessarily, but definitely earned."

Xander was quiet a second. "You think this could actually go bad? Like, bad-bad?"

"I don't know. Could. Doesn't have to. Depends who's actually reading the report, what they decide to do with it." I thought about Dad, about Cox, about all the ways careful information could turn into something else in the wrong hands. "Just — be careful what gets written down. That's the one piece of advice I've actually got."

"Noted. Passing that along to Giles."

"Good. He seems like the type who'd actually listen."

"He is. Genuinely is."

I told Dad that night. Watched the careful, familiar stillness cross his face — the one that means he's already three steps into worst-case scenarios before he lets himself say a word out loud.

"A written report," he said slowly. "Going up an actual chain of command."

"That's what Xander said."

"I don't love that."

"I know. Neither do I, honestly. But it's already happening. Nothing to do but hope it lands somewhere reasonable."

He turned that over a long moment. "I'd like to see that report, if there's any way to arrange it. Not to interfere. Just to know."

"I'll ask Xander. Can't promise anything."

"Understood. Just ask."

I carried the whole conversation with me a while after, turning it over the way I always do when something matters enough. Bigger and bigger, this whole thing. Boot print to god to Avenger to actual government paperwork.

Hoped it landed somewhere good. Didn't have much more control over it than that.
