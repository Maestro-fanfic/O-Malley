Chapter 48

Willow noticed something was different before Xander said a word about it, which didn't totally surprise him anymore after four years of her paying closer attention to his moods than he usually gave her credit for.

"You're quieter than usual," she said, sliding into the seat across from him at lunch. "Volcano trip get to you?"

"A little. Ground shook. Made the whole thing feel less like an adventure and more like an actual risk for about ten seconds." He picked at his food, less enthusiastic about it than usual. "I'm fine. Just needed a beat longer than I normally do."

"That's allowed, you know. You don't have to treat every trip like it's just material for my chart."

"I know. I don't always successfully remember that in the moment, though."

Faith arrived mid-conversation, dropping into her usual seat with her usual lack of ceremony. "Heard there was a scary moment. B mentioned it."

"There was a moment. Ground shook. I ran away from a volcano. Very reasonable reaction, I stand by it."

"Nobody's saying it wasn't reasonable." Faith studied him for a second, the teasing edge she usually led with noticeably absent. "You good, though? Actually good, not just saying it to end the conversation faster?"

"Actually good. Just had a genuinely scary ten seconds, and it's still sitting with me a little today. Not a whole crisis. Just a real moment I haven't fully shaken yet."

"That tracks. You've had a lot of scary moments lately that you mostly just turn into jokes right away. Maybe it's fine that one of them didn't immediately become a bit."

"Since when do you dispense emotional wisdom at lunch?"

"Since I've got my own stuff going on that's taught me you don't gotta turn every hard thing into a punchline the second it happens." Faith shrugged, a little self-conscious about the admission. "Sometimes you just gotta let it land a minute first. Then joke about it later, once it's actually settled."

Xander considered that, really, longer than he usually gave anything at this table. "That's actually good advice."

"I have my moments. Rare ones, but they happen."

"I'm gonna hold you to giving more of them."

"Don't push your luck."

Willow, who'd been quietly listening through most of this, finally spoke up, reaching over to rest her hand on his arm. "The chart's not going anywhere, and it can wait a day if you need it to. I'd rather you actually process a scary thing than rush straight into giving me data about it."

"I appreciate that. Genuinely." Xander managed a real smile, the first one since he'd sat down, turning his hand over to properly hold hers instead. "Ask me again tomorrow. I'll probably have the volcano timing all logged and ready by then."

"No rush."

"I know. But I think I'm actually ready now anyway. Talking about it helped more than I expected."

The table settled into its usual noise after that, gentler than usual for a few minutes before sliding back into its familiar rhythm — Jesse arriving to ask what he'd missed, the sandwich debate getting a halfhearted mention before everyone silently agreed to let it rest for one day, Faith stealing exactly one fry off Xander's tray on principle, same as always, the ordinary comfort of a group that knew when to push and when to just sit quietly together instead.

48.5. Chapter 48.5

Faith asked to come along on a whim, mostly out of curiosity about a job she'd heard described secondhand more times than she could count, and mostly because Giles' research had gone quiet for the week and she had nothing pressing to fill the gap.

"Figured I should actually see it," she said, shrugging into a borrowed pack, testing the weight of it with visible unfamiliarity. "Instead of just hearing about it after the fact. Just this once, though. Not making it a thing."

"No pressure either way," Xander said. "Grab my shoulder whenever you're ready. It's not exactly a countdown situation."

She grabbed it like she was defusing something, hand landing careful and precise, and the world went sideways and empty and strange all at once, the way it apparently did for everyone the first time, judging by the way Faith's whole body went rigid the instant the pull actually took hold.

The world turned out to be scrubland, dry and golden, dotted with rock formations worn smooth by wind and time into shapes that looked, from certain angles, almost deliberately sculpted. Faith stood very still for a long moment after they landed, taking it in with an expression Xander had never quite seen on her before — not fear exactly, more like someone whose entire understanding of how the world worked had just quietly, permanently shifted.

"Okay," she said, finally, voice a little unsteady. "Okay, that's — that actually happened. That's a whole different reality standing between me and Sunnydale right now."

"Yeah. Takes some getting used to. Everybody gets that same look, first time."

"I don't doubt it." She turned in a slow circle, taking in the rock formations, the endless gold grass, the sky doing nothing more dramatic than being an ordinary, cloudless blue. "This is nice. Quiet. Real quiet. Different kind of quiet than patrol quiet."

"Yeah, this kind doesn't usually come with the risk of getting jumped by something undead."

"Fair point. I'll take this kind."

They spent the first day working through the scrubland, Faith's patrol instincts proving genuinely useful in spotting hazards Xander might have otherwise missed — loose rock that shifted underfoot before he'd even registered the risk, a den entrance tucked into shadow that she clocked from thirty feet out, a stretch of ground that looked solid but sounded wrong when she tested it with a thrown stone first.

"You're good at this," Xander said, truly impressed, watching her navigate terrain with the same wary competence she brought to a graveyard at midnight. "Didn't realize how much patrol skills would actually transfer."

"Same instincts, different monsters. Or, well, no monsters here, but you get what I mean. Watching for what's wrong instead of what's right. Same muscle, different target."

They made camp that evening in the shelter of one of the larger rock formations, the shed staked out fast and easy on solid ground for once, and Faith spent a long while just sitting outside it, looking up at a sky much darker and notably more thickly starred than anything Sunnydale's light pollution ever allowed.

"You do this every week," she said, not quite a question.

"Every week. Four years now."

"Alone, this whole time. Before any of us started coming along."

"Yeah."

She didn't say anything else about it, but something in her expression shifted, some private reassessment happening behind her eyes that she clearly wasn't ready to voice yet.

They found the actual wildlife on the second day — a pack of small, fox-like creatures denning in the rock formations, wary but curious, watching the two of them from a cautious distance that gradually, over hours of patient stillness, closed into something almost like acceptance. One grew bold enough to approach within a dozen feet, sniffing at the air with obvious curiosity before retreating again, tail low but not frightened.

"They're kind of cute," Faith admitted, watching one investigate a discarded water bottle with visible suspicion, batting at it with one careful paw before deciding it wasn't worth further attention. "Didn't expect cute. Expected something scarier."

"Most of it's not scary, honestly. That's kind of the secret nobody believes until they see it. Mostly it's just quiet and weird and beautiful."

"Yeah. I'm seeing that now." Faith looked out at the scrubland stretching gold in every direction, something in her posture easing that had been tight since the moment they'd landed. "Glad I came. Needed something that wasn't patrol or research or waiting on Giles to find the next lead."

"Anytime. Seriously. I like the company."

They spent the third day documenting what they could, Faith proving a quick study at the actual mechanics of careful observation despite having no formal reason to have ever learned them, and by the time the jump pulled them home, both of them wore the good kind of tired that comes from a trip that asked something real of you and gave something real back.

"Good first trip?" Buffy asked, catching them at the door.

"Good trip," Faith said, considering the words carefully before settling on them. "Not sure I'm cut out to make it a habit, but glad I saw it once. Glad I understand now, even a little, what you've actually been carrying this whole time."

"Door's always open if you change your mind," Xander said, and meant it, filing the thought away without pushing further, the way he'd learned to with most things where Faith was concerned.
