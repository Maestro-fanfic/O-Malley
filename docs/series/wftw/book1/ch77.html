Chapter 77

Willow made him tell the story three times before she'd let herself fully process it, each retelling picking up small details he hadn't thought to mention the first time — the exact weathering on the fence posts, the crop rows, the angle of the tin roof catching light.

"A farm," she said, for what felt like the tenth time, staring at the photos spread across the kitchen table. "An actual, functioning farm. On a world that's supposed to be completely untouched."

"Not completely untouched, apparently. Just untouched by me, this whole time, because I've never happened to land anywhere close to wherever they are."

"Do you think it's just the one homestead? Or a whole community out there?"

"No idea. I didn't get close enough to find out. Didn't feel right, showing up unannounced on somebody's land without knowing anything about who they are or how they'd react to a stranger appearing out of nowhere."

"That was smart," Giles said, from across the table, having arrived within twenty minutes of Willow's call the night before and clearly not having slept much since. "Genuinely smart, Xander. Approaching an unknown human population without any context could have gone very badly, regardless of how curious the discovery made you."

"I wanted to go say hi. Every part of me wanted to just walk up and knock on the door. I just — didn't trust that it was actually the safe move."

"Your instincts served you well." Giles studied the photos again, expression carefully neutral in the way it got when he was working through something he hadn't fully articulated yet. "This does raise a rather significant question, though."

"Which is?"

"Whether this population arrived there naturally, through some means we haven't considered, or whether they arrived the same way you did." Giles met his eyes. "If it's the latter, this may be much more than a chance discovery."

The room went quiet.

"You think it's them," Willow said slowly. "Whoever the margin note was talking about. The other people who carry this."

"I think it's a possibility worth taking seriously. I don't think we should leap to that conclusion without more evidence. But a farm, built by someone, on a world otherwise totally devoid of human presence — that is not the sort of thing that happens by accident."

Xander went quiet, the size of it settling slowly. "So I might go back there and actually meet somebody like me. Somebody who's been doing this too."

"You might," Giles said. "Though I'd urge patience before rushing back. If this is what it appears to be, the stakes of getting the first contact wrong are considerably higher than any previous trip you've taken."

"I wasn't planning on rushing back tomorrow," Xander admitted. "Honestly, I don't know if I'm even ready. Four years of assuming I was the only one, and now there's a real chance I'm not, and I don't — I don't know how to feel about that yet. Excited. Terrified. Both, probably."

"That's an utterly reasonable response," Giles said. "Take whatever time you need before deciding how to proceed. This isn't a discovery that requires an immediate answer."

Willow had gone quiet partway through, still flipping back through the photos, and stopped cold on the last three — the wolf tracks, the distant tusked shape, the kill site.

"Xander," she said, voice climbing about half an octave. "Xander. What. Are these."

"Oh. Yeah. That was also a whole thing." He rubbed the back of his neck. "Wolf tracks, but bigger than they should be. Some kind of elephant-adjacent thing with tusks, way off in a meadow. And a kill site I didn't stick around long enough to properly document, because the puncture wounds on it made some very compelling arguments about not doing that."

"This is — okay, this is genuinely one of the biggest things you've ever found, and I say that fully aware you found an actual farm on the same trip." Willow was already reaching for a fresh notebook page. "Dire wolves. Actual dire wolves, or something close enough to matter. And mammoths, or mastodons, truly could be either, we'd need way better photos to even guess at the species — this world isn't just inhabited, Xander, it's got a whole surviving Ice Age ecosystem running on top of it."

"Which is either the coolest thing I've ever heard or extremely concerning, depending on how close any of that lives to the farm."

"Both. It's allowed to be both." Willow's pen was already moving, fast and sure. "Worth noting, though — most large predators tend to avoid humans by default when they've got other options. That's true on our Earth, and there's no obvious reason it wouldn't hold here too. You probably weren't in nearly as much danger as it felt like in the moment." She paused over the kill-site photo. "And honestly, if this world's got dire wolves and mammoths running around, the smart money's already on sabertooth for whatever left that kill site. It's kind of the obvious third member of the set."

"Yeah, that tracked before you even said it. Ice Age's greatest hits, apparently."

"Tell that to the kill site."

"The kill site had deer bones. You had a pulse and forward momentum. Statistically different categories of encounter."

"Comforting. Somewhat." Xander managed a tired smile. "So what, we just... sit on this? A whole possible sabertooth on top of the wolves and the giant tusked things, and we just don't do anything about it?"

"Not sit on it forever. Sit on it responsibly." Giles removed his glasses, cleaning them in the way that meant he was thinking carefully. "Should you wish to document — or, in time, attempt to bring back — any of what you found, it will require notably more preparation than the sloth did. An actual animal behaviorist's input on dosage, rather than your own careful guesswork. Alan's usual trailer arrangement is unlikely to scale to something the size of what you photographed. None of that happens on the next trip. It happens after proper planning, if it happens at all."

"After proper planning," Xander agreed. "Not today. Not this week. But — yeah. Eventually. I want to actually see one of them up close. Properly, not through a camera zoom from behind a bush."

"Then we'll plan properly," Willow said, already sketching a rough outline at the bottom of her notes — dosage question marks, a column for "bigger trailer," a column for "ask Alan who to even call about mammoths." "This is gonna take a while to get right. I'm okay with that. I'd rather get it right."

"Yeah," Xander agreed, looking at the photos once more — the fence, the field, the distant roof, all of it sitting there patiently, waiting for whenever he worked up the nerve to actually find out who'd built it. "Yeah. I think I need a minute. Maybe more than a minute."

"Take as long as you need," Willow said, reaching over to squeeze his hand under the table, steady. "We'll be here whenever you're ready."
