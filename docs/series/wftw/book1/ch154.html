Chapter 154

Joyce's list marked its own quiet milestone that Sunday, the entry she read aloud carrying more weight than any single line had in longer than any of them could remember: We faced something that could have ended notably more than just our own small corner of existence, and we came through it together, stranger and more connected than we ever expected to become. I'm choosing to call that a good outcome, however it got here.

"That's a big entry," Xander said.

"It's been a big month," Joyce said, passing the potatoes down a table significantly fuller than usual — Faith, Buffy, Willow, Jesse, Cordelia, Giles, Thor, all crowded around in the comfortable chaos that had, by now, become simply how Sunday dinner worked, chairs pulled from three different rooms to accommodate everyone, the whole scene warm with the noise of a family considerably larger than the one it had started as.

"Speaking of big," Faith said, "I still can't get over how casual everyone's being about the whole 'we prevented reality from unraveling' thing. Feels like that should be a bigger deal at this table specifically."

"We process big things by making them ordinary," Willow said. "It's basically the whole Sunnydale coping strategy."

"Deeply validating information about my adopted hometown," Thor said, entirely serious, and the table dissolved into the easy laughter that had become, over the past several months, its own kind of ritual.

Jesse, who'd spent most of dinner quietly working through his own version of processing, finally spoke up. "Can we talk about the fact that I've now met an actual Avenger, an actual sorcerer, and people from an fully different reality, and somehow none of that beats the napkin thing as my most embarrassing Sunnydale memory?"

"Nothing beats the napkin thing," Cordelia said, delighted. "That's just objectively true."

"I stand by my earlier defense. It was a small collection."

"It's never gonna stop being funny, Jesse. Make your peace with that."

Cordelia, who'd been folded into enough of the actual details by now to follow most of the conversation, finally spoke up herself. "Okay, but for real, is it actually over? Or is this just, like, round one?"

"Ilsa said the immediate crisis is resolved," Giles said. "The wider instability across affected realities will take far longer to fully settle, and may require ongoing monitoring for years. But the acute danger, the part that threatened genuine catastrophe — that's passed."

"So we might have to do this again someday."

"Possibly. Though hopefully with much more warning next time, and notably less immediate peril."

"I'll take that trade," Faith said, and the table murmured general agreement.

"For what it's worth," Buffy added, "I think we've earned a honestly long stretch of boring after this one. Universe owes us that much, at minimum."

"I would not count on the universe honoring debts," Thor said, "but I appreciate the optimism regardless."

Later, on the porch, Xander found himself sitting beside Faith, her hand loosely laced with his, both of them looking out at a street that felt, for the first time in longer than either could easily place, completely, wonderfully ordinary — porch lights flickering on up and down the block, a dog barking somewhere distant, the whole unremarkable rhythm of a town that had no idea how close it had come to something it would never have understood even if it had. Through the window behind them, he could still hear Buffy and Willow's laughter mixing with the general noise of dinner winding down, and something in his chest settled at the sound of it, complete in a way he hadn't quite let himself name out loud yet.

"Good month," Faith said, eventually.

"Good month," he agreed. "Feels weird to say that, given everything. But yeah. Good month." He pressed a kiss to her temple, the gesture long since settled into something easy and unremarkable after months of practice.

"Maybe complicated things are allowed to be good, even when they're also enormous and scary."

"That's annoyingly wise."

"I have my moments."

"That's still your line, though, right? Not somebody borrowing it?"

"Damn right it's mine. You people can borrow the multitudes thing. This one's staying with me."

They sat together a while longer, the evening settling into comfortable quiet, and Xander found himself turning over, one more time, everything the past year had actually held — four years of solitary jumps becoming something shared, a mystery that had turned into a family, a god who'd become a friend, a crisis that had, against every reasonable fear, resolved into something survivable.

'Big life,' he thought, watching the ordinary street settle into evening dark. 'Bigger than I ever expected, back when this was just me and a random world every weekend.' It felt, sitting here now, less like an ending and more like exactly what it actually was — one more quiet chapter, closing gently, in a story that would, he suspected, keep finding new and impossible ways to grow.

"Same time next weekend?" Faith asked, nodding toward the horizon, toward whatever ordinary, extraordinary world waited next.

"Same time next weekend," Xander confirmed, and meant it completely, ready, as he always somehow managed to be, for whatever came next.
