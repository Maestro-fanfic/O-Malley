Chapter 58

Joyce found out about the ritual the night before, which Buffy had been putting off telling her mostly because there wasn't a version of the conversation that didn't sound terrifying out loud.

"I need to tell you something," Buffy said, sitting down across from her at the kitchen table, "and I need you to let me finish before you react."

Joyce set down her coffee slowly. "Okay."

Buffy told her everything — the mural, the countdown, the counter-rite, the four anchor points — and watched her mother's face cycle through fear, then something that looked almost like pride, then back to fear again by the time she finished.

"You're one of the anchors," Joyce said finally. "You and Xander both. Neither of you have magical training."

"We don't need it. Just willingness and physical presence. Giles tested it. It works."

"That doesn't make it not terrifying to hear, Buffy."

"I know. I'm sorry. I wish I could make it sound less scary, but I don't think there's a version of tomorrow that doesn't sound scary, no matter how I say it."

Joyce was quiet for a long moment, hands wrapped around her coffee mug like she needed something solid to hold onto. "Is Faith ready? Really ready, not just saying so because it's expected of her?"

"She's ready. Scared, but ready. She's had more prep time than any of us, mentally — she's been walking toward this moment for weeks now, longer than she even fully understood at the time."

"And you trust the plan. Genuinely trust it, not just hoping it works."

"I trust Giles. I trust Willow's research. I trust that we've done everything we can to prepare." Buffy reached across the table, covering her mother's hand with her own. "I can't promise it'll be easy. I can promise we're not walking in blind, and we're not walking in alone."

Joyce nodded slowly, something settling in her expression that looked like acceptance even though it clearly cost her something to arrive at. "I want to be there. Not in the circle. Just — nearby. The way we were for the reservoir thing, whatever that turns out to mean tomorrow."

"I'd like that too, actually. Having you close by helped last time, even from a distance."

"Then I'll be there." Joyce squeezed her hand once, firm. "Whatever happens tomorrow, you're coming home. Both of you. All of you, actually, the whole ridiculous, wonderful group of you."

"We will," Buffy said, and found she believed it more than she expected to, sitting there with her mother's hand steady around her own, the kitchen quiet and ordinary around a conversation that had been anything but.

Upstairs, later, Faith found her on the landing, both of them unable to settle into actual sleep despite the day's exhaustion.

"Your mom take it okay?" Faith asked.

"About as okay as anyone takes hearing their kid's about to anchor a magical containment ritual. Which is to say, not great, but she's coming tomorrow anyway. Wants to be nearby."

"That's good. That she wants to be there." Faith leaned against the wall beside her. "You scared?"

"A little. You?"

"A lot, if I'm honest. Haven't been this scared in a long time." Faith looked over. "Glad it's you holding one of the corners tomorrow, though. Makes the scared part easier to carry."

"Same," Buffy said. "Get some sleep if you can. Big day."

"Yeah. Trying." Faith managed a small, tired smile. "See you on the other side of tomorrow, B."

"See you there," Buffy said, and meant it as a promise more than a farewell.
