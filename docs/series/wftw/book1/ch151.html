Chapter 151

The other tears closed within minutes of the epicenter's collapse, Ilsa's instruments confirming it with a kind of disbelieving relief that spread through the whole group like something physical.

"It held," she said, staring at her readings like they might change if she looked away and back. "It actually held. The reinforcement radiated outward, stabilizing every secondary point almost simultaneously."

"Just like that?" Fury asked, lowering the weapon he'd been holding ready this entire time, expression somewhere between relief and professional suspicion of anything resolving this cleanly.

"Not 'just like that,'" Strange said, dryly. "Considerable expertise, several converging systems working in tandem, and one extremely lucky variable nobody fully understood contributing at exactly the right moment." He glanced at Xander. "You'll want to figure out what you actually did there, eventually. For future reference, if nothing else."

"I truly have no idea," Xander admitted, hand finally releasing Faith's shoulder, both of them shaking slightly now that the adrenaline had nowhere left to go. "I just did the thing that felt right. Wasn't exactly following a manual."

"Story of your entire life, from what I understand," Ilsa said, something almost like a smile crossing her face for the first time since arriving.

Faith sat down hard on the ground, hammer resting across her knees, breathing like she'd run a marathon. "That was the scariest ten minutes of my entire life. And I include the last crisis in that ranking."

"You held it," Thor said, kneeling beside her, genuine pride in every word. "Completely. From the very first moment to the last."

"Didn't feel like holding anything. Felt like barely hanging on."

"That is, in my experience, precisely what holding something enormous actually feels like from the inside. It rarely feels as steady as it looks from the outside."

Faith managed a tired, real laugh at that. "Comforting. In a weird way."

Willow, still catching her breath from the walk back, couldn't resist. "For the record, Thor, 'never, or over drinks afterward' is honestly the best crisis-management advice I've heard all week."

"I have decided to take that as a compliment," Thor said, with as much dignity as a man freshly scolded mid-catastrophe could reasonably muster.

"You should. It was delivered with real precision."

"Do not," Thor said, "start with the precision."

"He got told off by a teenager holding back the end of reality," Fury said, arriving in time to catch the tail end of it, one eyebrow raised. "Mid-crisis. I heard the whole thing over comms. I'm not going to pretend that wasn't the highlight of my week."

"I was attempting to be encouraging."

"You were attempting to be precise while somebody was actively fighting off an apocalypse," Fury said. "Different thing."

"That is precisely what she said."

"Then maybe," Faith said, grinning despite the exhaustion, "you should've listened the first time."

"I contain multitudes of comforting weirdness," Thor said, entirely straight-faced, and the whole group, despite everything, despite the exhaustion and the fear still working its way out of their systems, dissolved into genuine, disbelieving laughter.

"That's still not your line," Xander managed, between laughs.

"I am beginning to suspect it belongs to whoever needs it most in any given moment," Thor said, and nobody had a good argument against that.

Cent found Xander a little apart from the group, still catching his breath, and didn't say anything at first, just leaned into his shoulder, both of them letting the adrenaline finish draining out in silence.

"You okay?" she asked finally.

"Getting there. You?"

"Getting there too." She managed a small, tired laugh. "Hell of a way to finally meet all your friends in person."

"Yeah, this wasn't exactly how I pictured introducing everybody."

"Could've been worse. Nobody died. Reality's still mostly intact. I'd call that a win, given the bar we were working with."

"Fair bar to clear," Xander agreed, and for a moment, despite the exhaustion, despite everything still humming through both their bodies, it felt like exactly the right kind of quiet to finally just breathe in together.

They stayed at the site another hour, confirming readings, making certain the containment held steady rather than merely appearing to, and by the time they finally made their way back into town, dawn was breaking pale and ordinary over Sunnydale, the whole impossible night settling, slowly, into something that felt almost like it could be believed.

"Is it over?" Willow asked, quiet, once they were all finally back at the house, the adrenaline crash hitting everyone in earnest now.

"The immediate crisis, yes," Ilsa said. "The wider Convergence — the instability across every reality currently affected — that will take longer to fully understand and address. But the epicenter's contained. The acceleration should slow considerably now that its source point is stabilized."

"Should," Xander repeated, echoing Ilsa's own earlier hedge.

"Should," she agreed, managing a tired smile of her own. "I'm learning to live with that word notably better than I expected to, this past week."

Miriam sat quietly at the kitchen table, watching the whole exhausted, relieved scene unfold with an expression Xander couldn't quite read until she finally spoke. "We did it. Actually did it. I wasn't sure, going in, that we would."

"None of us were," Petra said, dropping into the chair beside her, equipment finally set aside for the first time in days.

"I know. That's what makes it feel real, somehow. The uncertainty. If we'd been certain going in, I don't think I'd trust the outcome nearly as much." Miriam looked at Cent, something fiercely proud crossing her face. "You did good, coming here. Both of you."

"Learned it from my own mother," Cent said, quiet, and Miriam's eyes went a little bright at that anyway, something in Cent's steadiness clearly calling up an old ache of her own — all the ordinary mother-daughter moments her own years away from Home had cost her. She said nothing further, just reached over and squeezed Cent's hand once, hard, something instinctively warm in the gesture despite how recently the two of them had actually met.

The group settled into the exhausted, grateful quiet of people who'd faced something actually enormous and, against real uncertainty, actually come through it — not unscathed, not without real fear still working its way out of their systems, but together, and, for now, totally, gratefully alive.
