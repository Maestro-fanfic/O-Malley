Chapter 121

Everyone was tied up with Natasha's visit, which left the next jump to Xander alone, and he found himself almost grateful for the excuse to step away from a house currently hosting both a god and a spy.

The world was river delta, channels braiding through mudflats in a pattern that looked almost deliberate from a distance, wading birds working the shallows in numbers he hadn't seen since the wetland trip weeks back.

"Okay," he said, picking his way carefully along a drier ridge between channels. "Muddy one. Very muddy. At least it's not cold this time."

He spent the first day documenting the birds, herons and something crane-like moving through the shallows with careful, deliberate steps, and found a den on the second morning tucked into the ridge itself — otters, or something close enough to the concept that his brain filed them there immediately, sliding in and out of the water with the same loose, playful energy he remembered from an otter trip long before any of this Thor business started.

"You guys are a good reminder," he told them, crouching close enough that one investigated his boot with idle curiosity before losing interest and sliding back into the water. "Not everything's gotta be complicated. Sometimes it's just sliding into water because it's fun." One of the pups, notably braver than the rest, climbed halfway into his lap on its own initiative, completely unbothered by the whole encounter. "Okay, that's — yeah, you're coming home. Alan's gonna name you something ridiculous, I can already tell."

He spent the rest of the trip in that same easy rhythm, and by the time the jump pulled him home, he had bird and otter photos both, mud up to his knees, one very small and very pleased new addition tucked into the makeshift carrier, and nothing complicated behind him for once, in a stretch where everything else definitely had been.

Willow caught him at the door, Natasha visible in the background still deep in conversation with Thor, and kissed him hello despite the mud before she even asked. "Good one?"

"Really good one. Otters. Birds. Very grounding, honestly, after everything else going on."

"I bet. How was the mud?"

"Extensive. Deeply extensive." Xander glanced toward the living room. "How's the whole superhero situation going?"

"Surprisingly well. She's actually really easy to talk to, once you get past the whole assessing-everyone-immediately thing."

"Good. Glad it's going well." He headed for the stairs, mud-caked boots already coming off. "Gonna go get cleaned up, then I want the full debrief on whatever I missed."

"Deal," Willow said, and let him go track mud up the stairs, the god and the spy still deep in conversation in the next room, entirely unbothered by any of it.

122. Cent: "An actual Avenger?"

Xander told me about it the next time he made it back to the fence line, still visibly buzzing about the whole thing.

"So. Natasha's here."

"The actual Avenger."

"The actual Avenger. Spent most of last week camped out in my living room, actually, talking shop with Thor about the fracture thing."

That took me a second to absorb. "That's a lot of cosmic significance for one house."

"Tell me about it. I keep waiting for somebody to also mention aliens, just to round it out."

"Don't jinx it."

"Too late. Already thought it out loud."

I laughed. Good laugh, the real kind. "How's she taking all of it? The whole multiverse situation?"

"Better than I expected. Way more composed than I would've been, hearing my organization accidentally broke reality."

"That tracks. Probably takes a lot to rattle somebody like that."

"Yeah. Guessing so."

I told Dad about it at dinner. Watched him go quiet the way he does when something's genuinely landed.

"An Avenger," he said slowly. "That's — that's a whole different level of exposure than anything we've dealt with before."

"Exposure for Xander, maybe. Not really for us, though, if you actually work the distance out. Her whole organization's on a completely different Earth from the one Xander lives on, and his is a completely different Earth from ours. Two separate doors between anything they'd ever learn and anything that actually touches this family."

Dad considered that, something in his shoulders easing slightly. "That's... a fair point. Hadn't thought it through in those terms."

"Xander trusts her, too. Says she's careful."

"Careful people still work for organizations. Organizations still have their own agendas." But he said it without the edge it would've carried a minute earlier, already turning the actual math of it over instead of just the fear. "I'd still like to meet her myself, eventually. Get my own read. Not out of worry, particularly. Just good practice, knowing who knows how much."

"I'll ask."

"Not yet, though. Let this settle first."

"Yeah. Fair."

Mom looked between us, something thoughtful crossing her face. "This whole thing keeps getting bigger, doesn't it. Started with a boot print."

"Started with a boot print," I agreed. "Ended up gods and spies and time travel."

"World's a strange place," Dad said.

"Always was," Mom said. "We just didn't used to notice it this much."

That stuck with me a while after dinner, turning the whole strange year over one more time. Boot print. Marker. Note. Xander. Thor. Now an actual Avenger, sitting in a living room two thousand miles away, calmly discussing the end of predictable reality like it was just another Thursday.

Big year, I thought. Getting bigger.

Didn't mind it, though. Not even a little.
