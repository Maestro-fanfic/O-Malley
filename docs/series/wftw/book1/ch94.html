Chapter 94

"So it went well," Buffy said, the second Xander walked through the door glowing like a man who'd had an actually good day for once, instead of a merely survived one, already crossing the room to hug him properly before he'd even gotten his jacket off.

"It went really well," he said, into her hair, holding on a beat longer than a normal hello usually warranted. "Like, better than I let myself hope for. Her parents are great. Actual great, not just polite great."

Buffy pulled back enough to kiss him, quick and really pleased, before finally letting him go. "I'm so glad."

"Tell us everything," Willow said, already pulling out a chair like she was settling in for a full debrief, which she absolutely was.

He told them everything — Davy's handshake, Millie's cooking, the sloth photos, the plain relief of finally being somewhere that understood the shape of his last four years without him having to explain any of it from scratch.

"They know things," he said. "Real things, techniques, stuff I've been doing badly by accident that apparently has an actual right way to do it. Millie offered to help me figure some of it out."

"That's huge," Willow said. "Xander, that's genuinely huge. Four years of trial and error, and there's just — an instruction manual, basically, that you never had access to."

"Feels like that, yeah. A little overwhelming, honestly, realizing how much I've been doing the hard way that didn't have to be hard."

"Don't beat yourself up about it," Buffy said. "You didn't have the instruction manual. You did the best anybody could with what you had."

"I know. Still weird, though. Like finding out there was a shortcut the whole time."

Faith, who'd been quiet through most of the debrief, finally spoke up. "I get that feeling, actually. Found out a few months back that stuff I'd been white-knuckling through my whole life had actual names, actual explanations. Doesn't make the years before feel wasted, exactly. Just makes you wish you'd known sooner."

"Yeah," Xander said. "That's exactly it."

"You'll get there faster now, though. That's the upside." Faith stole a fry off his plate, on principle. "Sounds like a good family. Good instincts, if they took to you that fast."

"Really good instincts. Her dad clocked me in about four seconds, apparently. I didn't even notice it happening until Cent told me about it after."

"That's the good kind of clocking," Giles said, having arrived partway through and caught enough of the story to weigh in. "The kind that comes from genuine care rather than mere suspicion. I'm glad to hear it, Xander. Genuinely."

"Me too," Xander said, and meant it more than he'd expected to say out loud, four years of believing he was the only one who could do this finally, properly, no longer true at all.

94.5. The Wolf

The dire wolves had been sitting in the back of his mind for weeks, filed alongside the mammoth and whatever had made those punctures in the kill site, waiting on the "proper planning" Giles had insisted on. Proper planning, it turned out, mostly meant Millie.

"You want to dart one of the pack animals," she said, when he finally worked up the nerve to actually ask, not a question so much as a confirmation of something she'd clearly already guessed was coming. "Smart to start there instead of the big tusked ones or the cats. Wolves you can read. Pack behavior's honest, once you know what you're looking at."

"Honest how?"

"They tell you what they're gonna do before they do it. Ears, tail, weight shift. The two you're worried about don't give you nearly that much warning." She pulled a battered notebook off a shelf that looked like it had survived much worse than a Sunnydale kitchen. "Weight estimate's the hard part. Get that wrong and you either don't drop him fast enough or you drop him too hard. I've got rough numbers from Dad's old notes. We'll refine them once you've actually got eyes on the pack again."

Willow came along for the dosage math, notebook already covered in her own cross-referenced calculations by the time they actually made the jump, and Buffy came for exactly the reason Buffy always came on the dangerous ones — because she wasn't about to let Xander go stand near a pack of oversized wolves without backup.

The jump dropped all three of them a cautious distance from where the game trail had been, and they spent the better part of the first day simply relocating the pack, moving slow and quiet, letting Xander's four years of fieldwork do the work Millie's notes couldn't.

"There," Buffy breathed, the second afternoon, pointing toward a rise where several dark shapes moved with an easy, coordinated patience that made the hair on Xander's arms stand up despite the warm weather. "Okay. Those are wolves the way a bus is a car."

"Big," Willow agreed, already doing math against her notebook. "Bigger than my estimate, actually. Recalculating."

They picked the straggler — an older animal, trailing the pack's edge, moving with the particular deliberateness of something that had learned not to waste effort it didn't have to spend. Xander got close on his stomach through knee-high grass, closer than any sense of self-preservation strictly recommended, Willow's revised number running through his head the whole crawl.

The dart went in clean. The wolf's head came up fast, a low sound building in its chest that had all three of them go completely still, but the pack — reading something in its posture that Xander couldn't, exactly what Millie had meant by honest — didn't scatter, didn't charge, just watched, wary and alert, while their packmate's legs slowly stopped cooperating with the rest of it.

"Okay," Xander said, once it had gone down and stayed down, breathing slow and even. "Okay, that actually worked."

"That actually worked," Willow confirmed, sounding almost as surprised as he felt.

They didn't linger to admire it. Alan's team — briefed this time, truly briefed, not scrambling after the fact — had a notably larger transport crate waiting when the pull brought all three of them and one very confused, very sedated dire wolf home, and Millie's numbers, it turned out, had been close enough to right that Alan spent most of the debrief asking politely insistent questions about where, exactly, Xander had gotten such good dosage data.

"A friend," Xander said, which was true, and left it there.
