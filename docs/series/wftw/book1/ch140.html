Chapter 140

Fury arrived without fanfare, exactly as Natasha had promised, coming through SHIELD's crossing point same as she had and finishing the trip in an unremarkable sedan that could have belonged to anyone, parked a full block away from the Magic Box for reasons nobody bothered asking about, and made his way inside unhurried, long since impossible to impress by anything short of the actually world-ending.

Xander had expected, somehow, something much more dramatic — an entourage, at minimum, or at least a car that looked like it belonged to someone important. Instead, Fury walked in alone, coat plain, expression giving away exactly nothing, and the whole shop seemed to hold its breath for a moment as everyone present recalibrated their expectations all at once.

"Director Fury," Giles said, extending a hand. "Thank you for coming."

"Wasn't really optional, once Thor's report landed on my desk." Fury's eye swept the shop once, cataloguing it the same efficient way Natasha's had weeks earlier, pausing fractionally on Xander before moving on. "Let's get to it. I've read the report. I want the parts that didn't make it into writing."

They spent the first hour walking him through everything — Xander's ability, the Slayer-ritual origin, Thor's search, the hammer, the worthy-hands mechanism Faith had only just properly resolved. Fury listened without much visible reaction, occasionally asking a question sharp enough to reveal he'd absorbed notably more of the written report than his opening comment suggested, his single eye tracking whoever was speaking with an intensity that made Xander, more than once, lose his own train of thought mid-sentence.

"You alright, son?" Fury asked, at one point, catching Xander's hesitation.

"Yeah, sorry. Just — you're kind of intense to explain things to."

"I get that a lot." Something almost like humor flickered across his expression, gone as quickly as it arrived. "Keep going. You're doing fine."

"Here's what I keep coming back to," Fury said, once they'd finished, leaning back slightly, arms crossed. "Every piece of this traces back to the same source. The kid's ability. The hammer's landing here. The readings my people are tracking, and Strange's people independently, from what I understand."

"That's our working theory as well," Giles confirmed. "The fracture reaching backward and forward simultaneously, weakening boundaries between specific realities rather than causing broad, general instability."

"Which means this town isn't a coincidence. It's a symptom." Fury's expression didn't change, but something in his voice sharpened. "I don't like symptoms without a clear read on the underlying condition."

"Nor do we," Giles admitted. "We're rather hoping additional perspectives, once they arrive, might help clarify exactly that."

"Strange and his people. And whoever's coming from the other side, through the girl's family." Fury considered that, gaze moving briefly toward the window, toward the ordinary street outside that gave no hint of the enormity of what was actually unfolding inside. "Everyone converging on the same signal, from completely different angles."

"That does seem to be the pattern, yes."

Fury was quiet a long moment, working through something, the silence stretching long enough that Willow visibly resisted the urge to fill it. "I've spent my career assuming threats come from somewhere specific. An enemy. A weapon. Something with intent behind it." He looked at Giles directly, weight settling into the question. "This doesn't have intent, does it. This is just physics, breaking."

"That's rather less settled than I'd like it to be, if I'm being fully honest with you," Giles said, careful. "There's evidence pointing both directions, and I'd sooner give you an honest 'we don't fully know' than a confident answer I couldn't actually stand behind."

Fury's eyebrow rose slightly. "Evidence for intent, specifically?"

"Xander's own jumps are supposed to be entirely random," Willow said. "Four years of data backing that up completely, no exceptions, no pattern. Except three weeks ago, one of them wasn't random at all. It knew exactly where to put him. And I've started actually tracking something else, too — trips landing suspiciously close to exactly what we needed, right before the moments that mattered most. Small sample size still. Three data points. But it's not nothing."

"So something's choosing," Fury said, flat.

"Or something's responding," Giles said. "Which isn't quite the same as choosing, though I grant the distinction may prove less comforting than it sounds. Physics doesn't typically respond to need. Whatever this is might."

Fury considered that a long moment, something recalculating behind his single eye. "That's worse, in a lot of ways. Enemies negotiate. Physics doesn't. Something that responds might do either, depending on what it decides it's responding to."

The room went quiet at that, the weight of his observation settling over everyone present, even Thor shifting slightly in his seat as though the words carried a physical weight worth adjusting for.

"We'll get more data once the others arrive," Willow said, finally, breaking the silence, notebook already open on her lap. "Strange and Wong's people have been monitoring longer than us. And whoever's coming through Cent's family's connection at Home — they've got instruments Home's spent decades refining, way more sensitive than anything we've got access to."

"Good," Fury said. "Because right now, all I've got is the shape of a problem, not its actual size. I don't like operating that blind."

"None of us do," Giles said. "I suspect we'll all understand this significantly better once everyone's actually compared notes directly, rather than working from secondhand reports."

"Let's hope so." Fury's gaze swept the room once more, thoughtful, lingering a beat longer on Xander than on anyone else. "Because if this is accelerating the way your readings suggest, we're gonna need every piece of that picture, and soon."

"What happens if it's worse than we think?" Xander asked, quiet, the question he'd been sitting on since the meeting started finally finding its way out.

Fury considered him a long moment before answering, something almost gentle entering his usually unreadable expression. "Then we handle it. Together, with everyone this thing's already pulled in. You're not carrying this alone anymore, kid, whether you're used to that yet or not."

"Yeah," Xander said, something in his chest loosening slightly at the plain, unadorned reassurance of it. "Yeah, I'm starting to figure that out."

Nobody argued the point after that, the room settling into a quiet, focused determination that would carry through the following weeks — each new arrival bringing another piece of the same unfolding puzzle, all of them, in their own ways, being pulled toward the exact same source.
