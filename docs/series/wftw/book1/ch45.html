Chapter 45

The vampire hiding in the mausoleum had, by all appearances, been waiting there for hours, which Buffy only found out after she'd already walked past twice without noticing him.

"You're bad at this," she told him, once he finally lunged and she sidestepped without much effort. "Genuinely bad. I walked past you twice."

"I was being patient."

"You were being invisible in a way that only works if the person you're ambushing is also blind."

He swung again, wide and easy to read, and Faith caught his arm mid-swing from where she'd been leaning against a headstone the whole time, apparently having clocked him even before Buffy had.

"Rookie," Faith said, staking him with almost bored efficiency. "Didn't even check his surroundings before picking a spot."

"Some of them just don't get proper training," Buffy said. "It's honestly a little sad."

"It's a little sad that we're feeling sympathy for vampires now."

"I didn't say sympathy. I said sad. Different thing."

They cleared the rest of the cemetery without further incident, the night settling into the easy rhythm of a patrol that had turned up exactly one target and dispatched him without breaking much of a sweat, and ended up sitting on the low wall by the entrance afterward, the way they usually did when the night wound down early enough to actually enjoy the quiet.

"Giles thinks it might be a summoning circle," Faith said, without preamble, the words clearly having been sitting in her for a while before she let them out. "The mural thing. That's the current working theory."

"Summoning what?"

"Doesn't know yet. Says the style's not familiar." Faith picked at a loose thread on her jacket sleeve. "I keep running through what that could even mean. Demon. Something bigger than a demon. I don't love any of the options my brain keeps generating."

"You want backup on the research side? I can help dig through books, even if I'm not exactly Giles-level with the old texts."

"Appreciate it. Right now it's mostly a lot of waiting on Giles to find the right reference. Not much for extra hands to do yet." Faith looked over. "Thanks for asking, though. Means something, that you did."

"Anytime. It's still your thing to run. I'm just saying the offer's there if it turns into an all-hands situation."

"Noted. Filed under 'good friend, occasionally sappy.'"

"I'll take that filing."

They sat a while longer, comfortable in the quiet, before Faith finally stood and stretched, patrol clothes settling back into their usual loose, ready posture.

"West side's still uncovered tonight," she said. "You want it, or should I?"

"I've got it. You look like you could use an early night, given everything currently living rent-free in your head."

"Rude, but accurate." Faith managed a real grin despite everything. "Night, B."

"Night. Don't let the summoning-circle theory keep you up."

"No promises."

She didn't go straight to bed once she got in, either — paused instead in the doorway of Buffy's room, dark and quiet, moonlight catching along the hammer's handle where it still leaned against the closet door same as always. She crossed over without turning on the light, wrapped her hand around the grip, and pulled, gently at first and then with everything she actually had.

Nothing. Not so much as a shift.

"Yeah," she murmured, letting go, and blew out a breath through her nose, short and sharp, the kind she usually saved for a punch that didn't land clean. "Figured." She left before Buffy woke up and caught her at it, same as she always did, jaw tight the whole walk back to her own room, and didn't loosen it again until she was under the covers with the light off, staring at a ceiling that didn't have any answers either.

45.5. Chapter 45.5

Willow came along this time, glad for the chance after missing the last couple of trips to midterms, with absolutely no idea what she was walking into until the fog actually closed in around both of them.

The fog turned out to be a permanent feature, not weather passing through — a low mist that clung to the ground no matter the hour, thick enough to swallow anything more than a few yards off.

"Okay, this is atmospheric in a genuinely unsettling way," Willow said, peering into the gray. "Very haunted-moor energy."

"Very haunted-moor energy that's apparently also got wildlife in it," Xander said, catching movement at the edge of visibility. "Stay close. Don't want to lose you in this stuff."

They found the source of the strange calls they'd been hearing since arrival on the second day — creatures that moved like deer but called like something significantly stranger, voices carrying eerie and multiplied through the fog in a way that made both of them jump the first time they heard it up close.

"That is deeply unsettling," Willow said, notebook forgotten for a moment in favor of just listening. "In the best possible research way."

"Glad you're getting something out of my minor heart attack."

"I'm getting a lot out of it, actually. This is actually fascinating."

They spent the rest of the trip working carefully through the fog, cataloguing what they could, relying more on sound and touch than sight, and by the final day had developed an odd, comfortable rhythm — Willow listening for calls, Xander checking footing, both of them trusting the other in a way four years of solo work had never quite prepared him to expect from a research partner.

"This was a really weird one," Willow said, once the fog finally thinned enough that the last hours felt survivable instead of just strange. "Good weird, though."

"Yeah. Good weird's kind of the whole brand at this point."

They made it home with recordings Willow was already itching to analyze properly, tired in the good way that comes from a trip that asked something truly different of both of them and paid it back in full.

Buffy kissed them both hello in turn, then held Willow out at arm's length, studying her. "Okay, you've got a look. What happened."

"Deeply unsettling. Incredible. I loved every second of it," Willow said, already digging for her recorder to prove it.

"That tracks," Buffy said, grinning, and let herself be pulled toward the kitchen table to hear the whole thing properly.
