Chapter 75

Giles found the second reference on a Thursday night, alone in the Magic Box long after closing, and read it through twice more, polishing his glasses without quite noticing he'd done it, before deciding whether it was worth calling anyone about so late.

The passage was shorter than the first, tucked into a margin note rather than the main text, written in a hand different from the primary scribe's — an addition, apparently, from some later Watcher who'd read the original account and felt compelled to add something of their own.

The scattering did not end with the rite's completion, it read. Those who carry the echo have, on rare occasion, found one another across great distance, drawn by some sympathy neither fully understands. Whether by accident or by the echo's own quiet design, history does not say.

Giles read it three times, turning the implication over carefully.

Found one another. Not randomly discovered by outside research, but drawn — actively, if the margin note could be trusted at all, which was itself far from certain given how thin and uncertain the entire body of relevant scholarship remained.

He didn't call Willow that night. It felt too early, too speculative, exactly the kind of half-formed thread that deserved careful verification before he handed anyone else a hope that might not hold up under closer scrutiny.

But he copied the passage carefully into his own notes, dated it, and set it aside in a folder that was quickly becoming much thicker than he'd expected when this had all started as a simple question about a teenager's unusual habits.

'Drawn together,' he thought, closing the ancient text gently. 'If that's true, then finding another jumper may not be a matter of research at all. It may simply be a matter of time.'

He turned off the shop lights not long after, locking up carefully, and walked home under a sky that felt, for reasons he couldn't quite articulate, a little larger than it had that morning — full of old, quiet possibilities he hadn't fully appreciated until a teenager's strange weekends had cracked open a door into something notably older than any of them had bargained for.

Somewhere else entirely, on a world with no name that anyone back home would ever think to give it, the grass moved in a wind that had never once felt a human footstep disturb it, waiting, patient as it had always been, for whatever came next.
