Chapter 23

Joyce had started keeping a running list, taped inside a kitchen cabinet where she could consult it without anyone asking too many questions, titled simply Things I Now Accept Are Real. It had grown much since Buffy's arrival in Sunnydale, and Sunday dinner gave her a fresh entry roughly every other week.

"So this month we've had a sloth, a bee incident, and an emu rescue," she said, passing the salad down the table. "Did I miss anything?"

"Hillside," Xander said. "Don't forget the hillside. The hillside deserves its own entry."

"The hillside gets its own entry," Joyce agreed, adding it to her mental tally with the ease of a woman who'd long since stopped being surprised by anything involving this particular teenager's weekends. "How's your arm, by the way? The bee one."

"Mostly healed. Willow wanted photos of the swelling for her notebook, which, side note, is an actually unsettling request to get from a friend." Xander reached over and laced his fingers through Willow's under the table, mostly out of habit, the gesture small enough that Joyce pretended not to notice, the way she'd trained herself to over the past several months, while Buffy's chair had migrated close enough on his other side that she was more or less leaning against him for the second half of dinner, her head tucked against his shoulder between bites.

"It's for science," Willow said, entirely unbothered, squeezing his hand back without looking away from her plate.

"Everything's for science with you lately."

"Everything's interesting if you look closely enough."

"Gerald would disagree," Faith said with a grin. "Gerald just wanted to sit in a ditch in peace."

"Gerald's fine," Xander said. "Gerald's better than fine. Gerald's currently terrorizing his enclosure like nothing happened, which honestly, I respect the recovery time."

Joyce shook her head, smiling despite herself. "I remember when the strangest thing at this table was whether Buffy made curfew."

"Those were simpler times," Buffy agreed, stealing a piece of Xander's roll without asking, which he let happen without comment, the way he let most things happen where Buffy and food were concerned.

"I don't miss them," Faith said. "No offense to curfew, but a sloth's a way better dinner topic."

"None taken," Buffy said. "I don't miss them either. Actually, since when do you have opinions about my old curfew drama?"

"Since Xander told me the whole saga during patrol last week. Very dramatic. I give it a solid seven out of ten as far as teen drama goes."

"It was not a seven. It was way more dramatic than a seven."

"You weren't there for the delivery, B. Trust me, it played as a solid seven."

"Can I get a ranking too?" Willow asked. "I feel like my curfew drama never gets properly ranked, and I had a whole thing in ninth grade."

"Submit it for review," Faith said. "I take submissions."

"You're not an actual ranking authority, Faith."

"I've self-appointed. It's basically the same thing."

The table dissolved into good-natured argument after that, Xander weighing in with his own ranking of the curfew incident, Jesse — who'd been invited on the strength of having heard most of the recent chaos secondhand and therefore qualifying, by Summers-family standards, as extended family for the evening — trying to negotiate an actual scoring rubric, Willow quietly noting something in her ever-present notebook that had nothing to do with wildlife and everything to do with cataloguing this specific evening for reasons she didn't fully explain to anyone.

"I think that's everything," Buffy said eventually, once the argument had run its course and dessert had appeared without anyone quite noticing Joyce leave to get it.

"For now," Joyce said with a tired smile, and reached for the pie server, settling in for whatever the following Sunday would inevitably bring to add to the list.
