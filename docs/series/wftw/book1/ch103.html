Chapter 103

Xander wanted badly enough to just stay, given everything currently happening with Thor, that he caught himself hunting for a loophole again — a third close call in four years that neither of them was prepared to start treating as ordinary. Willow talked him down anyway, patient and firm, the same as she had both times before.

"It doesn't wait for gods to show up either," she reminded him. "You go when you go."

So he went, Buffy and Willow both along this time, and for one disorienting second the pull didn't feel like the ordinary lottery it always had — no scattering uncertainty beforehand, no held breath waiting to find out which world he'd open his eyes into. Just a single, sure certainty settling into his chest a half-beat before his boots actually hit ground, the same unmistakable rightness he remembered from every other time he'd landed at the fence line.

"Okay," Willow said, blinking, taking in the familiar field, the distant roofline, the fence itself stretching off toward the horizon exactly where it always was. "That's — that's not random, right? Landing somewhere you actually recognize has never happened before."

"Never once. Four years of nothing but random, and then—" He looked around, something settling and unsettling both moving through him at once. "This."

Buffy caught his hand, steadying more than asking. "You okay?"

"Yeah. Yeah, I think I actually am." He thought of the margin note Giles had mentioned once, almost in passing — jumpers finding one another, drawn by some sympathy nobody fully understood. "I think this might be that thing Giles found in the archive. The 'drawn together' thing. Didn't think it'd ever actually happen to me specifically. Didn't think it'd happen at all, honestly, given how thin he said the source was."

"You think the pull did this on purpose?" Willow asked, already reaching for her notebook despite the shock still visible on her face, old habit winning out even now. "Chose this, instead of just landing wherever?"

"I don't know if 'purpose' is even the right word for whatever this actually is. But it's the first time in four years it hasn't felt random, and that's gotta mean something."

Cent found them before they'd made it halfway to the house, Millie a few steps behind her, both of them already smiling at the sight of three people instead of the usual one.

"You brought company," Cent said, delighted, taking in Buffy and Willow both with open, curious warmth. "Finally. I was starting to think I invented you two."

"They're real," Xander confirmed. "Very real. Also apparently the reason the jump did something it's never done before, if my theory's right."

Millie's eyebrows went up at that, sharp interest replacing the easy welcome. "It's never landed you here on purpose before? Not once, in however many times you've made it back?"

"Not once. Every time before this was luck. This time it just — knew. Like something decided the odds didn't need to apply today."

"Huh." Millie considered that a long moment, something thoughtful moving behind her eyes. "That's worth writing down properly, if you're willing. We've got our own old stories about the door choosing, here and there, though nobody in this family's ever had solid proof of it happening in front of witnesses before."

"Willow's already halfway to a full page of notes," Buffy said, fond. "Give her ten more minutes and she'll have a working theory."

"Give me five," Willow said, without looking up.

They stayed the full three days properly this time, Cent walking Buffy through the parts of the farm Xander usually skipped past in favor of the fence line and the fields beyond it, Willow and Millie disappearing for hours into exactly the kind of technical conversation both of them had apparently been waiting years to have with someone who actually understood the questions.

"Okay, this is stupidly beautiful," Buffy said, on the second evening, all three of them sitting in the flattened grass beyond the fence watching the sky go orange, her head resting against Xander's shoulder while Willow leaned into his other side, the three of them tangled together loosely the way they usually ended up by the end of a long day. "After the last few weeks, I'll take stupidly beautiful."

"Can we talk about the whole Thor thing for a second," Buffy said, "Because I keep waiting for it to feel less insane and it's just not happening."

"Same," Willow admitted. "A literal god. In Sunnydale. Looking for a hammer that apparently decides who gets to swing it based on some cosmic worthiness test."

"And he wants to understand you better," Xander added, looking at Buffy. "Which, side note, still a weird sentence to have said out loud about my girlfriend."

"I know. I don't really know what to do with it either." Buffy picked at the grass, thoughtful. "Faith's been weird about the whole thing too. Quieter than usual."

"You noticed that too?"

"Hard not to. She usually can't resist a good comment. She's been sitting there just... not saying anything."

Willow looked between them, working through something. "You think it's about the hammer specifically? The whole not-being-able-to-lift-it thing?"

"Maybe. I don't want to push, though. Feels like the kind of thing she'll bring up when she's ready, not before."

"That's probably right," Xander said. "Faith's earned the right to process things on her own timeline. We've all seen where rushing her gets people."

They let the conversation settle into quieter things after that — the wildflowers, the strange, gentle wind, the easy comfort of three people who'd been through considerably more together than any of them had expected when this whole thing started.

By the time the jump pulled them home, they had a full catalogue of an fully new flower species, a memory card of really stunning photos, and a real calm that came from a trip that had, for once, been exactly as peaceful as it looked.

Joyce checked all three of them over out of habit, more out of relief than worry these days. "How's Cent's family?"

"Good. Really good," Buffy said. "No drama. Just flowers and a lot of talking."

"I'll take flowers and talking over penguins and bees any day," Joyce said with a grin, already turning back toward the kitchen, Thor's continuing presence in the living room sitting alongside everything else as just one more strange, manageable piece of an evening that didn't need to make a bigger deal of itself than that.
