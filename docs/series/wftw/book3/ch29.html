Chapter 29

Davy found me the next morning, coffee in hand, watching the horizon at the edge of the strip like it owed him an answer, and I got the sense he'd been waiting on purpose.

"Cent told me she told you," he said, no preamble. "About Apex Orbital. About Space Girl being my daughter, not some stranger on the news."

"She did. Still a little stunned, honestly. Watched the Grebenchekov footage same as everybody — never imagined the person under that visor was sitting across a cookfire from me a few weeks ago."

Davy managed something almost a laugh, proud and rueful at once. "That's Cent. Builds the big things quiet first, with people closest in, till it's solid enough to survive being seen. Did the same with the homestead." He looked out at the strip, settled and clear-eyed. "Millie and I helped where we could — I worked the pricing math, Millie took the first client call because Cent was too nervous. But the shape of it, the ambition — that's hers, completely. We just tried not to get in the way of something that good."

"Are you worried? The scale of what she's doing up there?"

"Every single time," he said plainly. "Doesn't ever fully go away. But I stopped letting the fear make her decisions a while back. Fear's never been a reliable guide to what's worth doing. Learned that the hard way, more than once, out here." A long breath. "Comms protocol stays. Sat phone, someone on the line every second, same as since that first five minutes nearly put Millie and me in an early grave from worry. Beyond that — I'd be a fool to stand in front of something this good just because it scares me."

Millie found us a few minutes later, the same settled clarity Davy had.

"She's going to do remarkable things," Millie said, watching the horizon alongside him. "Always suspected that, underneath the day-to-day of surviving out here. Just didn't expect the rest of you to find out this late."

"She won't need much from any of us," I said. "That's the part that gets me. Already worked out the pricing, the debris matching, without asking a single one of us for anything."

"She'll take advice if it's honest," Davy said. "Won't take charity, not even from people who love her. Never has." Something warm settling under the pride. "My job now isn't to build it for her. Just keep showing up on the other end of that sat phone and trust her with the rest."

"That's most of what any of us can offer anybody," Millie said. "Showing up. The rest, she'll manage."

We stood together at the strip's edge, and up at the compound I could hear Cent's voice, animated, already deep in conversation with Marie about something almost certainly orbital mechanics — two girls dreaming out loud about a future none of us had thought to imagine that large. Watching it unfold, easy and unafraid, I thought whatever the watcher had been measuring this whole strange month, it had gotten its answer plainly enough by now.
