Chapter 31

It came out to the rest of the group by accident, when Marie caught sight of the logbook still on the workshop bench and made the connection herself before anybody told her properly.

"Cent." Standing still, tablet forgotten, staring at a column header the same way I had. "These satellite IDs — same numbering scheme as the tracking catalog release from the Grebenchekov thing."

The room went still. Cent, to her credit, didn't flinch.

"Yeah," she said. "That was me."

"That was—" Marie sat down hard, shock and delight fighting for her face. "You're Space Girl. I watched that footage thirty times. Made Rick watch it. Made Joey watch it, and Joey doesn't watch anything that isn't cards or an engine."

"I did watch it," Joey said from the doorway, grinning. "More than once. Didn't clock the connection though. Very different look in a suit and visor than losing at cards on a Friday."

"We saw all the stuff about your rescue on the news," Rick added, quieter but no less genuine, "before we knew you existed as an actual person. Remember thinking, whoever that is, that's the bravest thing I've ever watched somebody do on live television." Something warm and awed under his usual reserve. "Didn't expect I'd end up teaching her to reset a transmission a few months later."

Cent's composure cracked, bright and embarrassed. "Wasn't that dramatic. Just did the math and went."

"Did the math and went, and pulled a dying man off a space station four hundred kilometers up with a jury-rigged suit and a phone call to General Sterling," Marie said. "You're allowed to let it be dramatic, just this once, for people who genuinely mean it."

Millie, catching the tail end, leaned in the doorway with quiet pride. "She'll never quite let herself sit in it. Comes home from something extraordinary and wants to talk about anything else within the hour."

"That's not true," Cent protested, without conviction.

"Completely true," Davy said, appearing behind Millie. "Had to schedule a conversation to get her to admit she was proud of the Fengyun debris remediation. Made three government agencies formally thank her."

"In writing," Millie added. "Actual letterhead."

Cent groaned into her hands, smiling underneath, and the room dissolved into the easy laughter that had become so ordinary I sometimes forgot how extraordinary the underlying facts actually were.

"For what it's worth," I said, once it settled, "I think Rick's right. Bravest thing I've ever watched somebody do, now that I know who was under the visor. And you get to be proud of it without it costing the quiet parts of your life you love just as much."

"Both things," Cent said, testing it out loud. "Space Girl and just Cent. Without one shrinking the other."

"Both things," Marie agreed. "Rather the whole lesson of this month. Nobody around here's just one thing anymore, if they ever were."

The evening wound on, Joey demanding a full account of deorbit mechanics with the enthusiasm of a man who'd been quietly obsessed with orbital physics since that first viral video and never had anywhere to put it — and watching the ordinary, extraordinary shape of it unfold, I thought that whatever the watcher had been measuring, it had gotten its answer plainly enough in exactly this kind of evening, over and over, long before any of us thought to ask it directly.
