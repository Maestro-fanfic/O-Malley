Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen opened on a flag rising over a ranch compound and two armed families approaching the same garden from opposite directions.

“In a world connected by an ancient wooden door, two groups of secretive, heavily armed, dimensionally complicated people will attempt first contact.”

Clara stood up from the grass with her hands raised.

“By not shooting first.”

This is Working for the Weekend: Baseline.

Meet Charlie, Clara, Rick, Marie, and Joey: the original Five, protectors of a wildside ranch, pilots, builders, researchers, and people who learned years ago that a ring of strangers appearing near the gate is rarely improved by panic.

Meet Davy, Millie, Cent, and Joe: a family of teleporters living on the same supposedly empty world, equally protective and equally capable of making a misunderstanding terminal.

Watch the opening standoff resolve through patience, raised hands, careful language, and everybody recognizing the specific look of people protecting a home rather than invading one.

Then watch strangers become monthly visitors, project partners, flying instructors, card-game rivals, and family in approximately the time most adults need to learn their neighbors’ last names.

Build airstrips. Establish the sat-phone protocol. Fly through the gate barn’s literal wooden door. Do not depict it as a glowing portal. Do not open it at the homestead for convenience. Remember the plane leg from Colorado.

“This Honest Trailer has been asked to emphasize,” the narrator said, “that the travel logistics are canon and the door has hinges.”

Follow Marie’s baseline instruments as they record proportional spikes around moments of crisis, trust, and response.

At first everyone thinks the anomaly measures danger.

It does not.

The presence behind the gate measures what people choose to become when danger arrives.

Watch Miriam confront eleven years of grief over Uncle Max, who died alone near the gate, and learn that his choices mattered to something larger even though knowing that cannot make him less dead.

The screen held on the padlock going warm and rigid while the transition flicker stopped halfway through.

The narrator spoke more quietly.

“The answer does not give Miriam her brother back. It gives the life he lived a witness. That is all the scene promises. That is enough.”

Watch Cent’s new friends discover that the girl losing at cards in their barn is Space Girl, the person they watched rescue a man from orbit on live television.

Cent tries to reduce the event to doing the math and going.

Marie declines to permit this.

Watch Davy learn to fly an actual airplane and explain to Cent that teleporting into orbit is not aviation, regardless of how strongly her successful landing record disagrees.

Watch Charlie and Davy compare the fear they carry, distinguish useful caution from the flinch that only makes a person smaller, and admit neither of them can always tell the difference in time.

Meanwhile, Joey loses at cards beyond statistical probability, Marie and Cent find someone who never needs an idea explained twice, Rick quietly proves excellent at everything nobody notices him starting, and Clara makes standing up first into the foundational act of two families’ shared life.

## The Baseline Drinking Game

Take a drink every time somebody says “same time next month.”

Take two once nobody needs to say it anymore.

Drink whenever a plane, truck, gate, and final ground leg are all required for one social visit.

Take a sip whenever Joey claims a loss was intentional.

Drink whenever Davy insists jumping is not flying.

Do not drink every time the manuscript carefully distinguishes the gate barn, hangar, and tack barn. The ranch has enough structures to defeat you.

## Starring

Charlie as The Boy Who Stood Up Second.

Clara as Impatience Mistaken for Bravery, Correctly.

Marie as Baseline Instinct.

Rick as Quietly Taking the Whole Board Apart.

Joey as The Mailbox Was Innocent.

Davy Rice as Teleporters Need Pilot Licenses Too.

Cent Rice as Space Girl’s Friday Card Game.

Miriam as The Gate Remembered Max.

The wooden door as No Shimmer, No Glow, One Padlock.

And Bessie-Two as A Cow With the Healthiest Relationship to the Plot.

Alternate titles appeared:

The Families on Both Sides of the Fence.

Same Time Next Month.

The Door Was Never the Point.

The narrator lingered on the last one.

“Excellent closing line. Still not the title.”

The card appeared.

# BASELINE

Directed by: the person willing to stand up first.

Written by: two families mistaking an introduction for a standoff.

Travel coordination by: a wooden door in Colorado and several pilots tired of explaining the route.

Special thanks to: the watcher, for measuring the answer instead of supplying it.

The last screen showed two families’ ordinary evening spilling across a strip of packed earth.

“What did the watcher measure?” the narrator asked.

Charlie looked toward the light and noise.

“We showed up for each other.”

“Right,” the narrator said. “That was always the baseline.”
