Chapter 32

Clara found me by the horse paddock a few evenings later, the compound quiet behind us, and something in the easy way she settled onto the fence rail told me this wasn't going to be a short conversation.

"I keep thinking about that first morning," she said, watching Bessie-Two graze in the near dark. "The flag going up. Riding out not knowing what we'd find. How much I assumed — poachers, something to handle and put down carefully."

"I remember."

"I think about how wrong I was, and it doesn't embarrass me the way I'd expect. Just humbles me. A little." She turned to look at me properly. "Two months ago I thought I understood your family's whole job. Managing a door. Now I've watched that understanding get taken apart and rebuilt twice over, and somehow it's ended up bigger and kinder than I'd have guessed."

"You held that standoff together better than I did," I said. "Standing up out of the grass, hands raised. Wouldn't have found the nerve half as fast without you going first."

"Wasn't being brave. Was being impatient." She laughed at herself, low. "Never been good at staying still inside uncertainty. Rather walk toward the answer, even a dangerous one, than crouch wondering what it might turn out to be."

"That's its own kind of brave, whatever you call it."

She let a moment pass, voice going softer. "Been thinking about what the watcher said too. About what people choose to become on the other side of an open door." She looked at me properly. "I think about you riding at a stampede without stopping to ask if it was smart. Your mother carrying weight none of us understood for years before setting it down. Davy building a life on nothing but stubbornness. Cent hiding a whole heroic double life because she wasn't sure any of us could hold both halves of her at once."

"What are you getting at?"

"I don't want to spend my life just watching people become extraordinary from the sidelines," Clara said, plain and direct. "I want to be part of whatever this turns into. Not just your girlfriend, tagging along on the interesting parts. Actually part of it. The homestead, the strip, Apex, whatever comes next none of us have thought to imagine yet."

Something in my chest went warm and unsteady, the good kind. "You've been part of it since the day you stood up out of that grass," I said. "Never a version of this where you weren't."

"Know that, logically." She took my hand, easy and sure. "Wanted to say it out loud anyway. Some things matter more once you've actually said them."

We sat at the fence rail a while longer, compound lights warm behind us, Bessie-Two grazing unbothered, living the uncomplicated life of a cow who never needed to understand any of the enormous things happening around her to be content.

"Same time next month," Clara said eventually, echoing the phrase that had already become the whole shape of what this month built between two families.

"Same time next month," I agreed. "And the month after. However long it takes to stop feeling like something we have to say out loud."

The stars came out, four times as many as any tame-side sky, and I sat with Clara's hand in mine, thinking that whatever the watcher had been measuring this whole strange month, it had gotten its clearest answer yet in exactly this — nothing dramatic, just two people choosing each other under a sky that never needed anybody's permission to be this beautiful.
