Chapter 33 of 59

False Gods

Buffy understood the Goa'uld before Daniel finished the first sentence.

“A snake crawls inside somebody, takes over the body, and pretends the face belongs to it.”

Daniel lowered the diagram of a symbiote wrapped around a human spine.

“That's an abbreviated description.”

“Is it wrong?”

“No.”

“Vampire with extra steps.”

Angel, standing near the library doors, looked faintly offended.

“Not the same,” he said.

Buffy glanced at him. “Predator hiding behind a human face and using the original person's memories?”

Angel considered that.

“Not entirely different.”

Teal'c stood before the table in civilian clothes that did nothing to conceal the gold emblem on his forehead.

“The host often remains aware,” he said. “Unable to control the body, but able to witness what is done with it.”

Willow went still. “For how long?”

“Centuries, in some cases.”

Xander listened from Destiny through the secure link. Eli's memories supplied names and faces the 1997 SGC had not yet met. He kept them to himself unless the immediate threat required otherwise.

Buffy looked at the weapons Carter had placed on the table: a staff weapon, a zat, and images of Goa'uld armor.

“How do we get the snake out without killing the person?”

O'Neill leaned against the circulation desk. “Usually, we don't.”

“Usually isn't the same as can't.”

“No.”

Teal'c regarded her with solemn approval.

“The Tok'ra possess methods of extraction. We do not presently know how to contact them.”

“Then we capture instead of kill when we can.”

“A Goa'uld will exploit such restraint,” Teal'c warned.

“So do vampires. We still don't stake the person they're holding in front of them.”

O'Neill looked at the others.

“We add capture to the list when it doesn't get our people killed.”

Buffy accepted the qualification. It was more than anyone had offered the hosts before she asked.

Daniel changed the display to the double-faced insignia.

“This Goa'uld has used the identity Janus for at least two thousand years. Teal'c's information describes him as a minor System Lord who survived by controlling travel between more powerful domains.”

“He taxes Stargates,” Willow said.

“He controls them,” Teal'c replied. “Those who enter his domain require permission to leave one settlement for another. Jaffa guard the Chappa'ai, mountain passes, bridges, ports, and archives. His priests teach that every change in a person's life occurs only by his consent.”

Giles looked toward the basement.

“The inversion is almost exact.”

“The real Janus opens doors,” Willow said. “The fake one locks them.”

Buffy sat on the edge of the table.

“Are we calling basement Janus real because he said so?”

The room paused.

“Thank you,” O'Neill said. “I've been saving that question.”

Daniel chose his answer carefully.

“We know he manifested during a working directed toward the historical Janus. We know his description matches accounts older than the Goa'uld cult. We know he provided accurate information while refusing worship, command, or intervention.”

“Powerful and weird,” Buffy summarized. “Not automatically morally perfect.”

“Precisely.”

Giles nodded. “There are beings worshipped as gods and beings that satisfy some definitions of the term. Neither condition makes them good.”

Teal'c touched the mark on his forehead.

“The Goa'uld are neither.”

He did not raise his voice. He did not need to.

✳

Basil's document cases contained the Council's history of Goa'uld encounters.

The Council had not called them Goa'uld. Its records used possessors, star serpents, speaking worms, and, in one Roman account, the gods beneath the skin.

Daniel read until anger replaced fascination.

The Council had recovered a broken hand device in 1863. It had concealed a Stargate address from the British government in 1911. It had questioned two dying Jaffa during the Second World War and classified their testimony as a secrecy risk.

None of the information had been shared.

None had been used to prepare governments, protect possible hosts, or identify an extraterrestrial threat.

The artifacts had been archived. The witnesses had been silenced. The Watchers responsible had praised one another for preserving institutional control.

“They knew,” Buffy said.

“They knew pieces,” Giles answered. “Enough to understand that the danger was not demonic and not confined to Earth.”

“And they filed it.”

Basil opened the last recovered folio.

“They filed this as well.”

The page dated from 1798. It described a hidden chamber beneath a ruined temple in southern Italy. Its walls bore a two-faced figure, a ring, and a key. The Watcher who entered it found no demon.

He found a communication device.

When activated, it displayed a man with shining eyes who named himself Janus and demanded the location of Earth's buried Chappa'ai.

The Watcher lied, destroyed the controls, and sealed the chamber.

That act had protected Earth.

The Council had rewarded him by removing him from field work for acting without authorization.

Giles closed the folio.

“They punished the man who made the correct decision.”

“He questioned orders,” Buffy said.

Nobody needed the rest explained.

Daniel examined the drawing of the device.

“This gives us more than history. Goa'uld long-range communicators use linked relay networks. If Janus maintained one connection to Earth, he may have others.”

Carter answered from the SGC.

“And the scout's narrow-beam bursts may be aimed at one of those relays.”

On Destiny, Xander brought the transmission times onto his console.

Three bursts. Separate. Incomplete.

Not navigational checks.

Pieces of a message.

The false god had begun telling his empire that the impossible door above Earth was real.
