Chapter 3 of 59

The Commander

Revival required twenty-seven minutes under ideal conditions.

Pod fourteen had not experienced ideal conditions in several years.

Destiny estimated thirty-four.

Eli spent the first six confirming that Young's vital signs were rising normally. He spent the next four trying to persuade the ship to open a communications search without flooding the display with every radio station on Earth.

He spent another minute looking at the date.

1997.

Every time he looked away, some part of him expected the numbers to correct themselves.

They did not.

The SGC existed. It had been operating in some form since the first Abydos mission, though Eli did not know how much of its later communications architecture existed this early. The frequencies in Destiny's expedition files came from more than a decade in the future. The encryption did not exist yet. Some of the satellites they referenced were not in orbit.

He could call NORAD.

That sentence would have sounded cooler if the situation had contained ten percent less terror.

Destiny had already identified Cheyenne Mountain through military signals and public records. Eli built a narrow transmission aimed at the complex, then hesitated over the message field.

If he announced an Ancient warship to the wrong duty officer, he could start a panic before Young opened his eyes.

If he waited, Earth might detect them first.

The ship answered that question by highlighting three ground-based systems that had begun tracking the anomalous contact above California.

“Right,” Eli said. “No pressure.”

He prepared a short identification burst but did not send it. Young needed to own the contact. That was not only military protocol. It was the difference between a terrified civilian making claims and the commanding officer of the expedition reporting an impossible emergency.

Pod fourteen chimed.

Eli was back beside it before the second tone.

The amber light turned white. Frost retreated from the glass. Young's chest rose sharply and stopped.

“Come on.”

It rose again.

The pod opened with a rush of cold vapor.

Young convulsed once, dragging air into lungs that had forgotten urgency. Eli caught his shoulder before he could pitch forward.

“Easy. Colonel, easy.”

Young's eyes opened without focus.

“Eli?”

“Yeah.”

His hand closed hard around Eli's forearm. “What happened?”

“The pod is stable. You're okay.”

“Why am I awake?”

There was no useful gradual version.

“We're at Earth.”

Young stared at him.

“Destiny dropped out, or moved, or something moved us. I checked the stars, the Moon, the atmosphere, satellite navigation, broadcasts—it's Earth.”

Young's grip tightened. “When?”

“1997.”

For the first time, Young's expression lost all command shape.

Eli helped him sit on the edge of the pod. Young's muscles trembled beneath the thin stasis garment, but his voice had already begun flattening into the tone he used when emotion threatened to interfere with the next necessary decision.

“Crew status.”

“All pods green. Nobody else awake.”

“Ship status.”

“Life support stable. Main drive available. Navigation can't explain our position. We're holding about thirty-five thousand kilometers above Southern California without orbital velocity.”

“Holding over what?”

“A town called Sunnydale.”

Young absorbed that as if Sunnydale might be the familiar part.

“Hostile contacts?”

“None in space. Earth tracking stations are starting to notice us.”

“Why did you wake me?”

The question was not accusation. Young wanted Eli's reasoning.

“Because you're the commander.”

Something moved behind Young's eyes.

He looked past Eli at the rows of pods.

“You didn't wake Rush.”

“No.”

“Good.” Young tried to stand. His knees failed immediately.

Eli caught him again. “Still thawing.”

“I noticed.”

“I can bring the display here.”

“I want to see it.”

Eli knew what he meant.

They made the journey slowly. Young leaned on him for the first corridor, then used the wall. By the second stairwell he could walk without either, though his face had gone grey with effort.

At the observation deck, the doors opened.

Young stopped.

Earth had turned while Eli worked. The western coast lay closer to night. Cities had begun illuminating the land beneath them, faint nets of gold spread along roads that both men knew.

Young approached the glass.

He did not touch it. He stood at attention without appearing to realize he had done so.

“Show me.”

Eli brought the verification onto the main display: stellar fix, lunar position, atmospheric composition, geographic match, satellite identities, time signals.

Young read everything.

“Could be an alternate Earth,” he said.

“Could be.”

“Simulation.”

“I checked internal processing. If Destiny is generating it, it's hiding the resource use from itself.”

“Time travel.”

“The date says yes. Everything I know about physics says stop saying that.”

Young almost smiled. It vanished before it arrived.

“Our younger selves?”

“Down there, probably.”

“Do not access them. Do not search for families. Nobody else wakes until we establish command authority and understand what happened.”

Eli's mother rose in his mind so vividly that he could see her at the kitchen table, pretending not to watch him eat.

“Understood.”

Young looked at him.

Eli corrected himself. “Yes, sir.”

“You said Earth is tracking us.”

Eli opened the alert. “Three systems have partial returns. None of them agree about our size. We're too high for most radar and in the wrong place for every orbit they understand.”

“Stargate Command?”

“Active. General Hammond should be in command by now.”

“Should?”

“It's 1997. My historical briefings were less detailed than I suddenly wish they were.”

Young considered the planet again.

“If George Hammond commands the SGC, we report to him.”

Relief arrived so sharply Eli nearly swayed. “I have a transmission ready.”

“Let me see it.”

The draft appeared.

CHEYENNE MOUNTAIN COMPLEX. ATTENTION STARGATE COMMAND. REQUEST SECURE CONTACT. COLONEL EVERETT YOUNG, USAF. DESTINY EXPEDITION.

Young read it twice.

“Add Hammond's name. Include my service number and current biometric file. Mark the records as future-dated and therefore non-authenticating. Append a sample of the Ancient database and our sensor solution.”

“That's going to get attention.”

“That's the idea.”

Eli made the changes.

“What if Hammond isn't there?”

“Then the officer who is there will find him.”

Young straightened. He still wore the thin clothes issued for stasis. His hands shook slightly from revival. He looked exhausted, displaced, and completely certain of the next step.

“Send it.”

Eli touched the control.

The transmission left Destiny as a narrow beam aimed at Cheyenne Mountain.

At Stargate Command, a technician listening to a dead military channel sat upright so quickly that his headset cord caught beneath his chin.

The message repeated.

He called his supervisor. His supervisor called the control room. The control room confirmed that the transmission had arrived from above California, then failed to identify the object that had sent it.

Three minutes later, General George Hammond entered the briefing room with Colonel Jack O'Neill, Captain Samantha Carter, Doctor Daniel Jackson, and Teal'c behind him.

The identification packet glowed on the screen.

O'Neill read the dates attached to Everett Young's service record.

“Either Personnel has started predicting promotions,” he said, “or Colonel Young has twelve years of service we haven't lived through yet.”

Carter stood close to a second display filled with Ancient characters.

“Sir, this isn't a decorative sample. It describes modifications to a Stargate addressing system. Parts of it are consistent with the material we've recovered, but the gate design is older than anything we've seen.”

Daniel looked from the symbols to the image assembled from ground tracking: an immense, indistinct shape hanging above the western United States.

“How old?”

“Possibly millions of years.”

Hammond read the message again.

“Can we answer?”

The technician nodded. “They've provided a return channel.”

Hammond took the microphone.

“Unknown vessel, this is Major General George Hammond of Stargate Command. Colonel Young, if you can hear me, identify your situation.”

On Destiny, Earth answered.
