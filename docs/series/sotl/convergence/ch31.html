Chapter 31 of 59

The Observer

“A parasite who has claimed his name,” Teal'c said.

Rush looked up sharply.

“That is impossible.”

O'Neill glanced at him through the screen. “You're going to have to narrow that down.”

Rush brought the scout's insignia into focus. Two profiles faced away from one another inside a ring of gold. Between them stood a narrow vertical shape that might have been a staff, a blade, or a key.

“Janus was an Ancient scientist.”

Daniel stared at him.

“Janus was an Ancient?”

“A Lantean. His work on temporal mechanics was restricted even by the standards of his own people. At least two devices attributed to him survived into the modern era.”

Carter looked from Rush to the secure feed from Sunnydale, where Giles and Willow stood in the damaged library.

“So we have a Roman god, an Ancient scientist, and a Goa'uld.”

O'Neill folded his arms.

“Which Janus?”

“Yes,” Xander said.

No one found the answer reassuring.

Daniel recovered first. “The name may not be coincidence. Ancient developed into Latin. If the historical god and the Ancient scientist were ever conflated—”

“The Goa'uld steals both traditions,” Giles said. “A divine identity from Earth and a technological legacy from the Ancients.”

Teal'c inclined his head.

“The Goa'uld called Janus controls few worlds compared with Apophis. He is old, cautious, and skilled at recovering what others have abandoned. His Jaffa guard Stargates, archives, roads, and conquered places of passage. He claims that no door may open without his will.”

Buffy's voice came from behind Giles.

“The real one would hate him.”

Daniel remembered the figure standing inside the circle, hands empty.

“I don't know whether hate is the right word.”

“I do.”

Young returned their attention to the scout.

“What has it seen?”

Park reconstructed the vessel's path from Destiny's restored sensor logs. The scout had entered the system while Destiny was approaching the Sun. It had remained behind Jupiter during the recharge, then moved far enough into the open to observe the return.

It knew an Ancient vessel could enter a star and survive.

It knew the vessel could disappear from Earth and return by a route the scout could not track.

It knew Earth had communicated with the ship.

Depending upon the sensitivity of its instruments, it might know that Destiny's arrival point centered on Sunnydale.

“Has it transmitted?” Hammond asked.

Volker studied the emissions record. “We detected three low-power bursts while it was behind Jupiter. Tight beam, aimed out-system.”

“Destination?”

“Unknown.”

“Content?”

“Unknown.”

Rush magnified the timing. “They may be navigational checks. Or fragments of a report intended to be assembled by a relay.”

“Assume report,” Young said.

Hammond nodded. “Agreed.”

O'Neill looked at the image. “Can Destiny catch it?”

Scott would have answered that question if Scott were awake. Young felt the absence before he considered the solution.

“The ship can,” he said. “Whether this crew can operate it safely in combat is another matter.”

Rush made a quiet sound.

“You disagree?” Young asked.

“I disagree with the implication that permitting the vessel to escape is safer.”

“That isn't what I said.”

“It is the practical result of delaying.”

Carter pointed to the scout's projected course. “It's still using Jupiter as cover. It hasn't accelerated for a system exit.”

“Why not?” Hammond asked.

Teal'c answered.

“Its commander does not yet believe it has been detected.”

Young examined the scout's position relative to Destiny's restored sensors.

The Goa'uld craft had hidden successfully from every instrument the ship possessed before the recharge. It had no reason to know those instruments now extended beyond Jupiter.

“Then we preserve that belief,” he said.

Rush turned from the display. “While doing what?”

“Preparing.”

Young faced Hammond.

“General, I recommend waking Lieutenant Scott, Master Sergeant Greer, Lieutenant James, and the minimum military and technical personnel required for flight operations, damage control, and internal security.”

“You intend to engage?”

“I intend to give you that option before the scout realizes we have one.”

Hammond considered the distance, the sleeping crew, and the single enemy vessel watching from the dark.

“Approved. Track the scout passively. No weapons fire and no course change toward Jupiter without my authorization.”

Young straightened.

“Yes, sir.”

Hammond looked beyond him to Earth.

“Colonel, if that vessel sends useful intelligence, we may be dealing with more than one scout.”

“Understood.”

The channel closed.

Young watched the small gold mark beside Jupiter.

“Keep every active system at its previous output,” he ordered. “As far as the observer is concerned, we are still half-blind.”

Destiny dimmed its newly awakened senses and continued pretending not to see.
