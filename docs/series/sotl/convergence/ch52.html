Chapter 52 of 59

The Trojan Scout

The captured scout entered Cardea's docking bay under its own power.

Carter had restored just enough propulsion to make the damage believable. Plasma scoring remained across the hull. One engine produced an irregular flare. The communications array transmitted the correct codes with the hesitation of a system routed through emergency control.

Inside, SG-1 waited behind false bulkheads.

Teal'c wore the armor of the scout commander's First Prime. The gold emblem of Apophis on his forehead remained exposed; concealing it would have drawn more suspicion than the story prepared to explain. Carter and Daniel occupied compartments shielded from routine life-sign scans. O'Neill waited inside a maintenance passage with enough explosives to turn the scout into wreckage if capture failed.

The docking clamps engaged.

Air moved between the vessels.

Teal'c lowered the ramp and walked out alone.

Cardea's Jaffa surrounded him before his second step.

Their First Prime wore a hinge-shaped mark pressed into his forehead. He examined Teal'c's armor, wounds, and foreign emblem.

“You bear the mark of Apophis.”

“I was taken in battle and placed aboard the observer as punishment,” Teal'c answered in Goa'uld. “The Tau'ri attacked. My commander fell. I returned the vessel through cunning denied to him.”

Contempt and ambition belonged naturally enough in the account that the First Prime recognized both.

“Where is the host called Limen?”

“Compromised. The last transmission was sent before capture.”

The First Prime raised a scanning device.

Inside the scout, Carter watched its signal pass through the shielded compartments. The Tok'ra material surrounding them absorbed the first sweep.

The second penetrated farther.

“We get one more,” she whispered.

Daniel held the stolen Jaffa robes closed over a compact pack of tools.

“Then?”

“Then they count four people where Teal'c promised one.”

O'Neill's voice came softly through their earpieces.

“Let's not wait for advanced math.”

Teal'c attacked.

He struck the scanner aside, drove one armored shoulder into the First Prime, and opened the docking-bay control panel with a code taken from the scout. Lights failed along the inner wall.

SG-1 left the ship in darkness.

O'Neill and Teal'c took the passage toward engineering. Carter and Daniel moved toward shield control. The docking bay sealed behind them before Cardea's guards restored power.

The Trojan scout had survived exactly long enough.

✳

Cardea knew they were aboard within three minutes.

She did not sound an alarm.

Instead, she sealed the internal compartments one by one and allowed her Jaffa to hunt in silence. A general alarm would warn Destiny that the deception had failed. Janus had ordered Cardea to preserve the approach until his flagship reached capture range.

SG-1 used that restraint against her.

Carter reached the shield-control junction and attached the first Tok'ra device. Daniel copied the command architecture while she worked. They could not lower the shields yet without revealing themselves, but the device would interrupt regeneration when Destiny struck.

O'Neill and Teal'c reached the hyperdrive.

The chamber rose through six decks, its central crystals rotating inside a field of blue light. Destroying it would prevent escape and might destroy the ship. Carter's charges were designed to disable the control paths instead.

“Two points,” O'Neill said.

Teal'c looked upward. “Three. Cardea will have added a redundant command route.”

“You sure?”

“She is known as keeper of locks.”

“Right. Three.”

They separated.

Carter's Tok'ra scanner activated as Cardea passed the deck above her.

The display showed one symbiote, one host, and two distinct patterns of cortical activity.

Both were awake.

The host's activity surged against every motor command the symbiote issued.

“Sam,” Daniel whispered.

“I see it.”

Cardea stopped overhead.

For half a second, the scanner recorded the host gaining control of one hand. It moved toward a wall panel. Cardea seized it back before the fingers touched.

The host remained present.

The host was resisting.

Carter transmitted the reading to O'Neill.

He received it while placing the second hyperdrive charge.

“Can the Tok'ra extract her?”

“If we capture Cardea alive.”

“Add it to the plan.”

Teal'c met his eyes across the machinery.

“It will reduce our chance of escape.”

“I know.”

It would not replace Earth's survival as the mission. It would change what counted as success if choices remained.

O'Neill armed the charge.

“Nobody gets volunteered for death because the snake got there first.”

The first Jaffa patrol entered the hyperdrive chamber.

Teal'c raised his staff weapon.

The quiet part of the operation ended.
