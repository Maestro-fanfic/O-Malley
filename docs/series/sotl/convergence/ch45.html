Chapter 45 of 59

The Host

The Goa'uld chose a church.

It had been abandoned after the 1971 earthquake and condemned after three separate attempts to renovate it ended in unexplained deaths. The Council had used its crypt as a temporary archive until 1986. Anne Mercer knew that because she had catalogued the contents before the Goa'uld took her.

She offered Basil the archive at midnight.

She demanded Xander Harris attend the exchange.

The answer was no.

“If we refuse outright, she disappears,” Carter said.

The planning room at the SGC held SG-1, Saroosh and Selmak, Basil, Buffy, Giles, and Young through the Destiny channel. A floor plan of the church covered the table.

“Then we make her think he's there,” O'Neill said.

Xander appeared on the screen. “Before we get to the part where I become pretend bait, can we define pretend?”

“Your voice and enough of Destiny's access signal to register on her equipment,” Carter said. “You remain aboard the ship.”

Young looked at Xander. “You can refuse.”

“I know. What happens if I do?”

“We develop another plan.”

Xander considered the photograph of Anne Mercer.

“Use the signal.”

Wray recorded the consent and its limit. Voice and technical signature only. No live control link. No location data. No representation that Xander had agreed to surrender.

Buffy watched until the language satisfied her.

Then they discussed force.

The simplest plan was a sniper.

Mercer's body would die. The Goa'uld might survive long enough to seek another host, but Teal'c could prevent that.

Buffy rejected it.

“Anne Mercer is still in there.”

O'Neill did not disagree. “A capture plan gives the Goa'uld time to activate weapons, kill the host, or take somebody else.”

“Then make a better capture plan.”

“Buffy,” Giles said, “if the creature possesses the means to transmit—”

She turned on him.

“You don't get to call killing the girl inside an acceptable test.”

The room went still.

Giles absorbed the blow.

“No,” he said. “I do not.”

Selmak provided the alternative.

The Tok'ra carried a compound that disrupted a symbiote's control over the host's motor nerves for several seconds. It had to enter the bloodstream. The dose had a narrow safe range. Too little would fail. Too much could stop both hearts.

“Meaning?” Buffy asked.

“It could stop Anne's heart and kill them both.”

“Can Anne take control when it works?”

“Perhaps briefly. More likely neither will control the body.”

“Briefly is enough.”

Carter designed a dart delivery system. O'Neill placed shooters on two angles. Teal'c would block the crypt exit. Selmak could sense the Goa'uld at close range and confirm the target. Daniel and Basil would conduct the exchange.

Buffy assigned herself the distance between the dart and the floor.

If Mercer activated a hand device, Buffy would stop her.

If the Goa'uld tried to kill the host, Buffy would keep the body alive long enough for Fraiser and the Tok'ra team to intervene.

No one asked whether that was possible.

They prepared as if it were.

✳

Mercer entered the church carrying a metal case.

She looked forty-five. The Goa'uld healing her from within had kept her almost unchanged for seven years. Her face in the Council photograph belonged to the same woman, but the expression did not.

Daniel waited near the ruined altar. Basil stood beside him. A small transmitter in Daniel's coat produced Xander's technical signature.

Mercer's eyes moved to it immediately.

“Where is the boy?”

“Safe,” Basil said.

“You misunderstand the exchange.”

“No. We reject its terms.”

Mercer set the case on a broken pew.

“Then the archive is lost.”

“There is no archive,” Daniel said.

Her eyes flashed.

“You are more perceptive than the Watchers.”

“A low threshold.”

The Goa'uld smiled.

It activated the device beneath its sleeve.

Xander's false signal strengthened.

“Now,” Selmak said from the crypt.

Carter fired.

The dart struck Mercer's shoulder.

The Goa'uld tore it free before the full dose entered. Its hand device opened. Gold energy threw Daniel across the altar and drove Basil to one knee.

Buffy crossed the church before the second pulse.

She struck the gauntlet sideways. The energy discharge shattered a stained-glass window instead of Daniel's skull. Mercer hit Buffy with strength no ordinary human possessed. Buffy went through two pews, rolled, and returned before the Goa'uld reached the side door.

Teal'c blocked it.

“Limen,” Selmak called.

The operative stopped.

“You know me,” the Goa'uld said.

“We know the names of those who sell their hosts to Janus.”

Limen's eyes burned white.

“The host opened willingly.”

From inside Mercer's body, one hand began to shake.

Selmak saw it.

“No,” Selmak said. “She did not.”

The compound was working.

Limen forced Mercer's arm toward the hand device. Buffy caught the wrist. The Goa'uld drove the other hand toward Mercer's throat, fingers shaped to crush the airway.

Buffy seized that wrist too.

For several seconds, they stood locked together: Slayer strength against Goa'uld strength, Anne Mercer's body between them.

“You cannot save her without saving me,” Limen hissed.

“Then you get to live through the arrest.”

Buffy forced both hands away from Mercer's throat.

Carter fired the second dart.

This one struck the thigh and remained.

Mercer's knees failed.

Buffy lowered her instead of letting her fall.

The glowing eyes faded.

For one breath, Anne Mercer looked out through her own face.

“My name,” she whispered.

Buffy leaned closer.

“Anne.”

Mercer's mouth trembled.

“Don't let it—”

Limen seized control again. The eyes flashed, but the body no longer answered.

Fraiser and the Tok'ra medical team entered from the rear of the church. They secured Mercer's airway, disabled the hand device, and placed a restraint at the base of her neck designed to prevent the symbiote from leaving.

Limen remained conscious.

So did Anne.

The metal case contained no archive.

It contained a long-range communication crystal keyed to the false Janus's relay network.

Carter examined its active buffer.

“Can we use it?” O'Neill asked.

Selmak's eyes glowed.

“Yes.”

For the first time, Earth possessed a channel the false Janus believed belonged to someone he trusted.
