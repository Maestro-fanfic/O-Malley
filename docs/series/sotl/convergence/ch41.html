Chapter 41 of 59

The Offer

The false Janus was mobilizing.

Selmak's intercepted orders showed Cardea recalling troops from three gate worlds and moving her Ha'tak toward a rendezvous outside Janus's territory. Two lesser Goa'uld had offered technical slaves familiar with Ancient ruins. A third had offered maps to worlds where temporal disturbances appeared in religious records.

Janus had not yet committed his flagship.

He was asking questions before risking it.

“He knows the vessel exists,” Selmak said. “He does not know its name, condition, or origin. Your captured packet prevented him from learning more.”

“How long before he gets answers elsewhere?” Hammond asked.

“Days, if one of his scholars recognizes the solar collectors. Weeks, if they do not. The profile is fragmentary, but the Goa'uld have catalogued Ancient vessels for millennia.”

Rush appeared on the screen from Destiny.

“Their catalogues are assembled from technology they barely understand.”

“Which has not prevented them from conquering much of this galaxy,” Selmak replied.

Rush accepted the point with poor grace.

The Tok'ra offer had four parts.

They would continue intercepting Janus's communications. They would supply current fleet positions, gate routes, encryption changes, and intelligence on Cardea. They would assist with the captured scout, the transport, and safe removal of the Goa'uld symbiote from its host if the necessary specialists could reach Earth.

In return, they requested the scout's archive, Destiny's sensor record of the engagement, information concerning the ship's capabilities, and supervised technical access to verify what Earth possessed.

Hammond answered immediately.

“No access to Destiny.”

Selmak did not object. “Not even under supervision?”

“Not at this stage.”

Young spoke from the screen.

“Destiny is under my command. Access requires General Hammond's authorization and mine. Neither will be granted during a first meeting.”

“Your caution is reasonable,” Saroosh said.

Selmak took control. “It is also inconvenient.”

“Those can both be true,” O'Neill said.

Hammond continued. “You will receive the portions of the scout archive required to identify its routes and contacts. Captain Carter may provide selected sensor data after technical review. Destiny's capabilities remain classified.”

“Janus already knows an Ancient vessel defends Earth.”

“Then you know as much as he does.”

Saroosh's mouth curved slightly.

“Perhaps more. He has never been told no without killing the speaker.”

“We try to offer new experiences.”

Basil examined the proposed exchange from the far end of the table.

“What prevents you from selling the same information to Janus?”

“Nothing you can verify today,” Selmak said.

The directness made O'Neill look at her more favorably.

“The Goa'uld consider us enemies. Janus has attempted to eradicate our cells on seven worlds. We oppose everything he wishes to do with the vessel. These facts align our interests. They do not require you to mistake alignment for affection.”

Wray spoke from Destiny. “And what prevents Earth from using your information to capture your agents?”

“Nothing we can verify today,” Saroosh answered.

The symmetry settled the negotiation.

They began smaller.

The Tok'ra provided one relay location. Earth provided the relevant section of the scout's transmission log. Carter and Selmak independently derived the same encryption sequence from different sources. Teal'c confirmed two of Cardea's commanders. Daniel verified that the language in the intercepted orders matched the captured archive.

Trust accumulated in facts that survived comparison.

Then Buffy asked about the host.

“The one inside the scout commander. Can you get the Goa'uld out?”

Selmak's answer lost its diplomatic distance.

“Possibly. Extraction risks damage to the host's spinal cord and brain stem. A Goa'uld may release toxins or deliberately injure the host while being removed.”

“But you've done it.”

“Yes.”

“Will you help?”

Saroosh answered.

“Yes.”

Selmak did not revise the promise.

Hammond authorized a Tok'ra medical team under the same restrictions that applied to Saroosh and Selmak. No one would enter Destiny. No one would receive unrestricted access to SGC systems. All exchanges would be copied, logged, and independently reviewed.

In return, the Tok'ra would establish a continuous warning channel and notify Earth if Janus's flagship moved.

It was not an alliance.

It was enough cooperation to survive the next week.

For now, that was more valuable.
