Duty in the Wrong Time

Giles began without greeting.

“Xander is a minor.”

“We've noticed,” O'Neill said.

“Then you understand that he cannot be detained aboard a military vessel without cause.”

“Technically, it's not our military vessel.”

“Colonel.”

“We're not detaining him. The only ride down is an alien shuttle controlled by a ship that arrived through something your friend described as a magical doorway. Our doctor wanted to make sure a return trip wouldn't turn him inside out.”

Giles removed his glasses, though O'Neill could not see it.

“Ethan is not my friend.”

“Good. I'd hate to meet your enemies.”

“Lieutenant Johansen has examined him?”

“She has. He's physically stable.”

Buffy's voice came faintly through the line. “Then bring him home.”

O'Neill shifted the receiver. “I'm guessing that's Miss Summers.”

“You can tell him I said please if that helps,” Buffy called.

“It did not sound like please,” Giles said.

“It was implied.”

O'Neill looked toward Hammond.

The general held out his hand for the phone.

“Mister Giles, this is Major General Hammond.”

Giles's tone changed, becoming more formal without becoming less determined. “General.”

“Mister Harris is not under arrest, enlisted, or in our custody. He is aboard a vessel whose commanding officer has accepted my authority but whose legal status has not been determined. I will return him as soon as transport can be conducted safely.”

“When?”

“Tomorrow, if the medical and engineering assessments permit it. SG-1 will accompany him.”

“To Sunnydale?”

“We need to examine the location beneath the vessel and the shop where the event began.”

Giles was silent.

“You have concerns,” Hammond said.

“Many.”

“So do I. We can discuss them in person.”

“General, the forces involved are not amenable to military containment merely because one possesses sufficient weapons.”

“I have learned the same thing about most problems worth taking seriously.”

That answer interrupted whatever Giles had expected to say next.

“Xander comes home tomorrow,” Buffy said in the background.

Hammond allowed himself the smallest breath of amusement. “That is the plan.”

✳

The first formal command conference between 1997 Earth and the Destiny expedition took place across a table built before either participant's country existed.

Young sat at one end. Wray sat opposite him. Rush remained standing, claiming the available chairs positioned him poorly to see the systems display. TJ occupied the place nearest Xander.

SG-1 represented Hammond aboard the ship. Hammond attended by secure video.

“Colonel Young,” Hammond began, “state your understanding of your status.”

Young had prepared the answer.

“I am an active-duty United States Air Force officer displaced from a future date. My original orders placed me in military command of the Destiny expedition. I recognize you as the senior officer presently exercising lawful command of the Stargate program.”

“And Destiny?”

“Remains under my operational command pending relief or modification of orders.”

“Do you claim authority independent of this command?”

“No, sir.”

Rush folded his arms.

Wray did not. “The civilian expedition members did not surrender their nationalities or rights when they passed through the Stargate. Colonel Young can recognize your military authority. He cannot do so on behalf of every person asleep aboard this ship.”

Hammond nodded. “Agreed.”

The immediate concession surprised her.

“Miss Wray, I do not intend to claim ownership of the vessel, its civilian personnel, or its technology. I do intend to prevent uncontrolled contact from altering lives before those affected understand the consequences.”

“Then we need a review structure that includes civilian representation.”

“Propose one.”

Wray glanced at Young.

He gave a slight nod.

“I will.”

Hammond turned to Rush. “Doctor, your preliminary assessment.”

Rush brought the ship diagram onto the display.

“Destiny is functional in the limited sense that it has not yet failed. The transit interrupted several automatic repair cycles. Power reserves are sufficient for current operations and stasis, but I cannot offer a long-term estimate without access to propulsion, primary power distribution, and the navigational core.”

“You have supervised access to noncritical systems.”

“Which is rather like asking a physician to diagnose a patient while remaining in the corridor.”

“A physician who has previously attempted to take command of the patient,” Young said.

Rush looked at him. “If you intend to relitigate every decision made aboard this ship—”

“I intend to remember them.”

Hammond raised one hand.

The argument stopped.

“Doctor Rush, Captain Carter will work with you. You will have the access necessary to assess immediate danger. Any attempt to activate propulsion or alter the ship's position requires Colonel Young's authorization, and he will report it to me.”

“The ship may not wait for your bureaucracy.”

“Then I suggest you make your report clear.”

Rush studied Hammond's image. “You are not what I expected.”

“You have the advantage, Doctor. I have no expectations of you yet.”

O'Neill lowered his eyes to hide a smile.

“Mister Harris,” Hammond said.

Xander straightened.

“You are medically cleared to return to Earth tomorrow. Before that happens, I need to establish what information you possess and what continuing connection exists between you and Destiny.”

Giles's warning echoed in his mind: military containment, sufficient weapons.

“What does establish mean?”

“An interview. Voluntary technical demonstrations. No invasive procedure without your informed agreement.”

Rush shifted his weight.

Young noticed.

“He is not a member of my command,” Young said. “He is not subject to an order to perform.”

Wray added, “And consent given by an isolated minor who believes the crew's survival depends upon him requires particular scrutiny.”

Xander looked from one to the other.

“I'm sitting here.”

“Yes,” Wray said. “Which is why the decision should be addressed to you.”

Hammond waited.

Xander considered the ship beneath his hands. Somewhere below, the stasis pods continued their slow consumption of power. He could feel no mystical leash, no command demanding he stay. Only Eli's knowledge of every problem waiting in the dark.

“I'll answer questions,” he said. “And I'll show Carter what happens when I use a console. I want TJ there.”

“Agreed,” Hammond said.

“No needles.”

“Unless medically necessary and authorized by Lieutenant Johansen.”

“No cutting me open to see if Ancient technology falls out.”

O'Neill looked offended. “That wasn't on the schedule until Thursday.”

Hammond gave him a look.

“No invasive experimentation,” the general said. “You will also have Mister Giles available by telephone during the interview.”

Xander nodded.

Young watched the exchange. “He returns to Sunnydale afterward.”

“Yes.”

“He will need a means to contact the ship.”

“We will provide one through the SGC.”

“Directly,” Xander said.

Everyone looked at him.

“If something happens to the people in stasis, I don't want a message sitting in somebody's in-box because I don't have clearance to know they're dying.”

Hammond considered him for a long moment.

“A monitored emergency channel,” he said. “Direct to the SGC and Destiny.”

“Okay.”

“Anything else?”

Xander thought of Buffy waiting beside Giles's telephone.

“Yeah. I get to tell my friends enough that they aren't wondering whether a secret military base is going to make me disappear.”

“We will determine the necessary classification together.”

It was not a yes. It was not a no.

For the first time in his experience with adults and secrets, Xander believed the difference might matter.
