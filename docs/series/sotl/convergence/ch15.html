Chapter 15 of 59

The Red Phone

The President answered on the fourth ring.

“General Hammond.”

“Mr. President.”

“The last time you called this line, an ancient spaceship had appeared over California.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Is it still there?”

“Yes, sir.”

“Is this call likely to improve my afternoon?”

“No, sir.”

The President exhaled. “Proceed.”

Hammond had condensed the report to two pages. He did not begin with vampires. He began with the citizen whose existence made them a matter of presidential responsibility.

“The location beneath Destiny is a town called Sunnydale. SG-1 has confirmed sustained hostile activity there involving nonhuman predators. The principal combatant containing that activity is Buffy Summers, an American citizen aged seventeen.”

Silence answered.

“Seventeen,” the President repeated.

“Yes, sir. She was called into service at fifteen.”

“Called by whom?”

“The origin of her abilities appears supernatural. Direction and training are provided by a private British organization known as the Watchers Council.”

“Does this organization operate with the knowledge of the British government?”

“Unknown.”

“Our government?”

“No confirmed knowledge at the federal level. Sunnydale's municipal statistics suggest either institutional concealment or willful failure to recognize the threat.”

“What exactly has the girl been doing?”

“Nightly patrols, direct combat, intelligence gathering, and response to events with mass-casualty potential. She previously confronted an entity called the Master beneath the town. She died during that engagement and was revived by Alexander Harris.”

“The boy from Destiny.”

“Yes, sir.”

The President was quiet again.

“How long has this been going on?”

“According to Colonel O'Neill, for most of recorded time.”

“Miss Summers?”

“Miss Summers has held the position for approximately two years. She is only the latest in a succession of adolescent girls. Most appear to have died in service in relatively short order.”

“Appear?”

“The Council controls the detailed records. Public sources are fragmentary.”

“Compensation?”

“None.”

“Medical care?”

“No institutional provision.”

“Insurance, pension, survivor benefits?”

“None.”

“Legal status?”

“None.”

The President's voice became quieter with each answer. “Her parents agreed to this?”

“Her father is not involved. Her mother is apparently unaware.”

“General, are you telling me that a foreign organization has a minor child waging an uncompensated war on American soil, and her own mother doesn't know?”

“Yes, sir.”

“Is she effective?”

“Exceptionally. Colonel O'Neill reports that she commands naturally in contact, demonstrates extensive combat experience, and has built a civilian support group that compensates for the resources the Council does not provide. She may be the first active Slayer deployed permanently to a Hellmouth in centuries.”

“A Hellmouth.”

“The term refers to a persistent weakness between dimensions beneath Sunnydale High School. It may be the reason Destiny appeared over the town.”

“Of course it is.”

The dry answer reminded Hammond that the man on the other end had received an impossible ship before breakfast.

“What do you recommend?” the President asked.

“Immediate authority to provide Miss Summers and her associates with communications, medical care, intelligence support, and emergency assistance. She should not be conscripted, detained, or subjected to involuntary testing.”

“Agreed.”

“I also recommend a federal review of Sunnydale's law enforcement, coroner, hospital, and municipal administration. We need to determine whether their failures are negligence, compromise, or active concealment.”

“Agreed.”

“The Watchers Council requires investigation at the highest level available to the British government. It may be older than the government itself and appears to possess international reach.”

“Do they claim ownership of the girl?”

“They claim authority over the Slayer. Their doctrine distinguishes the continuing line from the individual who carries it. Colonel O'Neill's assessment is that they regard each girl as replaceable.”

The silence that followed was longer than the others.

“No,” the President said.

It was not disagreement. It was a decision.

“Sir?”

“No private organization, foreign or domestic, owns an American child. I want that made clear before anyone from this Council reaches her.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Your authority is expanded to include the Sunnydale situation. Colonel O'Neill remains on site. Miss Summers receives protection, not orders. Any agency requesting access to her or Harris comes through you.”

“Understood.”

“Prepare a written summary for the Attorney General and the necessary defense personnel. Keep distribution narrow until we know which institutions are compromised.”

“Yes, sir.”

“I will contact the Prime Minister personally.”

Hammond looked at the report.

“Mr. President, there is one point I recommend you state exactly.”

“Which point?”

“An organization based in Britain has deployed an American child into an undeclared war on American soil. If it claims authority derived from the Crown, then Her Majesty needs to know what has been done in her name.”

The President repeated the sentence once, committing it to memory.

“Anything else?”

Hammond thought of Buffy returning a stake to her jacket and expecting to continue patrol while she bled.

“Yes, sir. Colonel O'Neill does not believe this is a case in which the Council attempted to support a Slayer and failed.”

“What does he believe?”

“That supporting the individual girl was never the system's purpose.”

The President's answer was immediate.

“Then we find out what was.”

The line disconnected.

Hammond remained at his desk for several seconds, one hand resting on the red telephone.

In Washington, another secure call crossed the Atlantic.
