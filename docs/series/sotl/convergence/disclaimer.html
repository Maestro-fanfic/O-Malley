Author's Note & Acknowledgments

This story exists because of two people: one who asked for exactly this, and one who never did. Both deserve real credit up front.

First, to Zaion, whose "Ship of the Line" personal challenge is the entire reason any of this happened at all. The premise, the rules, the shape of the whole idea, all of it traces back to that original challenge, and this story is one attempt at answering it.

Second, to Ack (ack1308), whose fics "Security!", "I, Panacea", and "I, Scion" — and the shared central conceit running through all three, a writer who keeps finding himself pulled bodily into the very stories he's writing — provided the specific shape and history of the fourth voice riding along inside Artemis in this story. That character concept, and the "literal drop-in" fanon built up around it, belongs to Ack, and this story borrows it with real gratitude and no claim of ownership whatsoever.

✳

Disclaimer

Buffy the Vampire Slayer belongs to Joss Whedon, Mutant Enemy, and 20th Television, none of whom asked for a spaceship. Stargate SG-1 and Stargate Universe belong to MGM and their respective creators, who definitely did not sign off on a teenager casually acquiring one of their more expensive Ancient warships. No Man's Sky belongs to Hello Games, who built an entire procedurally generated universe that I have now, with great affection, bolted directly onto a Hellmouth. The West Wing belongs to Aaron Sorkin, John Wells Productions, and Warner Bros. Television, and if any of them are reading this, I am so sorry about the em dashes. "Security!", "I, Panacea", "I, Scion", and the character concept they originated belong to Ack. The "Ship of the Line" challenge belongs to Zaion, who is fully responsible for setting this entire chain of events in motion and should be regarded accordingly.

None of it is mine. Not one line, not one lightsaber-adjacent starship hull, not one alien priest. No money is being made, largely because nobody has yet found a way to monetize "what if five unrelated properties had a baby." This is a not-for-profit work of fan fiction, offered up entirely for love of the source material, a genuine appreciation for chaos gods, and the specific, deeply unearned joy of watching Jack O'Neill and Rupert Giles exist in the same sentence.

Not my circus, not my monkeys. I just bought one ticket, somehow ended up owning five separate circuses, introduced all the monkeys to each other backstage, and am now legally required to clarify that I still don't own the tent.
