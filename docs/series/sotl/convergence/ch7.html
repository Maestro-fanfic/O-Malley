Quarantine

The first rule of coming home was that nobody was allowed to call home.

Hammond delivered the order without ornament.

“Until we know what has happened, Destiny is under communications quarantine. No transmissions except through the channel to this facility. No contact with family members, present-day counterparts, civilian authorities, or other military commands.”

Young stood before the observation-deck console, one hand resting beside the microphone.

“Understood, sir.”

“Passive sensors only. Captain Carter will coordinate the data requests. Do not transmit future operational history unless I specifically authorize it.”

“Yes, sir.”

“I am assembling a boarding team. That will take time.”

Young glanced toward Xander. “We will wake Lieutenant Johansen and complete an initial medical assessment.”

“Report as soon as she is functional.”

“Yes, sir.”

The visual link closed, though the encrypted carrier remained open. Earth continued turning beyond the glass.

Xander stared at the place General Hammond's face had been.

“He seems nice.”

Young gave him a look.

“In the sense that he didn't order anyone to shoot us.” Xander rubbed both hands over his face. The glasses shifted against his fingers. “My standards have adjusted.”

“Can you walk?”

“Probably. Is this a medical question or an order?”

“Both.”

Xander pushed himself upright. The deck remained beneath him, which felt like cooperation.

Young watched every movement. Xander knew why. Eli had occupied the same space a few minutes earlier. Every time Young looked at him, recognition began and failed.

Xander wished he could stop noticing.

He knew Everett Young's command voice. He knew the man's silences, his anger, the way exhaustion drew a deeper line beside his mouth. He remembered arguments he had never witnessed and forgiveness Eli had never managed to request cleanly.

He also remembered meeting Young less than an hour ago.

Both memories insisted they were first.

“Which way?” Young asked.

Xander pointed toward the door without thinking.

“You know the route.”

“Yeah.”

“Lead.”

The corridors lit ahead of them. Destiny recognized Eli's habitual path even when the person walking it did not match the stored body. Each open door felt like a question the ship was waiting for him to answer.

They reached the stasis bay.

Xander stopped at the threshold.

Rows of pods curved away beneath amber light. Faces waited behind glass: people Eli loved, disliked, mistrusted, depended upon, failed, saved. The emotional weight arrived without context, a crowd of feelings searching for owners.

Young stepped past him.

“Johansen's pod.”

Xander knew the number. He hated knowing it.

“Thirty-two.”

Young found it. Tamara Johansen lay inside with her hands relaxed at her sides. A medical officer in suspension, unable to give consent to being woken into her own past.

Young placed his palm on the controls.

“Destiny, begin revival of Lieutenant Johansen.”

The ship requested confirmation from its commanding officer.

Young gave it.

The indicator turned amber.

“Twenty-nine minutes,” Xander said as the estimate appeared.

“Sit down.”

“I'm okay.”

“That wasn't the question.”

There were benches along the center of the bay. Xander sat.

Young remained beside TJ's pod, reading the diagnostics as if attention could accelerate them.

“Colonel.”

“What?”

“Eli meant what he said.”

Young did not turn.

“About me.”

“I heard him.”

“Right. I just—he knew you'd think this was my fault even if you knew it wasn't.”

Young faced him then. “Is that what you think?”

Xander looked down at Eli's badge in his hand.

“I put on the costume.”

“Did you cast the spell?”

“No.”

“Did you bring the ship here?”

“Not on purpose.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

Xander tried to separate memory from inference. He found the instant of transformation: a street, Willow's sheet, a child in an astronaut helmet. Then Destiny, Earth, Eli's hand on the glass. Between them lay a doorway and something vast enough to decide that distance did not matter.

“I don't know.”

“Then neither do I.” Young looked back at TJ. “Until we do, you are not responsible for Eli.”

The wording left room for a future verdict. Oddly, that made it easier to believe.

Hammond waited until Young's image disappeared before issuing the next order.

“Nothing from this room leaves this facility.”

The technicians answered in near unison.

“The transmission is compartmented effective immediately. All tracking data comes here. If another command asks what is above California, they are to be told the contact is under NORAD review.”

O'Neill remained beside the table. “That answer has a shelf life.”

“I am aware of that, Colonel.”

“Something that size doesn't stay a radar glitch forever.”

Carter shook her head. “It may be harder to detect than its size suggests. The hull absorbs or scatters most active returns. The stations that found it only did so because it appeared inside volumes they were already tracking.”

“So our giant ancient spaceship is stealthy by accident.”

“Not stealthy. Difficult.”

“Much better.”

Hammond looked at the map. A line descended from Destiny's impossible position to a town on the California coast.

“Sunnydale.”

Daniel read from a second screen. “Founded late nineteenth century. Spanish settlement before that. The mission records are inconsistent.”

“In what way?”

“Populations disappear from several early surveys. A church was built and abandoned without diocesan explanation. There are references to earthquakes that don't appear in regional geological records.”

Carter opened municipal statistics. “The current mortality rate is also anomalous.”

“How anomalous?” Hammond asked.

She gave him the figure.

O'Neill stopped smiling.

“That's not a town,” he said. “That's a combat zone with property taxes.”

“Mister Harris provided three names,” Hammond said. “Find them. Quietly.”

O'Neill picked up the list.

Rupert Giles. Buffy Summers. Willow Rosenberg.

“The high-school librarian, a seventeen-year-old girl, and another seventeen-year-old girl.”

Hammond's expression did not change. “That is what he gave us.”

“I'll make the calls.”

“You will not mention the Stargate or the vessel until their identities are confirmed.”

“And if they ask where Harris is?”

Hammond looked toward the tracking image.

“Tell them he is alive. For now, that is the only fact we can give them with confidence.”

✳

TJ woke fighting.

Her hand struck Young's chest. Her other arm caught the edge of the pod and nearly pulled her to the floor. Young held her upright without trying to restrain her wrists.

“Lieutenant. TJ. Look at me.”

Her eyes found him.

“Colonel?”

“You're safe.”

“Why—” Her gaze moved past him to the rows of pods. “What failed?”

“Nothing in stasis. I woke you.”

TJ tried to stand. Young supported her while she tested her legs.

“How long?”

“We don't know.”

That answer reached her. “Where's Eli?”

Xander had been waiting near the far console, where she would not see him immediately. Young's eyes moved toward him.

TJ followed.

For one second her face opened with hope.

Then she saw him clearly.

“Who are you?”

Xander stood. “My name's Xander.”

“Where is Eli?”

Young answered. “Gone.”

“Gone where?”

“We don't know.”

TJ looked between them. “You woke me because Eli is missing?”

“I woke you because the ship underwent an unknown transit, Eli was physically replaced by a minor, and we are at Earth in 1997.”

She stared at him.

“I need you to perform medical assessments on all three of us and verify the stasis system.”

“You said Earth.”

“Yes.”

Young did not try to soften it. He helped her to the observation deck and let the planet do what explanation could not.

TJ stood before the glass for a long time.

When she finally turned, her eyes were wet and her expression was professional.

“Medical bay,” she said. “Both of you.”

✳

Destiny's medical equipment had been designed by people who regarded written instructions as evidence of insufficient intelligence.

TJ understood more of it than she had when the expedition began. Eli understood most of what remained.

Xander remembered both facts and possessed neither skill cleanly.

He sat on an examination platform while pale light moved from his head to his feet. Ancient symbols collected above him.

“Don't translate unless I ask,” TJ said.

“I wasn't.”

“You were reading ahead of me.”

“Sorry.”

“Don't apologize. Just let me determine what you know without assuming.”

Young stood near the door wearing clothes taken from an equipment locker. They fit badly but provided more dignity than stasis garments.

TJ reviewed the scan.

“Human male. Approximately seventeen years old. No evidence of stasis damage. No indication of radiation exposure from the transit. Elevated stress markers, mild dehydration, bruising consistent with recent physical activity.”

“Halloween involved running,” Xander said.

“It usually does?”

“In Sunnydale? More than you'd think.”

TJ adjusted the neurological display. “Destiny has Eli's previous scans. Your brain isn't his.”

Young's jaw tightened.

“But?”

“But the ship is registering activity in response to Eli's personal access patterns. I can't tell whether that's memory, learned behavior, or something its systems don't have a category for.”

“Can you tell if he's in there?” Young asked.

TJ looked at Xander rather than the scan.

“No machine can answer that question.”

“I did,” Xander said quietly.

“You answered whether you could hear him. That isn't the same as proving what remains.”

She lowered the display.

“Medically, you are Xander Harris. I will treat you as Xander Harris.”

He released a breath.

TJ turned to Young. “Your turn.”

“I'm functional.”

“That wasn't the question, Colonel.”

Xander looked at Young.

Young looked back.

Neither of them smiled.

He sat on the platform.

✳

The telephone rang in Giles's apartment shortly after midnight.

Buffy reached it first.

“If this is my mother, we're studying.”

There was a pause.

“This is Colonel Jack O'Neill, United States Air Force. I am trying to reach Rupert Giles.”

Buffy covered the receiver. “It's the Air Force.”

Willow stopped pacing.

Giles took the phone.

“Rupert Giles.”

“Mister Giles, I need to confirm the identity of a young man named Alexander Harris.”

Giles went still. “Where is he?”

“Alive.”

“That was not my question.”

“It's the answer I'm authorized to give until I know who I'm speaking to.”

Giles's hand tightened around the receiver.

“Then tell me how you obtained this number.”

“Harris gave us your name. We found the number ourselves.”

“Let me speak to him.”

“Soon. First, tell me what happened in Sunnydale tonight.”

Giles looked at Buffy and Willow.

“You would not believe me.”

“Mister Giles, I have spent the last hour talking to a future Air Force colonel on a prehistoric spaceship. Take your best shot.”
