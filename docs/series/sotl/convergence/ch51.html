Chapter 51 of 59

The Demand

The first Ha'tak emerged beyond the Moon.

Space folded around a pyramid large enough to cast its own night. Gold light ran along its edges as shields formed. Four Al'kesh appeared behind it, followed by gliders in paired formation.

Cardea arrived first.

Janus's flagship emerged ninety seconds later.

Destiny detected the hyperspace window before it opened and pretended not to. Volker kept the restored sensors passive. Scott allowed the ship to drift three degrees from stable orientation before correcting with a deliberately uneven thruster pulse.

“They are scanning us,” Park said.

Rush watched the false failures propagate through the external diagnostic layer Carter had built for anyone attempting remote analysis.

“They see what we intended.”

“Do they believe it?” Young asked.

“Belief is not a sensor reading.”

Xander felt the mooring tighten as two hostile ships entered the space around Earth. It did not restrain Destiny. It felt more like a road recognizing strangers at its far end.

“Ground link stable,” he reported.

Young opened the channel to Hammond.

“Both targets confirmed. Holding position.”

“Hold until they commit.”

“Yes, sir.”

The Goa'uld fleet advanced beyond the Moon.

Cardea's vessel separated toward a high orbit from which it could strike the SGC, Sunnydale, or Destiny's ground track. Janus approached the Ancient ship directly.

The captured scout transmitted Limen's identification code from the lunar shadow.

Cardea answered with a docking vector.

The first part of the trap remained alive.

Janus hailed Earth.

His image appeared at the SGC, aboard Destiny, and on every command screen participating in the defense. He sat before the dead Stargate mounted behind his throne. One side of his host's face appeared youthful beneath gold light; the scar divided the other into something older.

“People of the first world,” he said. “You stand before a door that was closed to you.”

Hammond answered from beneath Cheyenne Mountain.

Young remained visible but silent beside him.

“This is General Hammond, United States Air Force.”

Janus's eyes flashed.

“You speak for the vessel?”

“Colonel Young commands Destiny. I speak for Earth.”

The distinction interested the Goa'uld.

“Earth belongs to Ra.”

“Ra is dead.”

“Then Earth belongs to the hand capable of opening it.”

“It belongs to the people who live here.”

Janus smiled with someone else's mouth.

“All inhabitants believe themselves owners until the gate opens.”

Hammond did not debate theology with an invasion fleet.

“State your demand.”

Janus rose from the throne.

“You will surrender the Ancient vessel called Destiny. You will deliver the shol'va Teal'c, the human Alexander Harris, and every text and instrument associated with the threshold beneath Sunnydale. Your Stargate will be restored to its proper place beneath my seal. Your military will disarm.”

His voice deepened through the transmission.

“In return, your world will retain its cities, its children, and the privilege of passage by my permission.”

In Sunnydale, Buffy looked at the schoolchildren's drawings still pinned to one library wall.

On Destiny, Xander kept his hands flat against the console.

At the SGC, Teal'c stood where Janus could see the mark of Apophis on his forehead and the absence of submission in his posture.

Hammond said, “No.”

Janus waited for more.

Hammond provided none.

“You refuse the lives of your people?”

“I refuse your demand.”

“The result is the same.”

“Only if you choose to attack.”

For the first time, anger altered the symmetry of Janus's face.

“I choose which roads remain open.”

Hammond leaned slightly toward the camera.

“This one is closed.”

He terminated the transmission.

On Cardea's Ha'tak, the docking bay opened for the captured scout.

SG-1 crossed the shield boundary.

Janus's flagship began charging weapons.

Cardea launched the first ground transports toward Sunnydale.

The siege of Earth began.
