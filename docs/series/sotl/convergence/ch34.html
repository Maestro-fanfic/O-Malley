Chapter 34 of 59

The Signal

Xander knew the pattern because Eli had once used it to send a message through a failing Kino relay.

The technologies had nothing in common. The logic did.

“It's not three messages,” he said. “It's one message cut into pieces with overlap.”

Carter enlarged the burst records at Stargate Command. “Forward-error correction?”

“More aggressive. Each packet repeats the addressing header and enough of the packets before it to reconstruct missing data. You don't have to receive all of them.”

Volker ran the first three bursts through a reconstruction model.

Fragments appeared.

The symbols were Goa'uld. Teal'c and Daniel translated as the computer assembled them.

ORIGIN: FORMER DOMAIN OF RA.

ANCIENT VESSEL PRESENT.

TRANSITION OBSERVED—

The remainder collapsed into incomplete characters.

“How many packets until the recipient has the core report?” Hammond asked.

Xander studied the overlap.

“Five, probably. Seven for everything.”

“Probably?” Rush said.

“Unless you have the Goa'uld compression manual.”

Rush looked at Teal'c.

“I do not,” Teal'c said.

“Then probably.”

Park detected a fourth emission building behind Jupiter.

It was not broadcasting yet. The scout was aligning a narrow-beam array with something beyond the system.

“Can we jam it?” Young asked.

Carter answered from the SGC. “Not without revealing that we can detect it, and Destiny's jamming may be as informative as the report.”

“Destroy the transmitter,” Rush said.

“And the navigation records, intelligence, and crew with it,” Wray replied.

“Those are secondary concerns if the alternative is an enemy fleet.”

Young looked at the reconstructed message.

The first three packets might already have reached a relay. Destroying the scout would not recall them. It would tell the false Janus that the observer had found something important enough to die over and leave Earth knowing nothing about what else the scout had done.

“We disable and capture,” he said.

Rush turned. “With which fully functional weapon and experienced flight crew?”

“The ones we have.”

“Colonel,” Scott said from flight control, “the scout is faster than our current sublight limit. If it sees us coming before we're in range, it can leave.”

“Then it doesn't see us coming.”

Young transmitted his recommendation to Hammond.

Destiny would maintain its station and apparent sensor profile while plotting a faster-than-light exit behind the scout's line of sight. It would reappear close enough to prevent a clean escape, disable propulsion and communications, and demand surrender.

The maneuver carried risk. Destiny's FTL systems had only recently regained full power. No one aboard had used them tactically. Arriving too close to Jupiter's gravity well could destroy both vessels before weapons mattered.

Hammond read the plan.

“Alternatives?” he asked.

“Allow the report to continue or destroy the scout at maximum range,” Young said.

“Probability of capture?”

“Unknown.”

“Probability of preventing the next transmission?”

“Better than if we remain here.”

Hammond looked toward the President's secure line. He did not use it immediately. The standing authority for defense of Earth covered one hostile reconnaissance vessel. Escalating every tactical decision would leave Young commanding by committee across twenty-one years.

“Capture if feasible,” Hammond said. “Prevent further transmission. If those objectives conflict, Earth comes first.”

“Understood.”

Before Young could end the channel, Park spoke.

“Colonel, I found another contact.”

A much smaller craft had separated from the scout during Destiny's solar recharge. It had coasted inward with minimal power, hidden against the noise of the Sun. Restored sensors found its wake only after Park knew where to look.

Its course ended at Earth.

“Time to arrival?” Young asked.

“It may already be there.”

Carter searched the SGC tracking network. No radar contact matched the projected approach.

Teal'c recognized the profile.

“A Goa'uld transport. It may carry rings capable of placing warriors upon the surface without landing.”

O'Neill was already moving.

“Target?”

Volker extended the transport's last measurable course.

The line crossed California.

Its uncertainty cone narrowed around Sunnydale.

The fourth packet began transmitting.

Young turned toward Scott.

“Execute.”

Destiny vanished from above Earth.
