Chapter 21 of 59

New Terms

Sir Basil Covington arrived in Sunnydale with one suitcase, two legal cases, and no visible security.

O'Neill noticed the security anyway.

One man read a newspaper in the hotel lobby without turning a page. A woman near the entrance wore shoes selected for running rather than diplomacy. A rental car remained occupied across the street.

“Subtle,” O'Neill said.

Basil handed his coat to the desk clerk. “That is generally the aspiration.”

“Sir Basil Covington.”

“Colonel Jack O'Neill.”

They shook hands.

“General Hammond speaks highly of you,” Basil said.

“He hides it well.”

“A sound leadership practice.”

O'Neill decided he liked him provisionally.

✳

Buffy agreed to the meeting because Xander asked.

She chose the library because it was her ground and sat where she could see every entrance. Willow occupied the chair to her right. Xander sat on her left with his radio to Destiny clipped at his belt.

Giles remained behind the counter until Buffy looked at the empty chair across from her and said, “You should hear this too.”

He sat at the far end of the table.

It was not forgiveness. It was more than he had expected.

Basil entered with O'Neill, Carter, Daniel, and Teal'c. He carried only one of the legal cases.

He did not offer Buffy his hand until she offered hers.

“Miss Summers.”

“Sir Basil.”

“Thank you for agreeing to see me.”

“People keep saying that right before they tell me what my choices are.”

“Then I shall begin with what they are not.”

He opened the case and removed a document bearing several seals.

“The Watchers Council presently possesses no authority to issue you an order, restrict your movement, control your associations, conduct a test upon you, or replace Mr. Giles without your consent.”

Buffy looked at Giles.

“Presently?”

“The suspension is immediate. My expectation is that it will become permanent. I will not promise the outcome of proceedings that have not yet been completed.”

“Honest,” Xander said.

“Occasionally useful.”

Basil placed a second document on the table.

“The Crown has withdrawn protection from any Council member attempting to approach you without authorization. General Hammond has placed the equivalent American restriction upon all federal personnel and agencies.”

“So instead of them owning me, two governments do?”

“No.”

The answer came without qualification.

“You are an American citizen and a civilian minor. The United States cannot compel you into military service. The Crown has never possessed lawful ownership of the Slayer. The Council converted a duty to support into a claim of command.”

“And you're here to fix it.”

“No institution repairs centuries of abuse by delivering documents to one girl.” Basil sat opposite her. “I am here to tell you what has been stopped, what is being investigated, and what support is being offered. You will decide what you accept.”

Buffy folded her arms. “What's the price?”

“There is none.”

“There's always a price.”

“There are conditions on the use of classified communications and weapons. There will be accountability for public funds. Those are terms governing resources, not your obedience. Compensation is owed for service already rendered.”

“I didn't work for you.”

“No. You worked while those with the means to assist failed to do so. That distinction does not erase the obligation.”

Buffy looked at the documents but did not touch them.

Basil continued.

“The provisional American offer includes medical care without fabricated explanations, secure communications, emergency extraction, intelligence support, and equipment selected with your participation. Compensation for future service would be placed under a civilian arrangement, not military enlistment.”

“Past service?” Willow asked.

“A joint legal team is determining retroactive compensation and a protected trust.”

Xander leaned forward. “Survivor benefits?”

Buffy's head turned toward him.

He did not apologize for asking.

Basil answered. “Yes. Also disability provision and support for designated dependents.”

“I'm seventeen,” Buffy said.

“Which is why none of this should have been left to you.”

Her expression hardened.

“That doesn't mean it gets taken away from me now.”

“Agreed.”

She studied him, searching for the concealed instruction.

“Who decides when I patrol?”

“You do.”

“Who decides who comes with me?”

“You do, provided those people also choose.”

“Who commands when something crawls out of the Hellmouth?”

O'Neill answered. “You do. My people handle civilians, perimeter, communications, and anything you assign that we're trained to do. If it's a military threat, we coordinate. If it's your kind of ugly, you're lead.”

“And if your orders say different?”

“Then I call Hammond and tell him the orders are going to get people killed.”

“What if he disagrees?”

“Then he gives me a direct order and accepts responsibility for it. That's what command is supposed to mean.”

Buffy glanced at Giles.

The comparison hurt both of them.

Basil placed a smaller envelope beside the documents.

“Her Majesty has asked to speak with you, if you are willing.”

Buffy stared at him. “The Queen.”

“Yes.”

“Of England.”

“Among other places.”

“She wants to talk to me.”

“She does not require it.”

Buffy looked at Willow.

Willow mouthed, The Queen.

Xander's expression suggested space had finally lost the competition for strangest week.

“Okay,” Buffy said. “But everybody stays.”

“Of course.”

The secure call used the Air Force equipment installed behind the circulation desk. Basil authenticated both ends. The screen remained dark; the Palace had offered audio so Buffy would not have to perform for a camera.

“Miss Summers,” the Queen said.

Buffy sat straighter. “Your Majesty.”

“I have read your file.”

“Which one?”

A pause followed. Not offended. Considering.

“Both,” the Queen answered. “The Council's account and the record reconstructed from what you actually accomplished.”

“Oh.”

“I am sorry it required an American officer's report before the institutions of my country examined what was being done.”

Buffy looked down at her hands.

“I don't know what I'm supposed to say.”

“Nothing. This is not an audience in which you owe me anything.”

The room became completely quiet.

“Sir Basil has explained the suspension?”

“Yes.”

“Mr. Giles will remain your Watcher only if you choose him. The title will carry no command authority over you.”

Giles looked at the table.

“And if I don't choose him?”

“Then he will not.”

Buffy swallowed.

“What's going to happen to the Council?”

“That will depend upon what each person did. Those responsible for crimes will answer through the courts. Those who attempted to protect their Slayers will be judged on that conduct as well. The institution will not be permitted to conceal either behind tradition.”

“You found the other girls.”

It was not a question.

“We are finding them.”

“How many?”

“Four hundred and twelve named with certainty so far. There will be more.”

Buffy closed her eyes.

“Most of them died.”

“Yes.”

“Are you reading all of them?”

“Every single one.”

Buffy breathed in slowly.

“Good.”

The Queen did not mistake the word for gratitude.

“Sir Basil will leave the proposed terms with you. Take whatever time you require.”

“What if something happens tonight?”

O'Neill reached into his jacket and placed a secure radio on the table.

The Queen heard the movement.

“Then support is available whether or not you have signed anything.”

Buffy picked up the radio.

“Okay.”

“Miss Summers.”

“Yes?”

“Come home afterward.”

Buffy's fingers closed around the radio.

“That's the plan.”

The connection ended.

✳

Basil left the papers with Buffy.

She did not sign them.

She read every page with Willow and Xander. Daniel explained the legal references. Carter described what medical and communications support could actually be delivered. O'Neill answered the questions concerning command.

Giles answered only when Buffy asked him directly.

Near sunset, she reached the provision concerning her Watcher.

“Do you want to stay?” she asked.

Giles did not protect himself with the obvious answer.

“Yes.”

“Would you have done it?”

Everyone knew what she meant.

“I don't know what the man I was yesterday would have done when the order arrived,” Giles said. “The man I intend to be will not.”

“That's still not a promise about yesterday.”

“No.”

Buffy looked at him for a long time.

“You can stay while I decide.”

Giles inclined his head. “Thank you.”

“Don't thank me yet.”

“No.”

Xander's radio activated.

Young's voice filled the library.

“Xander, report your condition.”

Xander touched the receiver. “Normal. Why?”

“Destiny's positional field just changed.”

Carter was already moving toward her instruments.

“Changed how?”

Rush answered from behind Young, his voice sharp with something close to alarm.

“It is tightening around the ship.”

Xander looked upward.

Destiny felt closer than the ceiling.
