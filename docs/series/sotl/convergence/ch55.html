Chapter 55 of 59

Not Eli

Rush reached Xander before Young did.

“Eli rebuilt that bus after the drone attack. The bypass is in your memory.”

Xander stared at the dead weapons diagram.

The observation deck shook under another impact. Scott rolled Destiny through the debris of a destroyed Al'kesh while the remaining ship fired across their stern. Shields fell another six percent.

“I don't see it.”

“Then look.”

Rush pulled the damaged network apart on the console. Ancient pathways spread into layers: original construction, automatic repairs, expedition modifications, and the temporary routes Park had created before the battle.

“The forward bus failed during the drone engagement. Eli found a connection through auxiliary distribution.”

Xander remembered the engagement.

He remembered alarms, Rush shouting, Chloe bleeding, and his own hands—Eli's hands—moving across a console while Destiny came apart around them.

The memory offered fear before it offered engineering.

“I can't find the sequence.”

“It is there.”

Rush seized his shoulder.

“You have it. Stop resisting and retrieve it.”

The memories opened too far.

Xander was back between galaxies. He was hungry. He was brilliant. He was failing to repair the last stasis pod. He was watching Earth appear where no Earth could be. He was seventeen and twenty-five and neither age left enough room to breathe.

Young removed Rush's hand.

“Back away.”

“We are taking weapons fire.”

“Back away.”

Rush obeyed one step.

“Eli knew how to restore this system.”

Young placed himself between them.

“I don't need Eli Wallace to fix this. I need Xander Harris to tell me what he can actually do.”

The words reached Xander through the memory.

Young turned to him.

“What do you see now?”

Xander forced his attention toward the current console.

“The primary bus is severed in three places.”

“Can it be repaired?”

“Not during the battle.”

“What can you do?”

Xander looked at the remaining paths rather than the missing memory.

“The aft weapons still have local capacitors. They can't receive targeting and power through the same network.”

Park answered from engineering. “We can feed power locally.”

“But not commands,” Brody said.

Carter's voice broke through from Cardea's Ha'tak. Staff fire sounded behind her.

“Separate them.”

Xander leaned toward the channel. “What?”

“Don't rebuild the bus. Treat power and firing control as different systems. Feed the capacitors from engineering and route low-energy targeting through a surviving data path.”

Rush examined the map. “The surviving path lacks weapons authentication.”

Xander found a line running through the navigational core.

“The mooring route has command authentication.”

“It was not designed for tactical data.”

“Neither was I.”

The ship shook again.

Young said, “Risk.”

Xander answered before Rush.

“Targeting data could corrupt the return coordinate. If we isolate it at both ends, it shouldn't reach the gate buffer.”

“Shouldn't?”

“Nobody has done this before.”

“Options?”

“Remain unarmed.”

Young looked at the flagship charging another volley.

“Proceed. Protect the coordinate first.”

The solution belonged to no one person.

Park created local feeds from engineering. Brody balanced the capacitors so charging one weapon would not starve the shields. Volker isolated the return coordinate. Carter provided a filter design through the stolen Goa'uld channel. Rush rewrote the authentication layer. Xander used the access the ship had granted him to pass targeting commands through navigation without telling navigation they were destinations.

Eli's memory supplied fragments.

Xander used what fit and discarded what did not.

“Aft bank one responding,” Park called.

One white indicator appeared on the tactical display.

Then a second.

Two failed.

A third came online at half power.

“Three banks available,” Rush said. “Limited firing rate.”

It was not Destiny's full weapons network.

It was something the current crew had built from the ship they actually possessed.

Young faced Scott.

“Bring us around.”

Xander remained at navigation.

He was not Eli Wallace.

The weapons fired anyway.
