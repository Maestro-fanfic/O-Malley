Chapter 43 of 59

The Consent Question

Saroosh asked Xander to describe Eli's memories without using Eli's name.

They sat in a quiet briefing room at Cheyenne Mountain. Xander had returned from Destiny for the first time since the breakaway. Young attended through a secure screen; TJ sat beside him aboard the ship. Buffy occupied the chair nearest Xander without pretending she was not there to intervene if necessary.

“I don't understand the point,” Xander said.

“Try.”

He chose an easy memory.

“There's a kitchen. Morning light. His mom—”

Saroosh raised one finger.

“A woman is making breakfast,” Xander corrected. “She keeps pretending she isn't watching the clock because somebody stayed up too late. There's a cereal box on the table and a game paused in the other room.”

“Whose memory is it now?”

“Mine.”

“Whose life created it?”

“His.”

“Do those answers contradict each other?”

Xander looked at the table.

“They feel like they should.”

“Feeling is not obligation.”

Saroosh touched her own chest.

“Selmak remembers hosts who lived before me. I can enter those memories. I can love people I never met because I know how Selmak loved them. But the love does not give those people a claim upon my choices, nor does it make me the person who first felt it.”

Her eyes flashed.

Selmak spoke. “And Saroosh's memories have changed me. Partnership is not preservation beneath glass.”

Saroosh returned.

“You do not have a partner who can answer. That makes your burden different.”

“I can't ask what he wanted.”

“He told you some of it.”

Xander remembered the Kino recording.

This isn't his fault.

Don't make him prove he's me.

“He wanted the crew protected,” Xander said. “He wanted Young in command. He didn't want me blamed.”

“Then those are wishes you may honor.”

“And everything else?”

“Information. Grief. Inheritance. None is an order.”

Xander's fingers tightened around Eli's old badge.

“What if it feels like betraying him?”

“Then ask whether the betrayal is real or whether you have imagined a dead man who demands your life because carrying his memories makes refusal painful.”

Young moved on the screen.

Saroosh saw it.

“You disagree, Colonel?”

“No.”

He looked at Xander.

“I've done that.”

Xander waited.

“Every time I asked what Eli knew before I asked what you could do. Every time you used his mannerisms and I treated them like he was looking back at me.” Young drew a breath. “I told Rush not to make you prove you were Eli. I haven't always followed the order myself.”

“You missed him.”

“That's an explanation. Not permission.”

Buffy glanced between them.

“People keep confusing those.”

Giles's absence from the room was present in the way she said it.

Saroosh nodded. “Consent is simplest between equals who can both walk away. Most important decisions are not that simple. A commander and subordinate. A Watcher and child. A symbiote and host. An institution and the person it calls necessary.”

Her eyes flashed.

Selmak continued. “The greater the difference in power, the less sufficient a single yes becomes.”

Young considered every order he had given a stranded crew.

Buffy considered every duty she had been told was destiny.

Xander considered a god who had opened a door and refused to choose who walked through it.

“So what makes it consent?” Buffy asked.

Saroosh answered in her own voice.

“Knowledge. Alternatives. The ability to refuse. The right to change what can still be changed. And people willing to notice when freedom exists only on paper.”

Nobody tried to reduce the answer to a rule that solved everything.

Young addressed Xander.

“Your liaison agreement stands. You can change it. You can stop.”

“I don't want to stop.”

“I know.”

This time, neither of them mistook willingness for ownership.

The conversation ended when Carter entered with an intercepted message.

The false Janus knew Xander's name.
