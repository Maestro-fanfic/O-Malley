Chapter 40 of 59

Selmak

Hammond did not open the iris.

“Identify the Tok'ra.”

Teal'c stood beside him in the control room. The Stargate's event horizon cast blue light across the gold mark on his forehead.

“They are said to be Goa'uld who rebelled against the System Lords. The name means ‘against Ra.’ Apophis teaches that they are deceivers and assassins.”

O'Neill glanced at him. “And the non-Apophis version?”

“Until this moment, I did not know with certainty that they existed.”

The radio message repeated.

Hammond opened a transmission to the other side.

“This is Major General George Hammond, United States Air Force. Your warning has been received. Before I authorize entry, you will explain the nature of the person requesting it.”

The answer came in the same elderly voice.

“I am Saroosh. Selmak is a symbiote of the Tok'ra. We have shared one body for one hundred and ninety-three of your years.”

O'Neill looked at Daniel.

Daniel mouthed, Hebridan?

It was a question for another day.

“How many travelers?” Hammond asked.

“One body. Two persons.”

“Weapons?”

“None.”

“You will submit to medical examination and security screening. You will remain in designated areas. Any attempt to interfere with this facility or its personnel will terminate the meeting.”

The other voice answered this time, resonant and layered.

“Agreed.”

Then Saroosh returned.

“Selmak enjoys making agreements sound more dramatic than they are.”

O'Neill's eyebrows rose.

Hammond waited while Carter confirmed that the Tok'ra had transmitted a data sample matching information recovered from the scout. The relay coordinates were real. The encryption keys opened a section of the captured archive Earth had not yet accessed.

“Open the iris,” he ordered.

Saroosh stepped through the Stargate alone.

She was small, silver-haired, and walked with a polished wooden cane. No armor concealed her body. No hand device covered her palm. She stopped at the base of the ramp while six weapons remained trained on her and looked at each person holding one.

“A sensible welcome,” she said.

The wormhole closed behind her.

✳

Doctor Fraiser found the symbiote within minutes.

It curled around Saroosh's spinal column exactly where Teal'c said it would. The scan showed integration throughout her nervous system and changes to organs, blood chemistry, and immune response that could not be reversed casually.

“Does the examination hurt either of you?” Fraiser asked.

“No,” Saroosh said.

Her eyes flashed.

“Though Selmak objects to the temperature of your instruments.”

“Selmak can file a complaint.”

The eyes flashed again.

“Selmak asks where.”

Fraiser almost smiled.

Medical clearance did not establish trust. It established that Saroosh was what she claimed to be and that no visible weapon had entered with her.

The meeting took place in the briefing room.

Hammond sat at the head of the table. SG-1 occupied one side. Saroosh sat opposite them with an empty chair on either side, a deliberate reminder that one body did not mean one negotiator.

Young, Wray, and Xander attended from Destiny. Giles, Basil, and Buffy had come from Sunnydale aboard the shuttle. Buffy had refused to let the first friendly snake meeting happen without someone present whose job included recognizing monsters.

Saroosh found that explanation reasonable.

Selmak explained the Tok'ra.

They were the same species as the Goa'uld but rejected domination, worship, and involuntary hosts. They shared memory and control with willing partners. They fought the System Lords through infiltration, sabotage, intelligence, and an ability to abandon bases before superior forces arrived.

Buffy listened until Selmak finished.

Then she addressed Saroosh.

“Did you consent?”

Selmak's eyes flashed.

Buffy raised one hand. “I'm asking her.”

The glow faded.

Saroosh smiled without amusement.

“Yes.”

“Once? A hundred and ninety-three years ago?”

“Every day since.”

Selmak remained silent within her.

Saroosh continued.

“I was forty-seven when we blended. I had an illness my physicians could not treat. Selmak required a host after the death of the person who came before me. We spoke for six days before I agreed.”

“Could you change your mind?”

“Separation now would probably kill us both. That limits the meaning of withdrawal. We cannot pretend otherwise.”

Buffy looked at Teal'c. He inclined his head once.

“But I can refuse Selmak control of our body,” Saroosh said. “I can refuse a mission. I can speak privately when privacy is possible. We disagree. Sometimes loudly.”

Her eyes flashed.

Selmak spoke. “Saroosh understates the volume.”

Saroosh returned. “Selmak mistakes resonance for authority.”

Xander laughed before he could stop himself.

Saroosh looked toward his screen.

“You carry another person's memories.”

It was not a question.

“Yes.”

“But not the person.”

“No.”

“Then our situations are not the same.”

“No.”

“They may still rhyme.”

Xander considered that.

Buffy asked the question beneath all the others.

“If Selmak wanted to use your body and you said no, what happens?”

“We do not do it.”

“Even if the Tok'ra ordered it?”

Saroosh's expression sharpened.

“The Tok'ra do not own either of us.”

Buffy sat back.

It was not proof. It was the first answer that could become trust.

Teal'c regarded Saroosh for a long moment.

“No Goa'uld would permit the host to contradict them before strangers.”

“No Goa'uld would consider the host a person,” Selmak answered.

Saroosh's eyes ceased glowing.

“And no honest Tok'ra should ask you to accept that distinction without evidence.”

Hammond opened the intelligence package.

“Then let's begin with evidence.”
