Chapter 35 of 59

Hunters on the Ground

The Jaffa arrived in an abandoned cannery two miles east of Sunnydale High.

There were twenty-four of them.

They came through white light in three disciplined ranks, weapons ready before the transport rings withdrew into the cracked floor. Their commander wore black armor edged in gold. The mark on his forehead showed two faces divided by a key.

He examined a device on his wrist.

One light pointed toward the school.

Another pointed toward the sky.

“The vessel has departed,” his lieutenant said.

“It will return,” the commander replied. “All doors return to Janus.”

He divided the force into four units.

One would locate the threshold beneath the school. One would capture the Tau'ri youth whose signal corresponded with the Ancient vessel. One would establish an extraction point. The last would eliminate anyone capable of warning Earth's warriors.

They moved before the SGC found the building.

✳

Buffy saw the first Jaffa from a rooftop.

He did not move like a vampire.

Vampires prowled when they wanted to be seen and hurried when they did not. This man moved in short advances between cover while a second watched the street behind him. A third crossed only after the first two signaled.

They wore armor. They carried staff weapons. They checked windows, rooftops, alleys, and reflections.

“They're military,” Buffy said into the radio.

O'Neill answered from the school command post. “That's the rumor.”

“No, I mean actually military. Not matching-outfit military. They cover each other.”

“Can you fall back without being seen?”

Buffy watched the lead Jaffa stop.

His helmet turned toward the building across from hers, not toward her. Something had drawn his attention. A curtain moved in an upstairs window.

He raised one closed fist. The entire patrol froze.

“Already doing it.”

Buffy left the roof in the direction they had cleared, crossed two buildings, and descended behind the SGC's inner surveillance line.

Teal'c waited there with four Air Force personnel.

“How do they fight?” Buffy asked.

“With discipline. The staff weapon has greater range than your crossbow and may kill with one strike. Their armor will resist blades and small-caliber ammunition.”

“Weak points?”

Teal'c indicated the joints at his shoulder, throat, and beneath the arm.

“They expect opponents to fear them.”

“I can disappoint them.”

“Indeed.”

O'Neill joined them through a service passage. He had brought the street map and a secure handset linked to Destiny's remaining Kinos.

“We have four teams,” he said. “One moving toward the school, one searching houses along Xander's route, one holding near the cannery, one we haven't located.”

Buffy's expression changed.

“They know who Xander is?”

“The transport may have intercepted our ship-to-ground communications.”

“He's not on the ground.”

“They don't know that.”

“Good.”

O'Neill recognized the tone.

“No using yourself as bait without telling the rest of the class.”

“I wasn't going to use me.”

She looked toward the high school.

“They think Xander will be where the weird alien signal touches the ground. We let them keep thinking it.”

The plan formed quickly.

Willow recorded Xander's voice from the secure channel. Carter attached it to a low-power transmitter calibrated to resemble the leakage the Goa'uld had detected. The signal moved west through a sequence of relays, drawing the search team away from residential streets and toward an industrial yard the SGC had already cleared.

Buffy chose the terrain.

Teal'c chose the fields of fire.

O'Neill positioned the teams.

For eleven minutes, it worked.

Then the Jaffa stopped outside the yard.

Their commander had not come himself. The patrol leader studied the transmitter through a monocular device and sent two warriors around each side rather than entering.

“They know,” Buffy whispered.

“They suspect,” O'Neill answered. “Hold.”

The Jaffa advanced in pairs.

One found the first Air Force position without exposing himself. Staff fire struck the concrete barrier and exploded it inward. The defenders returned fire. Bullets hit armor and turned one Jaffa without dropping him.

The others changed formation immediately.

“Fall back to line two,” Buffy ordered.

Nobody argued.

The SGC personnel withdrew through covered routes prepared in advance. The Jaffa pushed into the yard, believing they had broken the ambush.

Buffy came down from the loading crane behind them.

She landed on the patrol leader's shoulders, drove him to one knee, and pulled his staff weapon across his throat before he could turn it. Teal'c struck the second warrior at the armor joint. O'Neill's team hit the flanks with zat fire.

The Jaffa did not panic.

Two pivoted outward to protect the others. One fired at Buffy while his comrade remained in the line of fire. She released the patrol leader and rolled beneath the blast. It cut through the crane support behind her.

Steel screamed overhead.

“Move!” she shouted.

Both sides scattered as the crane collapsed across the yard.

The ambush became six separate fights.

Buffy could not use the rhythm she used against vampires. Jaffa protected openings, recovered weapons, and attacked to create opportunities for one another. She stopped trying to beat them one at a time.

“Teal'c, left!”

He understood before she finished.

Teal'c drove two warriors away from the wall. Buffy kicked loose a suspended chain, swung across their line, and forced them toward the SGC's second zat team. Blue energy struck both.

O'Neill disabled the patrol leader.

The remaining Jaffa withdrew with their wounded rather than abandon them.

Buffy let them go.

O'Neill looked at her.

“They're leading us to the others,” she said.

Teal'c watched the retreat.

“They may also be leading us into an ambush.”

“Probably both.”

They took the patrol leader alive.

He woke bound in a secured room beneath the school. Teal'c stood before him with his forehead uncovered.

The prisoner's contempt was immediate.

“Shol'va.”

“I am Teal'c of Chulak. I served Apophis and learned that he was no god.”

“Then you fled one false lord and trespassed in the domain of Janus.”

“Your master bears a stolen name.”

The prisoner's eyes narrowed.

Teal'c told him what had stood inside the circle beneath the school: a being who demanded no kneeling, accepted no service, and claimed no mortal choice as his own.

“A deception,” the Jaffa said.

“Perhaps. Yet your master hides within the flesh of another and sends you to die while he waits beyond the stars.”

The Jaffa pulled against the restraints.

“Janus commands every passage.”

“Then why did Destiny pass beyond his sight?”

The prisoner had no answer.

Teal'c did not demand one.

He left the question with him and returned to the fight.

Above the school, an invisible Goa'uld transport shifted position.

The commander received the patrol's defeat and changed his objective.

If the boy could not be found, the doorway beneath the school would be taken or destroyed.
