Chapter 20 of 59

The Exception

The Council had indexed Slayers by date, region, Watcher, and manner of death.

It had not indexed them by success.

Basil noticed the absence on the second day.

The archive contained detailed measures of obedience: instructions followed, unauthorized alliances, deviations from patrol schedules, emotional attachments, disputes with Watchers, and suspected threats to secrecy. It tracked how efficiently a Slayer maintained her assigned territory. It recorded every time she questioned the Council.

It did not consistently record the people she saved.

“Build a second index,” Basil told the archival team.

“By what criteria?”

“Threats defeated. Civilian casualties prevented. Years survived. Apocalyptic events averted. Requests for assistance and the Council's response.”

The senior archivist looked at the shelves. “That will take months.”

“Begin with the active Slayer.”

They reconstructed Buffy Summers from sources the Council had kept separate.

Giles's official reports described a gifted but undisciplined Slayer whose emotional attachments repeatedly complicated field operations. The Operations Committee recorded her friendship with Willow Rosenberg and Alexander Harris as security vulnerabilities. Angel was classified as evidence of compromised judgment. Her mother's ignorance was considered useful for maintaining secrecy.

The incident files told another story.

Buffy had been called at fifteen with little preparatory training. After the destruction of her first Watcher and expulsion from school, she relocated to Sunnydale. The Council deployed Giles and provided no additional standing personnel.

Sunnydale sat upon an active Hellmouth.

The last confirmed permanent deployment of a Slayer to such a site had occurred centuries earlier. That Slayer survived eleven months.

Buffy survived the Harvest.

She disrupted demonic predation at the local zoo, stopped a technologically manifested entity from spreading through global computer networks, prevented repeated vampire attacks, and confronted the Master after prophecy declared her death unavoidable.

She died.

The Council record closed the event there and opened a succession file.

A separate report noted that Xander Harris had refused Giles's instruction to remain behind, forced Angel to guide him to the Master's lair, and performed cardiopulmonary resuscitation after finding Buffy drowned.

Buffy returned.

She killed the Master, closed the Hellmouth, and resumed active service.

The Council classified her continued attachment to Xander as an operational concern.

Basil read that sentence three times.

Willow Rosenberg appeared throughout the files as an unauthorized civilian researcher. Her work supplied translations, computer access, and historical connections the Council had not provided. Giles's most successful decisions repeatedly involved sharing information the Council preferred confined to Watchers.

Buffy's friends did not merely comfort her between battles.

They were the reason she survived them.

The analysts compared Buffy's first two years with every sufficiently complete Slayer record in the archive. They adjusted for length of service, threat frequency, population density, and verified events.

The result remained the same.

Buffy Summers was the most successful Slayer in the surviving record.

The Council had described every cause of that success as a defect.

✳

Basil delivered the analysis to the Queen directly.

She read the report at her desk while he waited.

The first list of names lay beside it. Four hundred and twelve Slayers had been identified with sufficient certainty to begin individual files. Hundreds more remained partial entries: a given name, a region, a year, a death described by a Watcher who had never thought the girl would require a surname in history.

The Queen had begun marking each completed file after reading it.

She reached Buffy's operational comparison.

“Miss Summers received less formal preparation than many of her predecessors.”

“Yes, Ma'am.”

“She was the first active Slayer assigned permanently to a Hellmouth in centuries.”

“Yes.”

“What additional support did the Council provide?”

“One Watcher.”

“Only Mr. Giles?”

“Only Mr. Giles.”

“And he was expected to support the operation from a salary barely sufficient for his own maintenance.”

“Correct.”

“Yet she survived a prophesied death and became their most successful Slayer.”

“By every measurable standard available to us.”

The Queen turned to the Council's behavioral assessment.

“They regard her as a failure of discipline.”

“The Council regards her friendships, independent decisions, and emotional attachments as departures from doctrine.”

“Miss Summers is not evidence that the Council's system failed, then.”

“No, Ma'am. The records indicate that the system is functioning substantially as designed.”

“Then in what respect is she exceptional?”

“She survived it.”

The Queen looked again at the comparative results.

“Despite refusing their methods?”

“No, Ma'am. The evidence suggests she is successful because she refused them.”

She closed the file.

“Then it is not Miss Summers who requires correction, Sir Basil.”

“No, Ma'am.”

“It is the Council.”

She placed Buffy's file beside the list of names rather than the charter.

“Has she been told about the Cruciamentum?”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

“Mr. Giles, in the presence of the American team.”

“And his prior knowledge?”

“He admitted it.”

“Did he intend to administer the drugs?”

“He says he had avoided deciding.”

The Queen considered that.

“An honest answer.”

“Not a comforting one.”

“Those are rarely the same.”

She opened the charter.

“Effective immediately, the Council will exercise no authority over Miss Summers, the second active Slayer, or any Potential in its custody. Training and research may continue only under external supervision and with the consent of those receiving it.”

“The government will require a formal instrument.”

“Prepare it with the Prime Minister.”

“Yes, Ma'am.”

“Then go to California.”

Basil looked up.

“Personally?”

The Queen's expression did not change.

“Must we revisit the meaning of direct?”

“No, Ma'am.”

“Miss Summers has received quite enough institutional concern delivered through other people.”
