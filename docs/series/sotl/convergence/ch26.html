Chapter 26 of 59

A Mooring, Not a Chain

“Sever it.”

Rush placed both hands on the console and enlarged the field model.

“We know the restraint exists outside Destiny's systems. We know it recognizes the connection to Harris. Disrupt that recognition for the few seconds required to engage the engines, and the ship leaves.”

Giles looked at the display without understanding its mathematics. He understood the proposal perfectly.

“No.”

Rush's eyes narrowed. “You have a technical objection?”

“A magical one.”

“Naturally.”

“The passage joins a ship from one time to a person in another by way of an unstable dimensional breach. If you tear it apart, the favorable outcome is that the connection ends.”

“And the unfavorable outcome?” Young asked.

“Destiny attempts to return to the instant from which it came. Xander is drawn with it. Or each part of the ship returns to a slightly different instant. Or the Hellmouth supplies another destination.”

Brody stopped entering figures.

“How slightly different?”

Giles removed his glasses. “A fraction of a second would be sufficient to prevent matter from remaining matter.”

Rush made an irritated sound. “We have no evidence that any of those outcomes is possible.”

“We had no evidence that a Bronze Age invocation could relocate this vessel until it did.”

“Roman.” Daniel looked up from the Janus text. “The relevant invocation is Roman.”

Giles gave him a look.

“Sorry.”

Carter brought Destiny's system map beside the magical diagram.

“We don't need to sever the anchor.”

She highlighted three systems: propulsion, navigation, and the Stargate chamber.

“Right now the field treats Sunnydale as Destiny's only permissible physical location. Every attempt to move is interpreted as an incomplete arrival and corrected. What if we change what the location means?”

Volker glanced between the diagrams. “Coordinates usually mean where something is.”

“Gate coordinates don't,” Carter said. “They identify a destination whether you're standing there or not.”

Rush's expression changed.

It was not agreement yet. It was the recognition of an idea worth attempting to own.

“Transfer the positional fixation to the Stargate.”

“And the navigational core. The gate gives the connection a destination structure. The core gives Destiny a way to retain that structure while moving.”

Park enlarged the ancient data pathways around the gate. “Those systems were never designed to share location data this way.”

“Neither was Xander,” Buffy said.

“The ship already stores gate positions as part of its route,” Carter continued. “We create a protected entry for Sunnydale, but we don't define it as current location. We define it as a point of return.”

Daniel placed two fingers on the stones drawn beside the ancient doorway.

“A milestone.”

Giles shook his head slowly.

“A mooring.”

“Moorings still hold ships in place,” Young said.

“Only while the ship is tied to them. Their purpose is also to give a vessel somewhere to return.”

Xander stood near the window with TJ. He could feel the idea moving through the room before he understood all of it.

Destiny would no longer be chained above him.

It would know where home was.

“Would it let the ship come back to 1997?” he asked.

Carter did not offer certainty she did not possess.

“It may preserve the path. The gate can't normally connect across time, and Destiny's faster-than-light drive doesn't travel through time. But this isn't a normal address. If the transferred anchor retains the temporal component, the ship should remain in this era and retain Sunnydale as the point where it entered.”

“Should?” Buffy repeated.

“There isn't an existing field of study for this.”

“There will be if Willow gets her way,” Xander said.

On the ground channel, Willow failed to look embarrassed.

“I've already started a binder.”

Young looked at Giles. “What does the magical side require?”

“The change has to occur at both ends of the passage. Here, where the vessel crossed. And at the Hellmouth, where the crossing entered this world.”

“Simultaneously?”

“As nearly as we can achieve.”

“What about Xander?” TJ asked.

Giles turned to him.

“He must release the correspondence.”

“Meaning?”

“The spell says that Eli Wallace is aboard Destiny and in Sunnydale. Xander must assert his own identity while the ship accepts Sunnydale as a destination rather than his position.”

Rush folded his arms. “And if adolescent self-affirmation is insufficient to rewrite a temporal field?”

“Then perhaps an Ancient navigational command supplied by the person Destiny recognizes will assist,” Giles said.

Buffy looked from one man to the other.

“You two are going to be adorable after the third apocalypse.”

Young addressed Xander.

“You understand that you don't owe the ship this.”

Xander looked at Earth. Then at the status display listing the sleeping crew.

“I know.”

“Rush doesn't get to decide for you. Neither do I.”

“I know.”

“Take time.”

Xander thought of Eli alone on the observation deck, expecting to die so everyone else might wake. He separated the memory carefully from his own decision.

Eli would have tried.

That was not a reason.

Young had woken because Xander chose to act. Hammond had answered because Xander and Young chose to call. Buffy was alive below them, not because prophecy had permitted it, but because people had refused to let her face death alone.

That was.

“I'll do it,” Xander said. “Under the rules from the last test. I can stop. TJ can stop it. And nobody decides that getting a useful result means I belong to the project afterward.”

Hammond answered from Cheyenne Mountain.

“Agreed.”

Young nodded once. “Agreed.”

Rush waited until everyone looked at him.

“Agreed.”

“Wow,” Xander said. “I think that one hurt him.”

“Profoundly,” Rush replied.

It was the first joke Xander had heard him make.

They began planning the transfer.
