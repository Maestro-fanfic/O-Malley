Chapter 44 of 59

A Leak on Earth

The transmission had not come from the captured scout.

Selmak proved that first.

Its encryption belonged to an older relay family. Its routing markers predated the scout's current mission by nine years. The message had entered the network through a dormant receiver that the Tok'ra believed was somewhere on Earth.

Daniel translated the recovered fragments.

ALEXANDER HARRIS. HUMAN MALE. SUNNYDALE.

VESSEL RESPONDS TO SUBJECT.

THRESHOLD BENEATH SCHOOL.

RAYNE INVOCATION—

The final line ended in damaged code.

Buffy read the screen twice.

“Somebody here told him.”

“Somebody here has been watching,” O'Neill said.

Hammond locked down every facility connected to Destiny, the Council investigation, and Sunnydale. Communications logs were preserved. Personnel with access to the named information were located and interviewed.

The obvious suspects led nowhere.

The captured Jaffa had not transmitted. The Council leaders remained separated. Ethan was still under guard in London and had spent most of the previous day attempting to negotiate better coffee.

Basil found the first real discrepancy in the Council records.

Dr. Anne Mercer had worked as an epigrapher for the Watchers Council for eleven years. Her specialties included Roman mystery cults, nonhuman scripts, and artifacts recovered from sites with unexplained astronomical alignments.

She had not been present during the London raid.

Her supervisor believed she was in Rome.

The Rome office believed she had been recalled to London.

Immigration records showed that she entered the United States through Los Angeles two days after Halloween using a diplomatic-support credential issued by an office that did not exist.

The hotel listed on her entry form had never heard of her.

Giles recognized the photograph.

“She attended the Council symposium in Prague four years ago.”

“Did you speak to her?” O'Neill asked.

“Briefly. She asked about the Master and the oldest prophecies concerning the Sunnydale Hellmouth.”

Buffy looked at him. “Four years ago.”

“Before you were called, yes.”

Daniel opened Mercer's personnel history.

Her work changed abruptly in 1990. Before that year, she published careful linguistic studies and repeatedly argued that field artifacts should be shared with universities. Afterward, her reports became secretive, politically skilled, and fixated on devices the Council classified as celestial rather than demonic.

“She was taken,” Buffy said.

Teal'c studied the two handwriting samples.

“The Goa'uld often retain the host's knowledge and habits while altering their purpose.”

“How did one get here?” Willow asked.

Selmak examined the old observer logs from the scout.

“A vessel entered your system in 1989 and remained for fourteen months. Its departure record lists one missing symbiote as a loss during a survey accident.”

“Not lost,” O'Neill said.

“Placed,” Selmak agreed.

Mercer had been excavating a Roman site in southern Italy in 1990—the same network of shrines connected to the communication chamber found in 1798. She disappeared for three days after a tunnel collapse. Her colleagues found her walking toward camp with no serious injuries.

Anne Mercer had returned.

Something had returned inside her.

The Goa'uld spent seven years building access. It entered the Council's archives, learned its security, and quietly redirected reports concerning gates, Ancient technology, and stellar visitors. When Destiny appeared, it used the Council's confusion and the British investigation to collect the rest.

“How did it get Xander's name?” Buffy asked.

Basil opened the raid-access log.

“Dr. Mercer remotely accessed the emergency index twelve minutes after the first team entered headquarters. That index contained the Sunnydale field file and Mr. Rayne's name.”

“And the ship link?” Carter asked.

O'Neill displayed surveillance photographs from Sunnydale.

Mercer appeared in three of them.

In one, she watched the school from a parked car while the ground-point equipment was unloaded. In another, she stood outside the emergency airfield after Destiny's shuttle landed. The third showed her reflection in a store window across from Xander's house.

“She followed us,” Willow said.

“She listened,” O'Neill answered. “We found a passive receiver in a utility box near the school. Good enough to collect names from unencrypted local radios before we locked everything down.”

Hammond looked at the message timestamp.

“Where is she now?”

The answer came from the secure Council line.

Mercer had contacted Basil's temporary office thirty minutes earlier. She offered to surrender a missing Janus archive in exchange for immunity from the criminal inquiry.

The archive was bait.

So was she.

“She knows we're looking,” O'Neill said.

Buffy looked at Mercer's photograph.

“Does Anne know?”

“Know what?”

“That the thing inside her is volunteering her body for a trap.”

Nobody could answer.

Buffy stood.

“Then we find out before anybody decides she's acceptable collateral.”
