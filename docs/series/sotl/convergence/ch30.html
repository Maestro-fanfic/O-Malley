Chapter 30 of 59

Return

Destiny emerged from the Sun with power to spare.

For the first time since its arrival, the ship possessed enough reserve to operate its sensors at full range. Rush wanted six uninterrupted hours to examine the restored systems. Young gave him two.

Then he ordered the return to Earth.

“The coordinate may not hold,” Rush warned.

“We knew that before we left.”

“The solar recharge may have disrupted the temporal component.”

“Has it?”

Volker answered before Rush could. “No measurable change.”

Rush glared at him.

Volker concentrated on his station.

Young looked at Xander. “Ready?”

Xander checked the navigation path.

The transformed anchor did not behave like an ordinary location. It contained the position of Earth, the rotation of the planet, the site of the Hellmouth, and a temporal value Destiny's computers displayed as an unresolved constant.

1997 waited inside it.

“Ready.”

Destiny engaged its faster-than-light drive.

Space opened around the ship.

The Sun vanished. Stars became a geometry Xander remembered without having traveled it. The return coordinate remained steady—not pulling, not commanding, simply available.

Volker watched the temporal readings.

“No drift.”

Carter's voice came from the open SGC channel. “We still have you.”

The signal should not have survived the drive.

Nobody commented until Destiny dropped into normal space.

Earth appeared ahead.

The date remained unchanged.

Cheyenne Mountain received the ship's authentication. Sunnydale received the first sunlight of the same morning on which it had watched Destiny leave.

In the school library, Willow shouted loudly enough to overload her microphone.

Buffy took the radio from her.

“You said come back, not deafen them.”

“They came back!”

Xander leaned over his console. “We noticed.”

Young permitted the exchange for five seconds.

Then Destiny resumed station above Sunnydale.

This time the ship moved there under its own power.

Thrusters corrected its approach. Navigation acknowledged the target without replacing every other possible destination. The engines reduced to station-keeping output and remained quiet.

Nothing forced them to stay.

“Position stable,” Volker reported.

Rush examined the field readings.

“The external fixation is gone.”

“And the anchor?” Young asked.

“Contained in navigation and the gate buffer, as designed.”

Carter corrected him over the channel. “As hoped.”

Rush inclined his head by the smallest possible amount. “As hoped.”

Xander closed his eyes.

Destiny remained present in the back of his awareness. He knew where the ship was. He could feel the quiet readiness of the console beneath his hand and the path descending toward Sunnydale.

But Eli no longer pressed against him like a second person trying to occupy the same outline.

The memories had edges now.

His own did too.

“How do you feel?” TJ asked.

“Like me.”

She waited.

“A me who knows way too much about air recyclers.”

“I'll accept that provisionally.”

Young opened the shipwide command display.

Power reserves stood above ninety percent. Automatic repairs were active. Entire branches of the sensor network had returned.

For the first time, Destiny could see farther than Earth.

An alert appeared.

Park looked up from engineering. “Long-range contact.”

Young crossed to her station. “Where?”

“Outer system. Near the orbit of Jupiter.”

“Natural object?”

“No. Artificial drive signature. Small vessel.”

Rush brought the restored sensor image onto the main display.

The craft was withdrawing behind Jupiter, using the planet to conceal its emissions. It had been angled toward Earth before Destiny emerged from the Sun.

Carter received the telemetry at Cheyenne Mountain. O'Neill and Teal'c stood behind her.

Teal'c leaned toward the image.

His face became still.

“I recognize this vessel.”

Hammond turned to him. “Goa'uld?”

“It bears the device of a Goa'uld who names himself Janus.”

For several seconds, no one answered.

Daniel looked toward the secure channel from Sunnydale.

“The Janus?”

“No,” Teal'c said. “A parasite who has claimed his name.”

Young looked out at Earth.

Home had not merely seen them return.

Something else had been watching.
