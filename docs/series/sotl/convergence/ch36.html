Chapter 36 of 59

Capture

Destiny left faster-than-light flight in Jupiter's shadow.

The planet filled the observation glass: bands of cream and rust, storms large enough to swallow Earth, and darkness where the Sun could not reach. The Goa'uld scout hung against that darkness with its transmitter extended.

It did not react for two seconds.

Scott used both.

He drove Destiny between the scout and its departure vector while Volker fed constant corrections into navigation. The Ancient ship was vastly larger, but size did not make it agile. Its damaged frame resisted the turn. Structural warnings opened across Greer's board.

“Hold together,” Greer told the ship.

Park heard him over the engineering channel. “Is that a technical instruction?”

“It is now.”

The scout cut its transmission and raised shields.

Packet four escaped.

The reconstructed fragment appeared on Xander's console.

ANCIENT VESSEL DEFENDS THE TAU'RI—

The rest had not transmitted.

“Communications array retracting,” Xander reported.

“Can it send again with the array closed?” Young asked.

“Lower bandwidth. Different emitters.”

“Scott, keep our hull between that ship and its beam path.”

“Yes, sir.”

Young opened a Goa'uld channel.

Teal'c's recorded translation carried his demand.

“Unidentified vessel, power down weapons and propulsion. You are surrounded. Surrender and your crew will be treated as prisoners.”

The response came from a man whose eyes shone white.

“There is no path beyond Janus.”

The scout fired.

Plasma struck Destiny's forward shield. Power surged through systems still repairing themselves. The observation deck flashed red. A relay exploded three levels below engineering.

Greer's damage teams moved before the alarms finished sounding.

“Forward shield holding at eighty-one percent,” Park called.

Rush opened the weapons grid. Half the available emitters reported ready. Three failed their own diagnostics when charged.

“Return fire,” Young ordered. “Target propulsion.”

Destiny's first volley crossed the distance in white lines.

The scout rolled. Most shots passed beneath it. One struck the aft shield and spread gold light across the pyramid-shaped hull.

“Their shields are asymmetric,” Xander said.

Rush looked at his display. “All shields are asymmetric under maneuver.”

“Not like this. The communications array draws from the same capacitors as the port-aft section. Every time they try to transmit, that shield drops first.”

Young asked, “Certainty?”

“Sensor analysis, not a guess.”

“Mark it for Scott and weapons.”

Xander sent the information. He did not select a target.

Scott altered course to keep pressure on the weak side.

The scout accelerated toward Jupiter.

“They're entering the atmosphere,” Volker said.

“They cannot survive at depth,” Rush replied.

“They don't have to. The radiation and magnetic field will blind us before the pressure kills them.”

Young saw the choice. Break off and allow escape, or follow a smaller vessel into an environment that could tear Destiny's weakened hull apart.

“Scott, cut them off. Do not follow.”

Scott drove Destiny across the scout's descending path.

The Goa'uld vessel fired continuously. Plasma walked across Destiny's shields and struck the hull where one emitter failed. Deck plating ruptured along an unoccupied section. Atmosphere vented before emergency barriers sealed it.

Greer's voice remained steady.

“Breach contained. Team six rerouting shield power.”

“Casualties?” Young asked.

“Two injuries. Both moving.”

Destiny completed its turn.

The scout found an Ancient warship between itself and Jupiter.

Its communications array began charging again.

The port-aft shield weakened.

“Fire,” Young said.

Destiny struck the exposed quarter.

The first shot collapsed the shield. The second severed the scout's main drive. The third hit the weapons emitter before it could discharge.

The Goa'uld ship began to tumble.

“Cease fire.”

Rush looked toward Young. “They may still possess secondary weapons.”

“Then we respond if they use them.”

Young reopened the channel.

“Your vessel is disabled. Surrender.”

The Goa'uld commander appeared through static.

“Every captive returns through the final gate.”

Rush's display changed.

“Energy buildup in the central compartment.”

Teal'c answered from Earth. “Self-destruction.”

“Time?” Young asked.

“Unknown,” Rush said. “Possibly less than a minute.”

The scout's crew preferred death to capture. The wreck contained the records Earth needed and people the Goa'uld commanding it was willing to kill.

Young faced Xander.

“Can Destiny interrupt the charge?”

Xander searched the sensor map. “Not directly. But the buildup is using their hyperdrive capacitors.”

“Options.”

“Drain them or make them discharge.”

Park understood. “Induce a false hyperspace initiation.”

Rush was already moving through the control sequence. “Their drive is damaged. If the field forms unevenly—”

“The ship comes apart,” Scott said.

“Can we stabilize it?” Young asked.

Carter answered from the SGC. “Destiny may be able to shape the field with its own FTL emitters. But you'll have seconds.”

Young made the decision.

“Do it.”

Xander gave Park the Goa'uld drive pattern preserved in Eli's SGC training files. Park translated it into an energy profile. Rush configured Destiny's emitters. Scott matched the scout's tumble while Volker held both vessels inside a shared navigation solution.

The scout's capacitors reached detonation threshold.

Destiny pulsed its FTL field.

The Goa'uld hyperdrive answered.

For an instant, the scout existed half inside a passage it could not complete. Energy poured out of the capacitors and into empty space. Its damaged drive tried to tear the hull in two.

Destiny's field held it together.

Then both drives shut down.

The self-destruct reading vanished.

No one on the observation deck celebrated.

Young waited for the next threat.

None came.

“Status?”

Park checked the scout. “No propulsion. No weapons. Life support active. Six life signs.”

“Prepare a boarding team.”

Greer answered immediately. “Ready in five.”

The shuttle carried Greer, James, and eight security personnel across the gap. Scott guided it against the scout's damaged docking collar while Destiny covered both vessels.

The Jaffa aboard resisted.

The fight lasted three minutes.

Two defenders were stunned. Three surrendered after their commander fell. The Goa'uld attempted to escape through a maintenance shaft and found Greer waiting at the other end.

He emerged in restraints, his host's eyes dark and terrified whenever the symbiote released control.

The scout's archive remained intact.

So did its crew.

Packet four did not.

Somewhere beyond the Solar System, a relay had received enough to warn the false Janus that an Ancient vessel protected Earth.

It had not received the vessel's condition.

It had not received the location of the Sunnydale anchor.

It had not received Xander's identity.

Young intended to keep it that way.
