Chapter 42 of 59

Information From Tomorrow

Saroosh recognized the silence around Jacob Carter's name.

It began when an aide entered during a break in the technical discussions.

“Captain Carter, General Jacob Carter left a message asking you to return his call.”

“My father,” Carter explained to Saroosh. “He thinks I work in deep-space radar telemetry.”

The statement was ordinary. The reaction was not.

On Destiny's screen, Young looked at Xander.

Xander looked away.

TJ became very interested in a medical display.

Carter noticed all three.

“What?” she asked.

No one answered.

Her eyes narrowed. “You know something about my family.”

Hammond ended the technical session.

“Captain, conference room. Colonel Young, Mister Harris, Lieutenant Johansen, remain on the channel. Doctor Rush, you're dismissed.”

Rush did not move.

“That was not a request,” Young said.

Rush left.

Saroosh remained because the question concerned her and Selmak. O'Neill, Daniel, and Teal'c remained as SG-1. Wray joined from Destiny at Young's request.

Carter stood rather than sit.

“Is my father involved with the Stargate program in their time?”

Xander knew the answer.

Jacob Carter, dying of cancer. Selmak, losing Saroosh. The blending that saved them both. Years of missions, arguments, reconciliations, and a death neither one faced alone.

He also knew that telling Samantha Carter could turn every future conversation with her father into preparation for a destiny he had never chosen.

“Nothing we know requires action today,” Xander said.

“That's not an answer.”

“It's the answer I can give without using somebody else's future against them.”

Carter looked at Hammond.

“Sir?”

Hammond did not enjoy refusing her.

“Mister Harris is correct.”

“You know what it is?”

“I know only that the expedition recognizes a possible future connection involving your family and Selmak. I have not requested details.”

Saroosh rested both hands on her cane.

“Selmak and I are involved?”

Young answered. “In the history we remember, yes.”

“Does this history concern my death?”

The question entered the room without fear.

TJ looked at her.

“Yes.”

Saroosh absorbed the answer.

Selmak did not take control.

“Is it imminent?” Saroosh asked.

“Not according to our memory,” TJ said. “But our arrival has already changed events. We can't promise timing or outcome.”

“Then I do not wish to hear more.”

Carter turned toward her.

“You don't?”

“I have had nearly two centuries more than I expected. I will not spend what remains rehearsing a death that may no longer occur.”

She looked at Xander.

“Nor will I ask Selmak to approach a future host as though that person were an appointment.”

Selmak's eyes flashed.

“Agreed.”

Carter sat down.

The decision did not satisfy her curiosity. It gave that curiosity a boundary she could respect.

Hammond used the moment to address the problem they had postponed since Destiny arrived.

The expedition came from approximately twenty-one years in Earth's future. Its members remembered wars, attacks, disasters, political decisions, technologies, births, deaths, marriages, illnesses, and private choices that had not happened.

That knowledge could save lives.

It could also destroy them.

“From this point forward,” Hammond said, “future information will be handled under four principles.”

Wray began recording.

“First: information necessary to prevent imminent loss of life or strategic harm will be disclosed to the authority capable of acting on it.”

Young nodded.

“Second: personal futures are private. No one will be told whom they marry, when they die, what illnesses they develop, or what choices they make unless withholding the information presents a clear and serious danger.”

Carter accepted the rule even while sitting inside its first application.

“Third: remembered history is intelligence, not fact. It will be independently verified wherever possible. Our presence has already changed the timeline.”

Daniel added, “And the farther events lie from the point of divergence, the less reliable they become.”

“Fourth: foreknowledge will not be used to coerce. No one is to be recruited, threatened, medically altered, detained, or manipulated because of something a future version of that person may do.”

Xander looked at his own hands.

“That includes younger versions of the crew,” he said.

“Especially them,” Hammond replied.

Wray added a fifth operational requirement.

No member of Destiny's crew would be treated as a database to be interrogated. Disclosure would be structured, voluntary where immediate safety permitted, and supported for the people being asked to revisit traumatic events.

Hammond approved it.

“What about large events that aren't imminent yet?” O'Neill asked. “Attacks. Wars. Disasters.”

“They will be reviewed by a compartmented group with authority to verify and prepare without public disclosure. We will not wait until the day before a mass-casualty event. We will also not assume that every remembered detail remains inevitable.”

“Who appoints the group?” Basil asked.

“The President, with allied participation where the information concerns allied territory.”

Basil nodded. “I shall advise the Prime Minister and Her Majesty.”

Saroosh looked around the table.

“You possess the temptation every ruler claims to want.”

“Knowing the future?” Daniel asked.

“Believing you do.”

Hammond closed the file.

“That's why these are rules, not privileges.”

Carter looked at Selmak.

Somewhere in a history that might never exist, the symbiote knew her father.

She did not ask how.

Selmak did not tell her.
