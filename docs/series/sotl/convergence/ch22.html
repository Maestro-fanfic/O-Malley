The Optimized World

Destiny's first true exploration run, not a rescue, not a crisis, just a genuine choice, made deliberately, to go somewhere entirely new because everyone aboard had finally properly earned the right to actually want that for once, took the whole gathered, unlikely family of it out past the edge of anywhere Earth's own charts had ever reached before.

Xander stood on the bridge with Young at his shoulder, Rush at a console pretending, without much success, not to be as excited as he clearly was underneath the composed exterior, and behind them, filling out a crew roster that had never once been part of any official mission plan: Buffy, Willow, Teal'c, Sam, Daniel, half a dozen of Destiny's own newly-woken crew members getting their first real look at the wider galaxy, and, trailing behind everyone else with an expression of open, unguarded wonder on his face, Jack O'Neill, who had come along, he insisted repeatedly, purely for the pie situation on other planets, which absolutely nobody on the ship believed for a single second.

His first proper look at Willow, weeks back now, was still a story people brought up occasionally, usually to make her laugh — the pointing, the so it's not just the one of you, Willow needing a full minute to recover from laughing before she could properly introduce herself. He'd never brought it up again after that day, same as with Buffy, which everyone aboard had come to understand was simply the going rate of admission: one loud, undignified moment of pure disbelief, paid in full, up front, and never mentioned again.

"Where are we headed?" Buffy asked, peering at the star chart with the particular curiosity that had, lately, started to feel less like Buffy's own idle interest and more like something layered underneath it: an old, patient hunger for the next unknown thing, never quite satisfied, never fully at rest, never entirely home wherever it happened to be standing.

"Somewhere quiet," Xander said, grinning. "For once. I checked. Twice, actually, because I didn't believe the first result."

The planet they dropped out of FTL above was, from orbit, breathtakingly ordinary: green and blue and clouded, a world that could easily have passed for Earth from a comfortable distance if not for the two separate moons hanging low over one hemisphere. It was only once the Jumper touched down, once the away team actually stepped out into open air that smelled like rain and cut grass and something floral nobody aboard had a proper name for, that the wrongness started, slowly and quietly, to show itself.

For the first hour, though, nobody was looking for wrongness at all.

"Okay," Jack said, standing in the middle of an actual, honest-to-God meadow, arms spread wide, turning a slow circle like a man determined to properly commit the moment to memory. "This is exactly the kind of alien world I signed up for. Sky's blue. Grass is green. Nobody's shooting at anybody. I could get used to this."

"You say that on literally every planet that isn't actively trying to kill us," Sam said, not looking up from calibrating her scanner.

"Because it's true on literally every planet that isn't actively trying to kill us. Consistency, Carter." He crouched down, plucked a wildflower, examined it with the theatrical seriousness of a man reviewing fine wine. "No pie situation yet, but I remain hopeful."

Buffy laughed, actually laughed, the sound surprising even herself, the specific, easy laugh of someone briefly, entirely unburdened, standing in a field with people she trusted, on a world that hadn't yet asked anything of her at all. Willow spun in a slow circle nearby, arms out, the light behind her eyes dimmed low and soft, just breathing it in, and for one whole hour, before anyone noticed the flowers were all exactly the same height, it was simply what it looked like on the surface: a good day, on a beautiful world, with good company.

"They're all exactly the same species," Sam said, eventually, crouching to examine a cluster of the nearest flowers, running a small handheld scanner over them with the same careful, patient attention she gave absolutely everything she didn't yet fully trust. "All the same height, down to the centimeter. Daniel, come look at this, please."

Daniel crouched beside her, frowning hard at the readout. "That's not a meadow," he said, slowly, working it through out loud. "That's a pattern, deliberately maintained."

They found considerably more of it as the day wore on... a forest whose individual trees were spaced with genuine mathematical precision between each one, a river whose banks curved in shapes far too regular to be the product of ordinary erosion, wildlife moving in patterns too efficient to be purely instinctual, every single predator taking exactly the amount of prey the local ecosystem could sustainably provide and not one single creature more than that, generation after generation.

"It's optimized," Rush said, that evening, back aboard Destiny, running the day's accumulated data through every analysis method he could throw at it. "Every system on this entire planet is running at maximum theoretical efficiency. No waste anywhere. No excess. No — and this is the part that genuinely concerns me most — no randomness anywhere in the data. Natural systems always have noise built into them, inherently. Chaos, in the mathematical sense. This planet simply doesn't have any at all."

"Somebody's tending it," Teal'c said, quiet, certain.

"Or something is," Sam said, and the whole room went very still around that word, hanging in the air.

They didn't find whatever was actually doing the tending, that first trip, not with the time they had available, not with the scans they managed to run before Young, cautious as ever, called it and pulled the whole team back aboard for the night rather than risk pushing further into the unknown. But they left something behind on that world before they departed: a single small sensor drone, tucked carefully into the treeline, quietly logging, watching, in case the pattern ever showed its hand more clearly.

"So," Jack said, back on the bridge later that night, arms crossed, looking out at a planet that had gone, over the course of a single day, from breathtaking to unsettling. "First trip out, and we found something creepy. Really setting a solid tone here for the rest of this whole exploring-the-universe thing."

"We don't know it's hostile," Xander said, though something in his own borrowed memory (a fragment, faint, unplaced, from a life he hadn't fully lived yet, from a future that hadn't quite happened) had gone cold and watchful at the shape of what they'd just discovered.

"No," Rush agreed, quiet, staring at the sensor readout a beat longer than the conversation strictly required. "We don't know that yet. Not for certain."

Nobody said the actual word that was, faintly, starting to occur to more than one person standing in that room. It wasn't time yet, not quite. But the seed had been planted, quiet and precise, the same way everything on that world had apparently been planted, tended, and grown.

They stayed in orbit one final morning before moving on, most of the crew scattered across the ship doing their own quiet work, and Buffy found herself standing at the observation window beside Willow, both of them looking down at the impossibly green world below.

"You feel it too, don't you," Buffy said, not quite a question.

"Yeah." Willow wrapped her arms around herself, though the deck wasn't cold. "Nada says it's — they say it reminds them of something. Won't say what, exactly. Just goes quiet whenever I think about it too hard."

"Artemis does the same thing." Buffy watched the slow curve of cloud drift across the meadow far below. "Whatever this is, I don't think it's finished with us yet. I think this was just — an introduction. A first look."

"That's a comforting thought," Willow said, dry.

"I didn't say it was comforting. I said I think it's true." Buffy glanced sideways at her friend, something steady passing between them without either one needing to say it out loud. "We'll figure it out. Whatever it turns out to be."

"Yeah," Willow agreed, and meant it, even without being sure yet what she was agreeing to. "We will."

Below them, the optimized world turned on, patient and perfect and silent, keeping whatever secret it was keeping for a little while longer.
