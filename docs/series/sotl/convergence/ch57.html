Chapter 57 of 59

The Turn

SG-1 detonated the hyperdrive charges.

Three control paths failed at once.

Cardea's Ha'tak dropped out of its maneuver with no route into hyperspace. Carter triggered the device attached to shield control. The damaged shield attempted to regenerate around Destiny's last strike and collapsed instead.

“Cardea is exposed,” Volker reported.

Rush brought the vulnerable vessel onto the tactical display.

“One concentrated volley will disable its power core.”

“SG-1 remains aboard,” Young said.

“Their mission is complete.”

“They remain aboard.”

Rush looked toward the flagship preparing another firing solution on Earth.

“If we divide our remaining output—”

“Cardea's vessel is not a target unless it fires. Focus on the flagship.”

Young opened a channel to the Ha'tak.

“SG-1, status.”

O'Neill answered between zat discharges. “Hyperdrive down. Shields down. Working on the charming woman in charge.”

✳

Cardea retreated toward the ring room.

Her First Prime and the last command guards formed a moving barrier around her. Teal'c pursued through one passage. O'Neill came through another. Carter sealed the route to the bridge. Daniel used the internal address system to order the Jaffa to surrender in Cardea's own command dialect.

Most ignored him.

Three did not.

They lowered their weapons after seeing the shields fail and receiving confirmation that the ground force had been defeated. Cardea killed the first herself.

The host fought her for the hand device before she could kill the other two.

Her arm locked halfway upward.

Carter's Tok'ra scanner showed the struggle as two minds issuing opposite commands to the same muscles.

“Now!” she shouted.

O'Neill fired the zat.

Cardea fell.

Teal'c disarmed the First Prime. Carter placed the Tok'ra restraint at Cardea's neck before the Goa'uld recovered. For one second, the host opened her eyes without light behind them.

“Neris,” she whispered.

Then Cardea returned and screamed through her.

O'Neill checked the restraint.

“Neris comes with us.”

Teal'c addressed the surviving Jaffa.

“Your god lies bound. Choose.”

The First Prime looked at Cardea, at the dead warrior she had killed, and at the unarmed host's hand still resisting the restraint.

He set down his staff.

The others followed.

Cardea's Ha'tak was captured.

✳

Janus saw his underlord's shields collapse.

He abandoned his firing line on Earth and turned the flagship toward her vessel.

Young had been waiting for that turn.

“Scott, port thirty. Give me the exposed quarter.”

Destiny moved between the flagship and Cardea's Ha'tak. Janus accelerated, unwilling to destroy the ship he intended to recover. His forward shields remained damaged. His flank opened as he tried to pass the Ancient vessel without firing through it.

“Weapons,” Young said.

Three improvised banks fired.

The first volley broke the weakened shield. The second destroyed two propulsion nodes. The third struck the flagship's main power distribution and darkened half the pyramid.

Janus's ship began to drift.

“Flagship weapons offline,” Volker reported.

“Propulsion damaged.”

“Life signs stable.”

Young opened a channel.

“Janus, power down remaining systems and surrender your vessel.”

The Goa'uld appeared before the dead Stargate in his throne room. Smoke moved across the image. Jaffa gathered behind him, waiting for a god to make defeat impossible.

Janus held the Ancient crystal key in both hands.

“You believe the threshold is yours because a child and a witch traced lines beneath a school.”

In Sunnydale, Willow heard him through the command channel.

Giles stood beside the restored circle.

“Do not answer,” he said.

Janus pressed the crystal into a device taken from his temporal archive.

“I bore this name before your nations raised their first walls. By it, every hinge knows its master. By it, every road returns to me.”

Energy left the flagship.

It crossed space through the same kind of correspondence the scout had detected. It struck Destiny's return coordinate and traveled downward toward Sunnydale.

The mooring did not obey.

It shook.

The false Janus mistook resistance for recognition.

“Open,” he commanded.

Every functioning door aboard his flagship opened.

Not by miracle.

His device sent the command through systems tied into stolen Ancient control pathways. Pressure doors retracted. Secure compartments exposed themselves. Jaffa stared as their master's invocation behaved like a machine receiving the wrong instruction.

Then one doorway opened onto a road that was not inside the ship.

A man stepped through.

The young face looked toward the Jaffa.

The old face looked at the Goa'uld.

An iron key hung at his throat. His hands were empty.

Janus stared at Janus.

Across the open transmission, Destiny, the SGC, the Tok'ra, and the defenders beneath Sunnydale High saw them meet.

“You are a projection,” the Goa'uld said.

“No.”

“An Ancient construct.”

“No.”

The Goa'uld raised the crystal.

“I am Janus.”

The god regarded him without anger.

“You are a traveler who stole the name written above a door.”

“The name is mine by worship, conquest, and time.”

“A name is not a key.”

The Jaffa listened.

The god did not command them to kneel or rise. He did not touch the ship, the crystal, or the false Janus. Destiny's weapons did not strengthen. The mooring did not stabilize because he stood there.

“You claim passage,” the god said. “You permit one road and close all others. That is not a doorway. It is a cage.”

The Goa'uld's host trembled.

For an instant, the human face beneath the scar tried to turn away.

“This body is mine,” the false Janus said.

“He did not enter it by choice.”

“These warriors are mine.”

“They stand where every road but obedience was closed.”

“Destiny is mine.”

“It has refused you.”

The god stated facts.

He changed none of them.

One Jaffa lowered his weapon.

The warrior beside him did not.

Both choices remained theirs.

The false Janus looked at his damaged ship, his captured underlord, and the planet that had denied him.

“If the door will not open,” he said, “nothing will remain beyond it.”

He tore the crystal from the temporal device and drove it into the main weapons control.

Power rerouted from life support, shields, and damaged engines into one surviving planetary weapon.

The real Janus watched.

He did not stop him.

Young saw the charge building.

“Target?”

Volker answered. “Earth. North American continent.”

Young transmitted one final demand.

“Power down the weapon. Evacuate your crew. This is your last warning.”

The false Janus smiled.

“There is no road beyond me.”

The real Janus turned toward the open doorway.

“Every ending says that.”

He stepped through and was gone.

The flagship fired.

Scott placed Destiny in the path.

The planetary strike hit the Ancient ship's remaining shields and broke them.

Energy tore across the hull. Consoles exploded. The observation glass filled with white fire but held.

The weapon began charging again.

“We cannot absorb a second strike,” Rush said.

Young looked at the flagship.

Its shields were gone. Its surviving crew still had time to abandon it. None of the escape craft launched.

The false Janus had closed that road too.

“Fire.”

Destiny's three remaining banks discharged together.

The shots entered the damaged power core.

For a moment, the false Janus stood before the dead Stargate and watched a door of white light open through his ship.

Then the flagship came apart.

Destiny shielded Earth from the first wave of debris.

When the light faded, one Ha'tak remained in orbit.

It belonged to the people who had chosen to surrender.
