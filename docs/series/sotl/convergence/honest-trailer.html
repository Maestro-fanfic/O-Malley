Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen opened on Halloween in Sunnydale, where buying a costume has historically been less a retail decision than an uncontrolled metaphysical weapons program.

“In a world where Ethan Rayne’s costumes once turned children into whatever they wore, three teenagers will learn the only thing worse than becoming the costume.”

Destiny appeared in Earth orbit.

“Becoming the expanded-universe continuity attached to it.”

This is Ship of the Line: Convergence.

Watch Xander Harris merge with Eli Wallace, inherit nine years of memories from a future that has not happened yet, and wake aboard an Ancient starship full of sleeping people whose younger present-day selves are alive on Earth.

It is survivor’s guilt, temporal ethics, and the world’s worst reunion logistics, all carried on one tablet by a teenager whose prior spaceflight experience was owning science-fiction merchandise.

Watch Buffy merge with Artemis from No Man’s Sky and Michael Allen from Ack’s self-insert stories, permanently becoming a sealed, starlit Traveller with three internal perspectives and no visible human face.

Watch Willow merge with Nada and become a full Korvax chassis, proving that the traditional Sunnydale Halloween question “what are you supposed to be?” can have an answer requiring several classified appendices.

The Sunnydale blind spot politely explains away both transformations until somebody says the truth out loud.

Outside Sunnydale, there is no blind spot.

Cue Jack O’Neill pointing at Buffy and delivering the official first-contact response:

“Nope.”

Meet Destiny, an Ancient vessel whose autonomous mission would normally continue into deep space until Xander uses nine years of borrowed foreknowledge to find the control chair and park it over Earth on day one.

“A bold adaptation choice,” the narrator said. “What if we solved the entire first season’s navigation mystery before lunch and used the remaining twenty-six chapters for emotional consequences?”

Contact the SGC through a Jumper’s built-in gate system. Explain one-way wormholes. Arrange a separate ship rendezvous because Earth cannot come back through the same connection. Hold meetings about the ninety sleeping future crew members and the ninety younger people on Earth who have not yet made the choices that created them.

Young decides to use the future crew’s knowledge to prevent the Icarus disaster entirely, handing every present-day counterpart the strangest gift imaginable: a future they no longer have to become.

Meanwhile, introduce Destiny to SG-1, Sunnydale, the White House, President Bartlet, Leo McGarry, Toby, Josh, CJ, and Mrs. Landingham, because this crossover looked at a starship, a Hellmouth, two alien franchises, and a presidential drama and decided what it really needed was a larger briefing room.

Watch Toby repeat “that’s not in the file.” Watch Josh speak before Leo can stop him. Watch Hammond maintain composure so hard it becomes a visible special effect. Watch Teal’c understand every joke and refuse to rescue anyone from it.

Then meet the Atlas, a machine intelligence that responds instantly in terse system text because even cosmic gods know nobody reads a long help message during an anomaly.

`NEW ITERATION DETECTED.`

Willow visits the Anomaly. Polo stays where Polo belongs. The station’s shadow becomes a door. Artemis’s grief collides with Mike’s central conviction that people matter even when they are trying to erase themselves from their own story.

The narrator slowed over Buffy’s blank visor.

“The transformation is permanent. There is no glamour to remove, no reset waiting after the credits. The story does not solve that loss. It gives Buffy people who look directly at what she became and stay. We’re not making the helmet the joke.”

Face Replicators. Save two hundred people. Confront one unfinished being whose continued existence creates a risk Xander can never fully calculate.

He makes the call alone.

He saves the station. He allows the small life to continue. He guarantees he will never know whether a worse future would have followed from the choice he did not make.

Jack arrives afterward, too late to help with the decision and exactly on time to sit beside him once the decision has become something he must carry.

“That,” the narrator said, quieter, “is the whole price. Not knowing. And somebody showing up anyway.”

## The Convergence Drinking Game

Take a drink whenever a new franchise enters the room and immediately needs the previous three explained.

Take two when the explanation creates a fourth problem.

Drink whenever somebody sees Buffy or Willow without the blind spot and loses a professionally significant amount of composure.

Take a sip every time Xander’s tablet reports something no teenager should be responsible for.

Drink whenever Teal’c is clearly in on the joke and refuses to admit it.

Do not drink every time Mike has previously been inserted into another story. Even the narrator lost count briefly.

## Starring

Xander Harris and Eli Wallace as Two Lives, One Tablet, Zero Age-Appropriate Responsibilities.

Buffy, Artemis, and Mike as Three’s Company: Sealed Exosuit Edition.

Willow and Nada as Robot Redhead, Minus the Redhead.

Destiny as The Ancient RV That Refuses to Ask for Directions.

Jack O’Neill as Nope, Okay, That’s an Alien.

George Hammond as Maintaining Composure at DEFCON Korvax.

President Bartlet as The West Wing of the Milky Way.

Toby as That’s Not in the File: The Miniseries.

Teal’c as Indeed, I Understood the Reference.

The Atlas as Cosmic Tech Support in All Caps.

And the Replicators as Bugs in the System, Extremely Literally.

Alternate titles appeared:

Everybody Is Somebody Else Now.

The Ship of Theseus Has a Stargate.

Crisis on Infinite Briefing Rooms.

The narrator paused on the last one.

“That one has range.”

The true title appeared.

# CONVERGENCE

Directed by: five franchises in a trench coat.

Written by: the question “what if the crossover kept going?” asked until the answer reached orbit.

Continuity managed by: a tablet, a project brief, and someone muttering about one-way wormholes.

Special thanks to: Jack, for arriving after the choice and understanding that after still matters.

The screen ended in four rooms: Xander and Jack on Destiny, the Scoobies in Giles’s apartment, the SGC around an exhausted report, and Bartlet reading what the decision cost.

“Two hundred people lived,” the narrator said. “One unfinished life kept its chosen name. One eighteen-year-old will never know whether he chose the better future.”

A beat.

“That’s not a clean victory.”

Jack sat down beside Xander.

“It is a real one.”
