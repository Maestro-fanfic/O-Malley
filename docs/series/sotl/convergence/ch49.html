Chapter 49 of 59

General Quarters

Young woke forty-three people.

Not the entire expedition.

Not everyone who would want the chance to see Earth or defend it.

He selected the people Destiny required to fight and survive damage: weapons technicians, pilots, engineers, medics, security personnel, damage-control specialists, and enough support staff to sustain continuous operations. TJ reviewed every pod. Wray reviewed every civilian assignment. Hammond approved the list before the first revival began.

Young stood beside each pod when it opened.

The explanations became practiced without becoming easy.

You are aboard Destiny.

The year is 1997.

Earth is below us.

An enemy fleet will arrive within two days.

Military personnel received orders after medical clearance. Civilians received requests and the right to refuse roles beyond immediate emergency obligations. Three did. Young reassigned their work and did not ask why.

The revived crew saw Earth in groups small enough for TJ's staff to watch them.

Some cried.

One laughed until laughter became something else.

An engineer asked whether his wife was alive below. Young answered yes and repeated the contact restriction. The man returned to duty without saying another word.

There would be time to reckon with that if they survived.

Destiny became a functioning warship by increments.

Scott established flight watches. James doubled security around the stasis bays and the transformed anchor systems. Greer placed damage-control teams in every accessible section between engineering and weapons control. Park and Brody restored redundant power routes. Volker built tactical navigation solutions around Earth, the Moon, and the safe limits of Destiny's faster-than-light drive.

Rush opened weapon systems the expedition had never possessed enough power to test.

Some answered.

Some reported damage older than the human species.

None promised victory.

Xander chose the station beside navigation where the ship recognized his name. His civilian badge remained visible above Eli's old identification tag, which he carried inside his sweatshirt rather than around his neck.

“You can still go to the ground,” Young told him.

“I know.”

“The false report makes this the first place they'll look.”

“It also makes this the place where I can tell when they try the things we told them would work.”

Young nodded. “Your role remains sensors and systems. Not weapons.”

“Agreed.”

Buffy chose Sunnydale.

There had never been another answer for her.

✳

The United States went to war without admitting a war existed.

NORAD tracked the approach vector using data it could not reveal. Air-defense units moved under exercise orders. Strategic commands increased readiness while being told only that an unidentified aerospace threat might enter the system. Hospitals near Sunnydale activated disaster plans for a chemical incident. The town's schools closed over a fabricated structural hazard.

The President notified congressional leaders through the narrowest legal channel available. He ordered continuity measures, protected command sites, and instructed Hammond to preserve the Stargate program's secrecy only while secrecy did not endanger the population it was meant to protect.

No conventional aircraft could challenge a Ha'tak in orbit.

They could evacuate, track, rescue, communicate, and defend the ground.

Hammond intended to use everything Earth could actually do instead of pretending it could do more.

Basil carried the warning across the Atlantic.

The Prime Minister received the military assessment first. The Queen received Basil immediately afterward.

“Direct means direct, Sir Basil,” she reminded him before he could begin.

“Yes, Ma'am.”

He reported the two capital ships, the likely ground assault, and Destiny's uncertain condition. He explained the deception and the roles Buffy and Xander had accepted.

“Miss Summers remains in Sunnydale?”

“By her choice.”

“And the adults responsible for supporting that choice?”

“Present in force.”

The Queen held his gaze.

“That was not a request for reassurance.”

“No, Ma'am. Colonel O'Neill shares ground command. General Hammond has deployed medical, evacuation, communications, and conventional defense. Mr. Giles and Miss Rosenberg will maintain the mooring. The Slayer will not stand alone.”

“See that she does not.”

The British government placed its own forces on alert, opened intelligence channels to Hammond, and prepared to respond if the attack widened beyond North America. The public saw exercises, transport delays, and unusually serious weather advisories.

Above them, Destiny continued to look wounded.

Power dimmed along its outer hull. Restored weapons remained cold. Thrusters made irregular corrections designed to resemble failing station-keeping. The ship that had emerged from a star presented itself as barely capable of remaining above one town.

Inside, every available person took a station.

Young opened the command circuit.

“This is Colonel Young. General quarters.”

Alarms sounded across the ship.

“An enemy force is approaching Earth. Our mission is to prevent that force from attacking the planet, seizing Destiny, or reaching the crew in stasis. We will operate under General Hammond's authority and the defense plan already distributed.”

He looked through the observation glass.

“Earth is home. Not the home we left and not the year we expected. It is still home.”

He did not promise everyone would survive.

“All sections report readiness.”

The answers began.
