Chapter 8 of 59

Boarding Party

The shuttle landed at Vandenberg before dawn.

Its approach had been hidden beneath a temporary closure of military airspace, three conflicting radar notices, and a weather report promising coastal fog that never arrived. The craft descended without wings, rotors, or any visible concern for the aerodynamic opinions of Earth.

It settled inside a cleared hangar on four broad landing assemblies.

O'Neill watched from behind a line painted on the concrete.

“You know,” he said, “I've always felt spaceships should make more noise.”

The shuttle's hull ticked softly as it cooled.

Carter did not look away. “It crossed thirty-five thousand kilometers in less than twenty minutes and stopped inside a building.”

“Exactly. It should brag.”

The hatch opened.

No pilot emerged.

A voice came from a speaker beside the door. Young's voice.

“SG-1, the shuttle is under automated control. Destiny will guide it back once you're aboard.”

O'Neill looked into the empty cabin. “Reassuring.”

“Colonel?” Hammond said over his radio.

“Boarding now, sir.”

The team carried weapons, environmental gear, medical supplies, recording equipment, and two duffel bags of clothing selected for approximate sizes. Carter had added enough portable instruments to make her pack an argument against gravity.

Teal'c entered first. He examined the walls and controls, then inclined his head.

“The vessel is Ancient.”

Carter followed. “You're sure?”

“Its construction resembles no Goa'uld craft I have encountered.”

“That's not quite the same thing.”

“No.” Teal'c placed one hand near a control without touching it. “It is older.”

Daniel stepped aboard and forgot to move out of the doorway.

Ancient characters ran in narrow bands around the interior. They were functional labels rather than decoration, worn smooth in places by hands dead for geological ages.

O'Neill nudged him forward with the equipment case.

“Gawk in motion.”

The hatch sealed.

There was no countdown. The floor pressed gently upward, the hangar disappeared from the forward display, and California contracted beneath them.

Carter sat beside the nearest interface. “May I?”

Young answered through the speaker. “Observe only until you reach the ship.”

“Understood.”

O'Neill fastened his harness. “He does sound like a colonel.”

“He is one,” Carter said.

“In the future.”

“The rank doesn't become imaginary because the date changed.”

O'Neill considered that. “Ask me again when I meet my younger commanding officer.”

Earth curved beneath the shuttle. Morning advanced over the continent. Daniel watched the atmosphere thin from blue to black.

“Destiny is directly above Sunnydale,” he said.

“That's the claim,” O'Neill replied.

“No, I mean directly. The shuttle's ascent path hasn't adjusted eastward for orbital motion.”

Carter looked at the navigation display without touching it. “The ship is maintaining a fixed geographic position. At this latitude, that's impossible without continuous station keeping.”

“Ancient spaceship,” O'Neill said. “Impossible may be a factory setting.”

“The engines aren't responsible.”

“Less reassuring.”

Destiny appeared against the stars.

At first it looked like a dark interruption in the sky. Then the shuttle altered angle and sunlight caught the hull.

The ship went on and on.

Age covered it more completely than damage. Its surfaces had been repaired, struck, scorched, and repaired again across a journey too long for human scale. Narrow lines of light marked inhabited sections. Other regions remained dark.

Carter forgot the instruments in her lap.

Daniel whispered something in Ancient.

Teal'c bowed his head.

O'Neill watched the three of them, then returned his attention to the ship.

“That's going to be difficult to put in a report.”

The shuttle entered an opening in Destiny's hull. Doors closed behind it. Gravity shifted by a fraction and settled.

Young waited outside the hatch in an ill-fitting black shirt and trousers. Xander stood several steps behind him. TJ remained close enough to reach either of them.

O'Neill descended first.

He and Young evaluated each other with the efficiency of men accustomed to determining whether a stranger would follow orders when frightened.

O'Neill saluted.

Young returned it.

“Colonel.”

“Colonel.” O'Neill glanced at the empty bay. “General Hammond sends his regards and several pounds of paperwork.”

“That may be the first thing about this that feels like home.”

The corner of O'Neill's mouth moved.

Young addressed the team. “Welcome aboard Destiny. Until Lieutenant Johansen completes clearance, you remain together and touch nothing without authorization.”

Carter nodded. Daniel looked as though authorization had become the principal obstacle between him and oxygen.

Teal'c looked at Xander.

“You are Alexander Harris.”

“Mostly.”

TJ's expression sharpened.

“Entirely,” Xander corrected. “With complicated footnotes.”

O'Neill handed him one of the duffel bags. “Clothes. General Hammond thought you might want something not selected by an extradimensional costume shop.”

Xander took it.

The ordinary canvas bag nearly made him cry.

“Thanks.”

“Your friends are waiting on the communications link.”

Xander's head came up.

“They're okay?”

“Giles, Summers, and Rosenberg. All alive. All extremely interested in explaining why I should regret delaying their call.”

“Yeah. Those are my people.”

Buffy answered before the first tone finished.

“Xander?”

The image was audio only. Hearing her was enough.

“Hey, Buff.”

She exhaled into the phone. Willow made a sound in the background that might have been a laugh or a sob.

“Where are you?” Buffy asked.

Xander looked through the observation glass.

“This answer is going to sound show-offy.”

“Try me.”

“About thirty-five thousand kilometers up.”

Silence.

“That is very show-offy.”

“I know.”

Willow came onto the line. “Are you on the ship?”

“You saw it?”

“I saw a corridor when you disappeared. I didn't see the whole ship.”

“Whole ship is... a lot.”

“Are you hurt?”

“No. A doctor checked. I'm me again.”

The qualification arrived too late to remove itself.

Buffy heard it. “Again?”

“The costume was a real person. Eli Wallace. I turned into him here.”

“Where is he now?” Willow asked.

“I don't know.”

Young stood near the far console, pretending to read a status display.

“I remember him,” Xander continued. “But he's not talking in my head or controlling me. It's just... crowded.”

Giles took the phone.

“Xander, listen carefully. Ethan invoked Janus. The spell caused everyone to become what their costumes represented.”

“We got that far.”

“Your disappearance was different. Willow saw an opening behind you.”

“A doorway,” Xander said.

The word changed the feel of the room.

“Possibly,” Giles answered. “Ethan claims the costume did not belong to his inventory.”

“He sold it to me.”

“Yes. He appears to regard those as separate matters.”

“Also very Ethan.”

“The spell is broken. Whatever continues is no longer being sustained by the original working.”

Xander looked at Earth.

“Destiny stayed.”

“So did you.” Buffy's voice returned. “Which is the part I'm focusing on.”

“Good part.”

“They're bringing you back, right?”

Xander looked toward Young and O'Neill.

“That's the plan.”

“When?”

O'Neill stepped closer to the microphone. “Once we know the shuttle trip won't turn him into a different person.”

“Is that Colonel O'Neill?” Buffy asked.

“Yes.”

“If Xander gets turned into anybody else, I'm holding you responsible.”

O'Neill looked at Xander. “I see why you gave us her name first.”

“Technically Giles was first.”

“I can still hear you,” Buffy said.

“See?”

Willow took the phone again. “Xander, I saw him.”

“Who?”

“Eli. When the door opened. He was on the other side.”

Xander closed his eyes.

“Did he look scared?”

“He looked surprised.”

That was kinder, whether or not it was true.

“I have to go,” Xander said. “They're doing space quarantine.”

“Of course they are,” Buffy replied. “Call again.”

“I will.”

“That's not a suggestion.”

“I know.”

After the line closed, Xander stood with his hand resting on the silent console.

Young stopped pretending to read.

“Your friends?”

“Yeah.”

“They believe you.”

“They've had practice.”

✳

Carter began with the air.

She sampled atmosphere, gravity, background radiation, and trace compounds while Daniel read the labels on sealed doors. Teal'c examined the construction and found no design convention he could confidently connect to Goa'uld vessels. O'Neill watched Young and TJ.

Young noticed.

“Something on your mind?”

“Several things. Most of them are classified.”

“Convenient.”

“Usually.” O'Neill nodded toward the corridor. “How many people?”

“More than we can support awake with current staffing and unknown ship status.”

“Military and civilian?”

“Both. International expedition under United States military command.”

“And they're all from our future.”

“Yes.”

“Including you.”

“We've established that.”

“I'm establishing how you think about it.”

Young regarded him for a moment. “I think they are my crew. I think General Hammond is the senior officer in the present chain of command. I think waking everyone before we know whether their younger selves are alive would be irresponsible.”

O'Neill nodded once. “Good answer.”

“It wasn't an audition.”

“Those are usually the best answers.”

TJ led them into the stasis bay.

The size of it ended the conversation.

Pod after pod waited beneath green lights. Carter counted the first row, looked at the curve of the room, and stopped trying.

Daniel approached the nearest pod without crossing the marked line beside it.

“These are all people from Earth?”

“Most of them,” Young said.

“Most?”

“Long story.”

O'Neill looked through the glass at a sleeping woman, then at Xander.

“I think we've got time.”
