Chapter 13 of 59

The Town Beneath

The shuttle landed six miles outside Sunnydale on a strip of cracked concrete the county still listed as an emergency airfield.

The field had not handled an aircraft in eleven years. Its windsock had become a nest. Grass grew through the taxiway in pale, stubborn lines.

Two Air Force vehicles waited beside a temporary communications van. The personnel wore no identifying unit patches and had been told only that SG-1 was arriving aboard an experimental craft.

They obeyed the order not to stare with varying success.

The shuttle settled without sound. Dust spread outward beneath it, flattening the grass. Its hatch opened.

Xander stood at the top of the ramp.

Home smelled like dry earth, gasoline, and eucalyptus. After Destiny's endlessly recycled air, the wastefulness of wind felt impossible. It touched every part of his face at once and continued past him as if air were something a planet could afford to lose.

He descended slowly.

O'Neill waited at the bottom rather than hurrying him. Carter remained close enough to catch him without advertising the fact. Daniel was already looking toward the hills. Teal'c watched the security personnel watching the ship.

Xander reached the ground.

Nothing happened.

He remained Xander. The shuttle remained a shuttle. Destiny did not pull him back into the sky.

“One small step,” he said.

O'Neill glanced at his shoes. “Looked medium-sized from here.”

The shuttle closed itself after them.

Young's voice came through Xander's new radio. “Destiny confirms no change in the field or Mister Harris's condition.”

“Copy,” O'Neill said. “We'll contact you from the secure site.”

“Maintain scheduled check-ins.”

“Yes, Colonel.”

Young hesitated for less than a second. “Xander.”

Xander touched the radio. “Still here.”

“Good.”

The connection ended.

One of the drivers opened the rear door of the first vehicle. Xander did not move.

Above them, the sky was empty blue.

He knew exactly where Destiny hung. The knowledge had nothing to do with sight. If he extended the line of his body upward, through atmosphere and sunlight and thirty-five thousand kilometers of distance, it ended at the observation deck.

Carter followed his gaze. “Can you feel the ship?”

“I know where it is.”

“Like direction?”

“Like remembering where my room is when I'm downstairs.”

She accepted the description without reaching for an instrument.

“Tell TJ if that changes.”

“And you.”

“And me.”

They drove into Sunnydale.

✳

Buffy waited outside the high school with Willow and Giles.

She had chosen the steps because they provided a clear view of the parking lot and because the library contained too many objects she might throw if the Air Force arrived without Xander.

The two dark vehicles entered together.

Four strangers got out of the first. Xander emerged from the second.

Buffy crossed the parking lot before his feet touched the pavement.

She stopped one pace from him.

Xander had expected a hug. Buffy had apparently expected one too. They looked at each other, suddenly uncertain whether the person who returned from space could be handled exactly like the person who had vanished from the street.

Xander opened his arms.

Buffy hit him hard enough to make Carter step forward.

“Ow,” Xander said into her hair.

Buffy let go immediately. “Are you hurt?”

“Not until the welcome-home rib damage.”

She hugged him again, more carefully.

Willow waited until Buffy released him, then wrapped both arms around his middle. Xander closed his eyes.

She smelled like shampoo, library dust, and home.

Giles stood behind them with his hands in his pockets.

“Hello, Xander.”

Xander stepped toward him.

Giles offered his hand.

Xander ignored it and hugged him too.

The Watcher went rigid, then placed one hand against the back of Xander's shoulder.

“Yes,” Giles said quietly. “Quite.”

O'Neill gave them time.

When Xander turned, he introduced everyone. The names sounded absurd in the school parking lot: Colonel Jack O'Neill, Captain Samantha Carter, Doctor Daniel Jackson, Teal'c. They belonged to Eli's history and yesterday's classified briefing. Now Buffy was shaking Carter's hand beneath a banner advertising the fall dance.

Giles examined their uniforms.

“You came armed.”

“We were told the school sits on a doorway to multiple hostile dimensions,” O'Neill said. “It seemed rude not to dress for the neighborhood.”

“The weapons remain secured unless required.”

“That's also the plan.”

Buffy looked at Teal'c. “And the forehead?”

“A mark imposed upon the First Prime of Apophis.”

“Is Apophis a vampire?”

“He is a false god.”

Buffy considered that. “We mostly get mayors and principals.”

“Buffy,” Giles warned.

O'Neill glanced at the school. “I'm beginning to see the overlap.”

✳

The library became the first joint command post because Giles had books, tables, and keys to the building.

Carter unpacked instruments beside the checkout desk. Daniel forgot the mission for thirty seconds when he saw the oldest shelves. Teal'c walked the perimeter once and selected a place from which he could see every entrance.

O'Neill looked at the weapons cage.

“You keep crossbows in a public school.”

“They are secured,” Giles said.

“With a padlock I could open using a disappointed look.”

“The cage was not installed to repel a military assault.”

“Neither was the front door.”

Buffy sat on the table beside Xander. She had not moved more than three feet from him since the parking lot.

“Show them the Hellmouth,” she said.

Giles's expression tightened. “The entrance itself is not readily accessible without damaging the library floor.”

“Also opening it,” Xander said. “We're against that.”

Carter adjusted a sensor. “I don't need physical access yet. I need a baseline above it and at the costume shop.”

“Ethan has gone,” Giles said.

O'Neill stopped examining the cage. “Gone where?”

“If I knew, he would be rather less gone.”

“You left him alone after he turned a town into monsters and relocated a teenager into orbit?”

Giles's face cooled. “I ended the spell. When I returned after searching for Xander, Ethan had fled.”

“Did you notify the police?”

Buffy and Willow looked at each other.

Giles removed his glasses. “Sunnydale's police are not particularly equipped to understand the relevant offense.”

“Kidnapping?”

“Ritual invocation of a Roman divinity resulting in mass ontological transformation.”

O'Neill paused. “Fair.”

Carter activated the sensor.

Every display filled at once.

She stepped back.

“That's your baseline?” O'Neill asked.

“No. That's the instrument exceeding its calibrated range.”

Willow moved closer, careful not to touch. “Radiation?”

“Not any kind this is designed to measure.” Carter changed modes. “Electromagnetic readings are elevated but not enough to produce this response. Gravity is normal. Temperature is normal.”

Daniel joined her. “What is it detecting?”

“A disagreement.”

“Between what?”

“I don't know.” She looked down at the library floor. “Possibly between where this room is and where something thinks it ought to be.”

Xander's awareness of Destiny tightened.

“The ship.”

Carter looked at him. “What changed?”

“Nothing. It just feels closer.”

Buffy stood.

“Closer how?”

“Not distance.” He pressed one hand against the table. “Like standing outside your house and knowing somebody inside just said your name.”

The radio at his belt activated.

“Xander,” Young said.

Everyone looked at it.

Xander answered. “I was about to call.”

“Destiny registered a field fluctuation when Captain Carter activated her equipment.”

“I'm feeling it.”

Young's voice sharpened. “Physical symptoms?”

“No. Just awareness.”

Carter stepped close enough to speak toward the radio. “Colonel, I'm shutting the instrument down.”

The display went dark.

The pressure in Xander's mind eased.

“Field has returned to prior levels,” Young reported.

O'Neill looked from Xander to the floor. “We are not poking the dimensional doorway again today.”

“Agreed,” Carter said.

“We still need data,” Giles replied.

“We need context first.” O'Neill looked at Buffy. “You said this town has other problems.”

“A few.”

“Show us.”

✳

They began patrol at sunset.

Giles objected to Xander joining them. TJ objected over the radio. Buffy objected in person.

Xander listened to all three and went anyway.

He carried no stake. His coordination remained his own, and several years of Eli's memories had not included improved hand-to-hand combat. O'Neill placed him in the center of the group beside Willow and Daniel.

Buffy led them through Restfield Cemetery.

The place looked theatrical in the last light: leaning stones, narrow paths, cypress shadows. O'Neill noticed the practical details instead. Too many graves had fresh soil. Too many headstones belonged to people under thirty. Several crypt doors bore scratches on the inside.

“How often?” he asked.

“Patrol?” Buffy said. “Most nights.”

“Contact?”

“Depends on the cemetery. Restfield's popular.”

“With?”

The earth moved beside Daniel.

A hand broke through the soil.

O'Neill drew his sidearm. Teal'c raised his staff weapon but did not open it. Carter pulled Daniel backward.

Buffy sighed.

“Tourism.”

The vampire came out of the grave in a black suit, face still human and dirt packed beneath its fingernails. It saw Buffy and lunged before it finished standing.

She stepped aside, caught its wrist, and threw it across the path.

O'Neill's weapon tracked the body.

“Don't shoot unless I say,” Buffy ordered.

He looked at her.

She was already moving.

The vampire struck hard enough to crack a stone marker. Buffy took the blow on one forearm, drove her heel into its knee, and used the collapse to force it against a crypt wall.

Its face changed.

The forehead thickened. Eyes turned yellow. Teeth lengthened into predatory points.

Carter's breath caught.

Daniel whispered, “That's not a mask.”

“No,” Willow said. “It's really not.”

The vampire twisted free and reached for Xander.

Buffy crossed the distance before O'Neill could fire. She drove a stake through its heart.

The body became dust.

The suit collapsed empty.

Silence returned to the cemetery.

Carter lowered her weapon slowly. “Mass-energy conservation just resigned.”

“It does that here,” Xander said.

O'Neill looked at the dust, then at Buffy.

Her sleeve had torn where she blocked the strike. Blood marked two shallow cuts along her arm.

She checked them once and put the stake back into her jacket.

“One vampire,” she said. “Young, dumb, fashion victim. We can keep going.”

“No,” O'Neill said.

Buffy turned.

“No?”

“We have confirmation.”

“You asked to see patrol.”

“I saw it.” He looked at the blood on her arm. “Who patches you up?”

“Depends how bad it is.”

“Doctor?”

“Doctors ask how you got bitten.”

“Medic?”

“Giles has a first-aid kit.”

O'Neill looked at Giles.

The Watcher understood before Buffy did.

“Colonel—”

“How old is she?”

“You know how old I am,” Buffy said.

O'Neill kept his eyes on Giles. “I want to hear him say it.”

Giles's voice was quiet. “Seventeen.”

“And how long has she been doing this?”

“Since I was fifteen,” Buffy answered.

O'Neill turned to her.

The anger in his face was not directed at her.

That unsettled Buffy more than suspicion would have.

“Patrol's over,” he said.

“You don't command me.”

“No. I don't.” He holstered his weapon. “But before I let this become another normal night, somebody is going to explain why a seventeen-year-old is bleeding in a cemetery while the adults responsible for her call it routine.”
