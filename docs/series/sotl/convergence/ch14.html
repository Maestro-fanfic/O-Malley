Chapter 14 of 59

The Field Report

Buffy refused to sit in the library's only comfortable chair.

That would have made the cut on her arm the center of the room, which was ridiculous. It was barely a cut. Giles had cleaned worse injuries while she complained about algebra.

She perched on the edge of the central table instead.

Carter had bagged samples of the vampire's clothing and what little dust remained. Her instruments confirmed that the material contained organic traces while refusing to explain how a human-sized body had become less than a kilogram of residue.

Daniel filled three pages with questions and stopped asking them when Giles glared.

Teal'c stood beside the weapons cage.

O'Neill faced Giles across the table.

“Start at the beginning.”

Giles folded a clean bandage around Buffy's arm. “There is no single beginning.”

“Pick the useful one.”

“Into each generation, a Slayer is called. One girl in all the world, granted the strength and instinct necessary to fight vampires, demons, and other forces of darkness.”

O'Neill glanced at Willow and Xander. “One girl.”

“Traditionally.”

“Traditionally?”

Buffy looked at Giles.

He secured the bandage. “Buffy died last spring.”

Carter stopped labeling the evidence bag.

“For how long?”

“A few minutes,” Xander said. “Maybe less.”

O'Neill looked at him. “You were there?”

“Angel and I found her. I did CPR.”

“The Slayer line passed to another girl,” Giles continued. “Xander revived Buffy, but the calling had already occurred.”

“So there are two,” Daniel said.

“There are.”

“Where is the other one?” O'Neill asked.

“The Council has not informed me.”

“The Council,” O'Neill repeated. “That's the adult responsible for this?”

“The Watchers Council is an organization, not an individual.”

“Where?”

“England.”

O'Neill's face became very still.

“A British organization has a seventeen-year-old conducting armed patrols in California.”

“The Slayer's calling is not created by the Council.”

“Didn't ask who created it. I asked who is running the operation.”

Giles removed his glasses and began cleaning them.

Buffy had watched him do it a thousand times. This time she recognized it as an attempt to find language that would sound less terrible to someone who had never agreed the terrible parts were normal.

“I am Buffy's Watcher,” Giles said. “I train her, provide research, and advise her in the execution of her duties.”

“Who pays you?”

“The Council provides a salary.”

“Enough to run this operation?”

Giles looked around the library: public-school books, privately purchased crossbows, bandages from the chemist, weapons repaired by hand.

“Barely enough to maintain myself,” he said. “The position here provides cover and additional income. The Council reimburses very little of what is required in the field.”

O'Neill looked at Buffy.

“And what do they pay her?”

Giles stopped cleaning his glasses.

“Nothing.”

Buffy folded her arms. “I get invaluable experience for my résumé.”

No one laughed.

She hated them a little for that.

“Weapons?” O'Neill asked.

“Primarily supplied by me.”

“Medical care?”

“I provide what I can.”

“Insurance?”

“No.”

“Disability?”

Giles did not answer.

“Survivor benefits?”

“Colonel,” Giles said, “there has never been a retired Slayer.”

The sentence changed the air.

“Because they can't retire?”

“Because another is called only when the current Slayer dies.”

O'Neill looked at the shelves filled with Watcher diaries.

“And the Council is comfortable with that arrangement.”

“The Council considers the Slayer sacred.”

O'Neill glanced toward Buffy. “Do they?”

Giles did not answer immediately.

“They consider the line sacred,” he corrected. “The individual Slayer is... replaceable. When one dies, another is called.”

“Replaceable,” O'Neill repeated.

“That is the practical assumption underlying much of Council doctrine.”

“Disposable, then.”

Giles removed his glasses again but did not contradict him.

Teal'c regarded him steadily.

“They do not support a warrior. They expend one.”

Buffy slid down from the table.

“Okay, that's enough.”

O'Neill turned toward her. “Is anything we've said wrong?”

“You don't get to walk in here, see one fight, and decide you know my life.”

“No.”

The agreement stopped her.

“I know one fight,” he continued. “I know you took point because you were the most qualified. I know everybody else followed your orders when contact happened. I know you got hurt and expected to keep going because nobody was coming to relieve you.”

“There is nobody else.”

“That's the problem.”

“It's my job.”

“Jobs pay.”

“This isn't—” Buffy stopped.

She had no word that made it better.

Duty. Destiny. Calling. None explained why Giles received a cheque and she received another grave to climb into.

“If I don't do it, people die,” she said.

O'Neill's anger softened. “I believe you.”

That was worse too.

“I'm not asking permission.”

“I'm not offering it. I'm asking where the people are who should have been making sure you came back alive.”

Buffy looked at Giles, Willow, and Xander.

“They're here.”

O'Neill followed her gaze.

He saw the answer. He also saw three civilians building with library books and first-aid supplies what an ancient institution had declined to provide.

“Yeah,” he said. “They are.”

✳

Carter recorded her statement first.

The secure communications van stood behind the school gymnasium. Its antenna remained trained on a relay satellite while Destiny maintained the higher encrypted link.

Hammond appeared on the monitor.

“Captain.”

“Sir, I directly observed a hostile humanoid rise from a grave. It demonstrated facial transformation, strength beyond normal human range, and continued function after injuries that should have incapacitated it. Captain Summers—Buffy—destroyed it by penetrating the heart with wood. The body underwent immediate conversion into particulate residue.”

“Alternative explanations?”

“None consistent with the evidence. This was not technology I could identify, a chemical effect, or a staged event.”

Daniel summarized the historical record. The Slayer appeared beneath different names and metaphors across cultures, but the pattern persisted: one young woman, unusual strength, nocturnal enemies, brief service, violent death.

“The Watchers preserved more complete records than any public source,” Daniel said. “Giles claims the institution substantially predates the modern British state.”

“Do you believe him?”

“Yes.”

Teal'c gave the shortest statement.

“Buffy Summers is a skilled warrior. She has been provided neither the personnel nor the resources appropriate to her enemy. Her continued survival reflects her ability and the loyalty of her companions, not the adequacy of those who claim authority over her.”

O'Neill went last.

He stood outside the van while he spoke, looking across the athletic field toward the library windows.

Buffy was visible inside, demonstrating something with a stake while Carter asked questions. Willow sat beside the instrument readout. Xander watched them both, one hand occasionally touching the radio connecting him to a ship in the sky.

“Sir, we have a seventeen-year-old American citizen conducting an ongoing war on United States soil. She has done so since she was fifteen. She receives no salary, medical support, legal protection, relief personnel, or government recognition.”

Hammond listened without interruption.

“She is directed by a representative of a private British organization. Giles is not indifferent to her welfare. Quite the opposite. But he accepts a structure in which the Watcher is underpaid and the Slayer is unpaid because everybody involved has been trained to call that tradition.”

“Does the Council control her actions?”

“They claim the right. She doesn't always recognize it.”

“And their position if she refuses?”

“Giles didn't answer directly. He did say the Council values the line and regards the individual girl as replaceable.”

Hammond's expression hardened.

“Your assessment of Miss Summers?”

“She's the field commander here whether anybody gave her the title or not. Do not send someone to take over her patrols because they have a uniform. They'll get people killed.”

“Recommendation?”

“Immediate support and protection. Medical, communications, intelligence, and a legal review. No recruitment. No detention. No testing. We need to determine what authority the Council actually possesses and what has been done to the girls who came before her.”

“And Sunnydale?”

O'Neill looked toward the cemetery beyond the school.

“The local mortality rate isn't bad record keeping. It's a body count.”

Hammond closed the folder on his desk.

“Return to the library. Maintain liaison with Miss Summers and Mister Giles.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Colonel.”

“Sir?”

“Good report.”

The screen went dark.

Hammond picked up the red telephone.
