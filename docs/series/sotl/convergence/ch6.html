Chapter 6 of 59

Convergence

Young caught a stranger wearing Eli's clothes.

The change happened between one breath and the next. Eli's weight vanished from Young's hands. The body that replaced it was shorter and leaner, all sharp shoulders and adolescent angles inside the grey sweatshirt. The glasses remained, though their lenses had disappeared. The identification badge struck the deck and swung from its lanyard.

ELI WALLACE.

The boy's scream stopped when he ran out of air.

Young lowered him before his legs could collapse.

“Medical,” Young called.

Destiny displayed the same answer on three nearby panels: no active medical personnel.

Of course.

“Colonel Young?” Hammond's voice came through the restored channel. “What happened?”

Young kept one hand on the boy's shoulder and checked his pulse with the other. Fast. Strong. Human.

“Mister Wallace is gone.”

The words resisted leaving his mouth.

“The other individual has physically replaced him.”

The boy opened his eyes.

For one terrible instant, Young expected Eli to look out through them.

Instead he saw naked panic.

“Where—” The boy turned his head and found Earth outside the glass. “Oh, God.”

“Can you understand me?”

“Colonel Young.”

Young's hand tightened involuntarily.

The boy saw it.

“I know you,” he said. “He knew you. I—” He pressed both hands against his temples. “There are too many things in my head.”

“What's your name?”

“Xander.”

“Full name.”

“Alexander Lavelle Harris.”

“Age?”

“Seventeen.”

Young looked toward the camera.

O'Neill had lost every trace of humor. Carter was speaking to someone beyond the frame, ordering a search. Hammond remained at the head of the table.

“Mister Harris,” Hammond said, “do you know where you are?”

Xander looked around the observation deck.

The answer came in layers. Destiny. An Ancient seed ship's successor. A vessel launched before Atlantis left the Milky Way. A home, a prison, a promise, a machine with failing seals in hydroponics and a damaged relay two corridors below the mess.

“Destiny,” he said.

Young's face closed.

Xander corrected desperately, “I remember it. That doesn't mean I'm him.”

“Who is ‘him’?” Hammond asked.

Xander looked at the badge on the deck.

“Eli Wallace.”

The name hurt. It did not feel like losing a fictional character or waking from a dream. It felt like discovering that someone he loved had died and that the someone was him.

Not him.

Both statements were true. Neither fit inside his head.

Young helped him sit against the base of the console.

“What is the last thing you remember as Xander Harris?”

“Halloween.”

“Be specific.”

“I was escorting kids. Buffy and Willow were with me. We bought costumes at Ethan's. There was—”

Xander closed his eyes.

He could see the street from above his own shoulder. Willow stood outside her body beneath the sheet. Buffy stared at a passing car as if it were a dragon. Behind Xander, a doorway opened where no doorway belonged.

“A spell,” he said. “Ethan did something to the costumes. Everybody turned into what they were wearing.”

O'Neill glanced at Daniel. “Any chance ‘costume spell’ means something less weird in Ancient?”

“No.”

“Didn't think so.”

Carter returned to the camera. “General, local power and communications across Sunnydale are normalizing. The anomalous energy source has collapsed.”

“And the vessel?” Hammond asked.

“Still there.”

On Destiny, Young looked toward Earth.

“The spell ended,” Xander said.

“You are certain?”

“Buffy and Willow changed back. I remember—no. I don't remember seeing it. I know it happened.” He swallowed. “That's different.”

Young crouched in front of him.

“Is Eli still present?”

Xander wanted to give him the answer he was asking for.

He searched.

Eli's memories surrounded him: his mother's kitchen, the proof hidden inside a game, Icarus Base collapsing, Rush's fury, Chloe's hand, Ginn's smile, the endless observation window. Xander could follow any of them until he forgot which life he had entered from.

But memory was not a voice. No one answered when he called Eli's name inside his own mind.

“No,” Xander said.

Young looked away.

“I'm sorry.”

“Don't.”

The word was harder than Young intended. He drew a breath.

“Don't apologize for something you didn't do.”

Xander nodded.

The kindness made the guilt worse.

Hammond gave them several seconds before speaking.

“Colonel Young, does the ship recognize Mister Harris?”

Young looked toward the console. “Can you access it?”

Xander reached up.

His fingers found a sequence he could not have explained yesterday. The interface awakened beneath his hand, displayed Eli's authorization, then overlaid it with an amber medical warning.

IDENTITY CONFLICT. BIOLOGICAL VARIATION. COGNITIVE KEY ACCEPTED.

Young read the Ancient text.

“It recognizes part of him.”

Carter leaned toward the screen. “Can you send that record?”

Young's expression turned flat.

“Not until he is medically stable and capable of consent.”

Carter blinked. “Yes, Colonel. Of course.”

Hammond stepped in before the moment could harden. “Mister Harris, are you injured?”

“I don't think so.”

“Do you know anyone aboard who can examine you?”

“TJ. Lieutenant Johansen. She's in stasis.”

Young's gaze moved toward the door.

“General, I recommend waking Lieutenant Johansen. We have experienced an unknown transit and a full physical transformation. I need medical support before I can certify the condition of the crew.”

Hammond considered it. “Approved. Lieutenant Johansen only. No one else until she completes her assessment.”

“Yes, sir.”

The exchange settled something in the room. Young had asked. Hammond had decided. An impossible future officer and a commander from the past had found the first intact piece of the institution they shared.

“Mister Harris,” Hammond said, “we are locating your family and associates in Sunnydale.”

Xander went cold.

“Don't call my parents.”

Hammond's eyes sharpened. “Why?”

“Because they'll either be drunk, fighting, or both. Call Rupert Giles at Sunnydale High School. Or Buffy Summers. Willow Rosenberg.”

O'Neill wrote the names down.

“Your parents don't know where you are?” Hammond asked.

“My parents don't always know when I'm in the same house.”

No one made the obvious joke. Xander wished they would.

“We will contact Mister Giles first,” Hammond said.

“Thank you.”

“Until we understand the situation, you will remain aboard Destiny under Colonel Young's protection. That is not a detention. We currently have no means of bringing you safely to the ground.”

“There are shuttles.”

The answer escaped before Xander consciously searched for it.

Young noticed.

“You can fly one?”

“Eli could.” Xander looked toward the corridor, knowing exactly how many turns separated the observation deck from the shuttle bay. “I remember how. That's not the same as having done it.”

“Then you won't be doing it tonight,” Young said.

“No argument.”

Hammond nodded. “We will determine a safe means of boarding and return as soon as possible. Colonel, wake Lieutenant Johansen and report when she is functional. Maintain current position. Passive sensors only. Keep this channel open.”

“Yes, sir.”

Young started to rise.

Xander caught his sleeve.

“Wait.”

Young looked down.

“The Kino.”

It floated near the glass, still recording.

Young retrieved it. The device displayed the last entry automatically.

Eli appeared above its projector, translucent and small. His face flickered as Xander's tried to replace it.

“If any of this stays,” the recording said, “this is Eli Wallace. Destiny is at Earth. Colonel Young is awake and in command. Stargate Command knows we're here.”

The recording continued through Eli's final warning.

“The person coming after me is Alexander Harris. Xander. He's seventeen. This isn't his fault.”

Young stopped the playback.

For a while, no one spoke.

✳

Willow ran back to the street where Xander had disappeared.

Buffy followed, carrying the shoes that had become impossible to run in the instant she became herself again. They found the children restored and crying, parents arriving from every direction, and Xander's assigned group clustered around the adult volunteer from the next block.

They did not find Xander.

His paper candy bag lay beside the curb. A miniature astronaut guarded it with both arms.

“He went through the light,” the child told Buffy.

“What light?”

The astronaut pointed up.

There was nothing visible in the night sky except stars.

Willow knew better.

“He's alive,” she said.

Buffy turned toward her. “How do you know?”

Willow tried to explain what she had seen as a ghost: the corridor behind him, the older boy turning, the sense of a path completing itself. Human language had not been designed for remembering the geometry of the dead.

“I don't know where he went,” she finished. “But it wasn't death. Death didn't look like that.”

Buffy picked up the candy bag.

“Good.”

It was not relief. Not yet. It was a promise to the universe that death had better not be what happened.

Giles emerged from Ethan's shop alone.

“He's gone,” Willow said.

“Ethan?”

“Xander.”

Giles looked at the place she pointed, then at the unbroken night sky.

“The spell has ended.”

“He didn't come back.”

Behind Giles, the bell above Ethan's door rang once.

The door remained closed.

✳

Young helped Xander stand.

Earth waited beyond the observation glass, unchanged by the fact that two different lives now remembered loving it.

“You asked who I am,” Xander said.

Young did not tell him that he had already answered.

Xander picked up Eli's badge. He turned it over once, then closed his hand around it.

“Xander Harris,” he said. “And I think Eli just brought you home.”
