Chapter 29 of 59

Into the Sun

Young offered Xander the shuttle.

Destiny had moved far enough to prove the anchor no longer held it above Sunnydale. The ship waited beyond its former station while Rush and the engineers inspected every system affected by the transfer.

“You can return to the ground before we leave Earth orbit,” Young said. “No one knows how the transformed connection will behave at interplanetary distance.”

Xander stood at the observation window.

California had turned away from them. The Atlantic reflected morning along the planet's curve.

“Do you need me for the recharge?”

“Need is Rush's word.”

“What's yours?”

“You have the access we just created, and you understand systems the rest of us are still learning. You would be useful.” Young paused. “That doesn't make you obligated.”

Xander considered the shuttle, Sunnydale, and the Sun waiting one astronomical unit away.

“Buffy will say this is stupid.”

“Is she wrong?”

“Not reliably.”

He activated the ground channel.

Buffy answered from the school steps. Dawn revealed broken pavement, scorched walls, and teams collecting what remained of the attack. She had a bandage above one eye and somebody else's blood on her jacket.

“Destiny can take me down before it leaves,” Xander said.

“But?”

“They could use me.”

Buffy closed her eyes briefly.

“Do you want to stay?”

“I want to see this through.”

“Then see it through and come back.”

“You're not going to tell me it's dangerous?”

She looked around at the wreckage of the school.

“I am currently standing on a Hellmouth talking to you on a radio connected to a spaceship from the future. I feel like we've established dangerous.”

“Fair.”

Her expression softened.

“Come home afterward.”

“That's the plan.”

Young heard the echo of the Queen's words. He said nothing.

✳

Destiny turned toward the Sun.

Earth began to recede.

The future crew gathered at the observation deck: Young, TJ, Rush, Wray, Brody, Volker, and Park. They had crossed galaxies believing Earth might remain beyond their lifetimes. They had found it beneath the glass, impossibly young and close.

Now they watched it become small again.

Park remained nearest the window.

“We just got here.”

“We're coming back,” Brody said.

“We think we're coming back.”

Volker studied the return coordinate. “The path is still stable.”

Rush adjusted a power estimate. “Which guarantees nothing.”

“Thank you,” Brody said. “That helped.”

Young let them watch until Earth became a blue-white disc.

Then he sent them back to work.

Destiny accelerated.

Its remaining reserves fell faster as propulsion drew power, but the field no longer fought every meter. The transformed anchor remained inside the navigational core and Stargate buffer, a quiet impossibility pointing behind them.

Sunnydale was no longer where Destiny had to be.

It was somewhere Destiny knew how to find.

As the Sun expanded across the glass, every automatic warning aboard the ship awakened.

Temperature rose along the forward hull. Shields intensified. Power reserves descended toward the stasis margin Rush had marked in red.

“Solar entry in four minutes,” Volker reported.

TJ monitored the pods. “Stasis stable.”

“Shield distribution?” Young asked.

Park answered from engineering. “Within predicted range. Starboard emitters are running hot.”

Rush did not look away from the collector status. “They will hold.”

“That's a prediction?” Young asked.

“It's an instruction.”

The Sun swallowed the window.

Xander knew this sight.

Eli had stood here during Destiny's first recharge and watched the ship descend into fire. Wonder rose from the inherited memory with such force that Xander's breath caught.

But his hands were smaller on the console. Young stood beside him now, not as Eli remembered him then. The return coordinate waited beneath controls Eli had never used.

The memory belonged to Eli.

The moment belonged to Xander.

Destiny entered the photosphere.

Light became substance around them. The shields turned the star's violence into a white ocean rolling against the hull. Damaged relays failed. Emergency barriers sealed. The observation glass darkened until the surface of the Sun became visible as vast fields of boiling fire.

“Reserve at critical threshold,” Brody called.

The collectors remained closed.

Rush worked the console. “Destiny is waiting for optimal depth.”

“Can we override?” Young asked.

“Not without risking the collection vanes.”

The power level fell again.

Across the ship, nonessential systems shut down. The observation deck lost half its lights. For three seconds, Xander felt every sleeping person as a name attached to a diminishing number.

Then Destiny opened its collectors.

Energy flooded the grid.

The first surge restored the shield emitters. The second reached the stasis bays. The third moved through ancient pathways that had been dark since before the expedition arrived.

Deck by deck, Destiny awakened.

Lights extended into sealed corridors. Repair systems resumed. Sensors unfolded from the hull. Consoles displayed sections of the ship no living crew member had ever entered.

The power reserve climbed.

Brody began laughing.

Park answered from engineering with something between a cheer and a sob.

Young gripped the edge of the console as Destiny passed through the Sun and began rising toward the surface.

Xander watched fire stream past the glass.

Eli's wonder remained inside him, undiminished by being shared.

“You saw this,” Xander whispered. “We made it again.”

Then he opened the return route under his own name.
