Chapter 39 of 59

The Intercept

The Tok'ra listening post had no name.

Names became liabilities when bases were abandoned as often as homes.

It occupied chambers beneath a salt plain on a world whose Stargate had not been used openly in four hundred years. Crystals grown through the walls intercepted Goa'uld relay traffic and passed it into a room where three people listened to an empire speak when it believed itself alone.

The fragment from Earth arrived under the seal of Janus.

It was incomplete.

That made it more dangerous.

Garshaw read the reconstruction twice.

“An Ancient vessel defending the Tau'ri.”

Martouf stood beside her while Lantash considered the encryption markers within their shared mind.

“The report originated inside their star system. Janus will send another observer.”

“No,” Selmak said. “He will send a claimant.”

Saroosh allowed Selmak's voice to carry the resonance of the symbiote. She was an old woman by human measure and older still by the experience they shared. Her hands rested steadily on the table.

The next intercepted messages confirmed the prediction.

Janus ordered his agents to locate scholars of Ancient vessels. He requested inventories of temporal artifacts, solar collectors, and ships capable of traversing an unexplained boundary. He offered control of two minor gate routes to anyone who could identify Destiny from the scout's fragmentary profile.

The name of the vessel had not escaped.

Its nature nearly had.

“If it is truly Ancient,” Martouf said, “the Tau'ri cannot operate it.”

“They destroyed Ra,” Garshaw replied.

“With a primitive explosive and great fortune.”

Saroosh spoke in her own unaltered voice.

“Fortune is the name survivors give preparation they did not witness.”

Martouf inclined his head.

The Tok'ra knew Earth as a contradiction. It was the world that had cast out Ra, buried its gate, forgotten the stars, and then returned through the Chappa'ai with weapons too crude to threaten an empire and decisions that repeatedly did.

They knew Apophis had lost his First Prime there.

They knew Ra had died because people from Earth carried a bomb through a gate and refused to remain conquered.

They did not know Earth had found Destiny.

“We must verify the report,” Garshaw said.

“We must warn them,” Selmak answered.

“Contact reveals us to a people who may be compromised by Goa'uld.”

“Silence leaves the vessel to Janus.”

Lantash spoke through Martouf. “If the Tau'ri possess an Ancient warship, they may be no safer with it than he would be.”

Saroosh looked at him.

“Then we should meet the people making the decisions before deciding what they deserve.”

Garshaw studied her. “You volunteer.”

“We do,” Selmak said.

Saroosh's eyes flashed briefly as control shifted between them. Then her own voice returned.

“Both of us.”

The distinction mattered in the Tok'ra tunnels.

It would matter more on Earth.

“The Tau'ri do not know us,” Garshaw said. “They will see a symbiote and assume Goa'uld.”

“They should,” Saroosh replied. “Suspicion is reasonable when a stranger arrives wearing another person's face.”

Selmak continued through her.

“Which is why Saroosh will speak first.”

Garshaw approved the mission.

They assembled an intelligence package containing the intercepted orders, the location of two Janus relay stations, and the last known position of Cardea's Ha'tak. No strategic Tok'ra records were included. Trust would begin with what Earth needed to survive, not everything the Tok'ra possessed.

The listening post dialed Earth.

At Stargate Command, the warning klaxons sounded.

The iris closed.

A radio signal arrived through the wormhole.

“People of Earth,” an elderly woman's voice said. “My name is Saroosh. I speak with Selmak of the Tok'ra. We have intercepted the orders of the Goa'uld who calls himself Janus.”

Hammond entered the control room as the message repeated.

“We request safe passage to offer warning and parley.”
