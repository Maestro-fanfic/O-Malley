Chapter 50 of 59

The Defense Plan

The plan depended upon Janus believing he had already won the important argument.

Destiny appeared trapped, underpowered, and dependent upon Xander. The false Janus believed he understood the doorway holding it to Earth. He believed its crew divided. He believed Earth's defenders possessed one damaged Goa'uld scout and no meaningful space force beyond the Ancient prize they could barely control.

All of that encouraged him to come close.

Young intended to make proximity expensive.

Hammond convened the final command conference six hours before the earliest arrival estimate. Every participant reported from the place they would fight.

Young stood on Destiny's observation deck.

Hammond occupied the SGC control room.

Buffy stood in the Sunnydale High library over a street map covered in defensive sectors.

O'Neill, Carter, Daniel, and Teal'c waited aboard the captured scout behind the Moon.

Saroosh and Selmak remained at the SGC with a Tok'ra communications crystal.

Basil coordinated civilian protection and allied reporting from Sunnydale.

“Command boundaries,” Hammond said.

He began with his own.

“I command the defense of Earth and authorize the use of national and allied resources committed to it. Colonel Young commands Destiny. Colonel O'Neill commands the off-ship operation until his team boards the enemy. Miss Summers commands the Sunnydale ground defense against supernatural and mixed threats. Colonel O'Neill's ground deputy commands conventional forces there and will coordinate with her.”

Buffy glanced at the Air Force lieutenant colonel standing beside her.

He nodded without visible distress at taking battlefield direction from a sixteen-year-old.

“The Tok'ra provide intelligence and communications,” Hammond continued. “They do not command Earth or Destiny forces. Sir Basil controls British support connected to the Council investigation and speaks for his government within that authority.”

Nobody objected.

The orbital plan came first.

Destiny would remain above Sunnydale with shields and weapons apparently degraded. Janus's flagship would approach to capture it. Young would conceal the restored weapon grid until both Ha'taks committed within range.

The captured scout would transmit a damaged identification code and approach Cardea's vessel as though Limen had escaped Earth's control with final intelligence. SG-1 would board when the scout docked or when Cardea lowered a shield section to recover it.

Their objectives were propulsion, weapons, and shields—in that order if Janus attempted withdrawal, the reverse if he opened fire on Earth.

O'Neill summarized. “Break the ship, don't blow up with it.”

“Preferably,” Carter said.

The Tok'ra supplied Cardea's current command codes. The codes might survive the opening challenge. Nobody expected them to survive prolonged scrutiny.

The ground plan came next.

The enemy had been told that the Sunnydale threshold controlled Destiny. They would attempt to seize it intact or destroy it if capture failed. Buffy arranged three defensive rings around the school, but this time she did not assume the attack would remain military.

“The Hellmouth gets worse when people are scared,” she said. “An invasion means panic. Panic means vampires hunt farther from the cemeteries, demons move, and everything under the school gets interested.”

Conventional troops guarded streets, evacuation routes, and air approaches. Teams trained by Giles held the inner approaches with weapons effective against vampires and demons. Teal'c's surrendered Jaffa had not been asked to fight, but six volunteered to advise on Goa'uld tactics. Their participation remained conditional and unarmed unless the defense line failed.

Giles and Willow would remain in the basement with the mooring.

“If it destabilizes?” Hammond asked.

Giles answered. “We reinforce it. Destroying it might strand Destiny or reopen the original crossing.”

“If the enemy takes the school?”

Buffy said, “They don't.”

Hammond did not accept confidence as a contingency.

They established one anyway. Willow could collapse the active surface connection without destroying the stored coordinate. It would blind Destiny's path to Sunnydale temporarily but deny direct access to the mooring. The procedure required three uninterrupted minutes.

Buffy placed her reserve around those minutes.

Xander's role remained aboard Destiny.

He would interpret the false technical assumptions built into Limen's report, monitor the mooring, and assist with Ancient access. He would not operate weapons or serve as a command substitute for Young.

“If they board?” Buffy asked.

Greer answered. “They don't reach him.”

“That wasn't the question.”

Young spoke. “Xander has a protected route to the stasis compartment and an independent guard detail. If boarding becomes likely, he chooses whether to remain at his station or withdraw. If he withdraws, nobody countermanding him gets through Sergeant Greer.”

Greer almost smiled.

Xander approved the arrangement.

Hammond reviewed the final contingencies.

Nuclear weapons remained a last resort and required presidential authorization unless loss of command was imminent. The Goa'uld transport would support evacuation, not attack, while its systems remained insufficiently understood. The Stargate stayed available for the Tok'ra medical and intelligence teams until hostile access threatened the SGC.

If Destiny fell, Earth would continue fighting.

If the SGC fell, Young retained authority to defend the planet.

If Young lost command, Scott assumed control of Destiny, followed by the designated watch officers.

No single person—not Hammond, Young, Buffy, Xander, or the President—was permitted to become the only hinge upon which Earth survived.

“Questions?” Hammond asked.

There were many.

They answered all that time allowed.

Then the captured scout moved out from behind the Moon and began pretending to limp home.
