Chapter 27 of 59

Ground Point

The Sunnydale High basement had never contained so many competent adults.

Buffy found it unsettling.

Air Force personnel established three security rings around the school. The innermost team carried weapons selected with Giles's help and treated his instructions about decapitation and invitations with complete seriousness. A second team cleared the adjacent streets under the cover of a hazardous-gas leak. The outer ring redirected traffic and kept the curious away.

Medical personnel waited in the gymnasium.

Two evacuation vehicles stood behind the school with their engines running.

Portable floodlights illuminated every corridor. Secure radios worked on every level. Spare batteries, first-aid kits, wooden stakes, fire extinguishers, and blessed water had been distributed according to an actual written plan.

Buffy walked through the command post in the library twice before O'Neill noticed.

“Something wrong?”

“I'm looking for the part where everybody tells me to handle the monsters while they argue about whether monsters exist.”

“We put that on tomorrow's schedule.”

She looked at the map spread across the table.

“This is weird.”

“The military presence in your high school?”

“People showing up before the screaming.”

O'Neill's expression changed by a degree.

“Could get used to it.”

Buffy considered that more frightening than the troops.

“Maybe.”

He gestured toward the map. “Your town. Your enemy. Where do you want us?”

No adult had ever asked her that before.

Buffy began issuing orders.

✳

Giles and Willow built the ground point over the Hellmouth seal in the school basement.

The Council texts prescribed iron keys, oil lamps, and chalk mixed with ash from a threshold burned in war. Carter's instruments required shielded cable, synchronized clocks, and three sensors whose names meant nothing to Giles.

They used both.

Willow drew the outer circle from Giles's measurements, checked it against Carter's geometry, erased two degrees of arc, and drew it again.

“Magic usually isn't this picky,” she said.

“Magic is always this picky. It simply waits until afterward to disclose which detail you misunderstood.”

“That is a terrible system.”

“Yes.”

Daniel translated the invocation into English, Latin, and the older ceremonial form. He eliminated every word that could mean bind, hold, compel, or possess.

“We are not commanding a god,” he said.

“Good policy,” Willow answered.

“We are describing a transition and asking the existing working to recognize it.”

“Still asking a god.”

“Politely.”

Basil supplied the keys.

They came from the seized Council archive: one from its first London headquarters, one from the oldest surviving Potential's residence, and one recovered from the house of a Slayer whose Watcher had recorded her as retired.

Buffy held the third key without speaking.

“We can obtain another,” Basil said.

“No.”

She placed it at the western edge of the circle.

“She gets to help.”

✳

At sunset, the Hellmouth noticed them.

Carter's instruments registered the first change as a low-frequency distortion under the school. Giles felt it as pressure behind his eyes. Buffy felt it in the old scar beneath her ribs where the Master had bitten her.

Across Sunnydale, vampires rose early.

Demons left cellars, warehouses, storm drains, and abandoned houses. Some moved toward the high school without knowing why. Others knew exactly what waited beneath it and wanted to be present when the boundary opened.

The first perimeter team reported movement six blocks away.

Then four.

Then every radio channel spoke at once.

Buffy stood over the library map with Teal'c and O'Neill.

“They're not coordinating,” she said. “They're converging.”

Teal'c marked the reports. “They approach along every available route.”

“Can we draw them away?” O'Neill asked.

Giles answered from the basement channel. “Not once the ritual begins. The Hellmouth is about to become one side of an active passage. Anything sensitive to dimensional energy will feel it.”

“How long do you need?”

Willow looked at the synchronized clock.

“Twenty-seven minutes to establish the pattern. After that, we hold until Destiny moves.”

“How long does Destiny need?”

Carter's voice came from orbit.

“Once both sides stabilize, six minutes to transfer the anchor and bring propulsion online.”

Rush spoke behind her. “Assuming the field behaves as projected.”

“It won't,” Buffy said.

Rush paused. “On what basis?”

“Hellmouth.”

Giles nodded. “She's right.”

O'Neill keyed the command frequency.

“All teams, revised expectation: the plan will go wrong. Maintain assigned positions until Summers tells you otherwise.”

Buffy looked at him.

“That's the order?”

“I've had worse plans.”

The first vampires reached the outer cordon.

They came in a group of twelve, moving through a drainage channel where the roadblock could not see them. A motion sensor found them. The floodlights came on. The vampires charged into crossbows, wooden rounds, and Teal'c waiting at the end of the channel.

None reached the school.

More followed.

Buffy moved between the defensive rings, correcting positions as the pressure changed. She sent one team away from a sewer access thirty seconds before something horned tore through the grate. She redirected another when the cemetery reports suddenly stopped, correctly reading silence as an ambush rather than safety.

Nobody asked how she knew.

Nobody overruled her because she was sixteen.

When a sergeant twice her age repeated her order over the command net, he called her Summers and expected to be obeyed.

It did not make her older.

It made her less alone.

In the basement, Giles lit the first lamp.

Willow turned the first key.

The floor moved beneath them.

Not an earthquake. A breath.

The sealed mouth under Sunnydale High opened just far enough to show darkness between its teeth.

Every light in the school went out.

Emergency floodlamps activated one second later.

In their hard white glare, something struck the underside of the basement floor.

The concrete rose.

“Ground point active,” Willow said, her voice shaking but clear.

Far above them, Destiny answered.

Every lamp bent toward the center of the circle.

The darkness between the broken sections of floor deepened. It did not spread. It acquired distance, becoming a road seen through an open doorway at night.

Footsteps sounded on that road.

The first belonged to a young man.

The second belonged to someone very old.

Only one figure crossed the threshold.

He wore no crown and carried no weapon. A plain iron key hung at his throat. The face turned toward Willow was smooth and unworried; the face reflected in Carter's black instrument screen was lined by more years than any human life could contain.

Giles could not move.

Daniel forgot every prepared form of address.

The figure looked down at the three keys marking the circle, then upward, through concrete, atmosphere, and distance, to the ship held above them.

“You have built a mooring,” Janus said.

His gaze settled on the open channel to Destiny.

“Now you require a traveler.”
