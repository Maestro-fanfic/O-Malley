Chapter 32 of 59

Recall

Matthew Scott woke calling Chloe's name.

Young was ready for it.

“She's alive. Still in stasis. You're aboard Destiny.”

Scott's eyes found him. Confusion yielded to remembered command.

“Colonel.”

“Don't stand yet.”

TJ monitored the revival while Young gave him water and the shortest version of events that could keep panic from filling the gaps.

Destiny had reached Earth.

It was 1997.

The crew remained safe in stasis.

Eli was gone.

An enemy scout was watching the system.

Scott absorbed each statement separately.

“Sir,” he said when Young finished, “I think the drugs are still affecting me.”

“They're not.”

Ronald Greer woke two pods away.

He came out fighting the restraints, recognized TJ, saw Young, and forced himself still.

“Situation?”

“Complicated.”

“Hostiles?”

“One confirmed. More possible.”

Greer nodded. “That's less complicated.”

Lieutenant James woke next.

The remaining revivals took place in groups selected by function: flight crew, damage-control teams, security personnel, and medics to support TJ. Every awakening consumed power they could now afford and created another person who had to be told that home was outside and their younger self was somewhere upon it.

Young briefed them without windows.

He learned from Brody and Park's experience. The crew received the date before seeing Earth. They received the rule against personal contact before hope could become a plan. They received Xander's name before they encountered Eli's memories inside a stranger's face.

Then Young brought them to the observation deck.

Earth ended the remaining disbelief.

Scott stood before the glass with his hands at his sides.

“My parents are down there.”

“Yes,” Young said.

Scott had lost both of them as a child.

The single syllable cost Young more than the briefing had.

James turned away from the planet. Greer did not. His expression revealed nothing, but he remained at the window after the others moved to their stations.

“Nobody contacts anyone,” Young said. “Not family. Not younger counterparts. Not the military units some of us haven't joined yet. General Hammond's order and mine.”

“How long?” James asked.

“Until contact can happen without damaging their lives or compromising this command.”

“And if that means never?”

Young did not offer a promise he could not keep.

“Then it means never.”

Scott faced him. “What happened to Eli?”

Xander stood at the far console wearing his grey sweatshirt and a temporary badge Wray had printed aboard the ship.

ALEXANDER HARRIS

CIVILIAN TECHNICAL LIAISON

Scott looked at him and went pale.

Xander did not wait for someone else to explain.

“Eli got Destiny to Earth. He woke Colonel Young, contacted Stargate Command, and transferred his work before the spell ended. I have his memories. I'm not him.”

Scott stared at the wire-framed glasses, the familiar posture around an unfamiliar body, and the badge that deliberately carried no rank.

“Do you remember Chloe?”

“Yes.”

“Do you—”

Scott stopped.

Xander knew the rest of the question.

“The memories have feelings attached. That doesn't make them promises I can keep for him.”

Scott looked toward Chloe's status on the stasis display.

“No,” he said. “I guess it doesn't.”

Greer walked to Xander.

Young shifted his weight, prepared to intervene.

Greer examined the civilian badge.

“You know the ship?”

“Some of it.”

“Can you do the job?”

“Which job?”

“Good answer.”

Greer moved to the damage-control station.

✳

Young rebuilt his command structure.

Scott assumed flight operations under Young's direct authority. James organized internal security and stasis-bay protection. Greer took damage control, dividing awakened personnel into teams capable of reaching the sections likely to fail under weapons fire.

TJ retained medical authority.

Wray established a roster recording who had been awakened, why, what duties they had accepted, and what contact restrictions applied. She placed the civilian personnel outside the military chain wherever their role did not require orders.

Xander's position required an argument.

Rush wanted him assigned to the sensor and navigation team.

Young refused the word assigned.

“He volunteered to advise.”

“That is a semantic distinction during an engagement.”

“It's the distinction between a civilian and a member of my crew.”

“He possesses essential knowledge of this vessel.”

“So does Captain Carter. You don't command her either.”

Rush looked at Xander. “Do you intend to help?”

“Yes.”

“Then the practical difference—”

“Is that I can say no,” Xander answered.

Wray wrote the sentence into the operating terms.

Xander would support sensor interpretation, navigation, and Ancient-system access. He would receive all threat information relevant to his decisions. He could refuse tasks outside the agreed role. He would not direct weapons, select targets, or be treated as tactical personnel.

Young signed.

Xander signed beneath him.

Rush did not receive a signature line.

By the time the scout began its next slow movement behind Jupiter, Destiny had a bridge watch, damage-control teams, internal security, medical coverage, and a civilian liaison sitting at a console that recognized his own name.

The ship was still damaged.

The crew was still incomplete.

It was no longer asleep.
