Chapter 54 of 59

Destiny Engages

Janus expected Destiny to fire too early.

Young gave him nothing.

The Goa'uld flagship advanced with shields layered across its forward quarter. Weapons charged along the pyramid's edges. Two Al'kesh moved wide to surround the Ancient ship. Cardea's Ha'tak held high orbit while its ground forces deployed.

Destiny drifted.

One false thruster failure rotated the ship enough to expose its damaged port side. Rush protested the maneuver even though he had helped design it.

“If they fire before the turn completes—”

“They won't,” Young said.

“You cannot know that.”

“Janus wants the ship.”

Xander watched the enemy sensor beams sweep across Destiny. Each one found the power instability, broken collectors, and inaccessible weapons described in Limen's report.

Behind those readings, Park held restored energy inside isolated pathways. Brody waited to reconnect them. The weapons crews stood ready at systems pretending to be dead.

Scott counted the closing distance.

“Flagship entering effective range in twenty seconds.”

Janus transmitted directly to Destiny.

“Colonel Young. Your general has closed the road before you. Open it, and I will permit your crew to wake.”

Young muted the channel.

“Ten seconds,” Scott said.

Cardea's Ha'tak altered position.

The captured scout had docked. SG-1 was aboard. The vessel's shields remained active, but its regeneration pattern carried the distortion Carter's device was meant to create.

“Cardea committed,” Volker reported.

On Earth, the third transport crossed Sunnydale's inner defensive ring.

Hammond saw every piece settle.

“Destiny, execute.”

Young faced Rush.

“Bring us online.”

Power moved through the ship.

The false failures vanished. Shield emitters rose from twelve percent to full available output. Thrusters fired together and stopped Destiny's drift. Ancient weapons awakened across the hull in lines of white light.

Janus had time to understand the lie.

Then Destiny fired.

The first volley struck his flagship's forward shield. Gold light spread across it in a wave. The second targeted the two Al'kesh, destroying one weapon array and forcing both craft away from the Ancient hull.

“Turn us,” Young ordered.

Scott rotated Destiny's undamaged side toward the enemy.

Janus returned fire.

Plasma filled the observation glass. Destiny's shields absorbed the first strike. The second drove power backward through a damaged emitter and blew three distribution nodes before Park isolated them.

“Shield at seventy-four percent,” Brody reported.

“Damage control moving,” Greer said.

Young tracked both capital ships.

Cardea's weapons were turning toward Destiny. SG-1 remained aboard. Her shields showed Carter's planted weakness, but firing a full volley risked killing the team before they completed their mission.

“Target Cardea's outer weapon nodes,” Young ordered. “Minimum power sufficient to collapse local shields.”

Rush looked at him. “Minimum power permits recovery.”

“It also permits our people to survive.”

Destiny fired three precise shots.

Cardea's shield failed along one edge. Two weapon emplacements exploded. The Ha'tak rolled away from Earth, buying SG-1 time and forcing Cardea's crew to divert power from internal security.

Janus's flagship used the opening.

It moved past Destiny's forward arc and aligned with Earth.

“He's targeting the planet,” Volker said.

Young did not hesitate.

“Interpose.”

Scott drove the Ancient ship across the firing line.

Janus discharged his main weapon.

The strike would have entered atmosphere above North America.

It hit Destiny instead.

The shield collapsed across the starboard quarter. Energy tore through two decks and opened an old fracture along the hull. Atmosphere vanished from sealed compartments. Greer's teams closed pressure doors while one team remained on the wrong side.

“Casualties,” Young said.

Greer did not answer for four seconds.

“Three injured. Two missing behind the breach.”

There was no time to stop.

“Keep us between them and Earth.”

Destiny returned fire.

Its weapons damaged the flagship's forward shield but did not break it. The ship was Ancient, powered by a star, and built for a mission longer than civilizations. It was also millions of years old, repaired by automation that had failed in entire sections, and crewed by people who had first fired its weapons minutes earlier.

Janus's next volley found the weakness.

Three impacts struck the same damaged quarter.

The weapons display went dark from bow to midship.

Rush entered commands. Nothing answered.

“Primary weapon bus is offline.”

Young looked at the enemy flagship turning for another shot.

Destiny remained between it and Earth.

For the moment, that was all the defense they had.
