Chapter 28 of 59

Breakaway

Daniel found his voice first.

“Which traveler?”

Janus looked at him with two ages of the same face.

“The one who has not arrived.”

On Destiny's observation deck, Xander stood before the navigation console. The open connection carried Janus's words without static or delay. For the first time since the ship appeared, its instruments agreed precisely about the distance between Sunnydale and orbit.

“Eli,” Xander said.

“Eli Wallace reached his ending,” Janus answered. “You carry what crossed from it.”

Young's expression hardened. “Did he choose that?”

The god turned his gaze upward.

“He chose to wake you.”

Young said nothing.

“He chose to call his world. He chose to place the sleeping in your command. He chose to name the boy who would follow him and to say the boy was blameless.”

Janus touched neither key nor lamp. His hands remained empty at his sides.

“Those choices were his. I claim none of them.”

Xander remembered every one.

Young waking in the stasis pod. The first impossible view of Earth. Hammond's voice answering across time. The Kino held in Eli's hands while his body ceased to remain his own.

Don't make him prove he's me.

“Did you kill him?” Xander asked.

“No.”

“Did the spell?”

“It opened a road at the end of the road he walked. What crossed it could not remain what it had been.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It is the answer. You ask for a separation between ending and beginning while standing inside both.”

Buffy entered the basement at a run, stopped at the edge of the circle, and raised her sword.

Janus glanced at the blade.

“If you're here to help,” she said, “start making sense.”

“I am not here to help.”

The Air Force personnel at the stairs shifted their weapons.

Janus did not look at them.

“Then why are you here?” Buffy asked.

“To answer.”

“Only answer?” Giles said.

“I opened one door. You opened this one.” Janus indicated the circle without crossing its lines. “Its power is yours. Its danger is yours. I will add nothing to either.”

Daniel stepped nearer, careful not to enter the working.

“What are we missing?”

“A destination is not made by instruments. A passage is not completed by naming its end. Someone must arrive.”

Giles looked toward the channel. “Xander must accept Eli's memories.”

“He has carried them from the first moment.”

“Then he must become Eli,” Rush said.

Janus turned one face toward the camera.

“No.”

The single word carried through every open speaker aboard Destiny. The consoles remained unchanged.

“A doorway permits change,” Janus said. “It does not make falsehood true. The ship knows Alexander Harris is not Eli Wallace. The working does not.”

Carter understood.

“We have to make them agree.”

“Yes.”

“How?” Xander asked.

“Name what you carry. Name who carries it. Choose where you stand when the crossing ends.”

“And Destiny?”

“Must answer you by the name that is yours.”

Park looked at the access architecture on her station. “We can create a new user identity.”

Brody shook his head. “Not with Eli's permissions. Command access is tied into every modification he made.”

Young moved to the central console.

“I have command authority.”

Rush looked at him. “To the ship, perhaps. Not necessarily to the field.”

Young addressed Janus. “If we do this, what happens?”

“If the boy claims what he carries without surrendering his name, and the vessel accepts him, the arrival ends. The mooring may take the weight you have prepared it to bear.”

“May?” Buffy said.

“I promised no outcome.”

“And if they fail?”

“The ship remains until it dies. Or the passage breaks where it is weakest.”

Janus looked at Xander.

“That is not the ship.”

TJ moved beside him.

“We stop if his condition deteriorates.”

“If you stop before the transfer begins, nothing changes,” Janus said. “If you stop after, the doorway will choose no gentler division because you regret opening it.”

Young absorbed that. “Point of no return?”

Carter checked the synchronized plan. “When we commit the new coordinate to the gate buffer.”

“Then nobody commits until every station is ready.”

Buffy's radio erupted with reports from the east perimeter. She listened for five seconds.

“I have to go.”

She looked at Janus.

“Was all this to save me?”

“I opened a door before you.”

“That's not what I asked.”

“Your friend refused to leave you dead. A colonel looked beneath the town. A general reported what he found. A president called across the sea. A queen demanded names.”

Janus's ancient face held no smile.

“Those choices were theirs. Do not give them to a god.”

Buffy lowered the sword.

“Good answer.”

She ran back upstairs.

✳

Young brought every station to readiness.

Rush and Carter opened the pathways between navigation and the Stargate. Brody balanced the power flow. Volker rebuilt the position model around two distinct values: current and return. Park waited to write the new identity into Destiny's access system.

On the ground, Willow and Giles held the circle.

Outside, the eastern security ring collapsed under a wave of vampires and two creatures Giles identified over the radio as Fyarl demons. Buffy pulled the defenders back one block, allowed the attackers to crowd the narrow approach, and sent Teal'c through their exposed side.

The line held.

“All stations ready,” Carter said.

Young looked at Xander. “Your decision.”

Xander placed his hand on the navigation console.

It opened for Eli Wallace.

Amber warnings spread across its surface.

IDENTITY CONFLICT. BIOLOGICAL VARIATION. COGNITIVE KEY ACCEPTED.

“Begin,” Xander said.

Carter committed the coordinate.

The Stargate awakened.

Its inner ring did not turn. No chevrons engaged. Light moved across the symbols in both directions as the gate received an address that belonged to no point in its network and every moment since Halloween.

In the basement, the three iron keys lifted from the floor.

Willow spoke the first line of the invocation.

Giles answered with the second.

The Hellmouth struck upward.

Concrete shattered around the circle. One of the Air Force guards fell as the floor pitched; Daniel caught her before she reached the broken edge. Something with too many fingers forced one hand through the gap.

O'Neill fired from the stairwell. Teal'c's staff blast followed his bullets. The hand vanished in burning fragments.

Neither shot crossed the circle.

Janus stood within it, untouched and inactive, watching mortals hold the door they had opened.

Aboard Destiny, the field tightened.

Eli's memories rose inside Xander all at once.

He was twenty-five. He was seventeen. He was standing in his mother's kitchen. He was giving Buffy CPR. He was solving the equation hidden in a game. He was holding Jesse as Jesse became dust. He loved Ginn. He had never met her. He had crossed galaxies. He had never left California.

The console disappeared beneath lives that both felt like his.

“Eli knows the next sequence,” Rush said.

Young stepped between him and Xander.

“Xander.”

No response.

“Look at me.”

Xander found Young's face in two different memories.

“What would Eli—” Rush began.

“I don't care.”

Young did not look away from Xander.

“What do you see?”

“The observation deck.”

“Now. What do you see now?”

Xander swallowed.

“You. Carter. The console.”

“What can you do?”

“Open the route table.”

“Do that.”

Xander opened it.

“What else?”

“Separate the fixed coordinate from local position.”

“Do that.”

“Eli wrote the interface.”

“Eli isn't standing here.”

The words hurt.

They also held.

Young pointed at the console. “I don't need you to remember what he would have done. Tell me what you can do.”

Xander looked down.

“I can finish it.”

“Then finish it.”

Xander moved through the controls. He used Eli's knowledge, Carter's architecture, Volker's corrected model, Park's access framework, and the position Willow held open below them.

None was sufficient alone.

Together, they gave him a place to stand.

Park opened the new identity record.

“Name?” she asked.

“Alexander Lavelle Harris.”

“Access basis?”

Young placed his hand on the command control.

“Civilian technical liaison. Authorization under my command.”

“Cognitive inheritance?”

Xander looked at Eli's name on the old record.

“Retained. Not identity.”

Park entered it.

Destiny considered the distinction.

The console beneath Xander's hands went dark.

For one terrible second, every system denied him.

Then the surface illuminated in blue.

ALEXANDER HARRIS. AUTHORIZED USER.

The magical field changed.

Xander felt Eli's life settle inside his own—not vanish, not become less real, but cease demanding that one of them be a mistake.

“My name is Xander Harris,” he said. “I carry Eli Wallace's memories. Destiny is where I arrived. Sunnydale is where I return.”

In the basement, Willow turned the final key.

The Stargate accepted the coordinate.

“Anchor transferred!” Carter called.

Young faced Rush. “Engines.”

Rush engaged them.

Destiny moved.

Space resisted for half a second.

Xander held the return coordinate and released the present one.

The ship moved again.

One meter.

Ten.

A hundred.

Earth shifted in the observation glass.

Volker laughed once, sharply, as every navigational instrument agreed.

“We're away.”

On the ground, the darkness beneath the circle folded inward. The grasping shapes below it vanished. Concrete struck concrete. The lamps went out.

Janus stood at the center until the last line of the doorway closed across him.

He raised no hand in farewell.

“A passage completed,” he said.

Then he was gone.
