Chapter 48 of 59

The Fleet Moves

The Goa'uld who called himself Janus received the message inside a hall built around a Stargate that no longer led anywhere.

He had removed it from a conquered world after its people attempted to bury it. The ring stood upright behind his throne, its chevrons plated in gold, its inner track fixed in place. Petitioners entered through it on foot and knelt before the man who claimed every journey ended at his feet.

His host had been beautiful once.

Centuries of sarcophagus use had preserved the face and emptied it. One side wore youth without warmth. The other carried a scar from temple to jaw, deliberately retained because Janus liked the resemblance to two profiles joined in one body.

Limen's report played above his hand.

He listened to the technical claims once.

He listened to Xander's name three times.

Cardea stood below the throne. Her armor carried the emblem of a hinge: two joined plates around a dark central pin. She ruled one Ha'tak, six gate worlds, and every prison through which Janus moved people he wished to keep useful.

“It is bait,” she said.

“All invitations are bait.”

“The Tau'ri captured your scout and defeated the ground force. Limen sends from a compromised world.”

“Limen's keys remain valid.”

“Keys may be stolen.”

Janus rose.

“Then the thieves have chosen what they wish me to believe.”

He moved through the dead Stargate behind his throne.

“A powerless vessel would not interest me. An invincible vessel would not invite me. They offer the shape between: damaged enough to take, powerful enough to justify the taking.”

Cardea followed.

“Then destroy it before another System Lord learns it exists.”

Janus turned.

His eyes shone.

“An Ancient vessel crossed a boundary no hyperdrive can perceive. It entered a sun and returned to the same world. It answers a human marked by passage.”

He closed one hand around the key suspended from his neck.

It was not a key. It was a fragment of Ancient control crystal reshaped to resemble one.

“I will not destroy proof of my dominion.”

Cardea lowered her head.

“What force?”

Janus wanted six Ha'taks.

He could assemble four without inviting questions from rivals. He could move two immediately without abandoning borders that would close behind him.

He chose two.

His flagship would seize Destiny. Cardea's would control Earth orbit and deploy troops against the Sunnydale threshold. Four Al'kesh and twelve death gliders would support them. Three transports would carry Jaffa selected for loyalty rather than experience.

It was more than Earth had hoped to attract.

It was less than the force Janus would have sent to exterminate a world.

The deception had preserved his desire to capture.

It had not made him cautious.

“Recall the gate scholars,” Janus ordered. “Prepare the host vessels. Bring the temporal archive.”

“And the boy?” Cardea asked.

“Alive.”

“If he resists?”

“Doors do not resist the hand that opens them.”

Cardea did not challenge the doctrine.

She had watched enough prisoners break to know that doors could remain closed until the person outside died.

✳

Tok'ra observers counted the ships as they left separate systems.

One Ha'tak departed Janus's throne world under full escort. Cardea's vessel joined it at a barren rendezvous. Support craft docked. Troop complements transferred. Course calculations pointed toward a concealed staging point two hyperspace jumps from Earth.

The Tok'ra warning reached the SGC eleven minutes later.

Hammond read the force assessment in silence.

Two Ha'taks.

Four Al'kesh.

Twelve gliders.

At least three thousand Jaffa.

Estimated arrival: thirty-six to forty-eight hours.

The President joined by secure line. Young appeared from Destiny. SG-1, Carter, Basil, Saroosh, and Selmak stood around the conference table.

“Can Destiny stop them?” the President asked.

Young did not pretend certainty.

“We can fight them. Stopping them depends on how much of the false report they believe, whether we preserve surprise, and whether the ship survives first contact.”

“Can Earth be evacuated?”

“No,” Hammond said. “Specific sites and personnel can. The planet cannot.”

The President looked at the fleet projection.

“Then we hold here.”

He authorized a covert national defense alert, allied notification through protected channels, and every military resource Hammond could use without revealing the Stargate program publicly before the attack began.

Hammond faced Young.

“Colonel, prepare Destiny for the defense of Earth.”

Young stood on the observation deck with the planet behind him.

“Yes, sir.”

Across Destiny, alarms changed from readiness to preparation for war.
