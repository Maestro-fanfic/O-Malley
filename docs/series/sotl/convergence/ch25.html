Chapter 25 of 59

The Living Coordinate

Destiny had arrived above Sunnydale.

The spell had not.

That was the conclusion Carter reached after comparing the propulsion test with every measurement taken since Halloween. The ship existed completely in 1997. Its mass, energy, crew, and damage had all crossed. No part of the hull remained stretched through another time.

The transition itself was still being maintained.

“Think of a telephone connection after the conversation has ended,” Carter said. “Both people are where they're supposed to be, but neither has hung up.”

Willow frowned at the equations on the library board.

“Except one telephone is a spaceship and the line runs through Xander.”

“Essentially.”

“And the phone company is a Roman god.”

Carter looked at the board.

“That's outside the analogy.”

Giles stood beside a diagram copied from the oldest Janus text. It showed a doorway, a traveler, and two carved stones marking the road on either side.

“The rite required a point of origin, a threshold, and a destination,” he said. “Ethan supplied the invocation. The Hellmouth supplied the threshold. Eli and Xander supplied the correspondence.”

“The badge gave Destiny's identity to Xander,” Daniel said.

“Not its identity,” Xander corrected. “Eli's.”

Daniel nodded. “Eli's identity gave the spell something aboard Destiny to find. But Xander was physically here, in Sunnydale, in 1997. He gave it a time and place to arrive.”

Carter drew a line between Xander's name and the ship.

“Destiny followed Eli's access signature to Xander's position. Now it keeps receiving two valid statements. Eli is aboard Destiny. Xander is in Sunnydale. The spell can't reconcile them, so it holds the ship at the point where both are functionally true.”

Buffy looked at Xander.

“Which makes him what? The rope?”

“The address,” Carter said.

Giles shook his head. “A living coordinate.”

Xander stared at the line on the board.

“I'd like to go back to being bad at geometry.”

✳

Rush wanted proof.

He proposed a complete neurological map while Xander operated Destiny's primary systems, followed by controlled separation at increasing distances from the Hellmouth. He described induced sleep, magnetic stimulation, and temporary suppression of selected memory pathways before TJ interrupted him.

“No.”

The discussion took place aboard Destiny, in the observation deck. Xander had returned by shuttle with Carter, Daniel, Buffy, and Giles. Willow remained below to prepare the first ground calculations with O'Neill and Teal'c.

Rush regarded TJ with disciplined impatience. “You rejected six separate procedures as though I proposed all of them simultaneously.”

“I rejected six procedures.”

“Some are entirely noninvasive.”

“Some are. Those can be explained to him individually.”

“We have a limited amount of time.”

Young stepped away from the window.

“Then stop wasting it.”

Rush turned. “Colonel—”

“Mister Harris isn't ship's equipment. He isn't under my command. You ask. He decides. Lieutenant Johansen can stop anything she considers unsafe.”

“And if he refuses the measurements necessary to free this ship?”

“Then he refuses.”

Xander looked through the glass at Earth.

Rush followed his gaze.

“There are hundreds of lives—”

“Which is why he deserves a choice made without you putting their coffins in the question,” Young said.

The channel to Cheyenne Mountain remained open. Hammond's voice came from the console.

“Colonel Young's ruling is confirmed. Informed consent is not a procedural inconvenience, Doctor Rush.”

Rush's jaw tightened.

“Very well.”

TJ set a written list before Xander and explained each proposed procedure in language stripped of Rush's urgency. Two involved ordinary scans. One involved placing his hand on an inactive console while Carter observed. Another would allow Destiny to test his access without opening any ship system. The last would ask him to describe whatever he felt while viewing the navigation display.

“What happens if I say stop?” Xander asked.

“We stop,” TJ said.

“Even if we learned something useful?”

“Especially then. Useful results make people greedy.”

Xander selected four procedures. He refused magnetic stimulation and every proposal involving altered consciousness.

Rush made no comment.

Young signed the limits beneath Xander's name.

The first scans confirmed what TJ already knew. Xander's body was his own. His neural structure did not match Eli's medical records. The memories had created new patterns of association, but they had not rewritten his brain into an adult man's.

The console test was less simple.

Xander placed his palm against a dormant control surface.

Ancient symbols illuminated beneath it.

A system notice appeared.

Carter translated slowly. “Recognized operational sequence. Registered user Eli Wallace.”

Rush leaned closer.

A second notice replaced it.

“Biometric mismatch,” Carter continued. “Identity confirmation denied.”

The console remained lit.

“It rejected him,” Buffy said.

“No,” Park answered from the engineering station. “It rejected the claim that he's Eli. It still accepted the command pattern.”

Xander moved his fingers across the surface. A basic diagnostic opened, but the protected controls remained sealed.

Destiny knew the habits of the person touching it.

It also knew he was not the man who had formed them.

“The spell forced a correspondence,” Giles said. “It did not erase the distinction.”

“Can you close the link from there?” Young asked.

Xander searched through the console, following knowledge that arrived with the wrong hands around it.

“No. The ship doesn't think there's a link. As far as navigation is concerned, this is its position.”

Carter displayed the result of the propulsion test.

“And any attempt to assign a different position is corrected before movement occurs.”

Xander closed the diagnostic.

For a moment the console resisted. Then the light beneath his hand went dark.

“It listened when I ended the session.”

Rush's attention sharpened. “Do it again.”

Xander withdrew his hand.

“No.”

Rush opened his mouth.

Young said nothing.

He did not need to.

After a moment, Rush looked away.

Xander studied the dark surface.

“Destiny doesn't think I'm Eli,” he said. “The spell does.”

Daniel considered the wording.

“Then perhaps the unfinished crossing isn't trying to bring Destiny to you.”

Giles followed the thought.

“It is waiting for Eli Wallace to arrive.”

Xander looked toward the rows of sleeping crew beyond the compartment.

Eli never would.

The doorway was waiting for a traveler who had crossed only as memory.
