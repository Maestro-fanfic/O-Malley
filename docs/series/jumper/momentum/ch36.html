Chapter 36 of 49

Cent POV: Dad's fine.

We were three days into the coast when Joe appeared beside my couch.

One instant there was empty air between me and the bulkhead. The next he was there on one knee, headset still on, with no handhold and no weight to keep the knee on the deck. He started drifting upward. I caught his sleeve before his head reached the overhead.

Childs had her restraints half open. Iverson's hand was on the alarm control. Grebenchekov was out of his couch before anyone else had finished deciding whether to move.

"Not a hostile," Joe said.

He was repeating Dad's words from the night they pulled me out of the warehouse.

"Dad's fine," he said. "Hunt too. Your dad put both men in the pit."

Then Joe was gone.

For a moment nobody said anything. He had come from mission control to a ship moving through space roughly sixty-seven million kilometers away, told me my family was alive, and left again. He could not have known exactly where the carrier was.

The main display changed when mission control's security feed finally crossed the light delay.

We watched knowing Dad and Hunt were safe. It didn't make Dad look any slower.

I watched Dad flicker and the first guy was gone. Then he flickered again and the second guy was gone too. Hunt had barely started reaching inside his jacket.

Then Joe appeared in the corridor on one knee, looked Dad over, and vanished.

Four minutes later Sterling confirmed Joe had returned safely and that Dad, Hunt, and everyone in mission control were unhurt.

The light delay still existed. It just hadn't made us wait to learn whether our family was alive.
