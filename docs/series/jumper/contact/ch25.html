Chapter 25 of 53

Truman (POV): I'd made a promise a long time ago, back when this whole thing was still small enough to fit inside a briefcase. I'd never once forgotten it.

Nobody on the observation deck said anything for a long moment once the Object finally resolved out of the dark, close enough now to actually see instead of just track on instruments. It was larger than any of the briefing renders had prepared any of us to feel in our own chests, dark and angular against the deeper dark behind it, catching what little starlight reached this far out into the gap between two stars and throwing almost none of it back.

"That thing looks a lot like the Obelisk!" somebody said, somewhere behind me, awed enough that nobody bothered correcting him, not even the two people on the deck who definitely knew better. It wasn't, of course. The real object from the old film everyone half-remembered was the Monolith, not the Obelisk, an easy slip anyone made under real awe, the kind of misremembering that happens when the actual sight in front of you is too large for careful vocabulary to keep up with.

Nobody corrected him. It didn't seem to matter, standing there, whether anyone got the reference exactly right.

"You think the Builders have a copyright lawyer somewhere," somebody else offered, quieter, the kind of joke that only works when the fear underneath it needs somewhere to go. "Because if that thing's been sitting out here for as long as everyone says, we've been ripping off their design for decades and nobody's paid them a dime."

Nobody quite laughed. A few people came close.

I found Sterling before I found anything else, before Daniel was even part of this story yet, before any of the arrival business that mattered institutionally had even begun to matter to me personally.

"Sterling."

I didn't need to shout it. He was already turning at the flat urgency in my voice, a register he'd learned, over years now, meant something real.

"Grab whatever you need to grab. You've got a seat, if you still want one." I let that land a second before I said the rest of it. "Told you the day we started this you'd have one the moment we actually got here." A beat. "Well. We're here."

Sterling didn't miss a step. "That's exactly why I packed my go-bag months ago!"

I laughed, real and sudden, because of course he had. Of course the man who'd spent so much of his career watching other people take the actual risks from the safety of a console had already had a bag ready and waiting for the exact moment somebody finally told him he didn't have to watch from a distance anymore.

He grabbed it, one hand still half on the console he'd been standing behind for longer than either of us probably wanted to count, and I put my own hand on his shoulder before he could think twice about letting go of it.

We jumped.

Some promises, I've learned, take years to actually pay off. This one had taken most of a decade, and it still felt, watching Sterling's face do what every new arrival's face did the instant the Object actually resolved in front of him instead of on a screen, like exactly the right amount of time for it to have taken.

*

The observation deck thinned out slowly, not all at once, small knots of people peeling off toward duty stations or bunks or just somewhere quiet enough to actually let the sight we'd all just seen finish landing. The ones who stayed drifted into a loose half-circle without anybody arranging it, still turned toward the viewport the way you keep facing a fire even after you've stopped needing the warmth from it.

Cent was one of the ones who stayed. She had her arms crossed, head tilted slightly, studying the Object the same unhurried way she studied most things she hadn't fully made up her mind about yet.

"We can't keep calling it 'the station' forever," she said, to nobody in particular, and then, when a few heads turned toward her anyway, "I mean it. Feels wrong, standing here, still not having a real name for the thing. It deserves better than a string of numbers Sterling's paperwork made up before any of us even knew what we were building toward."

"Hey, that string of numbers took me three tries to get the checksum right on," Sterling said, but he was already turning the actual question over, I could see it in his face, that particular stillness he got right before he landed on something. He was still visibly getting his own legs back under him from the jump, one hand braced on the rail, and it didn't slow him down at all. "Sagan."

Nobody spoke for a second. It wasn't confusion. It was the quiet of a room recognizing something true before anyone's actually said out loud why.

"Sagan," Cent repeated, testing the shape of it the way she tested most things before she let herself trust them. Then, slower, like she was working out loud through exactly why it fit: "The book this whole mission borrowed its own name from borrowed its name from him in the first place. Decades before any of us had a reason to know that would end up mattering this much." She looked back out at the Object, dark and patient against the dark behind it. "There isn't a better fit sitting anywhere else in this room."

"There isn't a better fit sitting anywhere else, period," somebody added, quiet, from further back in the half-circle.

"Sagan Station," Cent said again, and this time it didn't sound like she was testing it anymore. It sounded like she'd already decided. "Yeah. That's it. That's the one." Then, quieter still, something more careful underneath the certainty. "But not officially. Not yet. Not until his own family's actually had a say in it. We don't get to just take a man's name and bolt it to the biggest thing humanity's ever found without asking first. Not this family. Not after everything else we've promised them."

Nobody argued that part either. A few people nodded, slow, like it was less agreement than relief, the sense of a thing being done the right way instead of just the fast way.

"I'll go," Cent said, before anyone could offer to spare her the trip. "Feels like it should be in person anyway."

"Right," I said. There wasn't anything else that needed saying on top of it.
