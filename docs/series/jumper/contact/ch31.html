Chapter 31 of 53

Carter (POV): I'd run the same query four times, and it kept giving me the same answer, which was somehow worse than if it had given me four different ones.

"Say that again," Sterling said, over the comm, patient the way he always was right up until the exact moment he wasn't.

"The Obelisk's own internal chronometer. It's been logging continuously this whole time, we just hadn't had a reason to pull the full activation record until now." I pulled the trace up again anyway, as if a fifth look might change it. "Everyone's been assuming this thing woke up because we showed up. Ancient sentry, dormant for however many thousand years, finally got a visitor worth waking up for. That's not what the log says."

"What does the log say?"

"It says it woke up on its own. No visitor required." I made myself say the number plainly, because there wasn't a version of this that got easier to hear by hedging it. "Six years ago. Our time. Long before Wayfarer ever left dock, let alone before anyone got anywhere near this thing."

The silence on the other end of the comm went on a beat too long.

"Six years," Sterling repeated.

"A density threshold. Photophage concentration somewhere in this thing's own detection range crossed some level it was built to watch for, and it just... turned itself on. Nobody was here to see it happen. It's been sitting here, awake, alone, for six years, watching something we didn't even know was happening yet." I pulled up the second chart, the one that had actually made my hands go cold the first time I'd generated it. "You know what a Petrova line takes to develop? Years, at minimum, before it's even visible from Earth with passive instruments, and that's assuming somebody's actually looking in the right direction with the right equipment already pointed at the right star. We are nowhere close to that yet. If this thing hadn't already been sitting here when we showed up, we wouldn't have found this for years. Maybe a decade. Maybe longer, if nobody happened to be looking."

"So the crisis isn't historical."

"The crisis is happening right now, six years running, and we only just noticed because we happened to fly a family with an unrelated origin story straight into the one thing already watching it." I sat back, letting that finish landing on me the way it clearly needed to. "This isn't Contact finding a warning about something that already happened somewhere else, a long time ago. This is Contact finding out the clock's already running, on something close enough to matter, and it started running before any of us ever left home."

Sterling didn't say anything for a moment. When he did, his voice had gone very quiet.

"Get this to Millie," he said. "Now. Before anything else today."
