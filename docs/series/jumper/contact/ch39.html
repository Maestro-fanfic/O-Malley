Millie (POV): I have learned, since the first time his tendril ever found the edge of my ear, that The One Who Stands never once needs to be told a thing is important. He simply already knows, the same unhurried way he knows most things.

He was waiting for me before I'd finished deciding where to actually look for him, which had stopped surprising me somewhere around the third time it happened.

"You have a question," he said, without preamble, the way he said most things. "I would rather you ask it than carry it onto a jump unasked."

"Daniel thinks the jump trait doesn't just belong to us and the Eridians. He thinks it rides in phage itself, at the root, before any of the branches ever split apart." I made myself say the rest of it plainly, because plain was the only way I trusted myself to get through it without my own voice doing something I didn't want it doing in front of him. "If that's true, and if your own people share that root the way we now know you do. I need to ask you directly. Do the Builders have it too?"

He considered this for exactly as long as he considered most things, which was to say, not performing consideration at all, simply taking the time the question actually required.

"Yes," he said. "This is an ability we also share. And because we understand it so well, a large part of our own technology is built on these very principles." A pause, nothing dramatic in it. "You have been assuming, I think, that you were explaining something to us. You were not. You were confirming something we already knew, from the inside, long before your own species had a word for it."

I sat with that a moment, the actual weight of it settling somewhere behind my sternum. "How long have you known?"

"Longer than I have existed." He tilted his head, the small, familiar gesture that had come to mean something like warmth, coming from him. "I was built with certain memories already intact, the same way I was built with the memory of Prism. There is one more I have never yet had reason to show you. It predates my own creation by a span of time I do not have a comfortable word for in your language. Would you like to see it?"

"Yes." I didn't hesitate over that one at all.

"Please sit."

The tendril found the edge of my ear the way it always did now, familiar enough that the old animal flinch barely registered anymore, and then there was no seam at all, and then there was someone else's hands.

Small hands, four-fingered, working a length of something fibrous into a woven shape, the unhurried patience of a task done ten thousand times before and expected to be done ten thousand times again. A workshop, warm light through a canopy I now recognized without needing to be told, the same crystalline light I'd already stood under once, in the Library's own memory. This body was young. I could feel that the way I could feel my own age in my own bones, some knowledge that didn't need translating.

Something moved at the tree line. Not the phage-light, not yet, this memory sat further back than that one, in a stretch of ordinary time before anything had gone wrong at all. A predator, low and fast, breaking from cover with a silent economy that had already decided this exact moment was the one that would work.

The young one dropped the weaving.

There was no decision in it. That was the part I felt most clearly, borrowed hands finding no time at all between danger and elsewhere, the same nothing under the boots I'd felt on my own first jump, the same wrongness-that-wasn't-wrong, and then the workshop was gone, and there was only a quiet clearing forty paces distant, breath coming fast, hands still curled around a weaving that had, impossibly, come along too.

The predator never reached the tree line at all. It simply lost its meal to a place it couldn't follow.

The memory held there a moment longer, long enough for me to feel the young one's own confusion, wonder, something not unlike fear, and underneath all of it, already, the first dim understanding that whatever had just happened wasn't going to be explainable to anyone who hadn't felt it happen from the inside.

The Connection ended the way it always did, no seam, just me again, on my own knees, breath coming hard.

"That is the earliest memory I carry of it," The One Who Stands said, once I'd found my voice again. "Long before phage was ever spoken of as a warning. Long before the Library's own catastrophe. It was already there, waiting to be needed, exactly the way it apparently waited in your own family's blood, and in Erid's."

"Three separate species," I said slowly. "Three separate worlds. All of us carrying the same thing, waiting the same way, since before any of us knew to call it anything."

"Yes." Simple. Certain, the way he was certain about most things that turned out to matter. "You did not bring us a discovery today, Millie. You brought us confirmation of something none of us had ever had reason to say out loud to each other before. That is not a small thing, even if it is not the thing you expected to be carrying home tonight."

I stood, steadier than I'd expected to be, the case still waiting by the hatch where I'd left it.

"I have one more stop to make before Earth," I said. "But this one, I think, was the one that actually mattered."

"They usually are," he said, "the stops nobody schedules in advance."
