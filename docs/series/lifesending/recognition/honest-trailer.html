Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen opened on a planet under Goa’uld control and a woman who had survived alone for two years without knowing why.

“In a galaxy full of ancient races, false gods, lost technologies, and classified acronyms, one lieutenant will undertake the most dangerous mission of her career.”

Buffy stepped forward toward Shyla.

“Introducing herself.”

This is Life’s Ending, Life’s Beginning: Recognition.

Follow Buffy and SG-1 to P2X-416, where the official plan allows four hours to find Shyla, explain that she belongs to an ancient lineage of supernatural defenders, earn her trust, and extract before Heru’ur’s next patrol.

“Four hours,” the narrator said. “Plenty of time for first contact, trauma counseling, comparative mythology, and a ten-kilometer walk.”

Buffy asks for a contingency.

Jack provides the SGC’s real mission doctrine: stick to the plan until the plan stops working, then improvise.

Buffy recognizes it immediately as the Sunnydale approach, except the SGC has aircraft, radios, and adults who admit that improvising is part of the plan.

Find Shyla. Find the others. Discover the Slayer line seeded across eleven worlds, each culture carrying a different fragment of the same inheritance and almost none of them given enough context to know they are part of something larger.

Watch Buffy become the thing she once needed: the person who arrives from the dark and says, here is what you are, here is why, and you do not have to keep doing it alone.

Then watch that personal rescue become a network, because Buffy cannot encounter one abandoned duty without turning it into infrastructure.

Build contact routes. Build mutual support. Build a decentralized system that does not depend on Earth, the Mountain, or Buffy personally remaining available forever.

“That’s right,” the narrator said. “The Chosen One’s ultimate character development is eliminating the single point of failure called the Chosen One.”

Trace fourteen generations of hidden inheritance. Decode the Builders’ work. Learn that the device is not merely a device and recognition is not merely identification. Something ancient built the line with purpose, distributed it across worlds, and waited long enough to see whether its descendants would find one another without turning connection into control.

Meet the Edjet: the standing opposition, the permanent resistance, the thing that does not yield.

Also known as Buffy Summers, who has finally collected enough job titles to make “Slayer” look like the concise one.

Meanwhile, expose the people on Earth who thought they controlled the future. Dismantle their leverage. Replace acquisition with consent, secrecy with coordination, and central command with a network capable of surviving whatever Ba’al does next.

The screen held on communities across eleven worlds establishing their own links.

The narrator lowered its voice.

“This is the point where a lesser story would reveal Buffy as uniquely important and stop there. This one makes her important because she refuses to remain unique. The mission is not to stand above the others. It is to make sure there are others standing.”

Then watch Ba’al park a ha’tak in the outer system and patiently observe the Mountain, unaware that the thing he intends to stop has already spread beyond any single target.

He thinks Earth is still centralizing.

He is eight hours late.

“For a Goa’uld,” the narrator noted, “that is approximately one eternity of being smug in the wrong direction.”

## The Recognition Drinking Game

Take a drink every time Buffy recognizes her own loneliness in somebody from another planet.

Take two every time “we don’t leave her there” becomes operational policy.

Drink whenever Jack’s improvisation and Buffy’s Sunnydale experience turn out to be the same doctrine with different paperwork.

Take a sip whenever an ancient device communicates something enormous with less ceremony than the humans use to schedule the briefing.

Drink whenever Buffy adds another title without surrendering any previous one.

Do not drink for every world in the network. Decentralization should improve resilience, not destroy your liver across eleven jurisdictions.

## Starring

Buffy Summers as Chosen One, Now Hiring.

Jack O’Neill as Improvisation With a Mission Number.

Samantha Carter as The Contingency Has a Contingency.

Daniel Jackson as Comparative Mythology Finally Gets a Budget.

Teal’c as The Man Who Already Knows False Gods Hate Distributed Systems.

Shyla as Not Alone, Effective Immediately.

George Hammond as This Network No Longer Fits Inside My Mountain.

The Builders as Ancient Mentorship by Extremely Delayed Message.

Ba’al as Watching the Wrong Door.

And the Slayer line as Fourteen Generations of “You Should Have Killed Her Harder”.

Alternate titles appeared:

Slayers Without Borders.

The Sisterhood of the Traveling Gate Address.

Eleven Worlds and One Really Bad Day for Ba’al.

The narrator considered the first.

“Good nonprofit name. Wrong title card.”

The screen resolved.

# RECOGNITION

Directed by: the moment somebody sees you clearly and stays.

Written by: a lineage that was never meant to remain a list of isolated survivors.

Network architecture by: the Chosen One, who objected to the singular noun.

Special thanks to: every person who kept managing alone long enough to be found.

The final image showed Ba’al’s ship watching Earth while eleven worlds remained connected behind it.

“He’s going to find out he waited too long,” Buffy said.

“You’re confident about that?” Jack asked.

“Based on everything.”

The narrator let the titles accumulate across the screen.

The Edjet. The Chosen One. Lieutenant Buffy Summers, USAF.

“All three,” the voice said. “Always all three.”
