Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen showed a C-17, a drop zone, and one parachute failing in a way parachutes are not supposed to fail.

“In a world where Buffy Summers has survived vampires, demons, apocalypses, high school, and the United States Air Force Academy, one enemy will finally discover the perfect way to kill a Slayer.”

The reserve chute dissolved too.

“Gravity.”

Buffy hit the trees at sixty miles an hour, opened her eyes, and asked whether she passed.

“Gravity would like to file an appeal.”

This is Life’s Ending, Life’s Beginning: Freefall.

Watch Lieutenant Buffy Summers turn a double parachute failure into an unusually dramatic entrance exam for everybody trying to decide whether she is human, anomalous, classified, or merely having another Tuesday.

Meet Sergeant Abel, a man with thirty years around parachutes who needs approximately four seconds to determine that both failures were sabotage and considerably longer to process the woman underneath them having a strong, steady pulse.

Meet Jack O’Neill, who reacts to an assassination attempt on Buffy with the protective calm of a man already halfway through identifying which regulation he will technically not violate while destroying the people responsible.

Meet George Hammond, who must manage a Slayer, the SGC, military secrecy, federal conspirators, and Joyce Summers’s entirely justified questions, all while preserving the fiction that his blood pressure remains within Air Force standards.

Follow the evidence from acid-treated parachutes to Simmons, Fort Benning, and a second organization whose name is less important than its mission statement: Managing the Future.

Their plan is to identify anomalous people, acquire the useful ones, and eliminate whatever will not submit.

“It’s the Initiative,” the narrator said, “but with better stationery and somehow less institutional self-awareness.”

Watch Buffy survive a second attempt, expose the pattern, and discover that somebody does not merely want her dead. Somebody else wants her contained, studied, and pointed at future threats.

Which is awkward, because Buffy has spent six years being a weapon that chooses what to aim at and has extremely strong opinions about anybody reaching for the handle.

Witness the Stargate program slowly realize the Slayer line is not just one impossible woman from Sunnydale. There are others across the galaxy: people surviving wounds they should not survive, fighting things nobody assigned them to fight, and living alone without the one sentence Buffy once needed somebody to say to her.

You are not alone.

The narrator let the phrase sit without dressing it up.

“This continuation begins with Buffy falling out of the sky. The real freefall is discovering the conspiracy under her feet, the lineage beyond Earth, and the size of the work waiting when she lands. Yes, that was almost too tidy. No, we’re not taking it back.”

Watch Buffy respond to an organization trying to manage her future by doing the one thing institutions never plan for properly: building her own plan first.

Secure Joyce. Give Davis two weeks. Identify the leadership. Find the others. Reach P2X-416 before the people who think “asset” is a complete description of a human being can decide the next move.

And through all of it, watch Jack and Buffy communicate affection in the shared dialect of clipped operational statements, tactical planning, and using her first name only when the conversation has quietly become important.

When Jack finally says, “You’re doing well,” the screen did not cut away.

The narrator’s voice softened.

“Four words. No speech. No hug. No attempt to fix six years of damage in one scene. Just the right person noticing the work. We’re not riffing on that either.”

## The Freefall Drinking Game

Take a drink every time Buffy survives something that makes an experienced soldier question his understanding of medicine.

Take two whenever somebody uses the word “asset” in a way that proves they should never be trusted with one.

Drink whenever Jack says “yeah” and communicates an entire paragraph.

Take a sip whenever Buffy organizes trauma into operational columns because feeling it will have to wait until after the briefing.

Finish your drink when Joyce is assigned security and immediately becomes the most difficult protectee in Colorado Springs.

Do not drink every time somebody compares the new conspiracy to the Initiative. The federal government has apparently franchised the mistake.

## Starring

Buffy Summers as Airborne, Air Force, and Extremely Difficult to Kill.

Jack O’Neill as Protective Dad Energy, Classified.

George Hammond as The General Who Keeps Having to Add Mythology to the Org Chart.

Sergeant Abel as Gravity’s Most Disappointed Witness.

Major Davis as Two Weeks, Maybe Less.

Joyce Summers as Your Security Detail Would Like You to Stop Arguing.

Simmons as Acid Reign.

And Managing the Future as The Initiative 2: Somehow You Learned Nothing.

Alternate titles appeared:

Chute First, Ask Questions Later.

Buffy the Bureaucracy Slayer.

Terminal Velocity Is a Suggestion.

The voice considered the last one.

“Physics has already taken enough abuse in the other series.”

The title card appeared.

# FREEFALL

Directed by: gravity, unsuccessfully.

Written by: two ruined parachutes and the very bad decision behind them.

Operational planning by: a nineteen-year-old who would like everyone to stop being surprised she read the file.

Special thanks to: Sergeant Abel, for smelling acid and believing his own eyes.

The screen returned to Buffy at the heavy bag.

“The device said find them,” she said. “That wasn’t a suggestion.”

The narrator sighed as P2X-416 appeared on the mission board.

“Of course there’s a sequel.”
