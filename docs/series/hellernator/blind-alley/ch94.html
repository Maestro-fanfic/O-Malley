Turning the Mirror Back Around

"Turn the mirror around occasionally, or you'll forget what it's actually reflecting." — attributed to Angel

Tuesday, November 4, 1997: The Sunnydale Arms, Room 8,
Sunnydale, Early Morning 6:55am —

Faith Lehane hadn't slept, not really, not the kind of sleep that actually rested you, but she'd lain still on the bed for several hours with her eyes closed doing something that at least resembled it, running the same conversation back through her head on a loop she hadn't quite managed to turn off since Buffy Summers left her room the night before.

I don't think that's the same as it being good for you.

She sat up around six, gave up on the pretense of sleep entirely, and looked at the closet door across the room, the mirror on it still turned to face the wall, same as it had been every night since she'd checked into this room.

She got up. Crossed the small space. Stood in front of the turned mirror for a long moment, hand on the frame, not quite committing yet.

Diana Dormer, Buffy had said, gentle, not performing sympathy, just offering the actual name like it deserved to be spoken out loud by someone besides Faith herself. That's not a small thing to be carrying.

Faith turned the mirror around.

Her own reflection looked back at her — tired, a little rough around the edges, but steady, in the way she'd worked hard to look steady even when she didn't feel it, because looking steady had kept her alive plenty of times when actual steadiness hadn't been available.

She looked at herself for a long moment, the way she hadn't let herself look in weeks, and thought about Diana Dormer's specific dry patience, her exact way of saying again, Faith, and this time actually commit to the follow-through during training, the last real conversation they'd had before the thing that took her out had taken her out, six weeks and a lifetime ago now.

She thought about six weeks of operating alone since, telling herself alone was a preference rather than a wound she hadn't let herself name.

She thought about a girl who'd knocked on her door with her hands visible and empty, who hadn't tried to corner her, who'd just offered a thing and let it sit there without pushing, the way you offer something to someone you've decided is worth being patient with.

Faith reached for the room's ancient rotary phone, found the number Buffy had left with the front desk clerk two hours after that visit — Faith had watched her leave it, through the window, hadn't said anything about noticing — and dialed it before she could talk herself out of it, muttering something under her breath about this being the single most pathetic thing she'd ever done, dialing a number like a nervous kid asking someone to the dance, and doing it anyway.

It rang twice.

"Hello?" Joyce Summers's voice, warm, a little distracted, the tone of a woman who was clearly also managing something else happening in her kitchen at seven in the morning.

"Is, uh." Faith cleared her throat. "Is Buffy around? This is Faith."

"Oh, Faith, hello!" Joyce's voice brightened in a way that caught Faith off guard, warm and immediate and without the wary calculation Faith had half-expected from a Slayer's mother evaluating a stranger. "She just stepped out for a run, but she should be back any minute — can I take a message, or would you like to hold?"

"I can hold," Faith said, surprising herself with how easily it came out.

"Wonderful. And Faith—" Joyce paused, and something in her voice went gentler, more deliberate. "There's a meeting Monday. I don't know if Buffy's told you the details yet, but you're welcome, whenever you're ready. No pressure on the timing. I just wanted you to hear it from me too, in case it helps to know it's not just her asking."

Faith sat with the phone against her ear, in the small quiet room with the mirror finally facing the right direction, and felt something in her chest that had been clenched tight for six weeks loosen, just slightly, just enough to notice.

"Yeah," she said, quiet. "Yeah, that — that actually helps a lot, Mrs. Summers. Thank you."

"Joyce," Joyce said, warm. "Please. And here she is now — hold on, dear, I've got Faith on the phone for you."

There was a rustle, a brief muffled exchange, and then Buffy's voice, slightly breathless from the run, surprised and, underneath the surprise, clearly pleased.

"Faith? Hey — everything okay?"

Faith looked at her own steady reflection in the turned-around mirror, and found, for the first time in six weeks, that she actually believed what she was about to say.

"Yeah," she said. "Everything's okay. I've been thinking about what you said. About not doing this alone." A pause, the brave pause of someone committing to a thing out loud before they could talk themselves back out of it. "I think I'd like to come to that meeting. If the offer's still good."

"It's still good," Buffy said, and there was real warmth in it, no performance, no relief-tinged-with-management, just genuine gladness. "It's always going to be good, Faith. Whenever you're ready."

"I'm ready," Faith said, and meant it, and felt, for the first time in six weeks, like maybe alone had never actually been the safest place to be. It had just been the only door she'd known how to open by herself.
