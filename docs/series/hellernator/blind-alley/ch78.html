Paladin and Cahill Come to Call

"Some debts don't get paid back. They get paid forward, to whoever's standing in front of you when you're finally able to." — attributed to Tor Hauer

Tuesday, November 4, 1997: Chestnut Street Office, Sunnydale; Afternoon 4:00pm —

Xander had his clipboard out and his feet up on the corner of his desk when the bell over the door went, and he didn't look up right away, because the bell had been going all afternoon — Board business, mostly, people finding their way to the new office out of curiosity more than necessity.

"Harris."

He looked up at that, because the voice belonged to someone he hadn't heard say his actual last name, flatly, without a locker involved, in longer than he generally let himself think about.

Tor Hauer stood in the doorway in the same black gunfighter's getup he'd apparently been wearing, on and off, since Halloween — hat, low-slung holster, the silver knight emblem — with Heidi a half-step behind him in her own matching black, the Winchester slung easy over one shoulder like it had simply become part of how she carried herself now.

"Tor." Xander didn't take his feet off the desk, which cost him something to manage, old instinct wanting badly to make himself smaller the way it always had around this doorway. "Heidi. This is a surprise."

"Figured it might be." Tor came in, unhurried, and set something on the corner of Xander's desk — an actual silver dollar, old, worn smooth. "That's for the locker, freshman year. The one with your bike lock still on it when I shoved you in. Never did pay you back for the lock."

"You don't have to—"

"I know I don't have to." Tor's voice was different than Xander remembered it — not gentler exactly, but more deliberate, the formal courtesy of a man who'd apparently absorbed rather more of Paladin's actual code than anyone had expected a costume to leave behind. "Paladin's got a saying about debts. Some of 'em don't get paid back to the person you owe 'em to. They get paid forward, to whoever needs it when you're finally able to give it." He nodded at the silver dollar. "That one's small. There's bigger ones I mean to get to."

Heidi settled into the doorway beside him, arms crossed, watching the exchange with the quiet attention of someone who'd clearly had this conversation rehearsed between them beforehand. "We heard you've got a board now," she said. "Heard it's got a name and everything."

"Word travels fast."

"Small town, big Hellmouth," Heidi said. "Word travels the way it always has. Faster now, if anything, since half the kids who went to Ethan's that night still can't put a gun down without their hands remembering how good it feels."

Xander finally took his feet off the desk. "You looking to sign up for something."

"Lookin' to offer," Tor said, correcting him, mild but precise about the distinction. "Difference matters to me. I'm not askin' you for a spot at your table, Harris. I'm telling you Heidi and I have got skills your Board's probably gonna need eventually, and we'd rather you know we're available before you need us bad enough to have to go lookin'."

"We're not the same assholes we were sophomore year," Heidi added, blunter about it than Tor had been, though something in her tone suggested she meant it just as seriously. "Figured that was worth saying out loud, since we never really got around to it properly. Sorry about the lockers. Sorry about a lot of stuff, actually, now that I've got some distance on it."

Xander considered them both for a long moment — the black hat, the Winchester, the formal weight two former bullies had apparently decided to carry now that a Halloween costume had handed them, of all things, an actual code of honor to live up to.

"Apology accepted," he said, finally, and meant it. "And yeah. We're gonna need you eventually. I don't know exactly what for yet, but I've got a corkboard that's gonna have your names on it by tonight, and I think that's actually going to turn out to matter, considering everything else on it."

"Good," Tor said, satisfied, and touched the brim of his hat, an old gesture that had apparently become natural to him. "We'll be around."

"Tell Cordelia we said hey," Heidi added, already turning to go. "Heard she's had one hell of a month. Figured she could use knowing more people are in her corner than she probably thinks."

They left the way they'd come, unhurried, and Xander sat looking at the silver dollar on his desk for a long moment before picking it up and setting it, carefully, in the drawer where he kept the things that actually mattered.
