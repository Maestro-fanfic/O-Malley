The Name Landlorn Gave Them

"A name given freely is a different kind of gift than a name assigned." — attributed to Cassidy, a later record

Wednesday, November 26, 1997: Somewhere in the Never-After, aboard the Retribution,
Landlorn's Cabin, Same non-time —

Landlorn's cabin held more books than Giles had expected from a pirate king, shelves of accumulated centuries lining every wall, and he gestured for them to sit before settling into his own chair with the unhurried gravity of something about to say a true thing it had been carrying a long time.

"It calls itself the Reckoner," Landlorn said. "It is not a demon, precisely. It is closer to what your world might call a debt made manifest — an intention that accumulates weight the way rust accumulates on iron, slow, patient, entirely without hurry, because it has never once needed to hurry."

"Debt for what," Willow asked.

"For every soul the Hellmouth has claimed and never properly accounted for," Landlorn said. "Every death Sunnydale's residents have collectively agreed not to notice, for over a century. Every disappearance filed as a transfer, a runaway, a family relocating suddenly in the night. The town has been quietly, unknowingly building a ledger for a hundred years, and the Reckoner has been patiently waiting for the ledger to come due."

Angel was quiet for a long moment, two centuries of demon-world context settling into place around this new, darker shape. "Willy mentioned something about collecting," he said. "Not conquering. Collecting."

"That is precisely its nature," Landlorn confirmed. "It does not wish to rule your town, Watcher. It wishes to be paid. And when a debt of that particular size and age finally comes due, the payment it seeks is rarely something any of you would willingly offer."

Giles absorbed this with the careful weight of a man calculating exactly how much worse this made an already complicated month. "Can it be stopped? Reasoned with?"

"It can be paid," Landlorn said, simply. "Or it can be refused, and refusal, historically, has not gone well for the towns that attempted it." He looked at them steadily. "You have time. Weeks, perhaps a season, before the boundary thins enough for it to fully manifest. I would not waste that time, were I in your position."

Willow was already thinking ahead, the focused calculation Giles had watched her develop over weeks of careful training. "If it's debt made manifest — maybe there's a way to actually settle the ledger. Properly. Acknowledge everyone the Hellmouth's claimed, formally, instead of just letting the town keep pretending it didn't happen."

Giles looked at her with something like genuine hope breaking through the weight of the revelation. "That," he said, slowly, "may actually be worth exploring."

Landlorn watched the four of them with the patient, ancient interest of something that had clearly been hoping, all along, that Sunnydale's newest formation might prove equal to the challenge his own domain had been quietly warning them about.

"Go home," he said, finally. "Discuss this with your Board. And when you're ready to discuss the actual mechanics of a debt this size — you know where the door is."
