The TX Discovers Tutoring

"Teaching is just choosing, deliberately, to remember what it felt like not to know something yet." — attributed to the TX, private record

Tuesday, March 24, 1998: Sunnydale High School Library,
Sunnydale, Afternoon 3:30pm —

The freshman from December had brought a friend this time, both of them struggling with the same algebra assignment, and the TX found itself, three months into a habit it had never planned to develop, setting aside its current novel to help them work through the problem set with the patient clarity that Harmony's borrowed social calibration had never once produced but the TX's own careful, chosen kindness managed easily.

"You explain it better than Mr. Kessler," the friend said, surprised, working through the third problem with newfound confidence.

"I have found that most confusion in mathematics stems from an unclear initial framework rather than any actual difficulty with the material," the TX said. "Once the framework is clear, the rest generally follows."

"Are you tutoring, like, officially?" the freshman asked. "Because you're actually really good at this."

The TX considered this question carefully. "I had not considered it an official arrangement," it said. "I simply found myself present, and capable, and willing, and the tutoring emerged from those three facts rather than any formal decision."

"You should make it official," the friend said. "There's a tutoring program. You could sign up properly."

The TX turned this over, running it against no threat model at all, simply weighing it as a genuine, freely chosen possibility.

"I believe I would like that," it said, finally, and found, saying it, that the sentence carried a specific weight it had never once associated with Harmony's borrowed life — the weight of a thing chosen for itself, purpose selected rather than assigned.

Giles, passing through the library moments later, found the TX filling out an actual tutoring program registration form with careful, deliberate handwriting, and felt something in his chest, watching it, that he suspected he would carry, warmly, for a very long time.

"Good afternoon," he said.

"Good afternoon, Mr. Giles," the TX said, not looking up from the form. "I am becoming an official tutor. I find I am looking forward to it considerably."

"I'm very glad," Giles said, and meant it entirely.
