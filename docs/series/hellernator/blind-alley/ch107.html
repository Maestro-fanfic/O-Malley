Practicing Being Two People With One Set of Feet

"Partnership works best when both people know exactly what they bring, and neither one apologizes for it."
— Murphy's Laws of Combat

Thursday, November 6, 1997: Sunnydale Cemetery, Restfield,
Sunnydale, Night 11:00pm —

Faith's first patrol with Buffy went better than either of them had privately braced for, in the way that two people who'd spent weeks bracing for friction discover, sometimes, that the friction simply doesn't show up when they finally stop expecting it.

"You always talk this much on patrol?" Faith said, staking a fledgling with the economical efficiency of someone who'd done this a thousand times alone and was still adjusting to doing it with company. "'Cause fair warning, B, I'm used to quiet. Might take me a minute to adjust to Sunnydale's chattiest Slayer."

"Only when the company's good," Buffy said, matching her pace easily. "You're better than I expected, by the way. Not that I expected you to be bad. I just — I've never patrolled with another Slayer before. I didn't know what the rhythm would feel like."

"Better than expected. I'll take it." Faith flashed something quick and sharp, more performance than actual smugness, the specific brightness she reached for whenever a conversation got close enough to something real to need covering. "What can I say, I'm a natural. Some people gotta work at this. I just show up and look good doing it."

Buffy let that sit a beat, unbothered, apparently already fluent in exactly what that particular brand of deflection meant. "Uh huh," she said, easy, not pushing.

They walked in comfortable silence for a stretch, the cemetery quiet around them, and Faith felt the bravado run out of road the way it always eventually did when nobody actually took the bait — found herself, unprompted, filling the quiet with something she hadn't planned on saying at all, the joke nowhere left to hide behind.

"Diana used to patrol with me sometimes," she said, quieter now, all the performance gone out of it. "Not fighting — she was too old for the front line stuff. But she'd walk the perimeter, keep watch, talk me through the read on a situation before I went in swinging." A pause. "I miss that. The talking-through part. I didn't realize how much I missed it till just now."

Buffy didn't say anything immediately, just let the admission sit there, respected, the way Faith had learned, over the last few days, this whole household seemed to instinctively know how to do.

"I can do the talking-through part," Buffy said, finally, quiet. "If you want. Doesn't have to replace what you had with her. Just — doesn't have to be nothing, either."

Faith looked at her for a long moment, something loosening in her chest that had been tight since Diana Dormer's funeral, six weeks and a lifetime ago.

"Yeah," she said. "I'd like that." Then, because the quiet had gone on a beat too long for comfort, the armor came back up, easy and automatic: "Don't go getting used to the sincere version though. She's rationed. I only bust her out on special occasions."

"Duly noted," Buffy said, and didn't call her on it, which Faith appreciated more than she was anywhere close to ready to say out loud.

They finished the patrol together, two Slayers walking the same rhythm for the first time either of them had ever gotten to experience, and Faith thought, distantly, that maybe alone had never actually been a policy worth keeping quite so tightly.
