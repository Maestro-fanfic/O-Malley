Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

The screen opened on the morning after graduation. The sun rose normally, apparently unaware that the previous afternoon had included an Ascension, an evacuation, and significant damage to school property.

“After eight months building a supernatural war council to defeat the Mayor, the heroes of Sunnydale face their most unfamiliar challenge yet.”

Buffy looked around the quiet house.

“Wednesday.”

This is The Hell-er-Nator IV: Hard Reset.

Watch the Board discover that surviving a climax does not cancel the rest of your life. Summer begins. College approaches. Jobs become real. Relationships continue. Trauma declines to respect chapter boundaries.

And Dawn opens a new notebook because apparently one hundred sixty-seven chapters of municipal supernatural history were only Volume One of the minutes.

Follow Buffy through ordinary mornings that remain extraordinary mostly because ordinary is now a choice she has enough safety to make.

Follow Cordelia from crisis logistics toward community-college crisis logistics, proving there is an accredited program for the skill set Sunnydale forced her to invent barefoot.

Follow Xander into a salaried role as Board Logistics Coordinator, the inevitable career outcome for a teenager who used a clipboard as both office supply and emotional support object.

Follow Faith into a night class, where a B-plus means more than half the victories she knows how to brag about and is therefore much harder to discuss.

Her swagger arrives first. The truth slips out underneath. The armor goes back up before anybody can make the moment embarrassing.

“That,” the narrator said, “is not inconsistent characterization. That is the character.”

Watch Kendra and Wesley help drag the Watchers’ Council toward reform, one letter, report, vote, paternal disappointment, and precisely controlled Jamaican dialect marker at a time.

Meet Cassidy, permanently stabilized and invited to decide what unlimited time might be for.

Visit the Threshold, a legitimate nightclub run by people whose definition of “security” includes supernatural immortals, cosmic scouts, and a customer-service policy that probably has to specify whether dismemberment voids the tab.

Watch Peter Pan expand the Lost Boys’ alliance into actively finding children at risk, because growth in this series means changing what the dangerous immortal says, not sanding the danger out of how he says it.

Watch Angel paint. Watch Seven finish a music box. Watch the T-X select a hat for no functional reason. Watch people choose hobbies, classes, relationships, and names while a story called Hard Reset refuses to reset any of them back to who they were before Halloween.

“The title is a trap,” the narrator said. “The reset is not erasing the year. It is finally booting into the life they built during it.”

The reel turned to Faith in Boston, fifteen years old, Diana Dormer dead, Kakistos behind her, and four hundred miles of running ahead.

The gallery went quiet.

“The book gives the backstory a real scene instead of using it as shorthand for why Faith is difficult. It lets her run. It lets the cost remain hers. No joke here.”

Then return to Sunnydale, where the Council vote passes narrowly, Roger Wyndam-Pryce abstains instead of opposing, Wesley’s temporary posting begins to look permanent, and the old doctrine discovers that three active Slayers with families, choices, and institutional allies are much harder to reduce to a handbook paragraph.

One year after Halloween, everybody gathers without transformative costumes and tells the true thing instead of the nice thing.

Kendra’s worst night and best turning point were the same night.

Cordelia paid a debt and still carries its weight.

Willow releases borrowed fear.

Faith reports the B-plus.

The T-X, wearing a chosen hat, describes a year spent learning that wanting is possible and worth protecting.

Dawn writes down that the true thing was good enough by itself.

## The Hard Reset Drinking Game

Take a drink whenever somebody starts an honest sentence with a defense mechanism.

Take two when Faith’s joke runs out of road and the real thing appears underneath it.

Drink whenever Dawn decides an event belongs in the record.

Take a sip every time an immortal, android, Slayer, or Watcher tries something for no reason beyond wanting to.

Drink whenever Council reform requires one more formal report.

Do not drink every time a character says “choice” or “choose.” By Halloween you will have made no choices of your own for several hours.

## Starring

Buffy Summers as The Day After Tomorrow Was Just Wednesday.

Xander Harris as Coordinator Harris: Clipboard With Benefits.

Cordelia Chase as Community College, Crisis Logistics Major.

Faith Lehane as B-Plus the Vampire Slayer.

Kendra Young as Dear Council: De Doctrine Is Wrong.

Wesley Wyndam-Pryce as Temporary Liaison, Permanent Family Problem.

The T-X as Hat.exe Successfully Installed.

Cassidy as Immortality’s First Career Counselor Appointment.

Angel as The Brooding Period, Now in Oils.

Dawn as Previously on Everyone’s Emotional Development.

The Threshold as Cheers, but the Regulars Can End Civilization.

And the Watchers’ Council as Legacy Software Pending Mandatory Update.

Alternate titles appeared:

Everybody Lives, Then Has to Keep Living.

Ordinary Wednesdays of the Apocalypse.

The Board: Continuing Education.

The narrator shook its head.

“The real title already contains a computer joke and a character thesis. Keep it.”

The card appeared.

# HARD RESET

Directed by: the morning after.

Written by: one hundred fifty-eight chapters of people deciding what survival is for.

Minutes recorded by: Dawn Summers, who objects to the word “minutes” on historiographical grounds.

Special thanks to: the T-X’s hat, chosen for no functional reason and therefore functioning perfectly.

The final screen showed Dawn closing the notebook on November first.

Book Four is over. Not because anything ended. Because everything that needed choosing got chosen.

“What happens now?” she asked.

Giles looked around the breakfast table.

“We continue.”

The narrator sighed as a Book Five teaser began loading.

“Of course you do.”
