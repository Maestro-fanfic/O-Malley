Clearing the Room

The diner Jack picked was three towns over, which he claimed was for security reasons and Buffy privately suspected was actually because he'd once had a slice of pie there, back in 1997, that he still talked about, on the rare occasions the subject came up, like it had been something close to a religious experience.

"I love coming here," he said, sliding into the booth across from her with the specific, unguarded delight of a man who had been looking forward to this all week, maybe longer. "They have the best pie."

"You said that the last three times we drove past a diner together."

"Because it's been true the last three times, and I stand by every single one of those statements." He flagged down a waitress who took his order without so much as a raised eyebrow at the second, silent occupant of the booth: Jack had explained, the first time, with the breezy confidence of a man who'd had considerably longer than most to get used to the sight, that Buffy simply wasn't ordering today, and left it at that, and the diner staff had apparently long since learned not to ask questions about anything O'Neill brought through their door.

Neither of them brought up that first parking-lot introduction anymore, the pointing and the there's two of you doing this — it had become, over the weeks since, simply a thing that had happened once, loudly, and then never needed repeating, which she'd come to understand was his own particular way of saying it hadn't changed a single thing that actually mattered to him.

Once she'd walked away toward the kitchen, he folded his hands together on the table and looked at Buffy with an entirely different kind of attention than he'd been wearing a moment ago, the kind that made her sit up a little straighter in the booth without quite meaning to.

"You clear rooms," he said, no preamble at all.

"What?"

"You clear rooms. You did it walking in here just now: checked both exits, clocked the guy sitting by the register, picked the seat with your back to the wall before I'd even fully sat down myself. You've been doing it since the Jumper first picked us up off the X-301, actually, I just didn't say anything about it until now, wanted to watch the pattern a while longer first." He said it plainly, the way he'd apparently decided, at some point in the last several months, to just say most things that mattered. "That's not Slayer instinct, near as I can tell. I've seen Slayer instinct plenty of times by now. It's fast, physical, reactive, all adrenaline and muscle. This is slower. Trained. Procedural, almost bureaucratic in how careful it is. Somebody taught you to do that, or something did, and I don't think it was patrol duty that taught you."

Buffy felt the old reflex rise up, fast and easy, some version of maybe I just watch a lot of movies halfway assembled behind her sealed visor, and found, somewhere in the last several months of slowly getting used to carrying more than just herself, that she didn't want to reach for it this time.

He's gonna figure it out anyway, said a voice only she could hear, wry and a little resigned, unmistakably Mike's. Guy's sharp. I'd just tell him.

You could help, Buffy thought back, dry, instead of just narrating my own decision-making process at me. A beat, and something more serious crept into it. Is that you, by the way? The room thing. The exits, the seat, all of it.

...Yeah. Something rueful in the admission, almost apologetic. Old habit. Spent a long stretch making sure a room stayed clear for somebody who couldn't clear it herself. Guess it just never actually clocked out.

Sorry, sorry, Mike added, a beat later, when Buffy didn't say anything right away. Just saying, from experience, guys like that don't stop pulling on a thread once they've found the end of it. Might as well hand it to him clean.

"I'm not gonna hide it," Buffy said, out loud, to Jack. "There's someone else in here. A person, not just an instinct. His name's Mike. The room thing, that's him. Says it's an old habit, from keeping someone safe who couldn't do it herself."

Oh, we're doing full names now? Okay. Hi, Colonel. This is weird for me too, for the record.

"Mike," Jack repeated, testing the word, something in his face working through several reactions at once before settling on careful, genuine interest. "Not a character. An actual guy."

"An actual guy. He was just... playing a game, apparently, before any of this happened to him. Some game the whole Artemis thing is based on. And then it reached out and grabbed him, the same way Halloween grabbed me, except it happened to him first, somewhere else, in some completely different mess I don't fully understand yet. And now he's stuck in here too. With me. Being polite about it, mostly, which I actually really appreciate, considering the circumstances."

I try, Mike said, and Buffy could hear, even without a face to read it on, something like a wince behind the words. For what it's worth, sir — this is not how I pictured meeting a colonel. If I'd known, I'd have, I don't know, worn a better shirt. Metaphorically. I don't currently have a shirt situation to speak of.

Buffy huffed out something that wasn't quite a laugh, quiet enough that Jack tilted his head, curious.

"He says hi," she said, by way of explanation. "And that he's sorry this isn't how he pictured meeting you."

"Tell him the feeling's mutual, and that he's got surprisingly good manners for a guy who apparently got kidnapped by a video game." Something in Jack's expression had gone properly warm now, delighted in the specific way he got delighted by anything sufficiently strange and sufficiently sincere. "Does he talk to you a lot? Or is this a special-occasion kind of thing?"

"Mostly when it matters," Buffy said, after checking. That about right?

Yeah. I try not to just... sit there running commentary all day. Didn't feel right, at first, being that loud in someone else's head. Still doesn't, some days. But when it counts, yeah. I show up.

"He tries not to be too loud about it," Buffy relayed. "Says it didn't feel right, being that present in someone else's life, uninvited."

"Good instinct," Jack said, and meant it. "Tell him he's welcome here anyway. For what that's worth, coming from me."

Tell him thank you, Mike said, quiet now, something in it that Buffy recognized, by this point, as the particular shape of a man who hadn't expected to be told that in a very long time. That actually means a lot. More than he probably realizes. A beat. Can I just... say it myself? If that's okay. Feels like it should come from me, not secondhand.

Buffy let go, just slightly, of whatever quiet effort it took to keep her own voice entirely her own.

"Thank you." The word came out in her own voice, shaped by someone else entirely, something in the cadence of it faintly, unmistakably not hers.

Jack's eyebrows went up, fast. "Oh, you can talk through her mouth too? How... convenient." Not quite an accusation. Close enough to one that Buffy felt the air in the booth shift.

"With permission," Buffy said, before Mike could answer for himself, some old protective instinct of her own kicking in without her quite choosing it. "Every time. He asks first. Every single time."

Jack studied her a long moment, weighing it, and whatever he found in her face seemed to settle something in his own. "Okay," he said, easier now. "Just wanted to hear you say that part out loud."

I learned that rule the hard way, Mike said, quiet, unprompted. Second time this ever happened to me, I woke up inside a girl who could take a body apart and put it back together again with her bare hands, and the one thing she feared more than anything else on earth was doing exactly that to someone without asking first. Watched her carry that fear around every day, for a long while. Decided pretty early I never wanted to be the reason anybody else felt it.

"That's from Amy," Buffy said, quieter now, something gentler under it. "He learned it living inside someone who was terrified of doing this exact thing without permission. Didn't ever want to be the reason somebody else felt that."

Jack was quiet a moment, something in his face settling the rest of the way. "Good rule," he said, simply, and let it be enough, before the conversation moved on to the rest of what he'd actually called her out here to say.

"That tracks, for what it's worth," Jack said, once the moment had settled, leaning back slightly, considering it properly. "Close-protection instincts don't just switch off because the job's technically over. You don't clear a room the way you did walking in here unless keeping someone specific safe used to be your entire job, day in and day out, long enough that your body forgot how to stop." He was quiet a moment, turning something over privately. "You want my honest opinion on it? I think Mike's a net positive. Whatever he's carrying, it's not trying to hurt anybody, near as I can tell. It's trying to keep people alive. That's a genuinely good thing to have picked up, secondhand or not, and now you actually get to thank the guy directly for it, which is more than most people in your position ever get."

Buffy considered that, feeling something in her chest loosen slightly — not resolved, not fully, but noticeably less alone than it had felt an hour ago, sitting in a car on the drive out here.

"So what do I actually do with it?"

"You use it," Jack said, simply, like it was the most obvious thing in the world. "You don't fight the instinct, you sharpen it instead, same as you would with any other tool somebody handed you. And..." He paused, something shifting behind his eyes, the look of a man deciding, in real time, to actually offer help instead of just observing that help was needed. "There's something out there I think you're gonna want to go find, when you're ready. Crashed ship, matches some old signal profiles we picked up a while back that never made proper sense to anyone at SGC at the time. Wasn't ours. Wasn't anybody's we could ever identify, as far as we could tell from the readings. I can get you the coordinates, no problem. I can't come with you personally, got my own plate pretty full these days, but I can point you toward the door, at least."

"Why not come with me?"

Jack considered the question with more real seriousness than she'd expected him to give it. "Because I think this one's yours to walk through alone," he said, finally. "Some things you figure out better with company, no question about that. This doesn't strike me as one of those things, though. This one feels like it wants you standing there by yourself when you open it." He caught the waitress's eye as she approached with two plates balanced expertly on one arm. "Also — pie."

Buffy laughed again, real and warm this time, and let the moment be as small and unhurried and good as it wanted to be, before whatever came next arrived to complicate it.

✳

The coordinates arrived by encrypted text two days later, no explanation attached beyond a single line: Take your time. It'll still be there.

Buffy stared at the message a long while before she finally wrote back: thank you. for all of it, not just the coordinates.

Anytime, kid, came the reply, almost immediately, like he'd been sitting there waiting for her to say something. Go easy on yourself out there. Whatever you find.

She didn't answer that one right away. She sat with it instead, turning it over the way Jack himself seemed to turn most things over, and found, somewhere in the quiet of her own bedroom with the coordinates glowing on her phone screen, that she was genuinely ready to go looking.
