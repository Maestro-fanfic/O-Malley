Chapter 15 (6 of 54 in this book)

Honor's contact responded in forty-seven minutes.

The signal came back in the clipped encoded format of a military vessel responding to an emergency beacon, and it was only when Scotty had run it through the pinnace's decoder and stared at the result for a moment that his face did something she hadn't seen it do before, which was a particular species of relief that was also something else she couldn't name.

"What?" she said.

"It's the Prince Adrian's senior surviving officer," he said. "She's been looking for us."

"How long?"

"Three weeks." He looked at the signal readout. "She's been running a search grid." A pause. "There are other ships with her."

The something else she couldn't name on his face resolved itself. She thought it was the specific emotion of finding out that the world had been looking for you when you'd been operating as though the world had moved on.

She took that in without comment and let him have it.

~~~

The pickup took six hours.

During those six hours the garrison got their communications back, as projected, and sent a signal that was received by several parties including the incoming rescue force, which turned out to be three Manticoran vessels operating under a commander who had been conducting an extended search and who received the garrison's claims about escaped prisoners and the destruction of garrison property with the professional neutrality of someone making note of information they planned to address at the appropriate time and place.

The appropriate time and place not being right now.

The shuttle's thruster gave out completely at the four-hour mark, which meant redistributing everyone to the pinnace and the lead shuttle per Honor's contingency plan and leaving the second shuttle in the elliptical orbit that Ibanez had set, Ibanez himself being the last person off and managing this transition with the careful dignity of a man saying goodbye to something he'd been responsible for, which in a sense he had been.

She watched him close the shuttle's hatch from the outside for the last time.

She thought about Sunnydale. About the things you left behind when the contingency plan executed and there was no going back for them.

She went and stood beside him while he was watching the shuttle recede on the cameras.

He was nineteen and from a Grayson town she couldn't pronounce and had never intended to end up on a prison planet, and none of that was visible on his face right now, which was doing what Grayson faces did when Grayson men felt things they'd been taught not to put where others could see them.

She didn't say anything. She just stood next to him while the shuttle drifted.

After a while he said, quietly, "My grandfather's picture was in my kit bag."

"I'm sorry," she said.

"It's just a picture."

"I know. It's still the picture."

He was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "Thank you, Lady Buffy. For getting us out."

She thought about the things she could say in response to that. She thought about the things she actually believed.

"You did most of it," she said. "I just moved faster."

~~~

The rescue ships were called the Wayfarer, the HMS Mordred, and a third vessel whose name she couldn't parse from the signal that turned out to be a converted freighter with a Grayson crew and a name in the Grayson theological tradition that Carson said meant approximately Steadfast in Grace.

She thought that was a good name for a ship that had spent three weeks searching.

They were received aboard the Wayfarer first, the largest of the three, and the process of coming aboard a Manticoran warship for the first time was so thoroughly overwhelming that she simply turned off the parts of her brain that were trying to catalog and contextualize everything and moved through it on instinct, following where she was led, answering the questions she was asked in the brief direct fashion that medical intake procedures seemed to prefer regardless of what century you were in.

Fritz materialized at her elbow at the medical intake point with the focused expression of a man who had been waiting for exactly this moment for several weeks. "Left shoulder," he said, without preamble.

"It's healed."

"I'd like to confirm that independently."

"Fritz."

"Lady Buffy." He held her gaze with the steady professional courtesy that characterized all of his interactions with her, the courtesy that existed in a separate compartment from his curiosity and therefore never felt contaminated by it. "It will take ten minutes. The information is important for your long-term care."

She thought about arguing. She thought about how much she'd appreciated having someone who paid attention to her as a patient rather than as a category.

"Ten minutes," she said.

It took twelve. He confirmed the wound was fully closed and made notes she didn't try to see and then he looked up and said, "You should eat. You've been running high demand for several hours and the caloric debt is real even if the symptoms aren't obvious yet."

She thought about the last time she'd eaten. "When did I eat?"

"Your last meal before the operation was approximately nine hours ago." He said it in the tone of someone who had been tracking this and was unsurprised by her inability to recall it. "I'll arrange something."

"You don't have to�"

"I'm aware I don't have to." He began making the note. "I'm choosing to."

She looked at him. He looked at his notes.

"Fritz," she said.

He looked up.

"Thank you." She meant it in the way she'd meant it with Honor, the way that had weight. "For all of it. The paying attention."

He was quiet for a moment. Then: "You're a remarkable patient. In the clinical sense." A pause. "In the other sense as well, I think, though that's less within my professional purview."

She smiled. She thought it was maybe the first fully unguarded smile she'd produced since arriving in this century. It felt like something returning from a long distance.

He went to arrange food. She went to find the others.

~~~

The reunion happened in a wardroom on deck six.

She stood in the doorway for a moment before entering, watching it unfold, not because she was excluded but because she wasn't yet part of the specific vocabulary of this reunion and she knew it and respected the knowledge.

Honor and the Wayfarer's commander, a woman with iron-gray hair and twenty years of shared history in the way she crossed the wardroom and took Honor's hands. McKeon and the Mordred's captain, who had apparently served together somewhere and were doing the thing where you registered that the other person was alive and intact with the specific thoroughness of people who had been uncertain about it for a while. Carson and Ibanez and two others from LaFollet's team being met by a rating who was very young and very visibly relieved in the way that only people waiting for specific people could be.

Martingale, sitting slightly to the side with a mug of something hot and the expression of someone who was still completing the transition from four months of captivity to this, to a wardroom with carpeting and decent lighting and people who were glad to see them.

LaFollet, standing near the door on the other side from Buffy with his arm in a field dressing and his expression doing the Grayson thing, which was covering whatever was most present with the professional presentation until the professional presentation became its own kind of honesty.

She looked at him. He looked at her.

"Ensign," she said.

"Lady Buffy." He looked at her left shoulder. "You were hit."

"So were you."

"My duty is your protection. Yours is not my protection."

"That's an interesting way to frame a situation where I'm the one with the healing factor." She looked at the dressing on his arm. "How's the arm?"

"Functional."

"Fritz should look at it."

"Surgeon-Lieutenant Martingale already looked at it."

"And?"

"She said it would heal without complication." He paused. "She was considerably more certain about your shoulder's healing timeline than mine."

"Different biology." She looked at him steadily. "Andrew. You did the job tonight. Everyone got out."

Something moved behind his eyes. "Ensign Clinkscales�"

"Has a graze. Which he is going to recover from completely." She held his gaze. "You brought everyone out. That counts."

He was quiet for a moment. Then he did a thing she hadn't seen him do before, which was that the professional presentation relaxed by approximately two degrees, just enough to let her see what was underneath, which was exhaustion and relief in roughly equal measure.

"Yes," he said. "It does."

She went into the wardroom.

Carson found her before she found him, appearing at her side with his arm in a sling and his hair doing something that suggested the operation had not allowed for grooming, and said, "Lady Buffy," in a voice that was doing its level best to be even and not entirely succeeding.

"You got grazed," she said.

"I'm aware."

"Next time you're in a perimeter withdrawal�"

"I know. I was moving and I was watching the wrong angle." He said it with the directness of someone who had already done the self-debrief. "I know what I did wrong."

She looked at him. The arm. The face underneath the arm. The specific quality of a seventeen-year-old boy who had been in a firefight and come out the other side and was now standing in a wardroom on a Manticoran warship doing the inventory of what that meant.

"Carson," she said.

He looked at her.

"You're okay," she said. Not as reassurance. As information.

He took a breath. "Yes," he said. "I am."

She put her hand on his good shoulder briefly, the way you touched someone when words were doing all they could and the rest needed to be something more direct, and then she let go and they stood in the wardroom together while the reunion finished itself around them.

Scotty found her near the end, when the wardroom had mostly sorted itself into smaller conversations and someone had produced food from somewhere that was not a ration pack and the collective emotional temperature had come down from acute to something more sustainable.

He stood beside her with a plate of something she couldn't identify but which smelled considerably better than the past three weeks had smelled.

"Eat," he said.

"Fritz already told me that."

"Fritz is right."

She took the plate. She ate. The thing she couldn't identify turned out to be some variety of protein in a sauce that was not complicated but was genuinely hot and seasoned and her body's response to it was essentially a standing ovation from the parts that had been running on ration packs and adrenaline and healing-factor fuel demands for a month.

"Good?" he asked.

"Really good." She looked at the plate. "What is this?"

"Officer's mess version of a stew. Wayfarer's cook is apparently exceptional by warship standards."

"What are warship standards usually?"

"Functional." He paused. "This is better than functional."

She ate. He ate. Around them the wardroom held the specific warmth of people who had been through something together and were now on the other side of it, in a room with carpeting and decent lighting and food that was better than functional.

She thought about the after.

She thought about what Scotty had said in the pinnace, the offering he'd made that he knew wasn't fully informed and had made anyway. She thought about what it meant to accept something like that and what accepting it would cost and what refusing it would cost and which cost she was more willing to pay.

She thought about two thousand years and the stars and the Slayer's lineage running through her like a current and Carson saying she would see Grayson with a certainty that was not bravado.

She thought about Joyce Summers in the lit window of a house she would never see again and she let that sit and she breathed through it and she let it become background the way she'd learned to let the necessary things become background, present and real and not the loudest thing in the room.

The loudest thing in the room was this. The wardroom and the people in it and the food and the specific quality of the air on a Manticoran warship which was different from any air she'd breathed in weeks and which meant something, the physicality of it, the presence in a new space.

A new space.

She looked around the wardroom. At Honor and the iron-haired commander. At McKeon. At Martingale, who had nearly finished her mug of something and was beginning to look like a person who might sleep tonight. At Carson and Ibanez and LaFollet. At Scotty, who was looking at her without pretending he wasn't.

She thought: this is what after looks like.

It didn't look like any particular thing. It looked like a wardroom and stew and people who were tired and relieved and alive. It looked like the next thing, which was already present even before the current thing was fully finished, the way the next thing always was.

She'd been seventeen for a long time.

She thought it might be time to start the process of becoming eighteen.

"Hey," she said.

Scotty looked at her.

"Tell me about Manticore," she said. "What it looks like. Start from the beginning."

He looked at her for a moment with that expression she'd spent weeks learning to read, the one that received things without making a production of the receiving. Then he settled back in his chair and began.

She listened.

The wardroom held its warmth around them and outside the viewport the stars were all the wrong constellations and entirely present and she was here, in this specific place, in this specific moment, and that was enough.

That was, it turned out, quite a lot.
