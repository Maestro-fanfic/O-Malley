The fifth month she spent on Sphinx.

Not the whole month. Most of it. The specific decision to be on the property and in the garden and in the life that had been built there for most of the last month before the last month, when she'd said she was going to think about it properly.

She ran in the mornings through the Sphinx landscape, which was the landscape she knew best now after four years of running through it in every season. She ran and Vigil ran with her in the canopy, the bond's ambient quality a constant, and the morning air was the Sphinx morning air that she would know anywhere, that had become so specific to her body's knowledge that she thought she could find it in the dark.

She was in the garden one afternoon when the treecat did something unexpected.

Vigil came down. Not to the low branch, the approach branch. All the way down. To the ground, which treecats didn't do casually, the specific deliberateness of something that had decided the communication required a different proximity.

She held still.

Vigil crossed the garden.

It came to where she was standing and sat down in front of her and looked up at her with the green eyes that saw clearly and without apology.

She looked back.

The bond's quality was the quality it had when Vigil was communicating something significant, more directed than the ambient warmth, more specific in its character.

What she felt through the bond was not what she'd expected.

She'd been expecting something about the question. About the door and the decision and what Vigil had felt in her when it had surfaced the grief three years ago.

What she felt instead was simpler than that.

Vigil was showing her what Vigil felt about her.

Not as an assessment or a communication about the question. Just�what was there, in the bond, from the treecat's side of it. The specific quality of a consciousness that had been alongside her for three years and had accumulated what that time had produced.

It was a large thing.

She held it.

She thought about Honor and Nimitz and what Honor had described about the bond, the knowledge of being fully seen and not alone in the seeing.

She thought about what it meant to be fully seen by something that didn't have the capacity to see you other than truly, that had no investment in the version of you that was easier to see and therefore only ever saw the version that was there.

She crouched down so she was at Vigil's level.

Vigil looked at her.

She said: "I know."

She said it the way you said things you meant to the people who couldn't misunderstand you.

Vigil put its true-hand on her knee briefly. The specific communication of the touch. Then withdrew it.

She stayed crouched in the garden for a long time after Vigil had gone back up into the canopy. The afternoon was doing what Sphinx afternoons did in the late autumn, the specific low quality of the light, and the garden was around her in its established quiet and she was here, in it.

She thought about Vigil showing her what was there from the bond's other side.

She thought about what she felt in return, from her side, the thing that had been building since the winter morning three years ago when Vigil had come to the low branch in the snow.

She thought: this is the other thing I'm afraid of.

She thought it honestly, without the management.

Vigil. The bond. The specific irreplaceable quality of what had built itself between them, the thing that didn't translate, that was itself and only itself, that she would not have when she went through the portal.

The bond would not work across the displacement. The physics of what the bond was and the physics of what the portal did were not compatible. She'd thought about this and hadn't said it directly to anyone because saying it directly required acknowledging what it cost and acknowledging what it cost required being with the cost rather than around it.

She was being with the cost.

It was the largest single cost in the decision.

Not Scotty, who had Scotty's quality of groundedness, who would hold what they had while she was gone in the specific way that Scotty held things. Not the Vigil, which she'd built to hold itself. Not the life on Manticore and in Sphinx, which was real and established and would be there.

Vigil, and what was in the bond, and the fact that the bond was with this specific treecat and this specific place and what the portal would do to that.

She sat with the cost.

She didn't try to manage it or minimize it or place it in context against the larger considerations. She just sat with it and let it be the size it was.

Then she looked up at the canopy.

She said: "I'm not going forever."

Vigil's presence through the bond shifted slightly.

She said: "I'm coming back. Whatever it costs to come back, I'm doing it." She held the presence. "I need you to know that."

Vigil made the sound she'd come to associate with it receiving something and holding it, the low note that was its own kind of acknowledgment.

She said: "I don't know what the bond does while I'm displaced. I don't know whether it stays or whether it�" she stopped. "I don't know. But I know I'm coming back to where you are. Whatever else happens, that's true."

The bond's quality shifted again.

Not to the ambient warmth. To something different. Something she didn't have a precise word for but that had the quality of the treecat making a decision about something it had been considering for a while.

She waited.

Vigil came back down from the canopy.

All the way down again.

It came to her and this time it didn't stop at the distance it had stopped at before. It came closer, the specific closer of something that had decided the distance was no longer the right distance, and it settled against her side with the warm weight of a creature that had made up its mind about where it was going to be.

She held very still.

The bond's quality was something she didn't have language for.

She put her hand on Vigil's side, carefully, and Vigil allowed it and the bond was full in the specific way of things that were complete rather than full in the way of things that were overflowing.

She sat in the garden with Vigil against her side and the autumn doing its last things and the wrong stars coming in through the canopy gaps and the whole of what was here around her.

She thought: this is worth coming back for.

She thought: all of it is worth coming back for.

She thought about what she was going to find on Old Earth. She thought about what two thousand years had made of the place she came from. She thought about the Hellmouth, dormant in the interface, and what looking at it directly would do, what closing what needed closing meant.

She thought about her mother's house on Revello Drive. She thought about whether it still existed and what existed where it had been and what that was going to feel like to see.

She thought about the Vigil's work in this century and about what the long work needed and about what intelligence the other end of the displacement might produce, the view of the interface's history from the source rather than from its effects two thousand years downstream.

She thought about coming back.

She thought about Vigil, against her side, warm and real, the bond's quality doing what it was doing.

She thought: I'm choosing to come back.

Not because the alternative was impossible. Not because the other end of the portal was certainly worse. Because this was where she was choosing to be. Because the life and the people and the work and the treecat against her side were where she was choosing, and choice was the thing the Vigil had been built on from the beginning, the thing the old Council had taken away, and she was not going to make the decision unconsciously.

She was choosing.

She was going through, and she was choosing to come back, and the choosing was the thing that made the going something other than flight.

The autumn evening settled around her and Vigil was warm against her side and she sat in the garden of the property she'd been building for four years and let the decision arrive, not all at once, but the way things arrived when they were ready, gradually and then complete.

She thought: yes.

She thought: this is what I'm going to do.

She thought: I'm ready.

She scratched gently behind Vigil's ears and Vigil made the low sound and the garden held them both.

The last month.

She spent it exactly as she'd said she would, thinking about it properly.

Not to the exclusion of everything else. The work continued. The Vigil continued. The life continued. But alongside all of that, the deliberate thinking, the working through of the question from every direction until she'd seen every side of it and there was nothing left to see.

She talked to Haverstock about the physics.

The physics was solid. The window was confirmed. The presences had communicated their readiness through the channels, the Constant's quality shifting in a way she'd learned to read as fully prepared, Witness similarly, the others she'd met in the intervening years all aligned.

She talked to Fritz about the biology.

The healing factor, the channel, the modification�all of them would make the transit, he believed, in the same way they'd survived the original displacement. The biology was hers and it went where she went.

She talked to Allison about what she might find on the biological side at the other end.

Allison said: "Two thousand years of the lineage on Earth. In isolation, potentially, or potentially not�we don't know what institutional memory survived in two thousand years of human civilization." She paused. "But the modification persisted here. It should have persisted there. The people who carry it on Earth exist." She paused. "They've been existing without us for two thousand years."

She thought about that. The lineage on Old Earth, the origin point, the place where the first Slayer had been made and from which everything had spread.

She said: "If I can make contact. If the Vigil's knowledge can reach them�"

Allison said: "That's the operational value." She held Buffy's gaze. "But you know what the operational value is. I think the other value is what you're actually going to Old Earth for."

She held Allison's gaze.

She said: "Closing what needs closing."

Allison said: "Yes." She paused. "What needs closing?"

She thought about it.

She said: "I left in the middle of something. Not just the battle. The�" she looked for the word� "the arc of my life there. I was seventeen and I'd been the Slayer for two years and everything I'd built was there and I was in the middle of it and then I wasn't." She paused. "I've built a new life here and it's real and it's mine. But the arc of the other life is still�" she stopped. "Interrupted. I want to see the end of the interruption. I want to see what became of the place." She paused. "And I want to put something there. For them. The lineage on Earth. Whatever's left of them." She paused. "The knowledge that the Vigil exists. That there are people in this part of space who know what they are and are doing the long work." She paused. "I want to give them that before I come back."

Allison held her gaze for a long time.

She said: "You're going to be remarkable when you're forty."

She said: "I intend to find out."

Allison almost smiled.

She talked to Carson.

They sat in the archive room that the Vigil had developed, the proper one with the physical documents and the digital archive and the Clinkscales records and everything that had been collected and organized over four years of institutional work, and they talked for a long time about what the archive needed to be.

He said: "I want you to know that the record is complete. Everything you've told me is in it. The document you've been writing will be integrated. The first Clinkscales entry and the most recent entry are the same arc." He held her gaze. "Whoever reads this in a hundred years or two hundred years is going to have the full picture."

She said: "That's what I needed to know."

He said: "What do you need from me. For the going."

She said: "Be here when I come back."

He held her gaze.

He said: "That's a long tradition. The Clinkscales family keeping faith with the lineage." He paused. "I'll be here."

She held his gaze.

She said: "Thirty years from now, when the next generation is running this and you're explaining to them what happened in the founding period�" she paused� "tell them the truth about what it cost and what it was worth." She held his gaze. "Both parts."

He said: "I will."

She said: "Thank you. For everything." She held his gaze. "The thirty years before me and the three years with me and whatever comes after."

He said: "Don't�"

She said: "I know." She paused. "Carson. Thank you. I'm saying it."

He held her gaze.

He said: "You're welcome." He paused. "Come back safely."

She said: "Yes."

She talked to Yeva on the second-to-last day.

Not a long conversation. The long conversations had already happened. This one was brief and direct in the way of Yeva, who had become someone who said what needed saying in the words it needed and stopped.

Yeva said: "The Vigil is ready."

She said: "I know."

Yeva said: "I'm not going to make it into something you wouldn't recognize."

She said: "I know that too."

Yeva said: "I'm going to make it into something better."

She held Yeva's gaze.

She said: "Yes. You are."

Yeva held her gaze for a moment.

She said: "Come back so I can show you."

She said: "That's exactly the right reason."

They held each other's gaze.

Then Yeva said: "Go."

She said: "Yes."

She went to pack.

Not much. The portal physics didn't favor encumbrance and she'd thought carefully about what she was bringing. The knife, which was always with her. The communicator, though it almost certainly wouldn't function across the displacement. A copy of the document, the record, compressed into a data chip that she was going to leave on the other side for whoever she found. A letter she'd written for her mother, which was the most personal thing she'd ever written and which she wasn't sure would serve any function at the other end but which she needed to bring.

She packed these things.

She sat in the capital quarters for a while with the city outside the window.

She thought about the first time she'd seen the city. Standing at the window on the first morning, looking at buildings she didn't know, in a century she'd been reading about for six weeks, trying to understand where she was.

She thought about who she'd been then.

She thought about who she was now.

She thought: this is what after looks like.

She thought: I know who I am.

She thought: let's go find out what's there.
