Chapter 56 (47 of 54 in this book)

The window opened on a Tuesday.

Haverstock had given her the date three weeks before and she'd held it in the way she held things she was preparing for, with the specific quality of not-yet-using so the energy was available when it was needed.

Tuesday. The portal would be theoretically accessible from the current location of the Hellmouth's interface residue. The presences would be aligned. The window would be approximately eighty hours based on the final models.

She spent Monday on Sphinx.

Not doing anything. Being there. In the garden in the morning and on the property through the afternoon and with Scotty in the evening. She ran in the morning and Vigil was in the canopy and the bond was the bond and she let it be fully present, the weight of it, the specific fullness of something she was going to carry the memory of through whatever came next.

Vigil came down in the afternoon.

All the way down again, the ground distance, and it settled against her side and she put her arm around it and they stayed that way for a long time in the garden with the Sphinx autumn doing what it did.

She said: "I'll come back."

The bond's quality was the quality of something that had received and held this and was holding it forward.

She thought: yes. Hold it. I'm coming back.

That evening Scotty cooked, the real cooking, the kind he did when he was being present with his hands because his hands were how he was present when words weren't sufficient. She watched him cook and ate what he made and they talked about things that weren't the portal and weren't the decision and weren't the goodbye, just the things that people who have been building a life together talked about, the small specific things that were the texture of the life.

She thought: I'm going to remember this. This specific evening.

She thought: I'm going to come back to this.

She went to sleep in the Sphinx property and woke up at four and ran and Vigil was in the canopy and the bond was there and she said goodbye to the property in the way she said goodbye to places, which was to simply be in it for a moment with full attention and then go.

She went.

~~~

The activation point was on Old Earth.

She was not going to Old Earth physically. The physics of the portal activation was not a physical transit to the location of the Hellmouth. The activation worked through the channel, through the coordinated capacity of the presences, creating an interface event that could reach across the distance and the time to the dormant instability at the source location.

The effect, on her end, would be the portal opening around her wherever she was.

She was going to be in Haverstock's interface research facility on Manticore when it happened.

The facility had been prepared for this. Not dramatically. Haverstock didn't do dramatic. He did thorough. The mapping array was at full resolution. Every instrument he had was running. The presences were in their coordinated configuration, which the array showed as a pattern she'd never seen before in three years of looking at the maps, five distinct signatures in alignment that produced something the sum of whose parts was larger than any of them individually.

The channel in her was fully open.

Not something she'd done deliberately. The channel's response to what the presences were doing with the interface was automatic, the biology responding to the interface activity the way the eyes responded to light, the opening of something that was built to be open in exactly these conditions.

The Keeper was present in the most complete way she'd felt it since Beowulf.

Not beside her. With her. The distinction was real and she felt it clearly, the Keeper's consciousness in closer proximity than it had been since the opening of the record, the specific quality of something very old that was going to do something significant and was fully engaged with the doing of it.

Everyone was there.

Haverstock and Prieto at the instruments. Fritz, because Fritz was always present for significant biological events. Allison, because she'd asked. Carson, because she'd asked. Honor, with Nimitz. Yeva. Eris.

Scotty was there.

He was standing to her left in the specific position she'd come to associate with him, not close enough to be in the way and not far enough to be not present.

She looked at him.

He looked at her.

He said, very quietly: "Come back."

She said: "Yes."

She looked at the room. At all of them, the people who were the Vigil and the people who were her life and the people who were both, and she held the looking for a long moment.

She thought: this is what I'm coming back to.

She thought: I know exactly what I'm coming back to.

She turned to face the interface map.

The presences' alignment was complete, visible on the map as something she'd never seen before, five ancient signatures in a configuration that the map registered as unprecedented.

The Constant communicated through the channel: ready.

Witness communicated through the channel: ready.

The others, in sequence: ready.

The Keeper communicated: ready.

She took a breath.

She said: "Yes."

The window opened.

~~~

It did not open dramatically.

There was no blinding light and no sound like a thunderclap and no particular wind. What there was was a shift in the quality of the room, the specific quality of the air changing in a way that was not atmospheric and not entirely sensory, a change in what the channel was reading that was so complete and so present that it was its own kind of overwhelming without being overwhelming in a way she couldn't stand in.

The Slayer sense lit up.

Not in the threat register. In the register she associated with things that were enormous and real and present, the register of the first contact with the Constant, of the record opening, of the bond with Vigil in its first full moment.

The register of things that were exactly as big as they were.

The portal was there.

She couldn't see it the way you saw a door. It was present the way she felt the presences, in the channel, in the Slayer sense, as a quality in the space rather than a visible object in it. The quality was the quality of an opening, a direction, a vector toward something very far away in time and space that had been placed within reach by the coordinated work of the presences and the channel and the interface's dormant memory.

Old Earth.

The Hellmouth's residue.

Two thousand years.

She felt it.

She turned and looked at the room one more time.

Scotty's face.

She held it.

She said: "I'll see you soon."

He said: "I'll be here."

She looked at each of them in turn, the brief specific looking of someone fixing things in memory, and then she turned back to the opening.

She walked into it.

~~~

The transit was not instantaneous.

It was not long either, in any sense she could have measured with instruments. But it was experienced, which the original displacement had not been, or had not been remembered. The original displacement had taken her unconscious and deposited her on the Tepes without the experience of the passage.

This was different.

She was in the channel fully, the opening she'd walked into, and what the channel was doing in the transit was the specific thing it did at full activation, which was the simultaneous reception of everything available through it. The lineage running backward and forward. The presences in their positions, all five of them maintaining the alignment that held the window open. The Keeper, traveling with her through the Keeper's own relationship with the interface, which was not the same as her channel but was compatible with it and was present.

The Girl was there.

Very present. The most present she'd been since the merge on Hades. Running through the ancient dark at the edge of what the transit was, and the running was not the joyful running of the dreams. The running was purposeful. Directed. The Girl running toward something rather than simply running.

She felt the Girl's direction and understood it.

She was going home.

Not her home. The Girl's home. The ancient place where the first Slayer had been made, the original ground, the place before the Hellmouth was a Hellmouth and before there was a word for Slayer, when there was only the dark and the thing in the dark and the girl who ran toward it instead of away.

Old Earth.

The transit ended.

~~~

She arrived in darkness.

Not the jungle darkness of Hades or the city darkness of Manticore's early mornings. Absolute darkness, which resolved as her eyes adjusted into the darkness of enclosed space, underground, the specific quality of air that had not been circulated recently.

She was standing on stone.

The Slayer sense was active in every register simultaneously, the specific activation of something that was in a place of significance, and the significance was the oldest significance she'd ever felt, the interface reading at a density that made Beowulf's interface activity look ambient.

The Hellmouth.

Not open. Not dormant exactly either. Present in the way of something that had been there a very long time and had accumulated meaning the way stone accumulated age, the visible evidence of what it was.

She was underground. Below what had been Sunnydale, California. Below what Sunnydale had become in two thousand years, which she didn't know yet and was going to find out when she went up.

She stood in the dark and breathed.

She was here.

Old Earth.

She felt the Keeper, present and oriented with the specific quality of something that had returned to the place where it had been closest to its origin. Not made here. But connected to what was made here, the same thread running through both of them.

She felt the Girl, very present, the ancient consciousness of the first Slayer recognizing the ground it had come from.

She felt the lineage, running through this place in the residual way of things that had their origin somewhere and retained the connection across distance and time.

She stood in the Hellmouth's underground space and let herself feel all of it.

Then she pulled out the light she'd brought and turned it on and looked at what was there.

The space was old. Not the library basement that she remembered. Whatever structures had been built over and destroyed and built over again in two thousand years had done their work on the surface above. But the cave system beneath, the natural structure that the Hellmouth had occupied, had persisted in the way that geology persisted, patient and unchanged relative to the speed of human decision-making.

The interface was here.

She could feel it the way she felt the presences, as a quality in the space rather than a visible thing. The dormant instability that Haverstock had identified in the data. Present and quiescent and very old and the specific density of something that had accumulated two thousand years of history.

She looked at it for a long time.

She thought about Acathla. About the night she'd sent Angel through the portal that was meant to swallow the world, the worst night of her life that she'd been carrying the weight of ever since. She thought about the arc of that night and what it had cost and what she'd gone home to after, the empty house and the bags already packed and the bus out of Sunnydale.

She thought: that was the interrupted thing.

She thought: this is where the interruption happened.

She stood in the Hellmouth's space and looked at the interface and thought about closing what needed closing, and what she found when she looked directly at it was that closing it was not quite the right word for what needed to happen.

What needed to happen was acknowledgment.

Not ceremony. Not ritual. The specific human act of looking at a thing directly and saying: this happened. This is real. I was here and I was changed by here and the changing was not the end of me.

She said, out loud, in the dark: "I was here."

She said: "I was seventeen and I was the Slayer and this place was the whole world and it cost me everything I had and I survived it."

She said: "I came back."

The interface's quality shifted slightly.

Not in a threatening way. In the way of something very old that had been present for everything that had happened in this place and was acknowledging the acknowledgment.

She breathed.

She thought about her mother.

She took the letter out of her pocket.

She'd written it to Joyce Summers, who was two thousand years gone and for whom the letter served no practical purpose and every personal purpose, the letter she'd needed to write since the morning she'd woken up in the sickbay of the Tepes and understood where and when she was.

She said: "I found a life." She said it to the darkness and to the place and to the letter in her hands. "It's a good one. I did something that mattered. I found people worth being with." She paused. "I became someone. Not just the Slayer. Someone."

She held the letter.

She said: "I don't know if you could have imagined this. What I found. I don't think I could have imagined it from where I was." She paused. "But you would have been glad. I know that." She paused. "You would have made friends with everyone and fed them all and asked too many questions and you would have been glad."

She set the letter down on the stone of the Hellmouth's floor.

She left it there.

She thought: this is what I came to do.

She thought: this and the other thing.

She pulled out the data chip.

The other thing was the reason she'd come that the Vigil needed regardless of what the personal reason was. The intelligence value, the thing that going to Old Earth could provide that nothing else could. And the other thing she'd told Allison she wanted to give: the knowledge that the Vigil existed, for whoever was here, whoever carried the lineage on Old Earth in this century.

She had no way of knowing whether there was anyone to give it to. Two thousand years of the lineage on Old Earth without the Council's framework, without any connection to the interstellar civilization that had been building for eleven hundred years above them�she didn't know what that looked like. She didn't know whether the lineage had maintained itself or fragmented or produced what it had always produced, a series of called ones who didn't know what they were and navigated it alone.

She didn't know.

But the data chip went here regardless. Whatever happened to it, the information in it was in this place, the origin place, and if the channel had the qualities Allison's research suggested it had, if the lineage on Earth was connected to the residual interface at this location in any way�

She set the data chip beside the letter.

She thought: I was here. The Vigil exists. You're not alone.

She thought: find it.

She breathed.

She looked at the Hellmouth's interface one more time, the ancient dormant instability, and she understood in the looking that what she'd been carrying toward this moment was not grief exactly and not guilt exactly but something that was its own category, the specific weight of a thing left unfinished.

It was finished now.

Not the work, which was never finished while the work was ongoing. This. This specific interrupted thing. She'd come back to where the interruption happened and she'd looked at it directly and she'd said what needed saying and she'd brought what the place needed.

The door she'd been not-looking at was now a door she'd walked through.

She was ready to go back.

She oriented toward the channel and toward the presences, whose alignment she could feel from here, still holding, the window still open, the eighty hours not yet expired.

She thought about Scotty saying I'll be here.

She thought about Vigil in the Sphinx canopy.

She thought about the capital city's morning light on the harbor water.

She thought: home.

Not this place, which had been home and was now the origin.

The other place. The one she'd built.

She activated the channel at full opening and directed it toward the alignment and said, to the Keeper and to the presences and to the opening:

Now.

The transit home was faster than the transit out.

Not instantaneous. But faster, the sense of direction being clearer in one sense than the other, the way water flowed faster downhill. She moved through the channel with the lineage running around her and the Keeper present and the Girl present and the ancient darkness becoming the specific quality of the research facility's air�

And she was there.

She was back.

Standing in the same position she'd been standing in when she'd left, in Haverstock's facility, with the mapping array running and the instruments recording and everyone who had been there when she'd gone still there, because the physics of the window meant that what felt like hours to her had been a specific and measured interval from their side.

Four hours.

She'd been gone four hours.

The room was exactly as she'd left it.

She stood in it and breathed the Manticore air and felt the channel closing back to its ambient state and felt the presences releasing their alignment and felt the Keeper settle back into its accompanying rather than with-her quality.

She was back.

She looked at the room.

She looked at Scotty.

His face did the thing.

She said: "I'm back."

He said: "Yes." He said it in the specific voice of someone receiving the thing they'd been holding space for. "Yes. You are."

She looked at the room.

Everyone who had been there when she'd left was still there. The same faces, the same people, the same specific collection that was the Vigil and her life and the work, all of it present and immediate and real.

Nimitz on Honor's shoulder made the sound.

She looked at him.

She thought: Vigil.

The bond was there.

She felt it with the specific quality of something that had been held in a certain way while she was absent and was now resuming its normal quality, the ambient warmth, the presence, the specific consciousness of the treecat who had chosen her and was still choosing her.

She felt the bond and thought: you waited.

The bond's quality was the quality of something that had never considered not waiting.

She let out a breath.

She thought: home.

She thought: yes.

She said, to the room, to all of them: "It worked."

She said: "The Hellmouth's interface is accessible. The lineage on Earth has the Vigil's data." She said: "The door is closed. I'm back."

She looked at Scotty.

He crossed the room.

She met him in the middle of it and the room held everything it held around them, the instruments and the people and the work and the life, all of it present and real and hers.

She was thirty and she was back and the work was ahead and the life was here and she was, as she had been since the garden in Sphinx and the letter on the Hellmouth's floor and the transit home, completely and entirely herself.

Buffy Summers. The Slayer. The Vigil's founder. The person who had been displaced two thousand years and had built something in the displacement that mattered.

All three at once.

Home.

She held Scotty and she let the room be around her and she was here.

She thought: forward.

She thought: always forward.

She thought: yes.
