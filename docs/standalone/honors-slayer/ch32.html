Chapter 32 (23 of 54 in this book)

Haverstock arrived five days later.

The intervening five days were the days when the picture she'd assembled acquired detail and the detail changed what she thought she understood, which was the process by which working knowledge became actual knowledge, slow and not always comfortable.

She told Honor on the second day.

Honor listened with the same complete attention she always brought to things Buffy told her, and when Buffy finished she was quiet for a moment and then she said: "Something has been guiding Beowulf's research toward understanding the dimensional interface. Something that predates human habitation of this system."

"Yes."

"And the purpose is not yet clear."

"The direction is clear. The destination I don't have yet."

Honor considered this. "It's not the same thing as the network we found. The vampire network."

"No. Different in kind." She paused. "The Keeper doesn't register it as a threat. But I don't know yet whether the Keeper's read is complete."

"What does your read say?"

She thought about this. "That it's old and it's been patient and it's been doing something specific for a very long time without requiring acknowledgment for doing it." She paused. "That's not a threat profile. But it's also not something I can categorize as benign without understanding it better."

"And Haverstock."

"Haverstock is the person who can help me understand the interface better. Which is where the Constant lives. If I can understand the interface better I can understand the Constant better." She paused. "And possibly communicate with it."

Honor held her gaze. "Communicate."

"The Keeper is a consciousness at an interface. If the Constant is something at an interface � and I believe it is � then communication might be possible." She paused. "Might."

"What does the Keeper say about that?"

She checked the accompanying quality. The Keeper was attentive in the way it was attentive when something was directly relevant, the heightened focus it brought to things that connected to its function. "It doesn't say no," she said.

"That's not yes."

"No. But it's the closest thing to yes I have right now." She paused. "I'm not going to try to communicate before Haverstock has the interface mapping at a level that gives me a better read on what I'd be communicating with." She held Honor's gaze. "I'm doing the research first. I've learned to do the research first."

Honor nodded. "Good."

Allison, when she told her, had a different reaction.

Allison went still in the way of someone receiving information that reorganizes a large amount of previously organized information and who is watching the reorganization happen in real time. She was quiet for longer than she was usually quiet, which with Allison was significant because she was not a person who was usually quiet.

Then she said: "The Biological Survey Institute's founding documents. Eleven hundred years ago. There's a phrase in them that nobody has ever been able to attribute to a source or explain in context. It's been annotated and debated and no one has ever found where it came from."

She looked at Buffy.

"What phrase?" Buffy said.

Allison said: toward the light between.

Buffy held that.

She looked at the Keeper.

The Keeper's quality was the quality of something that had been waiting for exactly this moment, not in the way of the dramatic revelation but in the patient way of something that had known where the path was going and had been walking it steadily.

"Tell me about the founding documents," she said.

~~~

The founding documents of the Biological Survey Institute were eleven hundred years old and had been digitized and archived with the care that eleven hundred year old founding documents received in a civilization that understood what it had. She read them in Allison's study over an evening with Tomas present and Allison present and the Keeper's full attention accompanying every page.

Toward the light between appeared in the preamble. Its source was annotated as unknown, which in a document this carefully attributed meant it had appeared in the earliest drafts without any record of who had introduced it. The annotations across eleven centuries of scholarship speculated: a reference to enlightenment, to interdisciplinary collaboration, to the literal light spectrum in medical research.

Nobody had suggested: a reference to the dimensional interface, because nobody doing the annotation had the framework for that suggestion.

She had the framework.

She read the phrase and felt the Keeper orient toward it in the specific way of recognition.

She looked at Tomas. "The people who drafted this document eleven hundred years ago. Do you know who they were?"

"The founding council is well-documented," Tomas said. "Sixteen individuals. Their biographies are in the archive."

"Were any of them�" she paused, working out how to ask this� "unusual? In ways that the archive notes without fully explaining?"

Tomas looked at Allison. Allison looked at Buffy.

"One," Allison said. She had a look on her face that said she'd been thinking about this since the first day and had been waiting for the conversation to arrive at it. "A woman named Yuen Sharra. She's in the founding documents as the primary author of the research methodology section. The biographies note that she was � the language is careful but consistent � remarkably insightful. Insights that preceded the research that would have produced them." Allison held Buffy's gaze. "Insights that subsequent centuries proved accurate."

"She knew where the research was going," Buffy said.

"She knew," Allison said. "More than she should have been able to know."

Buffy checked the Keeper.

The Keeper's response to the name Yuen Sharra was not registering. But the Keeper's response to the description � someone who knew more than they should have been able to know, eleven hundred years ago, in the first generation of Beowulf's civilization � was the quality of something recognizing a category.

Not the Constant.

Something the Constant had used as a channel.

"She was a conduit," Buffy said. "The Constant communicated through her. The founding documents don't just record what Beowulf's founders wanted the Institute to be. They record what the Constant wanted the Institute to become." She paused. "Over eleven hundred years."

The room was quiet.

Tomas said, after a moment: "Toward the light between."

"Toward understanding the interface between dimensions," Buffy said. "Yes."

Allison looked at the ceiling for a moment in the specific way of someone organizing a very large amount of information into a structure that could contain it. Then she looked at Buffy. "Why?"

Buffy looked at the Keeper.

The Keeper's response was the most complex thing it had communicated since the record. She sat with it for a long moment, receiving it in the way she'd learned to receive the Keeper's communications, not as language but as direction and quality.

Then she said, slowly: "Because the thing that made the Hellmouth is also patient. And it's been preparing. And what it's preparing for requires understanding the interface to counter." She paused. "The Constant has been preparing Beowulf to be the civilization that develops that understanding." She paused again. "Not for now. For later. For when it becomes necessary."

"How much later?" Allison said.

"The same timeline as the record," Buffy said. "Centuries. Not our lifetimes."

Allison was quiet for a long moment.

Then she said, very calmly: "Then we have work to do."

It was, Buffy thought, exactly the right response. The practical response. The response of someone who received information about a very long problem and began immediately to think about what came next.

"Yes," she said. "We do."

~~~

Haverstock arrived and the work shifted into the register of active research rather than the register of investigation, which was its own kind of shift, the specific quality of a thing that had been building toward action beginning to act.

She spent time every day in Allison's facility and time every evening with Haverstock in his temporary workspace in the university's physics department and the two lines of work were beginning to converge in ways she hadn't fully anticipated when she'd started both of them.

Allison's work on the lineage modification was producing a picture.

The modification was in the genome, as Allison had hypothesized, but not in the way that genetic modifications usually operated. It wasn't a sequence that coded for a protein or a regulatory element that affected expression in the conventional way. It was something Allison described, in the careful language of someone operating at the edge of her framework, as a standing pattern that mediated the organism's relationship with the dimensional interface.

Every person in the lineage had it. To different degrees, the expression varying, most of them expressing it subclinically � faster reflexes, slightly elevated sensory processing, the dreams � and a small fraction of each generation expressing it fully, which was what the calling was, the full expression finding a host.

She was the full expression.

But the modification was in all of them.

"This changes the scale of the thing you're building," Allison told her, on the morning she presented the full finding. "You said the institution was for the fully expressed. The called ones. But if everyone with the lineage modification is part of the line�"

"Then the line is much larger than I thought," Buffy said.

"Much larger. The modification has been maintained through two thousand years of population expansion across dozens of star systems. The number of people carrying it is�" Allison paused. "We don't have the data to project it with precision. But it's not hundreds. It's not thousands." She paused. "It's more."

Buffy held this.

She thought about the institution she was building and the scale it needed to be at and the scale that much larger implied and the gap between those two things.

She thought about the record. The thing that was coming, not in their lifetimes but in the lifetime of this civilization. The preparation that was necessary.

She thought about what Allison had said: then we have work to do.

"Okay," she said.

"Okay?" Allison looked at her.

"Okay means I understand what the work is and I'm not running from it." She held Allison's gaze. "It's bigger than I thought. It's also not starting from zero. The institution is already beginning. The lineage search is already underway. This makes the scope larger but the direction is the same." She paused. "We scale up."

Allison looked at her for a long moment.

"You're remarkable," she said. "In the clinical sense." She paused. "And in the other sense."

"You sound like Fritz," Buffy said.

"Fritz is a good doctor," Allison said, without self-consciousness. "Now. Do you want to know what else I found?"

There was more.

Of course there was more.

There was always more, and she was getting very good at receiving more.

She said: tell me.

Allison told her.

The modification had a component she hadn't characterized yet, a sequence that was present in all carriers but that didn't fit the pattern of the rest of it, that seemed to be doing something separate from the interface mediation. Allison had been looking at it for a week and had developed a hypothesis that she described as preliminary but that she thought was significant.

The sequence, she said, looked like a communication channel.

Not metaphorically. In the literal sense of a biological structure designed to receive and transmit information through the interface in a targeted rather than ambient way.

"In the fully expressed," Buffy said slowly, "the channel is active."

"Yes," Allison said. "In the subclinical carriers it's present but inactive. In you�" she paused. "The channel is active and it's been active for longer than you've been in contact with the Keeper." She paused. "It was active before. You just didn't know what you were receiving."

Buffy thought about two years of Watcher dreams and Slayer dreams and the First Slayer and the sense of the lineage that ran through her like current. She thought about all the things she'd been receiving without a framework for receiving them.

"The dreams," she said.

"The dreams," Allison confirmed.

She sat with this for a long time.

Then she said: "The Constant. On Beowulf. Has it been using this channel?"

Allison looked at her. "I think that's the question," she said. "And I think Haverstock is the person who can help us answer it."

~~~

The evening she told Haverstock about the channel was the evening that the interface mapping reached sufficient resolution to be useful as a communication framework, which was timing she didn't think was accidental and which the Keeper's accompanying quality confirmed was not.

Haverstock looked at the genetic sequence Allison had isolated and the interface map he'd built and spent forty minutes in silence that she recognized as the silence of someone doing very fast and very large thinking, and then he said: "The channel is tuned to the Constant's frequency."

She'd thought this might be true. Hearing it stated was different from thinking it.

"Yuen Sharra," she said.

"Probably," he said. "If Sharra was a carrier with an activated channel, the Constant could have communicated through her in exactly the way you described. Using the biological interface the modification provides." He paused. "Which means�"

"Which means it can communicate with me," she said. "Directly. If I let it."

"Yes." He held her gaze. "Do you know enough to do that safely?"

She looked at the Keeper.

The Keeper was still. Not the stillness of withholding but the stillness of something that had arrived at a moment it had been working toward and was holding it with the care the moment required.

"No," she said. "Not yet." She paused. "But I'm closer than I was yesterday."

He nodded. "Then tomorrow."

"Tomorrow," she agreed.

She went back to Allison's house and ate the dinner that was waiting and sat on the terrace and looked at the city and thought about all of it.

A letter arrived from Scotty.

It was short. Three sentences.

The first sentence said that the Talbott Cluster situation had resolved and that the resolution was significant and that he would be able to explain in person sooner than she'd expected.

The second sentence said that he was requesting leave and that it had been approved.

The third sentence said: I'm coming.

She read the three sentences and let the reading of them settle in the way of something that had been expected and was still, in the moment of arrival, more than expected.

She looked at the city.

She looked at the stars beyond it, which were wrong and were hers.

She looked at the Keeper's accompanying presence and thought about tomorrow and the interface and the Constant and all the work ahead that was large and specific and hers.

She thought: I'm here.

She thought: I know what I'm doing.

She thought: he's coming.

She held all three of those things at the same time and they held.

The city did its nighttime things.

She sat on the terrace of Allison Harrington's home on Beowulf, the oldest inhabited system in human space, with the wrong stars above her and the work ahead of her and the people in her and she was, she thought, entirely where she was supposed to be.

That was new.

That was, as it turned out, enough.
