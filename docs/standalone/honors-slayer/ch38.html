Chapter 38 (29 of 54 in this book)

Honor came back in the spring.

Not for good. Nothing in the shape of the war allowed for back for good. But she came to Manticore with the changed quality that the past several months had produced in her, the same quality Buffy had seen beginning when she'd returned from wherever the war had sent her in the autumn, but further along now, more settled.

She came to Buffy's quarters on the second evening and they sat in the way they sat and Nimitz went where Nimitz went and they talked.

Honor said: "The next several months are going to be significant. The strategic situation is�" she paused, choosing words. "Moving toward a resolution. Not a fast one. But directional."

Buffy looked at her. "How long?"

"Years," Honor said. "But the direction is clearer than it was." She paused. "I've been offered something. I'm going to take it." She paused. "I wanted to tell you before it becomes official."

"Tell me."

Honor told her.

She listened and when Honor finished she said: "That's what comes after."

"Part of it," Honor said. "The part that's mine to take." She held Buffy's gaze. "There's going to be a political restructuring. Not immediately. But the shape of what's coming in the next few years is going to change things." She paused. "The Vigil's formal recognition, when you're ready for it, is going to be possible in a way it might not have been in a different shape." She paused. "I wanted you to know."

Buffy thought about the institutional timeline. The Vigil in its current state was a year old and solid enough to be real and not yet solid enough for recognition, which was where she'd intended it to be. The timeline for recognition had been a variable she couldn't predict.

"Good," she said.

"Good?" Honor looked at her. "That's all?"

"That's all," she said. "I know where we are and I know what we need and I know the direction you're describing is the direction that makes it possible. That's what I needed to know." She held Honor's gaze. "The rest is work."

Honor looked at her for a moment with the layered expression.

"You've changed," she said.

"I've been here over a year," Buffy said. "Things were going to change."

"Not that way," Honor said. "Or not only that way." She paused. "When I met you on Hades you were�" she stopped. "You know what you were."

"Yes."

"You're not that now."

She thought about Hades and what she'd been and the specific distance between that and this. "No," she said. "I'm not."

"You're�" Honor paused. "Settled."

Scotty had used that word in Sphinx. She thought it was the right word. "Yes," she said.

"Settled in what?" Honor said. Not challenging. Genuinely asking.

She thought about it. "In what I'm doing and why," she said. "In where I am. In who's in it." She paused. "I spent two years in the previous century not quite settled in any of those things, doing the work anyway because the work was necessary whether I was settled or not." She paused. "Being settled makes it better."

"It makes the work better?"

"It makes the work and everything else better," she said. "I can't separate them anymore. The work is part of it and the people are part of it and the place is part of it." She paused. "I used to think you had to choose. The mission or the life." She looked at Nimitz, who was on Honor's shoulder with his green eyes doing what they did. "You don't have to choose. You have to build them together. The one makes the other possible."

Honor was quiet for a moment.

"I've been learning that too," she said. "In my own way."

Nimitz made a sound.

"Tell him I know," Buffy said.

Honor told him. His tail tip moved.

They sat for a while longer in the good silence of two people who had each other's context and didn't need to fill the space with anything it didn't require.

Buffy thought about the shape of the thing.

Not the immediate thing. The whole thing, the arc of it from the portal and Hades to here, from the feral clarity of the jungle to the trained and deliberate clarity of now. She thought about what it had taken to get from there to here and she let herself see it fully, the cost and the gift of it both.

She was here.

That was not nothing.

That was, as it kept turning out, quite a lot.

The Vigil's first formal gathering happened on a Thursday in late spring.

Not a ceremony. She'd been specific about not a ceremony. It was a working meeting, the kind of meeting that produced documents and decisions and assignments rather than the kind that produced photographs and speeches. Ceremonies came from institutions that had existed long enough to need them. The Vigil was not there yet and she intended to make it to that point before letting it become what it needed to become.

The people in the room were the people the Vigil currently was.

Carson, who had become its institutional memory and its archivist in the way that was consistent with the Clinkscales tradition and also something new, something that went beyond archiving into the kind of active coordination that the network required.

Fritz, who was its medical director and the most complete picture of the lineage's biology available anywhere.

Haverstock, who had come to Manticore six months ago and had not gone back to the university because the work here was better and because Prieto had followed him and together they were producing research that was already changing the theoretical framework in three separate scientific disciplines.

Usher, who was its intelligence arm and who had by this point absorbed the Vigil's requirements into her operational infrastructure so thoroughly that it was not clear whether the Vigil was using Usher's apparatus or Usher's apparatus was using the Vigil as a framework and she'd decided it didn't matter.

Castellan, who had taken the specific role of operational coordination in ways that her background made her precisely suited for and which no one else in the room could have filled.

Thomas Clinkscales, who had come to Manticore for the gathering with the expression of someone who had been waiting his entire adult life for exactly this room and was receiving it with the careful fullness of someone determined to receive it properly.

Yeva, Decker, and Priya, who were the first generation of the Vigil's trained lineage members and who were in the room as full participants, not observers, because she'd been specific about that too.

Dr. Tomas, from Beowulf, by remote link.

Lieutenant Chen, representing the Queen's office.

And Scotty, who was between postings and whose role in the Vigil was the role of someone who saw the whole of it and thought alongside her, which was not a formal role and was also the most important role in the room.

She stood at the front of the room and looked at all of them and thought about what she was looking at.

She'd built this.

Not alone. Not through any one decision or any one conversation. Through the accumulated specific work of a year and a half in a century she hadn't planned to be in, finding the people and the resources and the direction and doing the next piece every day until the pieces were a whole.

She looked at the room.

The Keeper was very present.

She said: "The Vigil exists to serve the lineage and through the lineage to serve the long work. The long work is not ours to complete. It is ours to advance." She paused. "We do not control the people in the lineage. We support them. We do not direct the research. We resource it. We do not manage the threat. We prepare for it." She paused. "The purpose of this meeting is to make sure that what we're doing is consistent with those principles and that we know what each of us is responsible for and that we have what we need to do it."

She looked around the room.

"Then we get back to work," she said. "Because the work is what we're for."

The meeting lasted three hours and produced what it was supposed to produce, which was clarity and assignments and the specific quality of people who know what they're doing and why moving forward with it.

Thomas Clinkscales cried once, briefly, during a discussion of the archive's integration into the Vigil's formal record-keeping. Yeva noticed and reached over and put her hand on his briefly without making anything of it.

Buffy noticed that too.

She filed it in the category of things that said the Vigil was becoming what it was supposed to be.

After the meeting she sat with Scotty in the room after everyone else had gone, the two of them at the table with the evidence of the working meeting around them.

He said: "Well."

She said: "Yes."

He said: "You did that."

She said: "We did that."

He held her gaze. "You did it first," he said. "The rest of us followed."

She thought about following and leading and the specific question of what you were doing when you were doing both at once. "I did the part I could do," she said. "Everyone else did the part they could do." She looked at the table. "That's how it's supposed to work."

He reached across the table and took her hand.

She looked at his hand holding hers and thought about the airport in Los Angeles when she'd been fourteen, before any of it, before the calling and the Hellmouth and the weight of the mission that had become her life. She'd held her mother's hand in the airport when they were going somewhere, she couldn't remember where. She'd been fourteen and distracted and had held the hand without noticing she was holding it.

She noticed now. She noticed the hand she was holding and who it was and where she was and what she'd built and what was ahead.

She noticed everything.

"Hey," she said.

He looked at her.

"Thank you," she said. "For being part of this."

He looked at her for a moment. "I'm not going anywhere," he said.

"I know," she said. "Thank you for that too."

He squeezed her hand.

She looked at the table and the room and thought about everything the room had held today and everything it was going to hold in the days ahead, all the work and all the people doing it and the long shape of the thing they were all part of.

She thought: this is mine.

She thought: yes.

She held his hand and let the room be quiet around them and the spring evening came in through the window with its Manticoran quality and she was here, in this place, in this life, and it was good.

It was very good.
