The something else took three weeks to become actionable intelligence.

Usher came to Grayson and received the information and went back to Manticore and ran it through the apparatus she ran things through, and what came back was a picture of Beowulf that was more complex than Buffy had been given in the standard briefings and that Usher had access to because of the specific kind of work Usher did.

Beowulf was the oldest inhabited system after the Sol system itself. It had a medical research tradition that was unparalleled in human space. It had a relationship with the Solarian League that was ambiguous in the specific ways that old and powerful things had ambiguous relationships with other old and powerful things. It had the kind of accumulated institutional complexity that came from being eleven hundred years old as a civilization and having made a great many consequential decisions in that time.

It was also, according to what Usher assembled, home to something that had been established there long enough to be effectively invisible in the accumulated complexity, hiding not through active concealment but through the specific camouflage of things that had been present so long that they were part of the background.

She studied the picture for three days.

She studied it alongside Fritz, who was now operating as something between her chief medical officer and her research director, a role that had accumulated to him without either of them planning it. And she studied it alongside Carson, who was back on Manticore now with his leave extended further, something his Academy posting was accommodating because the Academy had been briefed that Carson's duties in the current situation were considered by the Queen's office to be of operational significance.

The picture said several things.

It said that the thing on Beowulf was not a vampire. The activity signature was different from the distributed predatory pattern she'd been reading. Whatever was on Beowulf was not operating a feeding network. It was doing something else with the infrastructure it had built over eleven hundred years on the oldest inhabited system in human space.

That was more worrying than the vampire network, not less.

She brought this to the Queen.

The Queen read the picture that Usher's people had assembled and was quiet for a longer time than she was usually quiet, which was itself information.

"Beowulf is not Manticoran territory," she said finally.

"No."

"Operating in Beowulf requires Beowulfan cooperation or significant discretion." She paused. "At the current political moment, Beowulfan cooperation is�complicated."

"I know." She'd read the political context carefully before this conversation. "What I'm asking for is access to the relationship. Not an operation. Not yet." She paused. "I need to understand what I'm dealing with before I know what dealing with it requires."

"And you think Beowulf will cooperate with that."

"I think Beowulf has its own reasons to want to know what's been hiding in its institutions for eleven hundred years," she said. "I think when that's framed correctly, the cooperation is available."

The Queen looked at her. "You want to go to Beowulf."

"I want to go to Beowulf," she agreed. "Not immediately. There are things to do here first." She paused. "And I'm waiting for something."

The Queen waited.

"Honor," she said. "You said two months in her letter. It's been seven weeks."

"Yes," the Queen said. "She's coming." A pause that had something behind it that the Queen was choosing not to specify. "She has things to tell you that I'll let her tell you."

Buffy looked at her.

"Good things?" she said.

The Queen's expression did the thing it did when she was not going to be drawn. "Let Honor tell you," she said.

She let Honor tell her, which required three more weeks of patience and the specific patience of waiting for something you've been told is coming without knowing its exact shape.

~~~

Honor arrived on a Tuesday.

She didn't come through the main docking facility this time, no ceremony and no cameras, which was itself information. She came through the naval annex and she came directly to Buffy's quarters with Nimitz on her shoulder and a look that was the same as always, level and precise and carrying its priorities, with something underneath it that was different from the last time.

Buffy looked at her.

She looked at Buffy.

Nimitz made a sound that was not a greeting sound exactly and was not an assessment sound exactly and was something in between.

"You look good," Honor said.

"You look like you've been doing things that cost something," Buffy said.

"Yes." Honor came in and sat down, and Nimitz flowed from her shoulder to Buffy's in the specific way that treecats moved when they'd decided to be somewhere, which was decisively and without negotiation. He arranged himself across her shoulders and put his head against her cheek briefly and withdrew it.

"Hi," she said to him.

He made the sound.

"The Queen said you had things to tell me," Buffy said.

"She has no patience," Honor said, without heat. "Yes. I have things to tell you." She paused. "The war is changing. I can't give you details yet because some of them aren't mine to give. But the shape of the next several months is going to be different from what it's been." She paused. "Better, I think. But different."

Buffy waited.

"Personal things," Honor said. "Some of what I've been doing touches things that are�" she paused. "I've been thinking about what comes after the war. In a way I haven't let myself think before, because the before part needed all of it." She looked at Buffy. "I thought you should know that."

"What comes after?" Buffy said.

"I don't know yet," Honor said. "That's new for me." She paused. "I used to know. Or I used to think I knew, which is different but felt the same." She looked at her hands, which was something Honor rarely did. "I know what the war needs from me. After is less clear."

Buffy thought about what she was hearing.

"You're thinking about what you actually want," she said. "Rather than what's required."

Honor looked at her. "That's it exactly."

"That's a good thing to be thinking about."

"It's disconcerting," Honor said. "I'm not accustomed to not knowing."

"I'm accustomed to it," Buffy said. "It gets easier." She paused. "Not knowing becomes information eventually. You start to read the shape of what you want from where you find yourself looking."

Honor looked at her for a moment with the expression that was several things at once. "When did you get wise?"

"I'm not wise," Buffy said. "I'm just chronologically confused in a way that gives me perspective."

Nimitz made the sound that she'd come to recognize as laughter.

"Don't encourage him," she said.

Honor almost smiled. Then she looked at the desk, at the map and the files and the accumulated evidence of the work. "Tell me what you've been doing."

She told her.

All of it, again, the way she'd told her everything in the shuttle the night before the operation. The Eirene Constantia and the case and the record and Yeva and the Grayson candidates and Thomas Clinkscales and the Grayson node and what the oldest one on Grayson had told her before the end.

Honor listened the way she always listened, with complete and unhurried attention, and when Buffy finished she was quiet for a moment and then she said: "Beowulf."

"Yes."

"That's a complicated conversation."

"I know."

"The people I know on Beowulf," Honor said slowly, "the ones I trust and who have the kind of reach this would require�" she paused. "There's a woman. Dr. Allison Chou Harrington."

Buffy looked at her.

"She's my mother," Honor said. "She practices medicine on Beowulf. She has relationships there that I don't have, that go back decades." She paused. "She's going to want to meet you regardless." She paused again. "She's been wanting to meet you since the moment she heard about you."

"Your mother," Buffy said.

"My mother," Honor confirmed. "Who is not a conventional person in any way and who is going to have approximately forty-seven thousand questions and who is going to want to run tests that Fritz hasn't thought of yet and who will also feed you extensively and without mercy."

Buffy thought about all of this.

"I want to meet her," she said.

"I knew you would." Something settled in Honor's expression, something that had been slightly unresolved coming to rest. "I'll arrange it." She paused. "It means going to Beowulf."

"I know." She looked at the map. "That's where I need to go anyway." She looked at Honor. "Will you come?"

Honor looked at her.

"When my current obligations allow," she said. "Yes."

Nimitz made a sound on Buffy's shoulder that she felt as much as heard, a low satisfied rumble that was the treecat equivalent of something working out the way it was supposed to.

She reached up and scratched behind his ears.

"Don't be smug," she said.

He was smug anyway.

She looked at the map and at Honor and at the window where the city was doing its late afternoon things and thought about the shape of what was building.

Beowulf. Honor's mother. The thing on Beowulf that had been established for eleven hundred years in the oldest civilization in human space.

And somewhere in the Talbott Cluster a ship called the Hexapuma with a letter from her in its communications queue that she hadn't gotten a response to yet because the Talbott Cluster was far and couriers were slower than she wanted them to be.

She thought about Scotty telling her about Sphinx. The northern range. The cold she hadn't imagined.

She thought: come back when you can.

She thought: I have things to tell you.

She looked at the city and the light and the wrong-colored harbor and the sky that had been the wrong sky for seven months and had become, in the accumulated way of things you inhabited long enough, simply the sky.

Her sky.

She was here.

She was building something.

She was, she thought, all right.

She was, she thought, considerably more than all right.

She turned from the window.

There was work to do.
