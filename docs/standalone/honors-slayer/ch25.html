Chapter 25 (16 of 54 in this book)

She opened the record the following morning.

The room she'd chosen was small and had no windows, which she'd asked for without fully explaining why. Fritz and Petrakis were present. Carson was present. Allison was there, sitting at a separate display with the genetic data she'd been working with, because she'd said she wanted to see what happened to the channel in the biological data when contact was made.

Honor was near the door with Nimitz.

Buffy sat across from the case and let herself be still.

The presence in the case oriented toward her immediately. She'd felt it do this every visit and it still had the quality of something fresh about it, the recognition that didn't diminish with repetition.

"I'm going to open the record," she said. "I don't know what that's going to feel like. I don't know how long it's going to take." She paused. "I'm asking you to help me."

The presence pressed against the edge of the case.

She reached out and opened the case.

The cylindrical object was cool in her hand and the markings on it moved under her fingers in a way that was not mechanical, the sense of them changing their configuration without her being able to catch the change.

The presence came out of the case.

Not dramatically. Not visibly. She felt it emerge and orient and settle into a position she couldn't have pointed to physically but which felt like beside rather than inside or above. Beside. Accompanying.

The object opened.

Not literally. But something in it opened in the direction of the sense she was using and the contents became accessible.

The record was not a text.

Living knowledge was the closest description she had, the direct transmission of what someone had known rather than what they'd been able to put into words. She received it the way she'd received the Girl's memories in the dreams, being rather than watching, except that she was still also herself and the anchor of Carson's presence in the room held her in the room while the record did what it did.

What she received was this:

The Hellmouth had not been a natural feature of the Earth. Something had made it. A long time before humanity had developed the vocabulary to describe what a Hellmouth was, something had deliberately introduced a point of dimensional instability into the fabric of a world that had not had one naturally.

The effect of the Hellmouth's presence had been the concentration of supernatural activity around it. The Slayer line had evolved in response, the specific capability calibrated to the specific threat.

The long dark in the archive's notation was not a metaphor.

Something was coming. Not soon by human timescales. But eventually. The thing that had made the Hellmouth had not stopped existing. It was patient in the way of things that measured time in geological epochs.

It was going to try again. Not on a single world. The record was clear: the thing had observed humanity's expansion through the stars and had revised its approach accordingly. Not one Hellmouth. Many. Distributed. Each small enough to be dismissed as local anomaly. Each connected beneath the surface to the others in the way of roots connecting trees that appeared separate above ground.

The Slayer lineage was the specific instrument that could address this.

One Slayer was not sufficient.

She came back to the room slowly. Fritz and Petrakis were exactly where they'd been. Carson was closer than he'd been, not in front of her but near. Honor was still near the door. Nimitz was very still.

She looked at her hands.

"How long?" she said.

"Four hours," Carson said.

She looked at the cylindrical object. It was closed now, the opening she'd felt in it no longer present. The presence was still beside her, its quality different, less patient in the waiting sense and more present in the accompanying sense.

"I know what the work is," she said.

She looked at Fritz. "I need paper. I want to write down what I received before any of it goes the way memories go." She paused. "And then I need to talk to the Queen."

~~~

The meeting with the Queen was the longest conversation she'd had with anyone since arriving in this century.

Four hours, in the informal sitting room with Ariel and tea and the late afternoon light.

She told the Queen everything.

The Eirene Constantia and the network. The case and the Keeper. The record and what the record said. The thing that had made the Hellmouth and what it was doing and the specific shape of the work that needed to exist in response to it.

The Queen listened.

When Buffy finished the Queen was quiet for a long time.

"You're asking me to help you build something," the Queen said. "Not a short-term operation. Not a resource allocation for the current political situation." She paused. "An institution."

"Yes."

"On the basis of information received from an object of uncertain origin via a mechanism I have no framework for, telling you about a threat that won't fully materialize in the lifetime of anyone currently alive."

"Yes." She held the Queen's gaze. "I know how that sounds."

"It sounds like an extraordinary ask." The Queen paused. "It also sounds consistent with everything I know about you and everything Ariel tells me about you." She looked at the treecat. "He thinks you're telling the truth."

"I am telling the truth."

"I know." The Queen looked at her. "Tell me what the institution looks like."

She said: "Connected. The Slayer lineage distributed across human space, which it already is whether we want it to be or not. Instead of isolation and control, support and information. Instead of one Council at the center managing the Slayers as assets, a network that serves the Slayers as people." She paused. "The lineage needs to know what it is. Not to control it. To give it what the knowledge gives, which is context for something that's already happening to them regardless of whether they understand it."

The Queen looked at her. "You're describing something that operates across sovereign systems."

"Yes."

"That's politically complicated."

"I know. Everything significant is." She paused. "But the threat doesn't respect sovereign boundaries. The network we found operates across eleven star systems. Whatever is coming will not stop at the Manticoran border." She paused. "The institution has to match the scale of the problem."

"And Manticore leads it."

"Manticore provides the initial support and infrastructure," she said. "Whether Manticore leads it is a different question. I'd argue the institution needs to be understood as serving the lineage rather than serving any specific political entity. Otherwise it becomes the Council."

The Queen held her gaze for a long moment.

"I'll help you build it," she said. "Not all at once. Not without constraints. There are political realities that apply even to this." She paused. "But I believe you, and I believe the record, and I believe that what you're describing is real and that the work you're proposing is necessary." She met Buffy's eyes. "What do you need first?"

"Resources," she said. "Support. People who will help me build whatever life I'm building here." She paused. "And your personal word that you will not use me as a symbol without telling me you're doing it."

"And in exchange?"

"Honesty," she said. "When the political process is asking something of me that I can't give, I'll tell you."

It was an unusually honest offer on both sides.

"I'll take it," the Queen said.
