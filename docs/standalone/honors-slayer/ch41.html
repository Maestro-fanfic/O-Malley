Chapter 41 (32 of 54 in this book)

The summer brought three things.

The first was a message from Tomas on Beowulf indicating that the Constant had been active in a way it hadn't been in recorded history, the interface mapping that Haverstock's remote array was now maintaining on Beowulf showing increased activity in the specific register that meant the Constant was doing something rather than its usual ambient presence.

She contacted Haverstock immediately. He came to her quarters with his tablets and they spent three hours with the data and at the end of it he said: "It's not threatening. It's�" he looked for the word� "communicating. But not with the channel. With something in the interface structure itself."

"What's in the interface structure?"

"Currently?" He looked at the data. "The Constant's pattern. The Keeper's pattern. And something new." He paused. "Something that wasn't present the last time we mapped at this resolution." He paused again. "I don't have a category for it."

She sat with this.

The Keeper was very present.

"Let me contact the Constant," she said.

She contacted the Constant through the channel that evening, alone in her quarters with the city outside and the Keeper accompanying and Vigil presumably somewhere in the vicinity of wherever Vigil was when she was not on Sphinx, because the bond, as it turned out, was not limited by physical proximity in quite the way she'd expected.

What she received from the Constant was the quality she associated with important information being carefully transmitted to a receiver that would need time to process it.

She processed it.

The something new in the interface was not a threat. It was more like a message, a pattern placed deliberately by something that had access to the interface in the way the Constant had access to it, that was communicating with the Constant in the way the Constant had communicated with Yuen Sharra eleven hundred years ago.

Something else was out there.

Something old, not the same as the Constant but in the same category, present at the interface in a different system with a different perspective on the same long work.

She sat with this for a long time.

Then she wrote to Haverstock and asked him to expand the mapping array to cover as much of the explored systems as the current technology allowed.

He wrote back in two hours to say he'd already been thinking about exactly that and had some thoughts about how to do it.

She said: do it.

He did it.

The second thing the summer brought was Honor's announcement.

She'd known it was coming. The quality of the conversations in the months leading up to it had the character of something building toward a statement, and when Honor came to her quarters on a Saturday afternoon and sat down with Nimitz and the specific settled quality she'd seen increasing over the months, Buffy waited.

Honor said: "I'm resigning my commission."

Buffy looked at her.

Honor said: "The war is in a phase that doesn't require what I specifically bring to it anymore. What it requires is administration and political navigation and the kind of sustained institutional management that other people can do as well or better than I can." She paused. "What I want to do doesn't fit within the commission structure."

"What do you want to do?"

Honor looked at her for a moment with the layered expression.

She said: "The Vigil."

Buffy held still.

Honor said: "Not in the way you're thinking. I'm not asking to take it over or reshape it or change what it is. I know what it is and I know it's right." She paused. "I want to be in it. In the way that the lineage candidates are in it, as a full participant in the long work rather than a supporting player." She paused. "I don't have the lineage modification. I know that. But I've been part of the work since Hades and I�" she stopped.

"Honor," Buffy said.

She looked at her.

Buffy said: "The Vigil is for everyone who serves the long work. Not only the lineage." She held Honor's gaze. "You've been part of the Vigil since before it had a name." She paused. "The commission is separate from that."

Honor was quiet for a moment. "I know," she said. "I know the commission is separate. I'm resigning the commission because what I want to do next doesn't fit within it, and what I want to do next is�" she paused� "build something. With you. The political and diplomatic infrastructure that the Vigil needs that it doesn't have yet." She paused. "I know people. Across the Alliance and beyond it. I have relationships that could serve the Vigil's work in ways that nobody else in the room currently can." She paused. "I want to use them for this."

Buffy looked at her.

She thought about Honor since Hades, the steady presence of her, the quality that had been thinking about after in a way she hadn't let herself think before. She thought about what Honor was describing, which was the institutional and diplomatic infrastructure that the Vigil genuinely needed and that she'd been aware of needing and hadn't known how to acquire.

She thought about the Vigil's formal recognition, which was still pending, and what Honor's relationships and standing could do for that recognition.

She said: "Yes."

Honor looked at her.

"Yes," Buffy said again. "I want you in the room. The real room. Not the supporting role." She held Honor's gaze. "The work needs you in it. Not the commission. You."

Something happened in Honor's expression that was the most direct she'd ever seen it, the layered quality dropping away for a moment to reveal what was underneath without the layers. Just Honor, with the weight of a decision made and a direction found.

"Thank you," she said.

"Don't thank me yet," Buffy said. "The diplomatic and political infrastructure is going to be a significant project."

"I know," Honor said. "I'm looking forward to it."

Nimitz made a sound that was satisfaction without apology.

"Don't be smug," Buffy told him.

He was smug anyway.

She looked at Honor.

She thought about Hades and the first conversations and the quality of the specific trust that had built between them, the thing that was neither friendship exactly nor family exactly but something that contained both and was its own category.

She thought: I'm glad she's here.

She thought: I'm glad she's going to stay.

She held that with both hands.

The third thing the summer brought came on a Tuesday in August.

She was in the garden on Sphinx. Not the capital garden, the Sphinx one, the one she'd planted in spring that was now in the full expression of what it could do in its first season, which was not yet its best version but was already better than she'd expected.

The rosemary equivalent was in.

She was standing in front of it with her hands in the soil, doing the thing you did with garden plants in high summer which was maintenance and assessment and the specific sensory engagement of being in proximity to growing things, and she smelled it when her hands disturbed the leaves and the smell was not the same as rosemary and was close enough to rosemary that the distance between them was the specific kind of not-the-same that she'd learned to live in.

She crouched in the garden and smelled the leaves and thought about Joyce Summers.

Not with grief. With the specific quality she'd been developing, the love that didn't require presence, the doing for someone who wasn't there.

She thought: Mom. This is for you. All of it.

She thought about the chicken and the rosemary and the kitchen and the way Joyce had moved in the kitchen and she let herself think about it directly and fully and the thinking was still something she breathed through but it didn't flatten her the way it had, it was hers to hold and she held it.

Vigil was in the canopy above her.

She could feel it there, the bond doing the thing bonds did, the constant low awareness of the other's presence regardless of line of sight. She was still learning what that was. She was going to be learning it for a long time.

She held the plant and breathed the approximate rosemary and thought about her mother.

Then she said, out loud, to the garden and the summer and the wrong stars that were hers: "I'm okay, Mom. I'm doing something important. I'm okay."

Vigil made a sound in the canopy.

Low and present and the quality she'd come to recognize as the specific warmth the bond produced, the treecat's empathic sense receiving what she was feeling and offering something back through the connection.

She looked up at the canopy.

"I know," she said. "Thank you."

The summer afternoon held her in its Sphinx specific way, the light through the trees that were fully leaved now, the sounds of the property doing its summer things, and she was here, in this garden, in this life, and it was real and it was hers.

She got up from the garden.

She went inside.

Scotty was at the kitchen table with his work and he looked up when she came in with the expression he had when he saw that she'd been somewhere internally and was coming back.

She said: "The rosemary equivalent is in."

He said: "Is it good?"

She thought about it. "Different," she said. "And also good."

He held her gaze for a moment.

"Same," he said.

She looked at him.

She thought about different and good and the specific relationship between those two things, the way they coexisted rather than competing, the way the new thing and the old thing could both be real without one displacing the other.

She thought about what she'd said to Yeva months ago, when Yeva had asked what the good part was. She thought about her answer.

You're enough for something that matters. That's the good part.

She was enough for something that mattered.

The something was the Vigil and the lineage and the long work and the garden and the person at the kitchen table and the treecat in the canopy and the Keeper present at the channel and the Constant patient on Beowulf and Honor and Carson and Fritz and all of it, the whole specific shape of it.

Enough for all of it.

She sat down at the table across from Scotty and picked up the book she'd been reading, the one about the history of Sphinx's colonization that she'd been working through for two weeks, and she read and the afternoon moved through its stages and the garden was outside and Vigil was in the canopy and the kitchen smelled of the things Sascha had left and it was summer.

It was very good summer.
