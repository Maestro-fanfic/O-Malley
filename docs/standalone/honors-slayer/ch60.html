Chapter 60 (51 of 54 in this book)

The communication framework took four months to establish.

Not because the technical challenges were insurmountable. Because the social challenges were. The people on Old Earth who had made the contact were careful in the specific way of people who had been operating without external support for a very long time and had developed the caution that came with that, the specific wariness of having been alone long enough that contact with something claiming to be on their side required verification rather than automatic trust.

She respected this entirely.

She didn't push the pace. She let the framework build at the pace the other end was comfortable with, which was slow and incremental and which produced, over four months, something that was reliable enough to sustain a real conversation.

The first real conversation happened on a Thursday.

She was in the Vigil's working space with Carson beside her and the communication link open to a relay that eventually reached a system she wasn't going to specify even in the document and from there to a receiver on Old Earth that she also wasn't going to specify, and on the other end was a woman named�

She was going to call her the woman, in the record. For reasons that were both practical and that the woman had specifically requested.

The woman was sixty-two.

She had been doing what the woman did for forty years without a name for it, which was to say she had been the Earth lineage's equivalent of what Buffy had been in Sunnydale before she'd known she was the Slayer, except that on Old Earth there had been no Watcher arriving to give the name and the context and the specific terrible gift of knowing what you were.

Forty years.

She thought about thirty and fifty-one and what she'd built and what this woman had been doing in the same forty years without any of it.

She said, to the link: "Thank you for reaching out."

The woman's voice was the voice of someone who had been managing something alone for a long time and was deciding to speak. It came through the relay with the slight delay of the communication chain and the slight compression of the technology, but it was clear enough.

She said: "The chip was in the cave for years before I found it. I didn't know what it was for a long time." A pause. "I knew the cave. I'd been going there since I was a girl." Another pause. "Something there."

Buffy said: "The interface."

The woman said: "I didn't have a word for it. I still don't entirely." A pause. "Your chip had words."

Buffy said: "I know. I wrote them for people who didn't have words yet."

The woman was quiet for a moment.

She said: "There are others here. Who have�the thing. What I have."

Buffy said: "I know. The lineage has been present on Earth for as long as it's been anywhere. Longer." She paused. "We've been looking for you."

The woman said: "How long?"

Buffy said: "In this specific form? Twenty years." She paused. "In the larger sense � longer than that. The looking was built into the work from the beginning."

The woman said: "The Vigil."

Buffy said: "Yes."

The woman was quiet for a longer moment.

She said: "The things in your chip. The record. It describes what we are."

Buffy said: "It describes what we know so far. The record is ongoing."

The woman said: "You describe the lineage's origin. As being here. On Earth."

Buffy said: "Yes."

The woman said: "There are stories here. Very old ones. About the first one. The first of what we are."

Buffy held the link.

She said: "Tell me."

The woman told her.

The stories were old in the way that oral traditions were old, carried through the lineage's subclinical carriers over two thousand years without the institutional framework that would have maintained them as doctrine, and therefore transformed in the way that oral traditions transformed, the specific details changed and the underlying truth intact.

The story of the first Slayer was there.

Not with that name. With a different name in a different language that the woman translated as something close to the First One Who Ran Toward Rather Than Away, which was accurate in the way that translations of very old things were accurate, capturing the essence rather than the surface.

The story of the Hellmouth was there. Called differently. The story of the place where the boundary between what was real and what was waiting to be real was thinnest, where the things that wanted the world had been held and defeated and held again.

The story of the Slayer line was there, compressed into the lineage's oral tradition, the specific knowledge that each generation produced someone who was more than the others, who could do what the others could not, who ran toward rather than away.

She listened to all of it.

She thought about the Clinkscales archive and about Thomas sitting a boy down and telling him what the family kept and about four generations of faith and about what two thousand years of oral tradition passing through the lineage's subclinical carriers without institutional support had produced.

It had produced truth.

Not the same truth as the archive. A different angle on the same truth. And together, the archive and the oral tradition, they produced something more complete than either alone.

She said: "I want to write this down. With your permission."

The woman said: "Write it."

She said: "And I want to know more. Everything you have. The full oral tradition, in as much detail as you can give it."

The woman said: "That will take a long time."

Buffy said: "I have time."

The woman was quiet for a moment.

She said: "You sound like someone who has been doing this for a long time."

Buffy said: "Thirty-three years. Counting from when I was called."

The woman said: "Called."

Buffy said: "When the channel opened fully. When I became the fully expressed version." She paused. "You have the word for it in your tradition."

A pause. Then the woman said a word in the language of her tradition.

Buffy held it.

She said: "Yes. That."

The woman said: "I am not that. Not the fully expressed."

Buffy said: "I know. But you found the chip. You built the communication. You've been doing this for forty years." She paused. "The work doesn't only belong to the fully expressed. I spent a long time building the infrastructure to make that point formally. You knew it informally before I said it."

The woman was quiet.

She said: "There is someone here. Young. Who may be what you are."

Buffy held the link.

She said: "Tell me."

The woman told her.

Seventeen. A girl in a city that had been built over what had been the central California coast in the time before the orbital period, the centuries of environmental disruption and managed recovery and eventual reconstruction into something different from what Buffy remembered and the same in its underlying geography.

Seventeen and fast and strong and dreaming.

She thought about herself at seventeen. She thought about Yeva at sixteen. She thought about the fourteen-year-old on Grayson who had asked what the good part was.

She said: "Does she know what she is?"

The woman said: "She knows the tradition. We teach the tradition to all of them. She knows she is one of the carriers." A pause. "She does not know yet that she is�more."

Buffy said: "Is she all right?"

The woman said: "She is seventeen."

Buffy said: "Yes." She paused. "That's an answer."

The woman said: "She needs�" she stopped. "What you needed, I think. What your record says you needed."

Buffy said: "Someone to tell her what she is. Before she figures it out alone." She paused. "Before the alone costs too much."

The woman said: "Yes."

Buffy said: "We'll get information to her. Through you. The record, the biology, the practical knowledge." She paused. "And when the next window opens�" she held the link. "We're going to come. In person. Not through the channel. Actually there."

The woman was quiet for a moment.

She said: "When is the next window?"

Buffy said: "Three years."

The woman said: "Three years."

Buffy said: "We'll be ready."

The woman said: "So will we."

She held the link.

She thought about three years and what three years would contain and who would be there in three years and what it was going to mean to stand in the Hellmouth's underground space with someone from the other side of the connection, the two ends of the thread finally in the same room.

She thought: thirty-three years from being called.

She thought: this is what it was building toward.

Not this specifically. This wasn't the end of the building. But this � the contact, the connection, the thread completing a circuit that had been open for two thousand years � this was the thing that made the building visible in a way it hadn't been before.

She said: "I'll speak to you again next week. There's more I want to ask and more I want to tell you."

The woman said: "I will be here."

She said: "Good." She paused. "One more thing."

The woman waited.

She said: "The seventeen-year-old. When you tell her what she is � tell her this first. Tell her she's not broken. There's a name for what she is. And she gets to decide what she does with it."

A pause.

The woman said: "Those are your words. From the chip."

She said: "Yes."

The woman said: "I'll tell her."

She said: "Good."

The link closed.

She sat in the quiet of the working space for a moment.

Carson, beside her, said nothing. He'd been there for the full conversation and he was doing what he did when he'd received something significant, which was hold it in the specific Clinkscales way before speaking about it.

She looked at him.

She said: "The oral tradition."

He said: "I heard."

She said: "Two thousand years of the lineage on Earth keeping the story alive without the archive. Without the institutional framework. Without any of it." She held his gaze. "They had the truth anyway."

He said: "The Girl doesn't forget." He held her gaze. "What's in the channel doesn't require an institution to persist. It persists because it's the lineage's nature to persist."

She held his gaze.

She thought about what Allison had found, the channel as the vehicle for the accumulated lineage consciousness, the Girl always there.

She thought about the oral tradition as the lineage's own record-keeping, the specific way the biology's channel had expressed itself through the stories people told rather than through institutional documentation.

She said: "We've been keeping the record in the archive. They've been keeping it in the telling. Both are real."

He said: "Both are in the Vigil now."

She said: "Yes."

She looked at the communication equipment.

She thought about the woman on Old Earth who was sixty-two and had been doing this for forty years and who had found the chip and spent years developing the means to respond.

She thought: I want to know her name.

She thought: I'll ask. When the time is right. She'll tell me when she's ready.

She stood up.

She went to find the rest of the Vigil and tell them what had been said.
