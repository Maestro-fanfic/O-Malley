Chapter 30 (21 of 54 in this book)

Scotty's response to her letter arrived nine days after Honor did.

She was in Dr. Haverstock's office at the university when it came through, sitting across from him and a colleague he'd brought in, a woman named Dr. Prieto who worked in information theory and who had developed what she described as a protocol for addressing organized patterns at dimensional interfaces, which was the most precise language anyone had yet produced for what the Keeper was.

She felt the message arrive on her communicator the way you felt things you were waiting for, the specific alertness of attention that had been positioned toward something finally having something to land on. She let it sit for twenty minutes until the meeting reached a natural pause and then she read it.

It was long.

Scotty's letters were not usually long. His letters were the letters of someone who wrote precisely, who said what he meant in the minimum words that accomplished that, and who understood that courier time made length a different calculation than it was in real-time conversation. This letter was long for him, which meant it was about six hundred words, and she read it twice in the twenty-minute break while Haverstock and Prieto debated something at the whiteboard.

The letter said:

That the Talbott Cluster was a situation that he couldn't fully describe in a letter but that was developing in ways he hadn't expected when he'd arrived and that required the kind of attention that didn't leave much room for anything else.

That he'd read her letter three times.

That the thing she was building � the lineage search, the institutional structure, the long work � was exactly the thing she'd said she wanted, which was something worth fighting for, and that reading about it in her letters over the past months had given him something he hadn't had words for until he'd tried to write this letter, which was the word proud, which he recognized was not his pride to feel exactly but which was what he felt and he was done with the habit of having feelings and not stating them directly.

That Commander Terekhov's current operation was moving toward a resolution he couldn't specify but which was going to change the shape of things in the Talbott Cluster and by extension in the broader political situation, and that when the resolution arrived he expected to have a clearer sense of what his next assignment would be.

That he was thinking about after in a way he hadn't let himself think before.

That the northern range in autumn was specifically the kind of cold she hadn't imagined and he wanted her to experience it in autumn rather than winter for the first time because autumn was the spectacular version and winter was the version you had to have been prepared for by autumn to properly appreciate.

That he missed her.

That was the last sentence, stated plainly and without the hedging that earlier versions of the same sentiment might have required.

She read that last sentence twice.

Then she put the communicator in her pocket and went back to the meeting and was present for it with the part of her that was always present for work regardless of what else was happening, which was most of her, because the Slayer's focus was not optional and she'd stopped fighting it years ago and started working with it.

After the meeting she sat in the university garden, which was a formal garden in the Manticoran style with the specific quality of a space maintained for thinking, and wrote back.

Her letter was longer than his. It had been getting longer. She'd noticed this and had decided it was fine.

She told him that she missed him too, which was true and which she'd been circling in the letters for a month without landing on directly, and that stating it plainly felt like the right response to his plain statement.

She told him about Honor being back and about Beowulf and about the thing the oldest Grayson vampire had told her before the end, which she'd been holding back from the letters because she was still processing it and she'd found that the things she was still processing came out better on the second or third try.

She told him that the Keeper had been present while she wrote this letter and that its accompanying quality was something she'd describe as approval, for what that was worth.

She told him about autumn.

She said she wanted to see it. The autumn version. Before the winter version. She'd take his word for the sequencing.

She sealed it and sent it and sat in the garden for a while longer with the afternoon doing what it did and the Keeper present in its accompanying way and thought about the geometry of the situation she was in.

Scotty in the Talbott Cluster coming toward a resolution.

Honor back in Manticore coming toward whatever the war's changing shape was going to require of her.

Beowulf ahead, with the thing that had been established there for eleven hundred years and Honor's mother who had forty-seven thousand questions and relationships that reached into the places she needed to reach.

The lineage candidates building their understanding with the support of the people around them. Yeva who had written her two letters already, one asking about the practical management of the physical profile in a school gymnastics context and one asking about the dreams in a way that was more personal than the first letter and said she was starting to trust the channel.

The institution that didn't exist yet and was beginning to exist in the preliminary ways, the conversations and the documented decisions and the slow accumulation of the agreements that were the substrate of real things before they became visible.

She sat in the garden and let all of it be present at once, which was a thing she was getting better at, the capacity to hold the multiple simultaneous shapes of a life without any of them displacing the others.

Then she went back to work.

~~~

Two weeks after Honor's return, something happened in the war.

She learned about it in pieces, the way you learned about large things that were happening through the secondary effects of how people around you responded to them rather than through the primary event itself. Lieutenant Chen's manner changed. Usher was unreachable for thirty-six hours. The Queen's office cancelled two non-urgent appointments.

Then Honor came to her quarters at seven in the evening and sat down and told her.

She wasn't going to be able to write it in a letter to Scotty. That was the first thing she thought, which was itself information about where her mind went when she received significant information. She thought: I need to tell Scotty this and I can't yet.

What Honor told her was this: a significant Havenite naval engagement had resulted in an outcome that was changing the strategic situation in ways that were still being assessed but that the initial assessment said were good, from the Alliance's perspective, in ways that good hadn't been available for a significant portion of the war.

Not over. Honor was specific about this, not because she thought Buffy would misread it but because Honor was specific about everything. Not over. But different.

"What does different mean for you?" Buffy said.

Honor was quiet for a moment. "Less defined than it was," she said. "The next several months are going to be politically complex in ways I can't fully predict. There are things that need to happen in Manticore. Things that need to happen in Grayson." She paused. "And there's Beowulf."

"Yes."

"My mother is expecting us," Honor said. "When I told her about you she was�" she paused, and something in her voice did the warmth thing that her voice did when she talked about her parents, a specific register that existed in her alongside everything else� "enthusiastic is not sufficient. She's been making preparations for three weeks."

"What kind of preparations?"

"I genuinely don't know. With my mother the preparations could range from a research protocol to a dinner party to both simultaneously." Honor paused. "She's remarkable. I want to be clear about that before you meet her, because she can be a great deal in person and I want you to know what it is."

"You said that before."

"I'm saying it again because I mean it twice." A pause. "She's also going to see things about you that I haven't seen. She does that. She pays a specific kind of attention that produces information people don't realize they've given."

Buffy thought about the Queen, who did something similar. She thought about what it said about Honor that the people who mattered to her tended to have this quality. "Okay," she said. "I can work with a great deal."

"I know you can." Honor looked at her with the layered expression. "When can you leave?"

She thought about the current status of everything. Yeva and the other candidates were in sufficient contact that a gap of a few weeks was manageable. Haverstock and Prieto had enough material to work with for months. The Beowulf matter was the thing that was actively pending.

"A week," she said. "I need a week to arrange things."

"I need ten days," Honor said. "There are things here first."

"Ten days," she agreed.

Honor stood to leave and then stopped. "Nimitz wants to know if you're all right," she said. "He says something is different."

Buffy thought about what was different, which was the letter in her pocket and the last sentence of it. "Tell him I'm good," she said. "Better than good."

Honor looked at her with the expression that wasn't quite reading but was close to it. "All right," she said.

She left.

Buffy sat for a moment in her quarters and then she went to the window and looked at the city and thought about ten days.

She could do a great deal in ten days.

~~~

She spent the ten days doing several things.

She spent two of them on Grayson, not for an operation but for the second round of meetings with the lineage candidates and Thomas Clinkscales and the formal establishment of something that Thomas called the Keeper's Archive and she called the beginning of the thing she was building. They signed a document that was largely ceremonial and entirely real, the kind of paper that said an agreement existed that people were going to honor.

Thomas cried again. She was beginning to think this was going to be a feature of her relationship with him and she was making her peace with it, the peace of someone who understood that some people expressed significant things in tears and that there was no undignified way to mean something.

She spent three days on administrative work for the institution, which had not yet been named and which she was deliberately not naming yet because names had weight and weight came with constraint and the thing was still finding its shape. She worked with Chen on the formal agreements with the Queen's office and with Usher on the intelligence-sharing protocols that connected the institution to the existing infrastructure and with Fritz on the research framework that would feed information about the lineage candidates into the understanding of what the lineage was and what it needed.

She spent one day with Haverstock and Prieto, who had made progress on the Keeper question that was genuinely surprising and that required her to sit for two hours while Prieto ran what she described as an interface mapping procedure and which was the most peculiar experience Buffy had had in this century, including the record, because the procedure produced a map of the Keeper's presence that was three-dimensional and dynamic and which both physicists stared at with the expression of people who had just received the experimental confirmation of fifteen years of theory.

The Keeper's response to being mapped was something she read as bemused.

She spent the remaining days writing. Not letters, though she wrote those too. Writing down what she'd received from the record, the things she'd put on paper the night after the opening and which she'd been refining since, working them into something that had the structure of a document rather than the structure of immediate notes. She wasn't a scholar. She hadn't been trained to write this way. She did it anyway because the information needed to exist in a form that could be transmitted and used and because the doing of it was a way of understanding what she'd received more completely.

Carson read the draft.

He sent back comments that were the comments of someone who had been living in primary documents for months and had developed opinions about how information was most precisely expressed. The comments were good. She incorporated most of them.

On the ninth day a letter arrived from Scotty.

He couldn't say much. The Talbott Cluster situation was at the point where what he could commit to paper was limited by operational security. But he said that the resolution he'd anticipated was moving faster than expected and that the shape of what came after it was becoming visible and that he was thinking about leave.

The last line said: I looked up the transit time from the Talbott Cluster to Manticore. It's manageable.

She read that line twice.

Then she packed her bag for Beowulf.
