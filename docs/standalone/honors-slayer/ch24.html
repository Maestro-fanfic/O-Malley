Chapter 24 (15 of 54 in this book)

Fritz arrived forty minutes later.

Castellan had managed the ship and its human crew with the specific competence Buffy had been counting on. Four of the crew had been aware of the master's nature. Three were cooperating. One was not yet cooperating but was being given the time to reconsider.

She stayed with the case and waited for Fritz.

Fritz came in with Petrakis, who had committed fully to the recalibration project and who turned out to be unexpectedly useful because he brought the rigorous external skepticism that prevented her and Fritz from confirming each other's interpretive biases.

Fritz looked at the case and at her.

"You haven't touched it," he said.

"No."

"Tell me what you're feeling from it."

She thought about how to describe what the Slayer sense was doing. "Supernatural. Not demonic. Not vampiric. Something else." She paused. "The closest thing I have in my experience is a nexus point. Like a spot with concentrated mystical energy. Except contained. As if someone took a nexus point and put it in a box."

Fritz and Petrakis ran the scanner over it. The results made Petrakis look at the device and then at the case with the expression of someone whose instrument had just told them something its manufacturers had not anticipated.

"The material is organic in origin," Petrakis said. "Something that was alive at some point and is not alive now." He turned the scanner toward Fritz. "The interior shows two readings. One is a physical object. Small. Roughly cylindrical. The other is—" he paused— "the scanner is reading a second physical presence that I can't localize. As if there's something in the case that the instrument can detect but can't locate."

She looked at the case.

She thought about two thousand years. About mystical energy and nexus points and the Powers That Be and the category of supernatural that she'd never fully understood, that Giles had called the higher order.

She thought about the Girl, who was very present and very still in a way that was different from the usual quality of her presence, more focused, the attention of something ancient recognizing something familiar.

"I'm going to open it," she said.

Fritz and Petrakis stepped back.

She looked at the mechanism. It was not a lock in any conventional sense. It was more like a seal, and the seal's logic responded to her presence with a resonance that she recognized as the same resonance the Slayer sense produced, the same frequency, the same register.

She put her hand on the seal and pressed.

The case opened.

Inside, nested in a material she had no name for, were two things.

The first was a small cylindrical object, dark, approximately the length of her hand, with markings on it that were not writing in any alphabet she knew but which the Slayer sense registered as significant in the specific way of things that had been made deliberately, imbued with intention.

The second was not a physical object.

It was a presence.

A very small presence, compressed and patient in the way of something that had been waiting for a long time and had learned to wait well. She felt it the moment the case opened, felt it orient toward her the way a compass oriented toward north, and the orientation had the quality of recognition.

"Hello," she said.

The presence pressed against the edge of the case.

Not threatening. Not asking anything. Just present, and oriented toward her, and she had the strong impression that it had been waiting specifically for something like her, not her precisely but something with the frequency of what she was, and it had been waiting for a very long time.

She looked at Fritz without moving her hand from the case. "You remember when I said there were things I didn't have a scientific framework for?"

"Yes," Fritz said carefully.

"This is one of those things." She looked back at the case. "I need the Clinkscales archive. Carson." She paused. "And I need to talk to the Queen."

~~~

They spent three days in the archive.

Not the physical archive, which was on Grayson and not immediately accessible, but Carson's memory of it and the scans he'd been making of the most significant documents for the past six months and the digital records the Clinkscales family had maintained since digital records had become available.

The archive had four references to the Keeper's Seal. All terse, all clearly written by people recording received knowledge rather than firsthand experience.

Stripped of interpretive overlays, the primary references said:

That the Seal had been made in the time before the Hellmouth's closing.

That it contained a record of what the Hellmouth had been and what had made it and what preventing another required.

That the record was not text but something else, something the notation's author had described as living knowledge.

And that the Keeper was not a created being but a called one, something that had chosen the function in the way that she'd chosen the Slayer's function, through the intersection of capacity and decision.

She read that last part twice.

"The Keeper chose," she said.

"Yes," Carson said.

"Then it's not a guardian in the sense of a created tool," she said. "It's a person. Or something like a person. Something that decided this was what it was going to do."

She looked at the case. "The Eirene Constantia's master had this. He was keeping it."

"Or he'd taken it from whoever was supposed to have it," Carson said. "We don't know how he came to have it."

She thought about what she'd read in the master in those last moments. The direction, the sense of something above him in the hierarchy that was older still. She thought about the master's long leash and his autonomy.

"He took it," she said. "From whatever is at the top of the network. And he kept it because he understood what it was." She paused. "He was protecting it."

Carson looked at her. "Why would a vampire protect something that belongs to the Slayer line?"

She didn't have an answer to that. She filed the question.

"I need to open the record," she said. "The cylindrical object. I think the Keeper is the key for that. But I need to know what I'm opening first." She looked at Carson. "Anything about the Keeper's Seal. Anything about what the record contains."

They kept reading.
