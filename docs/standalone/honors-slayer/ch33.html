Chapter 33 (24 of 54 in this book)

She didn't sleep well the night before.

Not anxiety exactly. She knew the difference between the pre-operation alertness that was anxiety and the pre-operation alertness that was the Slayer sense doing its preparation work, and this was the second kind, the quiet activation of something that knew something significant was coming and was getting ready for it. She lay in the dark of Allison's guest room and let her body do the preparation and looked at the ceiling and thought about tomorrow.

The Keeper was very present.

More present than it had been since the night after the record, the night she'd sat on the roof of her building in Manticore with the city below her and the shape of the work visible for the first time. That night the Keeper had been present in the quality of something that had waited and was now watching the thing it had waited for begin to happen.

Tonight it was present in the quality of something that was also preparing.

She looked at the ceiling and said, quietly, "Is it going to work?"

The Keeper's response was not language. It was the quality of a particular kind of certainty, not the certainty of a guaranteed outcome but the certainty of a correct direction, the sense of something that knew the path even if it didn't know every step on it.

She'd learned to take that.

"Okay," she said.

She slept at two and woke at six and ran through the Beowulf morning, which was doing the thing mornings did, the specific assembly of a day from its component parts of light and air and the sounds of inhabited space coming awake. She ran and the Keeper ran with her, which was a thing she thought about as she ran, the quality of an ancient consciousness accompanying a seventeen-year-old girl through the streets of the oldest city in human space.

Eighteen, she corrected herself.

She'd turned eighteen three weeks ago on a planet that didn't know the word January. Carson had sent flowers. Fritz had sent a formal medical assessment with a handwritten note at the bottom that said happy birthday in the careful script of someone who didn't write by hand often but had decided this warranted it. The Queen had sent a letter that was brief and precise and warm in the way the Queen's warmth operated.

Scotty had sent a letter that she'd read four times.

She'd thought: eighteen.

She'd thought: I made it.

She ran through the morning and came back and ate an enormous breakfast that Allison watched her eat with the frank scientific interest of someone who had been studying the caloric math of the healing factor for weeks and was still revising her estimates upward, and then she went to the university.

~~~

The room Haverstock had arranged was in the physics department, a research space that had been cleared of its usual equipment and replaced with the interface mapping array that he'd spent three weeks assembling and reconfiguring and that produced, when active, the three-dimensional dynamic map that he and Prieto had developed.

The map was currently active.

She stood in front of it and looked at the Constant's signature in the map, which Haverstock had isolated over the past week by a process of elimination that he'd described as subtracting everything he knew until what remained was what he didn't know. The Constant appeared in the map as a stable pattern at the interface that was distinct from the Keeper's pattern, which she'd learned to recognize, and distinct from the ambient interface activity that the mapping revealed throughout the dimensional boundary.

It had the quality of something organized. Deliberate. Old in the way that organized things were old, not like geology was old but like intention was old, the specific age of a purpose that had been maintained across time rather than merely endured it.

Haverstock was at his instruments. Prieto was present by remote link, her face on a display in the corner with the focused expression of someone watching something they'd been preparing for. Fritz was here, standing slightly to the side in the position he took when he was monitoring rather than acting. Allison was here, sitting at a separate display with the genetic data she'd been working with, because she'd said she wanted to see what happened to the channel in the biological data when contact was made.

Honor was here.

She was standing near the door with Nimitz on her shoulder and the expression she had when something significant was happening and she was present for it as witness rather than participant, the specific quality of Honor being a witness, which was complete.

Buffy stood in front of the map and checked the Keeper.

The Keeper was oriented toward the Constant's signature with the attention of something that was about to do something it had been building toward and knew how to do.

"Okay," she said. "Walk me through it."

Haverstock said: "The channel Allison identified in the genetic sequence has an interface expression. I can see it on the map. It's quiescent right now, which matches the baseline readings from before. What we're looking for is activation." He paused. "Activation means the channel opens. What goes through it is�" he stopped. "Unknown. That's your part."

"And the Constant?"

"The Constant's signature is stable. It's not approaching. It's not reacting to our presence here, as far as I can tell." He paused. "But the map's resolution has limits. Things that happen below a certain threshold I may not be reading accurately."

She nodded.

She looked at the map. She looked at the Constant's signature, steady and old and patient in the way of things that had been patient for eleven hundred years and could afford to remain patient for whatever it took.

She thought about what she was doing.

She was going to open the channel. The biological channel in the lineage modification. She was going to open it and she was going to direct it toward the Constant's interface presence and she was going to see what the Constant said.

She'd communicated through the channel before without knowing that's what she was doing. The dreams were the channel. The Keeper's communication was through the channel. The lineage's connection had always run through the channel.

Opening it deliberately was different from receiving what came through it. She didn't have a complete framework for the difference. She was going to find out what the difference was.

The Keeper's quality shifted slightly, the shift of something that was moving from readiness into action.

She breathed.

She let the Slayer sense do the thing she'd been asking it to do for the past week, the specific focusing of the non-visual non-auditory read toward the dimensional interface, not diffuse in the way it normally operated but directed, a conscious direction of something that was usually reflexive. It was like the difference between peripheral vision and looking directly at something, the shift from ambient awareness to focused attention.

The channel opened.

She felt it as a change in the quality of her own perception rather than as a sensation in the conventional sense. Not quite like the record, which had been receiving. Not quite like the Keeper's communication, which was beside. Something different from both. A direction, a vector, an opening toward.

The Constant was there.

Not immediately. Not with the sudden presence of a thing arriving. More like the way a room became apparent when your eyes adjusted to its light, gradual and then complete, the Constant's presence assembling itself from what had been background into what was foreground over the course of several seconds.

It was very old.

She'd thought she'd understood very old from the vampires. She'd thought the Eirene Constantia's master had been a lesson in the specific weight of very old. This was older than that in a way that the vampires hadn't prepared her for, not because the vampires weren't old enough but because the Constant wasn't old in the same register. The vampires' age was the age of accumulated experience, of memory layered on memory for centuries. The Constant's age was something different, the age of a presence that had existed in relation to time rather than in time, that had been what it was when the first humans had arrived in this system and had been what it was before and would be what it was after.

She held her ground.

Not because she was afraid. Because the weight of what she was encountering required her to be present for it rather than to be flattened by it, and she'd spent two years and then seven months in this century developing the practice of being present for things that were larger than her. She stood in the room and breathed and stayed.

Something in the Constant's presence shifted.

She felt it the way she felt the Keeper's communications, as quality rather than language, and the quality was recognition. Not the recognition of something familiar. The recognition of something expected. The sense of something that had been waiting for this specific contact and was now receiving it.

She didn't try to speak. Not yet. She received first, the way she'd learned from the Keeper's communications that receiving produced better information than asking.

What came through the channel was not the simultaneous gestalt of the record. It was slower and more sequential, the accommodation of something very old learning to communicate with something very young in the way the young thing could receive. She felt that accommodation happening in real time, the Constant adjusting its output to the channel's bandwidth and her channel's specific character.

What came through was this:

Old. Older than humanity's presence on this world. Older than this world's star in its current configuration. A presence at the dimensional interface that had been here since before.

Watching.

Not passively. Not indifferently. The watching of something with an investment in what it was watching, the specific attention of something that cared about the direction of events without being willing or able to intervene in them directly.

The lineage.

The modification had not been accidental. The Constant had been present when the first Slayer was made, had been present at the making, had not made it but had been there when it was made and had understood what it was and had understood what it meant.

The thing that made the Hellmouth was known to the Constant.

Very old and very patient and working on a timeline that was geological. Not hostile in the way of something that wanted destruction. Hostile in the way of something that wanted replacement, the conversion of the experienced universe into a state that served its purposes and not the purposes of anything that currently occupied it.

The Constant had been watching this since before humanity existed.

It had been watching it since humanity appeared, with the specific interest of something that had seen this kind of conflict before and knew the shape of it.

The lineage was not an accident. The Constant had not made the lineage. But the Constant had understood what the lineage was when it appeared and had been, in the eleven hundred years since humanity reached Beowulf, doing what it could within its constraints to ensure that the lineage survived long enough to be ready.

The constraints were real and significant. The Constant could not intervene directly. Could not make choices for the people whose choices were the operative variable in the long conflict. Could only create conditions. Favor probabilities. Place a phrase in a founding document.

Toward the light between.

She stood in the room with the interface map and the Keeper beside her and the Constant in the channel and held all of it.

The question she'd been building toward arrived fully formed.

She asked it through the channel.

She didn't ask it in language. She asked it the way the channel worked, in the quality of the question rather than its words, directed toward the Constant's presence with the specific focus of something that wanted to know and was ready to receive.

What do you need from me?

The Constant's response was long.

Not in duration exactly but in density, the kind of communication that had been compressed into the available bandwidth and expanded in the receiving. She held it for a long time after, standing in the room with everyone watching her and not saying anything, because the holding was necessary before the articulation was possible.

Then she opened her eyes, which she hadn't known she'd closed.

Haverstock was at his instruments with the expression of someone watching something his instruments were recording that he didn't have a framework for yet. Allison was at her display with the expression of someone watching the biological data do something that was going to require several papers to adequately describe. Fritz was steady in the corner, watching her directly rather than his instruments.

Honor was still near the door. Nimitz was very still on her shoulder.

She said: "It needs two things."

The room was quiet.

She said: "It needs the lineage connected. Not just informed. Not just supported. Actively networked, in contact with each other, operating as a coherent whole rather than isolated individuals. That's the first thing." She paused. "The second thing is the interface research. Haverstock's work. Allison's work. It needs to continue and accelerate and it needs to produce, eventually, a complete understanding of the interface." She paused. "Because the thing that made the Hellmouth works through the interface. Understanding the interface is the only way to understand the mechanism it uses. And understanding the mechanism is the only way to counter it."

She looked at Haverstock.

He held her gaze. "Fifteen years," he said. "That's how long I've been working toward that."

"I know," she said. "You're closer than you were. We're going to help you get there."

She looked at Allison.

Allison was looking at the biological data display with the expression of someone who had just received the confirmation of something she'd suspected and was now thinking about what came next with the focused energy of someone who found next things interesting rather than daunting. She looked at Buffy. "The channel," she said. "In the subclinical carriers. If we could activate it�"

"Not all at once," Buffy said. "Not without the infrastructure to support what activation means." She held Allison's gaze. "That's the institution. That's why it has to exist before we can do the next part."

Allison nodded. "Then we build it faster."

"Yes," Buffy said. "We build it faster."

She looked at the interface map, at the Constant's signature still present and patient and old in the way it was old. She looked at the Keeper's signature beside it, the two patterns at the interface that were different from each other and both relevant to the same long work.

She thought about the dream she'd had weeks ago, the woman in a library who had said she'd been watching for a long time. She thought about the quality of the Constant's presence, which was not identical to the woman in the dream but was in the same register, the same kind of old, the same kind of watching.

She had a question she hadn't asked through the channel. She held it and looked at the map and decided it could wait.

Some questions needed time before they needed answers.

"Okay," she said. "Let's get to work."

~~~

The work that followed was three kinds simultaneously.

The interface research, which was Haverstock and Allison and Prieto and the apparatus they were running, producing data that was unprecedented in volume and specificity. She contributed to this in the specific ways she could contribute, which was as a subject of the biological study and as a channel to the Constant when specific questions required that channel, which happened twice in the two weeks that followed with results that Haverstock described, both times, as revolutionary.

The lineage network, which was the institution she'd been building and which was now building faster. The Queen's office was fully engaged. Carson was coordinating from Manticore, his Academy posting having been formally converted to a role in what the official documentation was calling the Survey of Lineage Continuity, which was accurate and which was also exactly the kind of name that attracted minimal attention from people who didn't know what it meant.

The Constant work, which was the ongoing process of understanding what she'd received in the channel and what it meant for the shape of everything she was doing.

She spent the evenings writing. She'd started a document that was not the record notes, not the institutional frameworks, something different. Something that was part personal account and part operational record and part the kind of document that was written by someone who knew they were in the middle of something that would need to be understood by people who came after.

The Keeper, when she wrote, was very present.

She noticed this. She mentioned it to Fritz, who mentioned it to Haverstock, who developed a hypothesis about the channel remaining partially activated after direct contact with the Constant and the Keeper using the activation to be more present than it had previously been.

This seemed right. The Keeper was more communicative since the contact. Not with language even now, but with the quality of its communications becoming clearer, more specific, more accessible to interpretation. She was starting to be able to distinguish between its responses to different things, the specific texture of approval versus concern versus the quality that meant pay attention to this.

It expressed pay attention to this a lot when she was writing.

She took that as encouragement and kept writing.

~~~

Scotty arrived on a Thursday.

She knew he was coming on Thursday because he'd sent a message three days earlier from a transit point with the arrival time and because she'd been aware of the approaching Thursday with the specific peripheral awareness of something that was not the Slayer sense and not the channel and was simply the ordinary human awareness of waiting for something.

She met him at the docking facility.

Not with a plan about how the meeting would go. She'd learned that plans for the meetings that mattered were less useful than simply being present for them, and she was present, standing in the docking facility at the time the ship came in.

He came through the hatch with his kit bag and the look of someone who had been on a ship for long enough that the idea of being somewhere specific was still recalibrating. He saw her and his face did the thing it did when he saw her, which she'd learned over the months of their correspondence was an accurate read and which she was not tired of reading.

He said: "Hi."

She said: "Hi."

They stood in the docking facility for a moment.

Then she said, "Allison's cooking tonight."

He picked up his kit bag. "Let's go."

She fell into step beside him and they walked through the docking facility and out into the Beowulf afternoon, which was doing the Beowulf version of afternoon, different from Manticore and from Grayson and like itself.

"How was it?" she said.

"Long," he said. "Significant." He paused. "I'll tell you properly later. When I've had time to organize it." He looked at her sideways. "You look different."

"Different how?"

"More�" he paused, looking for the word. "Settled."

She thought about this. "The Constant," she said. "The contact. Something about making contact with something that old and that certain about what it's doing settles you. The scale of it. Everything small stops feeling small and everything big stops feeling overwhelming." She paused. "Both just become the work."

He looked at her.

"Both just become the work," he repeated.

"Yes."

"I've been away for four months," he said. "In four months you made contact with a consciousness that predates human civilization."

"Among other things," she said.

He was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "Tell me everything. From the beginning."

She told him everything, from the morning she'd first arrived in Beowulf's atmosphere to the contact to what the Constant had communicated to the research work and the institution and all of it, the whole four months in the version she could tell while walking through the afternoon of an alien city.

He listened the way he always listened, with the complete attention that he brought to things that mattered and the specific quality of his listening that received information without needing to interrupt it, and when she finished they were at Allison's building and the afternoon had become early evening.

He was quiet for a moment.

Then he said, "What do you need?"

She looked at him.

"From me," he said. "In this. What do you need?"

She thought about what she actually needed, the honest answer rather than the manageable one. "Someone who sees the whole of it," she said. "Not just the operational piece or the research piece or the political piece. All of it, together, as one thing." She paused. "Someone who doesn't need me to explain the context every time. Who already has the context and can think with me rather than needing me to think at them."

He held her gaze. "I can do that."

"I know," she said. "That's why I said it."

He almost smiled.

She almost smiled back.

They went up.

~~~

Allison's reaction to Scotty was immediate and characteristic.

She took one look at him in the doorway and said, "You're the one from the letters," and then did not wait for confirmation before directing him to the kitchen where dinner was in its later stages and which required his assistance with something, she said, that he did not need a culinary background for, only hands and willingness.

He followed her without visible surprise, which told Buffy something about his capacity for Allison.

Honor was already there, Nimitz flowing between Honor's shoulder and the back of the kitchen chair in the way he did when he was comfortable and was choosing his own positions, and McKeon was there, which was unexpected. McKeon had arrived two days ago on the same ship as Scotty but earlier than Scotty because the same ship had two separate arrival times for its passengers depending on their internal transit point, which was a navigational detail she was still not fully fluent in.

McKeon looked at Scotty in the way of two people who had known each other long enough that greetings were a formality and said, "Terekhov sends his regards."

"I'll return them in writing," Scotty said, and accepted a role in the dinner preparation that Allison assigned him without preamble.

Fritz was there, in the corner with his notes in the way of Fritz in any social situation, present and documenting and content. Haverstock had declined dinner because Haverstock frequently declined dinner when data was current and the data was always current, but he'd sent a message of the specific kind that said he was working and happy rather than working and depriving himself, which she read as him being fine.

The dinner was large in the way of Allison's dinners when there were people who needed to eat and people she wanted to feed, which in Allison's framework were not always the same people and sometimes were, and the table held all of them in the specific configuration of a group that was not a family and was something that the word family gestured toward without containing.

She sat at the table and ate and listened to the conversation move and thought about something.

She'd been in this century for nine months.

Nine months ago she'd been on Hades, coming back to herself from a state she didn't like to examine too closely, in a jungle on a prison planet with a small group of people who were the only people in the universe who knew where she was.

Now she was at a dinner table on the oldest inhabited planet in human space with those people and the people they'd connected her to and the work that had found her and the shape of a life that was, in its broad outlines, recognizable as a life. Not finished. Not settled in the way of something that didn't move anymore. Settled in the way of something that had found its ground and was growing from it.

She thought: this is how it works.

She thought: the after doesn't look like anything in advance. It looks like this when you're in it.

Scotty was across the table, listening to something McKeon was saying with the expression he had when he was filing information he was going to use later. He looked up and caught her looking and his expression shifted in the small way it shifted when it was just her.

She looked back.

Nimitz made a sound on Honor's shoulder that she'd come to recognize as the treecat's version of are you going to do something about that.

"Mind your own business," she said.

Honor, without looking up from her conversation with Allison, said: "He never does."

Nimitz was entirely unrepentant.

She looked back at Scotty.

She thought: later. Not yet. Soon.

The dinner continued.
