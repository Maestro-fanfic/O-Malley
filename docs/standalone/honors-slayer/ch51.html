Chapter 51 (42 of 54 in this book)

It started with Vigil.

She was in the garden on a morning in the third year's winter, the kind of morning that Sphinx produced in January that was cold in the way she'd learned to dress for and still found bracing in a way that felt like a gift. The garden was under its winter cover, the established plants doing what established plants did in the cold, which was rest with the specific patience of things that knew spring was coming and had done this enough times to trust it.

She was just standing in it.

Not working. Not thinking about the work. One of the mornings where she'd come outside because the inside had been too much and the outside was what she needed, the cold air and the bare winter architecture of the trees and the specific quality of Sphinx at rest.

Vigil was in the canopy.

She felt this through the bond in the ambient way she always felt it, the low steady awareness of the other's presence that had become as much a part of her baseline as the Keeper's accompanying quality. Both of them present, both of them constant, the specific texture of not being alone that she'd been building toward since Hades.

Then Vigil did something.

Not dramatically. Treecats didn't do things dramatically when they were doing them on purpose. They did them with the specific deliberateness of something that had decided to communicate and was choosing its method carefully.

Vigil came down from the canopy to the low branch, the approach branch, the one it had used since the beginning, and it looked at her.

She looked back.

The bond's quality shifted.

Not the ambient warmth she was used to. Something more directed, the specific quality of the bond when Vigil was actively communicating rather than simply present. She'd felt this before, usually about things in her immediate environment, things Vigil had noticed that she hadn't.

This was different.

The quality was internal. Directed not at something around her but at something in her, the specific quality of the bond when Vigil was reading something she was carrying and responding to the reading.

She held still.

She felt what Vigil was reading.

It was not a clear picture. The bond didn't work in pictures. It worked in the quality of things, the emotional register of what was present, and what Vigil was reading in her and reflecting back was something she recognized the moment she felt it reflected.

Grief.

Not the grief she knew she carried, the managed version, the grief for her mother and Sunnydale and the world she'd lost that she'd processed and integrated and that was part of her without being the loudest thing in her. That grief she knew. She'd made her peace with it in the way you made peace with permanent losses, not by not feeling them but by feeling them in their proper proportion.

This was different grief.

This was the grief she hadn't known was there.

It didn't have a face. It didn't have a name. It had the quality of something that had been underground long enough to lose its surface features, to become simply a weight she'd incorporated into how she moved through the world without knowing it was additional weight.

Vigil held the reflection for a moment.

She breathed.

She felt the shape of what was being shown to her.

Not the losses themselves. Not the people and the place and the world she'd come from. Something underneath the losses. A question she hadn't let herself ask because asking it made the losses louder and she needed the losses to be manageable and asking the question was not compatible with the losses being manageable.

The question was: is the door still there?

Not open. She wasn't asking if it was open. She wasn't asking to go through it. She was asking whether it existed, whether the mechanism that had displaced her was a one-way event or whether there was a possibility, theoretical, however remote, of a direction back.

She had not asked this question.

She had not asked this question since the first months when asking it had been too raw and then the question had gone underground and she had stopped being aware that she wasn't asking it.

Vigil's true-hand came off the branch and settled on her shoulder briefly, the specific communication of the touch, and then withdrew.

She stood in the garden for a long time.

The winter held her in its quiet.

She thought: I haven't asked.

She thought: I need to ask.

She went inside.

~~~

She didn't ask immediately.

That was the honest account of what happened. She went inside and she ate breakfast and she worked and the day was the day and she carried the awareness that she needed to ask alongside all the other things she carried, and she worked with it the way she worked with things that needed time before she could approach them directly.

She told Scotty that evening.

Not everything. Not yet. But the outline of what Vigil had shown her, the thing she'd been not-thinking about, the question she hadn't asked.

He listened.

When she finished he said: "How long?"

She thought about it. "Since the beginning," she said. "I think. I think I stopped asking in the first weeks when asking was too much and I never started again." She paused. "I didn't know I wasn't asking. I thought I'd settled it."

He was quiet for a moment.

He said: "You settled the grief. You didn't settle the question."

She held this.

She said: "Yes. That's right."

He held her gaze. "Is it a question you want to ask?"

She thought about what wanting meant in this context, the specific character of wanting something that was also frightening, the particular complexity of wanting to know an answer that could be many different things.

She said: "I need to ask it. Whether I want to or not." She held his gaze. "I've been building here. What I'm building is real. What we're building is real." She held his gaze steadily. "Knowing whether the door exists doesn't change any of that. But not knowing�" she paused� "not knowing is a weight I didn't know I was carrying."

He held her gaze for a long moment.

He said: "Then ask."

She said: "Yes."

She said: "I'm going to need to go through the Constant."

He said: "When?"

She said: "Tomorrow."

He said: "I'll be here when you get back."

She looked at him.

She thought about what he'd offered, the specific quality of it, the steadiness that didn't require her to manage how she was before or after or to protect him from the size of the question.

She said: "Thank you."

He said: "Ask first. Thank me after."

She did.

~~~

She contacted the Constant the next morning.

Not the channel's full activation, not the kind of contact she'd initiated on Beowulf. The lighter version, the ambient contact that the channel allowed without full opening, the way you could hear through a door without opening it.

She asked the question.

She asked it directly and without hedging, because the Constant received direct questions better than indirect ones and because she'd learned that the quality of what came back through the channel corresponded to the quality of what went in.

The question was: is there a way back?

The Constant's response was long.

Not in the dramatic sense. Not the kind of response that arrived with weight and restructured everything. The careful response of something very old that was being asked about something very specific and was taking the question seriously.

What came back through the channel was this:

The portal that had displaced her was not a unique mechanism. It was a specific application of something that existed in the interface's natural physics, a point of instability that had been held open by the ritual and that had closed when the ritual's energy was expended.

Closed was not the same as gone.

The interface retained the record of what had passed through it in the way that water retained the record of what had moved through it, not visibly, not permanently, but as a residual configuration in the local interface structure at the point of passage.

The point of passage was Earth.

Specifically, the location in Sunnydale, California that had been the Hellmouth, which was also the location where the portal had opened, which was also the location that had the highest concentration of interface activity in the geological record of that region of space.

The Hellmouth was not a random location. She'd known this from the record. Something had made it a Hellmouth deliberately, introducing the instability that the portal had exploited.

The instability at that location was not closed. It was dormant.

The interface's physics, at a location with that history, did not return to neutral. It retained the configuration, the way a channel in soil retained its shape after the water had passed. The configuration was not active. It was not open. But it was there.

Whether it was activatable from this end, from this century, without the ritual that had originally opened it � the Constant did not know. The physics was Haverstock's question rather than the Constant's.

She came back from the contact.

She sat in the room for a long time.

She thought about what she'd received.

The door was not gone. The door was dormant. Whether the door could be opened from this side, from this century, across two thousand years of time and however many light-years of space, was a question she didn't have the answer to yet.

She wrote to Haverstock.

She wrote carefully, because the question she was asking had implications that went in several directions and she needed to be precise about which direction she was asking about.

She wrote: the portal that displaced me was a specific application of interface instability at a location on Old Earth that has the highest concentration of historical interface activity in the geological record of that region. The Constant tells me the instability is dormant rather than closed. I want to know whether it is theoretically activatable from this side, from this century. Not whether we should. Whether it's physically possible.

She sent it.

She sat at her desk and looked at the case with the first Clinkscales document in it and thought about what she'd asked and what the answer might be and what the answer meant for the shape of things.

The Keeper was very present.

She said, to the Keeper: "You knew about this."

The Keeper's quality was the quality it had when it had known about something and had been waiting for the right moment.

"The right moment was Vigil," she said.

The Keeper's quality did not deny this.

She thought about treecats and the bond and the specific gift of something that read what you were carrying and reflected it back at the moment when reflecting it back was what you needed.

She thought about what she owed Vigil for this.

She thought: carrots. Sascha had mentioned that Vigil had a fondness for a specific variety of Sphinx root vegetable that was in the same general category as carrots. She thought: a great many carrots.

She held the thought alongside the larger thought and let them both be present simultaneously, which was a thing she'd gotten better at, the capacity to hold the small human reality and the large consequential reality in the same moment without one displacing the other.

Then she went to find Scotty and tell him what the Constant had said.

~~~

Haverstock's response took four days.

Four days was fast for Haverstock, who thought at the pace of someone whose thinking was thorough by nature and who had learned that incomplete thorough thinking was less useful than complete thorough thinking regardless of the timeline.

The response was long.

She read it twice.

The theoretical answer was: yes. With significant qualification.

The interface instability at the Hellmouth location was real and retained in the interface's structure in the way the Constant had described. The physics of the channel that the lineage modification provided had, in theory, the capacity to interact with a dormant instability at sufficient range if the interaction was mediated by a sufficiently powerful interface presence.

The sufficiently powerful interface presence was the question.

The channel as it currently operated, even at full activation through the lineage network, was not sufficient to reach across two thousand years of time and however many light-years to interact with a dormant instability on Old Earth.

The interface variation, however, was relevant.

The interface variation that the mapping array had been tracking, the slow movement of the interface boundary toward human space over the past forty years, was reducing the effective distance between the current interface structure and the historical one. Not quickly. Not on a human timescale. But measurably.

At the current rate of variation, the effective distance would reach the threshold at which a sufficiently powered interface interaction might be possible in approximately � Haverstock's estimate was careful and ranged � forty to sixty years.

Not in her lifetime.

Unless.

The unless was the part that required her to sit with for a long time.

Unless the interface variation was not the only variable. The variation was being caused by something, which she knew. The thing that had made the Hellmouth was doing the causing. The interface was being moved for its own purposes, which were not her purposes, and the movement was producing a side effect that was potentially useful to her in a way the mover hadn't intended.

But the presences. The Constant and Witness and the others. They had their own capacity for interface interaction, independent of the variation. If they were coordinating, if they were applying their combined capacity in the same direction at the same moment�

Haverstock's estimate for the combined scenario was different.

He wrote: if the presences can coordinate their interface interaction toward the Hellmouth location's dormant instability, the effective threshold might be reachable within a decade. The uncertainty in this estimate is very high because I don't have good data on the presences' individual interface capacity and the combination effect is theoretical. But the physics doesn't prohibit it.

She read that paragraph three times.

A decade.

She was twenty-one.

She was going to be thirty-one in a decade.

She sat with this for a long time.

Then she went to the Vigil's working space and found Yeva, because whatever she was going to do with this information she wasn't going to do it without Yeva in the room, and Yeva needed to know.

Not because the question was Yeva's to answer. It wasn't. The question was hers. It had always been hers.

But the Vigil was not something she did alone and the things she was working through were not things she worked through alone, and Yeva had become, over three years, the person inside the Vigil who understood the full shape of what Buffy was in a way that nobody else in the room quite did in the same way.

She said: "I need to tell you something."

Yeva said: "Tell me."

She told her.

All of it. The Vigil showing her the submerged grief. The question she hadn't asked. The Constant's response. Haverstock's physics. The decade estimate.

Yeva listened with the complete attention she always brought and when Buffy finished she was quiet for a long time.

She said: "What are you going to do with it?"

Buffy said: "I don't know yet."

Yeva held her gaze.

She said: "You've been building here for three years. Everything you've built is real." She paused. "The question of whether you go back is separate from whether what you've built is real."

"I know," Buffy said.

Yeva said: "Do you?"

She held Yeva's gaze.

She thought about the specific worry she'd had that looking at the door would make what she'd built feel provisional. She thought about whether that worry had been prescient or protective or both.

She said: "I'm working on knowing it."

Yeva said: "That's honest."

She said: "Yes."

They held each other's gaze.

Yeva said: "Whatever you decide. The Vigil exists. I exist. What you've built doesn't go away if you go back. It goes forward. Without you for a while, maybe." She held Buffy's gaze. "But what you built was always meant to go forward without you eventually. That's why you built it the way you built it."

Buffy held this.

She thought about the Council and its model, the one Slayer always at the center, everything dependent on the one. She thought about the Vigil and the deliberate building of something that distributed the carrying rather than concentrating it.

She thought: I built it so it didn't need me.

She thought: that was always the point.

She thought: I didn't know until this moment that was also preparation for this.

She said: "I need to think."

Yeva said: "Take the time."

She said: "The work continues."

Yeva said: "The work always continues." She held Buffy's gaze. "That's what you taught us."

She looked at Yeva for a long moment.

She thought about who Yeva was becoming and about what twenty-five looked like on her and about the ceiling that was still not visible.

She thought: yes. She'll be fine. Better than fine.

She said: "Thank you."

Yeva said: "You're welcome." She paused. "Now go think."

She went.
