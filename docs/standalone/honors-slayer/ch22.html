Chapter 22 (13 of 54 in this book)

The boarding team assembled eight days before the Eirene Constantia's scheduled arrival, which gave them six days to brief and train for a situation none of them had trained for.

There were five of them, selected by Usher with the Queen's approval, and they had in common the specific quality of people who had been selected for difficult things before and had come through those things without losing the capacity for the next difficult thing. They ranged from thirty to fifty, three men and two women, with backgrounds in naval special operations and intelligence work and one, a woman named Reva Castellan, whose specific background Usher described as diverse in a way that Buffy understood to mean things Usher wasn't going to specify.

She met them in a secure room in Usher's building and told them the truth.

She told it the way she'd learned to tell it in the past two months, which was directly and completely and without the hedging that turned necessary information into a negotiation. She told them what vampires were and what they could do and what killed them and what didn't and what the Eirene Constantia likely contained and what the boarding was going to find.

The room was quiet in the specific way of professionals receiving unexpected information and processing it through the framework of their existing competence rather than through the framework of disbelief.

Castellan asked the first question. "The stake. Hardwood specifically?"

"Hardwood works better. Other woods work but less reliably. Composite materials generally don't work at all." She paused. "Decapitation works regardless of material. Sunlight works. Fire works but is operationally messy." She looked around the room. "We're going to be in a ship. Fire is not an option."

"What about energy weapons?" That was one of the men, an older man named Brandis who had the bearing of someone who had spent significant time in zero-gravity environments.

"Depends on the energy level and the delivery. A graser at the levels they use in ship combat would work. A hand stunner probably won't. I've never tested it systematically." She paused. "Stakes are reliable. Decapitation is reliable. Those are the tools we plan around."

"The ship's crew," said the second woman, Osei. "Human or�"

"Likely both." She'd thought about this. "The master's immediate circle is probably vampires. The crew will be a mix. Some human, possibly some turned, possibly some who are aware of what they're working for and some who aren't." She paused. "We treat human crew as non-combatants until they demonstrate otherwise. We don't kill humans."

"Even if they're complicit?"

"Complicit humans are a legal problem for after the operation." She met the questioner's gaze. "We're not judge and jury. We're the boarding team."

The briefing went for three hours. She answered every question they asked and some they were working toward but hadn't formulated yet, and by the end the room had the specific quality that rooms had when people had absorbed something fundamentally reality-altering and had decided to work with it anyway.

Castellan stayed when the others left.

She was forty-two and had the face of someone who made difficult judgments regularly and had made her peace with the cost of that. She looked at Buffy with dark eyes that were doing something different from the assessment they'd been doing during the briefing.

"How old are you?" she said.

"Seventeen." Buffy held her gaze. "Almost eighteen."

Castellan looked at her for a moment. "I've been in this line of work for twenty years," she said. "I've done things in that time that I don't regret but that I know the cost of." She paused. "I want you to know that I understand what you're carrying."

Buffy looked at her. "Do you?"

"Probably not fully," Castellan said. "But more than most people you're going to meet." She paused. "I wanted to say that before the operation. After operations there's less time for it."

Buffy thought about the things people had said to her that had mattered. The ones that had come at the right moment and taken root. She thought about what Castellan was doing, which was offering a specific kind of recognition that was different from the recognition she'd received from Honor and from the Queen and from Scotty, different in kind rather than in quality.

"Thank you," she said.

Castellan nodded once. "I'll see you in six days."

~~~

The six days she spent doing two things simultaneously, which was becoming her characteristic operational mode.

She was addressing the remaining logistics of the boarding, working through the approach with Brandis and Osei, who had the most relevant boarding experience and who were, she found, exactly the kind of professionals she worked best with, which was the kind who said what they thought and did what they said and didn't require management.

She was also running the Slayer sense through the warehouse district every night, following the remaining population and tracking the changes that had started happening since she'd eliminated six of the seven.

The changes were informative.

The remaining drone, the one she hadn't found yet, had gone to ground in a way that said it knew something had changed. Its movement pattern, which Usher's files had documented over years of observation, had become compressed and irregular, no longer the established rounds of a confident predator but the compressed hiding behavior of something that had registered threat and was waiting for additional information.

Something was giving it information.

The communication device she'd taken from the sixth had been transmitting when she found it. She'd interrupted the transmission. Which meant something on the other end had noticed the interruption.

The Eirene Constantia already knew something was wrong in Landing.

She told Usher.

Usher was quiet for a moment and then said, "Do we move up the timeline?"

She thought about it. "No. Moving up the timeline means losing the advantage of the scheduled port visit. If the ship changes course now it goes somewhere we don't control and the intelligence value of the communication device becomes the only thing we have." She paused. "But we need to assume the master is already in a different operational posture than they would have been."

"More alert," Usher said.

"More alert. Which means the boarding approach has to account for that." She thought. "It also means the remaining drone is probably going to receive instructions. If I can find it before it acts on those instructions�"

"You'd know what the instructions were."

"Yes." She looked at her map. "I need to find it tonight."

~~~

She found it at 0100 in a part of the warehouse district she hadn't worked yet, a section closer to the water where the older buildings had been standing long enough to develop the kind of accumulated history in their structure that provided excellent concealment for things that needed concealment.

The drone was old enough to be careful and young enough to have been established here rather than predating the Eirene Constantia's route, which made it a local creation, and it was in communication with something, she could feel the specific quality of purpose that direction gave to a vampire's movement.

She followed it rather than addressing it immediately.

She followed it through four blocks of the warehouse district to a location she hadn't previously mapped, a building that was not a warehouse but a commercial facility of some kind, shuttered and dark, with the specific quality of a space that was being used for something other than its registered purpose.

She watched it go in.

She waited fifteen minutes.

Then she went in.

The building had three levels. The drone was on the second, in a room that had been a storage space and had become something else, and it was not alone.

There were three of them. The drone and two others she hadn't seen before, which meant either they'd been here and she'd missed them or they'd arrived since she'd started working the district. The latter seemed more likely. The Eirene Constantia's network had other nodes. It had been able to send reinforcements.

The three of them were in the middle of a conversation that stopped the moment she entered, which was the moment after her entry rather than during it because she'd entered quietly enough that the moment of awareness was delayed and the delay was enough.

She addressed the drone first because it was the one she had information about and therefore the one whose capabilities she could predict. The other two were faster than the drone which meant older and she worked through them in the specific sequence of fastest to slowest in reverse, the counterintuitive order that let her use the slower ones' movement patterns to constrain the faster ones' options.

It was not the cleanest fight she'd ever had. The confined space was a problem and the second of the older ones had a weapon she hadn't expected, a blade that it used with the specific competence of something that had been using edged weapons since before she was born.

She collected a cut across the left forearm, deep enough to be consequential in the short term and not consequential in the medium term, and dealt with the remaining two in quick succession and stood in the storage room with the dust settling around her and her arm bleeding in a way that would stop in approximately four minutes.

She looked at what remained, which was dust and a room that had been used for something and the specific emotional residue of a place where decisions had been made.

She'd interrupted something.

She didn't know yet what she'd interrupted. But the arrival of reinforcements and the activation of this specific location in the window before the Eirene Constantia's scheduled arrival said that the something was preparation.

The master was not coming to port on a scheduled visit.

The master was coming to find out what had happened in Landing, and possibly to do something about it.

She pulled out her communicator and called Usher.

"The situation has changed," she said. "We need to talk."

~~~

The new plan was simpler and more dangerous than the old plan, which was the direction that plans generally evolved under pressure.

The Eirene Constantia would be met at the port by the boarding team in the guise of a routine inspection, which was something that happened to commercial vessels in a system at war and which would not, by itself, trigger the master's caution before they were already aboard.

Once aboard, they would locate and address the master and whatever the master had brought with it and secure whatever intelligence the ship contained.

Buffy was going in first. This was not negotiable and she didn't negotiate it. She was going in first because the master was going to be old and capable and because the boarding team, however well briefed and however competent, did not have the Slayer sense and she did and in a situation where the immediate threat was going to be faster and stronger and more experienced than anything they'd trained against, the Slayer sense was the operational advantage that everything else depended on.

Brandis argued for a different approach. She listened to the argument completely and then explained clearly why the approach wouldn't work and watched him arrive at the same conclusion.

Castellan didn't argue. She looked at the plan and said, "What do you need from us?"

"Cover the exits," she said. "Everything that comes out of that ship that isn't me and isn't verifiably human crew, you treat as a threat. If I'm not out in twenty minutes, you come in."

"And if what comes out is�"

"If I come out and I'm not acting like myself, treat me as a threat," she said. "I won't be turned. The biology doesn't work that way. But I could be under a degree of coercion." She paused. "Trust the Slayer sense over my verbal instructions if there's any doubt."

Castellan looked at her. "You're asking me to go on gut instinct."

"I'm asking you to trust your training to recognize when something is wrong," she said. "It's different."

A beat. "All right."

She thought about Scotty, who was somewhere in the Talbott Cluster on a ship called the Hexapuma dealing with whatever the Talbott Cluster was dealing with. She thought about Honor, who was somewhere doing the thing the war needed her to do. She thought about Carson at Saganami Island and Ibanez and McKeon and Martingale and all the people who had become her context.

She thought about the Queen, who had offered resources and asked for honesty.

She was going to need to be very honest when this was over, about the scope of what she'd found and what it was going to take to address it.

But first she was going to go aboard the Eirene Constantia and deal with whatever was on it.

That part first.

She spent the night before sharpening her stakes and checking the knife and sleeping six hours and running at four in the morning and eating an enormous breakfast and going to the waterfront and standing at the railing one more time.

The harbor water was still the wrong color. She looked at it.

The Eirene Constantia was due in six hours.

She looked at the water and thought about the thing she'd been thinking about since the communication device and the map of explored space and the scope of what the network implied. She thought about the long shape of what was ahead, which was not just the boarding and not just the Landing population and not just the Manticore system, which was the whole of explored space and the thing that had moved through it and established itself in ways that two thousand years of human expansion had never adequately addressed because nobody had known it was there.

The Slayer lineage was out there.

The network was out there.

Both of those things were true simultaneously and the thing that connected them was her, the fact of her, this specific person at this specific intersection of two thousand years and a portal and the decision she'd made in a library on Hades to let herself become what she was becoming.

She turned from the railing.

Six hours.

She was going to be ready in five.

She walked back into the city and the city held her, already familiar in the way of a place you've inhabited long enough to stop noticing the ways it doesn't fit and start noticing the ways it does. She moved through it and it moved around her and she was here, in this place, at this time, with this purpose.

That was enough.

That was, as it turned out, exactly enough.
