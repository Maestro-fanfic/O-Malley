Chapter 59 (50 of 54 in this book)

She was thirty-one when she wrote the last entry in the document.

She wrote it in the Sphinx kitchen on a morning in late spring, with the garden outside the window doing what established gardens did in late spring, which was the full expression of what they'd been building toward through the winter and the early spring, everything that had been holding itself in reserve now present all at once.

She wrote:

This is the last entry I'm going to write in this form. Not the last thing I'll write � the record continues and I'll continue contributing to it for as long as I'm here to contribute. But this specific document, the one that started as notes on what I'd received from the record and became the account of what happened in this generation, is complete.

I want to say a few things to whoever reads this.

First: you are not alone. Whatever you're carrying, whatever you're navigating, whatever the specific version of this work looks like in your time � you are part of something that has been held by many people across a very long time, and those people held it because they believed it mattered and because they couldn't not hold it. You are in that tradition. The tradition is in you whether you know it or not. Now you know it.

Second: the work is large. Larger than any one person or any one generation. This is not a warning. It is the condition of the work, and the condition is what makes it possible to give everything you have to the part that is yours without requiring yourself to carry the whole of it. Your part is yours. Someone before you held the part before yours. Someone after you will hold the part after yours. Trust the distribution. It is what makes the long work possible.

Third: the Slayer is not the Vigil and the Vigil is not the Slayer. The Slayer is what the lineage produces when the channel opens fully in a specific person. The Vigil is what the lineage produces when it is connected and supported and given the infrastructure it needs to be what it is. Both are necessary. Neither is sufficient alone. If you are reading this and you are a fully expressed Slayer, you need the Vigil as much as the Vigil needs you. If you are reading this and you are a subclinical carrier doing the research or the diplomacy or the archival work or any of the other forms the Vigil takes, you are as essential as the Slayer. Remember that.

Fourth: be afraid of the right things and not the wrong things. The right fears are the ones that orient you toward caution and preparation. The wrong fears are the ones that tell you what you've built is not real or that the people in it cannot carry it without you. I spent time with both kinds of fears before I learned to tell them apart. The telling apart is the work.

Fifth: the Girl is always there. The channel carries her. She is the lineage's accumulated consciousness and she is in every person who carries the modification, fully expressed or subclinical, and when you feel something at the edge of perception that you cannot fully articulate, that is her, and she is trying to tell you something, and it is worth listening to.

Sixth: love the people in your life. Not instead of the work. Alongside it. The work and the life are not in competition. The one makes the other possible and the other makes the one sustainable. I learned this slowly and imperfectly and I am still learning it and it is the truest thing I know about how to do this.

Seventh and last: the door is not always closed. What seems like an ending is sometimes an interrupted thing waiting to be completed. Look at the doors you've been not-looking at. They usually lead somewhere important. The looking is the first step.

She stopped writing.

She read what she'd written.

She thought: yes. That's what I wanted to say.

She thought: that's the thing I would have needed to read.

She closed the document.

She looked out the window at the garden.

The rosemary equivalent was in its fifth spring and it was doing what things in their fifth spring did, which was to be entirely itself, no longer tentative, no longer finding its ground, simply present in the ground it had found, and the smell of it came through the window on the morning air and it was not her mother's rosemary and it was the rosemary she had here and it was good.

Scotty was somewhere in the house, the ambient quality of him present the way the Keeper's quality was present, the specific warmth of knowing someone was there.

Vigil was in the canopy.

She felt the bond.

She thought: thirty-one.

She thought: here.

She got up from the table.

She went to the door and out into the garden and the late spring morning was doing what it was doing and she walked into it and it held her in the specific way of mornings in places you have lived long enough to know.

The garden was around her. The trees were above her. The Sphinx air was the Sphinx air. The bond was the bond. The Keeper was present. The work was ahead.

She was here.

She was completely, entirely, specifically here.

She thought: this is what after looks like.

Not a single moment of arrival that announced itself. Not a destination. The ongoing specific quality of a life that was happening, that was in its middle rather than its ending, that had the work and the people and the wrong stars that were hers.

She thought about all the versions of herself that had existed before this one. The fifteen-year-old in Hemery who hadn't known yet. The seventeen-year-old in Sunnydale who had known too much too fast. The seventeen-year-old on the Tepes who had been something the Slayer reduced to its most ancient form. The girl who had come back to herself in the jungle and the girl who had walked into Manticore for the first time and the girl who had sat across from the Queen at the harbor railing and the girl who had stood in the Hellmouth's underground space and set a letter on the stone floor.

All of them were her. All of them had been building toward this, the specific version of herself that was standing in the established garden of a property she'd built on a planet that had been new to her and was now home.

She thought about the record and what she'd written and the seven things and the document that was going to be in the archive and would be read by people she'd never meet in a future she couldn't picture.

She thought: they'll have this. They'll know what we were doing.

She thought: forward. Always forward.

And then she stopped thinking about the future and was simply present in the morning, the late spring morning of her thirty-first year on a planet she hadn't known existed when she was seventeen, with a treecat in the canopy and a garden established and the work ahead and the life here.

She breathed.

She let it be what it was.

It was very good.

Epilogue

She was thirty-nine when the second window opened.

Not exactly thirty-nine. Thirty-nine years and four months, which was close enough that she thought of it as thirty-nine. The calculation had proved approximately correct: eleven years from the first window, the presences' alignment producing the second opportunity to reach the Hellmouth's dormant interface.

This time she did not go alone.

Yeva went with her.

Not because she needed Yeva operationally. She'd been through the transit once and knew what it was. But the Vigil was eight years further along and Yeva was its operational lead and the intelligence value of two people with two perspectives at the other end was double the intelligence value of one, and more than that, the second visit to Old Earth should have more than one set of eyes and one set of hands.

She went with Yeva.

The transit was the same and different. The same in its mechanics, the channel at full activation, the presences in their alignment, the Keeper present. Different in the quality of the passage, the Girl's presence not the directed urgency of the first transit but something more settled, the consciousness that had been home once and knew the way.

They arrived underground in the dark and turned on their lights.

The space was the same.

She'd wondered if it would be the same. Eight years was nothing on the geological timescale but it was enough for things to change in human terms. The space was the same. The letter she'd left was gone, which was expected, decomposed into whatever eight years had done to it. The data chip was still there, which was not expected, the specific material of it having proved more durable than she'd assumed.

She picked it up.

She said to Yeva: "We left this the first time."

Yeva looked at it. "And it's still here."

She said: "Which means either no one found it or someone found it and put it back."

Yeva said: "Or someone found it and couldn't take it and put it back."

She held the data chip.

She said: "Someone found it."

Not certainty. The Slayer sense wasn't giving her certainty. But something in the quality of the space, the interface reading differently than it had the first time, a specific way in which the dormant instability had been�touched was the word she arrived at, the sense of something having interacted with the interface at this location since her last visit.

She said: "Someone with the channel."

Yeva looked at her.

She said: "Someone came here. Someone who could feel the interface at this location. Someone who found the chip and couldn't interpret it and put it back." She held the chip. "Someone was here."

The lineage on Earth.

She said: "We leave more this time. We leave something that doesn't require technology to receive."

Yeva said: "What does that look like?"

She said: "Writing. Physical." She looked at Yeva. "Did you bring the�"

Yeva was already reaching into her kit. She'd thought of it before the transit, which was Yeva, who thought of the things you needed before you knew you needed them.

She handed Buffy the materials.

Buffy wrote.

Not the full record. The compressed version, the essential things, in the clearest language she could produce. She wrote on the material Yeva had brought and she wrote: the Vigil exists. You are not alone. The lineage is connected across many worlds. If you feel the thing you cannot name in the dark, it is the Girl, and she is real, and the lineage is real, and we are looking for you. We will come back. Leave a mark if you can.

She left it beside the data chip.

She stood up.

She looked at the Hellmouth's interface.

She said, to the space: "We're coming back. Keep the faith."

She thought about Thomas Clinkscales saying those words across four generations, the specific faith kept without confirmation.

She thought about what it would mean to whoever read what she'd written to know that somewhere out there the faith was being kept in the other direction.

She thought: they'll know.

She turned to Yeva.

She said: "Ready?"

Yeva said: "Ready."

They activated the channel and the presences held the window and they came home.

She was fifty-one when the Vigil made first contact with the Old Earth lineage.

Not through the window. Not through the portal. Through the data chip, which had been found and which had taken twenty-one years to produce a response in a form that could travel through channels she hadn't anticipated, a specific piece of intelligence that arrived in Usher's apparatus through a chain of intermediaries so long and so unlikely that the confirmation took three months to verify.

But verified.

Someone on Old Earth had found the chip. Someone on Old Earth had spent years developing the capacity to respond. Someone on Old Earth had chosen to reach across whatever infrastructure they'd developed to say: we're here.

She was in the Sphinx kitchen when Carson came to tell her, which was where she was often at seven in the morning, and he came in with the expression she recognized from Hades, the expression of someone who had been right about something for thirty years and was standing in the presence of that rightness fully confirmed.

She read the confirmation three times.

She looked at Carson.

He said: "We found them."

She said: "They found us."

He held her gaze.

She said: "It took thirty years."

He said: "The Clinkscales family kept faith for four generations."

She held his gaze.

She said: "Set up the communication framework. Whatever they can receive and send, we match." She held his gaze. "I want to talk to them."

He said: "Yes."

She said: "And Carson." She held his gaze. "Write this down."

He said: "I'm already writing."

She looked at him, at the man who had been the Vigil's keeper of the record since before it was the Vigil, who had been doing what his family had done for four generations and was now watching the thing the four generations had kept faith with bear fruit in ways that Thomas Clinkscales had spent his life hoping for and had not quite seen and which his grandson was seeing now.

She thought about Thomas. He'd been gone for three years. She thought about him with the warmth of someone who had known and loved someone and had the knowledge that the loving had been real and the knowing had been real and the absence was real and none of those three things displaced the others.

She thought: he would have been glad.

She thought: I'll be glad for both of us.

She said: "Call the full Vigil. Everyone. This is the kind of news that's told in person."

Carson said: "I'll call."

She stood up from the table.

She looked out the window at the Sphinx garden, at the established garden, the thing that had been growing for nine years and was doing what things did when they had enough time and enough care and enough ground to grow in.

She thought about the lineage on Old Earth, carrying the modification and the dreams and the Girl's ancient presence without the vocabulary for any of it, doing what the lineage had always done, which was continuing.

She thought about what she was going to say to them.

She thought: you're not alone.

She thought: we've been looking for you.

She thought: the door was never closed. We just needed time to find each other.

She went to tell the Vigil.

She went, and the morning was the morning and the garden was the garden and Vigil was in the canopy and the bond was the bond, and she was here, fifty-one, alive and building and still going forward, and the lineage was connected, and the work was ongoing, and it was good.

It was very, very good.

And she was here.

She was always here.

Forward.

Always, always forward.
