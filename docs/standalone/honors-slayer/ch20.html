Chapter 20 (11 of 54 in this book)

Six weeks after Scotty left, something came out of the dark.

Not metaphorically. Or not only metaphorically. The distinction was becoming harder to maintain as she spent more time in this century, which had its own relationship with the dark and what lived in it, and the relationship was more complicated than the previous century's had been even when the previous century had included the Hellmouth.

It started with a dead man in the warehouse district.

She learned about it from Fritz, which was itself a piece of information. Fritz learned about it from Petrakis, who had been called as a consulting physician and who had come away from the consultation with the expression of a man who had encountered something that didn't fit his existing framework and had immediately thought of someone who collected things that didn't fit existing frameworks.

Fritz came to her quarters at seven in the evening and sat down across from her and said, "The police found a body this morning. In the warehouse district. Drained of blood."

She put down the book she'd been reading.

"Drained how?" she said.

"Completely. With no obvious external wound consistent with the volume." He met her eyes. "There were two puncture marks on the neck."

She looked at him.

He looked back at her.

"No," she said. "That's not possible."

"I know." He was quiet for a moment. "And yet."

She thought about two thousand years. She thought about the Hellmouth and the way demon activity clustered around nexus points of mystical energy and the way the specific cluster of activity she'd grown up in had been one of the most active in human history but had never been the only one. She thought about the fact that she was the Slayer and that the Slayer existed because something had always needed slaying, in every era, in every civilization, regardless of whether the civilization had a word for it or not.

She thought about two thousand years of human expansion through the stars and about what might have come with humanity into those stars, patient and quiet and waiting.

"Where's the body?" she said.

"The city morgue. Petrakis has access." Fritz paused. "He doesn't know what to make of it. He's filed the preliminary report with standard unexplained cause of death notation. He's holding the body pending further consultation."

"I need to see it."

"I assumed you would." He paused. "I also need to tell you something else."

She waited.

"This isn't the first one," he said. "I ran a search through the city's medical records after Petrakis told me. The notation pattern is consistent with two other deaths in the past month. Both filed as unexplained. Both in the warehouse district."

Three deaths. A month. A pattern.

She knew what a pattern looked like. She'd been reading patterns since she was fifteen.

"Okay," she said. She stood up. "Let's go."

~~~

The city morgue was a building that did its function with the particular spare efficiency of a place that had been doing the same function for a long time and had optimized everything about itself around that function. It smelled the way morgues smelled, the universal smell that crossed every cultural and temporal boundary she'd encountered, antiseptic over something that was never entirely masked.

Petrakis met them at the entrance with the expression of a man who had been thinking very hard since his conversation with Fritz and who had reached the conclusion that thinking was not going to be sufficient and that someone with a different kind of expertise was required.

He looked at Buffy in the specific way that people looked at her when they were about to ask her for something that they didn't entirely have language for.

"Lady Buffy," he said. "Thank you for coming."

"What can you tell me that's not in the preliminary report?" she said.

He was quiet for a moment, the kind of quiet that assembled itself into an answer. "The exsanguination is complete by every measure I can apply. There is no blood remaining in the circulatory system. The heart shows evidence of continued function after the blood loss began, which is�" he paused� "not consistent with traumatic blood loss of this speed or volume."

"The heart kept going after the blood was gone."

"For some time. Based on the myocardial evidence, yes." He paused. "As if the blood was removed continuously, faster than the body could fail in response to it." He looked at her. "I don't have a mechanism for that."

"I do," she said. "Show me the body."

He showed her.

The body was a man in his late thirties, unremarkable in every way that bodies were unremarkable in death, which was to say completely. The puncture marks on the neck were small and precise and already beginning to close in the way of wounds that had been made by something that contained a coagulant.

She looked at them for a long time.

The Slayer sense was doing something she hadn't felt it do since she arrived in this century, a specific activation that was different from the general hypervigilance of her daily operational state. Something had been in this room and was not here now and the trace of it was still present, the way a scent was still present after the source had gone, faint and specific and unmistakable.

She knew that trace.

She straightened up and looked at Fritz and Petrakis, who were both watching her with the focused attention of professionals encountering something outside their expertise and managing this professionally.

"It's a vampire," she said.

The silence that followed was the silence of two highly trained physicians receiving information that required complete recategorization of their existing model.

Petrakis said, carefully, "The theological literature�"

"This isn't theology," she said. "It's biology. A specific demon subtype that infects humans and converts them into something that requires blood to function and that is technically dead by every standard measure except mobility and intention." She paused. "I've been dealing with them since I was fifteen. These marks are consistent."

Another silence.

"On Earth," Fritz said. Not a challenge. Working out the implications.

"On Earth two thousand years ago, yes." She looked at the body. "The question is how it got here. Which I don't know yet." She paused. "The secondary question is how many. Which based on three deaths in a month is at least one and possibly more."

"If it's been here longer than a month�" Fritz started.

"Then there could be significantly more." She looked at him. "I need to understand the city's unexplained death records for the past year. Longer if you can access it. The pattern will be there if you know what you're looking for."

"What am I looking for?"

"Exsanguination. Any note of neck wounds. Deaths that were attributed to causes that felt wrong to the filing physician but couldn't be explained otherwise." She paused. "And missing persons. Vampires don't always leave bodies where they're found. Sometimes the body becomes another vampire."

Fritz was making notes. Petrakis was making notes. She looked at the dead man on the table and thought about what she owed him, which was the same thing she owed everyone who ended up like this, which was the effort of preventing it from happening to someone else.

"I'm going to need to patrol," she said.

Both physicians looked at her.

"The warehouse district. At night. That's where the bodies are and that's where whatever is doing this is going to be operating." She paused. "I need you to not tell anyone I'm doing it. Not officially. The last thing this needs is an official response that puts uninformed people in the vicinity of a vampire."

"The Queen's office�" Fritz started.

"Lieutenant Chen can know. I'll tell her myself." She paused. "But carefully. Framed correctly." She looked at the body one more time. "This is what I'm for," she said. It came out quieter than she'd intended. "This specific thing. Right here."

Fritz looked at her with the expression he had when he was thinking about her rather than thinking about the data she represented. "Yes," he said. "I know."

She went home and sharpened her stake.

She didn't have many stakes. She'd had more on the Tepes, improvised from the ship's materials, but they'd been left behind in the operational chaos of the escape. She had two that she'd made on the Wayfarer from a piece of equipment housing that had been scheduled for disposal and which she'd requested from the maintenance chief with an explanation that was technically accurate if not complete. She had the black knife, which was not a stake but which she knew how to use in the relevant ways.

She sat on her bed and looked at them and thought about the specific shape of what she was about to do, which was the shape she'd been doing her whole Slayer life, and felt it settle into her like water finding its level.

This was the work.

Everything else�the Queen and the politics and the war and the long project of building a life in this century�all of that was also real and also necessary and she was committed to it. But this was the oldest part, the part that had been true since the first girl in the first dark had been made into the thing that fought back.

She picked up a stake and turned it in her hands and felt the Girl very present in the back of her mind, attentive and still, not pushing, just there.

Ready.

~~~

She went out the first night at midnight.

The warehouse district was three kilometers from her quarters, on the harbor side of the city where the working water that she'd looked at from the railing connected to the infrastructure that served it. At night it had the character that warehouse districts always had, functional and depopulated, the daytime industry having packed itself away and left behind the specific emptiness of purpose-built space between purposes.

She moved through it the way she moved through everything at night, which was with the completeness of the Slayer sense operating at full register. The air here was the air of a working port, overlaid with salt and industry and the specific smell of stored things, and beneath all of that the trace she'd caught in the morgue was present in a diffuse way that said the source of it used this area as territory.

Territory was the right word. She'd learned early that vampires were territorial in the specific way of predators who had a feeding ground and defended it against competition. Understanding the territory was the first step toward understanding the population.

She moved through the district systematically, not rushing, letting the Slayer sense do its mapping work. The territory was large, maybe fifteen city blocks, and had the characteristic signs of established use rather than recent arrival. Certain alleys had more of the trace than others. Certain areas showed less of it, which meant either the territory had boundaries or there was more than one source with different preferred zones.

She filed it all.

She didn't find anything on the first night.

This was not unusual. Finding something on the first night of a new patrol was the exception rather than the rule. The rule was patience and accumulated information and the moment when the pattern became dense enough to act on.

She went back to her quarters at three in the morning and slept and woke up at seven and told Lieutenant Chen.

Chen's response surprised her, which was itself information.

Chen listened to the complete picture, the three deaths and the morgue and the patrol and what she intended to do, with the attentive composure that characterized all of Chen's professional conduct. And then she said, "I'll need to inform Her Majesty."

"I expected that."

"Her Majesty will want to support this operation."

"I expected that too. I'd rather operate alone. Support in the conventional military sense creates more risk than it addresses."

"Her Majesty will understand that," Chen said. "What she'll want to provide is information access and the ability to act on anything you find that requires official response." She paused. "Not control. Resources."

Buffy thought about the Queen at the harbor railing. The offer she'd made, which was resources in exchange for honesty about what she could and couldn't give. "Okay. Information access would help. I want everything the city has on unexplained deaths in the past three years, not just the past month."

"I'll have it by this afternoon."

"And I want a direct channel. If I find something on a given night that needs immediate official response, I need to be able to reach someone who can act without a two-hour approval chain."

"I can arrange that." Chen paused. "Is there anything else?"

She thought about it. "Stakes," she said. "I need more stakes. Wood, hardwood specifically. Something that can be machined to a point."

A pause that was slightly longer than the previous ones. "I'll add that to the list," Chen said, in the tone of someone who was adding something to a list they hadn't anticipated adding.

The information arrived by afternoon. She spent four hours going through it.

The pattern was there.

Going back eighteen months, if you knew what you were looking for. Twelve unexplained deaths with characteristics consistent with exsanguination. Four missing persons who had disappeared from the warehouse district and its adjacent neighborhoods. A cluster in the winter months that suggested either a spike in activity or a period when something had been less careful.

She built a map.

The territory was cleaner than she'd thought. One central zone with two satellite areas, the pattern of a small group rather than a single hunter. Three to five, based on the activity density. The winter cluster suggested something that had been disturbed during that period, a change in the population, either an addition or a departure that had disrupted established patterns before they'd re-stabilized.

She looked at the map for a long time.

Then she went back out.

~~~

She found one on the third night.

Not by seeing it. By the Slayer sense registering its presence in an alley off the main warehouse thoroughfare, the specific signal of something that was functionally human and fundamentally wrong, the two components of the reading that together added up to the only thing they could add up to.

She went into the alley.

It was young. That was the first thing. In vampire terms young meant recently turned, still carrying significant trace of whoever it had been before the turning, the human patterns of movement and expression not yet entirely subsumed into the predator's economy. This one moved like a person who had been a person recently, with the hesitation and the physical uncertainty of the newly made.

It saw her at the same moment she saw it.

For a moment they looked at each other.

She'd had a thousand variations of this moment. The moment before, where everything that came after was implicit but not yet real. She'd learned not to use the moment for anything except reading whatever was readable. Some things could be read in this moment. Some things couldn't.

What she read in this one was confusion and hunger in proportions that said the confusion was losing the ongoing argument with the hunger, and that the hunger hadn't won yet but would, and that the window for anything other than the standard resolution was very small and closing.

"You don't have to do this," she said.

It looked at her. The human face was young, male, maybe twenty before the turning, the residue of someone who had been someone's son and probably someone's friend and had ended up in the warehouse district at night for reasons she would never know and which had led to this.

"What are you?" it said. The voice was the voice of the turning, rough-edged, not yet settled into the register that vampires developed when they'd been vampires for a while.

"The Slayer," she said.

It didn't know what that meant. Of course it didn't. The word was two thousand years old and the specific tradition that had given it meaning was lost to time and the thing in front of her had been human a month ago and had no framework for it.

"I'm the person whose job this is," she said. Which was a more functional answer.

It lunged.

She put it down with the efficiency of someone for whom this was muscle memory across two years and two thousand years simultaneously, the Girl's ancient knowledge and her own modern training arriving at the same conclusion through different paths. The stake was in her hand and then it was in the vampire and then the vampire was dust and she was standing in an alley in a city she'd been in for three months and the dust settled on the harbor-salt air.

She stood there for a moment.

This was always the moment that had a quality to it that nothing else had. Not triumph. Not grief exactly. Something that was its own category, the feeling of a thing having been what it was going to be, the specific completion of an act that was irreversible and necessary and still cost something she couldn't name.

She'd never found a word for it in any language she'd spoken.

She filed it the way she filed all the things she didn't have words for yet and went back into the thoroughfare and continued the patrol.

~~~

She found two more that night.

Not the young one's maker, she could tell by the age of the trace. Something older, more established, the kind of vampire that had been doing this long enough for the human residue to be entirely subsumed and what remained to be purely the predator with no ambiguity about it. And another young one, this one turned longer than the first, maybe three months, who was in the process of feeding on a dockworker who had made the wrong choice about which shortcut to take home.

She pulled it off the dockworker.

The dockworker ran. She didn't watch him go; she was already dealing with the vampire, which was old enough to fight back with a level of capability the younger one hadn't had and which required her full attention and got it.

The vampire went to dust.

She found the dockworker at the end of the block, where he'd stopped running and was standing with his hands on his knees and the specific physiological presentation of someone whose body had just informed him in very direct terms about what adrenaline was for.

She checked the wound on his neck. Shallow. The feeding had been interrupted early enough that the blood loss was minor. He'd have a headache and some muscle soreness from the adrenaline and he'd be fine.

He looked up at her when she crouched beside him.

"What," he said. And then he stopped because apparently what was sufficient.

"You're going to be all right," she said. "The wound is minor. You should go to a medic and have it looked at." She paused. "Don't go through the warehouse district at night for a while."

He looked at her. He looked at the alley where the fight had been. He looked back at her. "What was that thing?"

"Something that shouldn't exist here," she said. "I'm working on that."

He opened his mouth and then closed it again and then said, with the pragmatism of a man who had decided that the direct evidence of his own experience was going to have to be sufficient: "Right. Okay." He straightened up. "Thank you."

"Go get that looked at," she said.

He went.

She stood in the street for a moment and thought about the word shouldn't. It was the right word. Whatever had brought vampires to this planet in this century, it was wrong, and she was going to find out what it was, and she was going to fix it.

That was the job.

She went back to work.
