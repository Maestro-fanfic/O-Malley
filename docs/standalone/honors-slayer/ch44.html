The message from Haven came on a Wednesday.

Not through official channels. Not through any of the diplomatic routes that the war's slow winding down had been opening over the past six months. Through Usher, which was how messages came when they were being careful about being messages.

She read it in Usher's office with Usher across the desk and the specific quality of the room that Usher's office always had, which was the absence of anything extraneous.

The message was from someone in Haven's internal intelligence apparatus who had used a contact chain that crossed four different intermediaries and had arrived at Usher's desk with the provenance that Usher trusted, which was the only provenance Usher worked with.

The message said: we have a situation in the Barnett system that is outside our operational capacity. We understand you may have resources that are relevant. We are requesting contact.

She read it twice.

"Barnett," she said.

"A Havenite system," Usher said. "Deep within their space. Significant naval installation." She paused. "Also, based on what the message implies and what I've been developing from other sources for the past month, something that looks familiar to your work."

She looked at Usher. "How long have you known?"

"That something unusual was happening in Barnett? Four months. That it was in the specific category relevant to the Vigil?" Usher met her gaze. "Six weeks."

"You've been developing it for six weeks."

"Yes."

She held Usher's gaze. This was the kind of thing that six months ago she'd have had a more complicated response to, the intelligence professional's habit of developing things before bringing them. She understood it better now. Understanding it didn't make her entirely comfortable with it.

"Tell me what you have," she said.

Usher told her.

The Barnett situation was three months old in its current acute form and older than that in its background form. Deaths with the pattern she knew, in a system that was nominally at war with the one she was operating in. A population that Usher's sources in Barnett estimated at fifteen to twenty, which was larger than anything she'd addressed so far. And something else, something that Usher's sources described with the careful language of people who had encountered something they didn't have categories for and were trying to be accurate anyway.

Something in the Barnett system that was not the standard predatory operation.

Something that had a facility. A location. An infrastructure.

She sat with this.

"Haven is asking for help," she said.

"A faction within Haven is asking," Usher said carefully. "The request is not official. The people making it are operating outside their chain of command to make it." She paused. "Which is itself information."

"They're scared enough to go outside their chain of command."

"Yes."

She thought about the war and where it was in its slow winding down and about Haven and the people who lived there who were not the regime that had prosecuted the war, who were the ordinary people of a civilization that had made political choices she found reprehensible and who were also just people, and she thought about fifteen to twenty in a facility in Barnett and about what a facility implied.

"I need to talk to Honor," she said.

"I assumed you would," Usher said. "She's aware. I briefed her an hour ago."

She looked at Usher.

Usher held her gaze with the specific expression of someone who had made a judgment call they were prepared to defend.

"All right," Buffy said. "Next time tell me first."

"Noted," Usher said.

Barnett was three weeks in transit each way.

She thought about Decker being able to hear it.

She thought: this is working.

She thought about the war winding down and what came after the war, the relationships that could exist between Haven and the Alliance that couldn't exist while the war was ongoing.

She said: "Yes. That's useful." She held his gaze. "Talk to Honor Harrington when we get back. She's building the diplomatic and institutional relationships. She'll know how to use what you have."

Tolman looked at her.

He said: "The war is almost over."

"Yes," she said.

He said: "And then we all have the same problem."

"Yes," she said. "We do." She held his gaze. "That's the long work. The problem that doesn't respect the boundaries we draw between each other."

He was quiet for a moment.

Then he said: "All right. I'll talk to Honor Harrington."

She thought: this is how it builds.

She thought: piece by piece. Person by person. The Vigil becoming what it needs to be one conversation at a time.

She looked at the room, at Decker and Castellan and Larise and Tolman, and she thought about Eris Vandamme somewhere in the city with the accompanying presence and the work she'd been doing for eleven months and the work she'd continue doing, alongside now rather than alone.

She thought about all of it.

Then she went to sleep in the chair because the after required sleep and the chair was where she was and the floor was the alternative and she'd slept in worse positions.

She slept for four hours and woke up with the Barnett morning coming through the window and the Keeper present and the work ahead of her and she was here.

She was, as it turned out, always here.

That was the thing she'd learned in two years and two thousand years simultaneously.

You were always here.

That was where the work was.

That was where everything was.

Here.
