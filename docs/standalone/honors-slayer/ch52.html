Chapter 52 (43 of 54 in this book)

She thought for three weeks.

Not only thought. She worked, because the work was always there and the work continuing regardless of personal circumstances was both necessary and, in this case, stabilizing. She worked and she thought and she let the question exist alongside everything else without forcing it to resolve before it was ready.

She told Scotty the full version on the second evening.

Not the outline she'd given him before but the complete picture, Haverstock's physics and the decade estimate and what it meant and what she didn't know yet.

He listened the way he always listened.

When she finished he was quiet for a longer moment than usual.

He said: "A decade."

She said: "Approximately. If the presences can coordinate. If the physics holds at Haverstock's estimate. If�" she stopped. "There are a lot of ifs."

He said: "But it's possible."

She said: "The physics says it's possible."

He held her gaze.

He said: "What would you find? If you went back."

She'd been thinking about this for three weeks. She had an answer that was the honest answer, which was the only kind she was willing to give him.

She said: "Not what I left. Two thousand years have passed. Sunnydale�" she paused. "Whatever Sunnydale became in two thousand years, it's not what I remember. The people I knew are two thousand years gone." She paused. "The Hellmouth might not exist anymore. The Earth might be very different. The supernatural landscape on Earth in this century is something I have no information about." She paused. "Going back would not be going home. It would be going to a different version of the place I'm from."

He held her gaze. "Then what's there?"

She thought about it.

She said: "The place itself. Old Earth. The first place." She paused. "The lineage's origin. The Hellmouth's history." She paused. "Whatever is there now, whoever is there now, they're part of the same long story." She paused. "And the thing that made the Hellmouth has been doing something with that location for two thousand years. Whatever it's been doing there matters for what we're working toward here." She held his gaze. "The intelligence value alone is�" she stopped. "That's not the only reason."

He said: "I know it's not."

She held his gaze.

She said: "I want to see it. I want to see what it became." She paused. "I want to know if anything of what I knew is still there. Not the people. The place." She paused. "And I want to close something. I don't know exactly what. But there's something that's been open since the portal and I want to close it." She paused. "Or at least look at it."

He was quiet.

He said: "And then?"

She held his gaze.

She said: "Then I come back."

He held her gaze for a long moment.

She said: "This is where my life is. The Vigil. The lineage. The work." She held his gaze. "You." She paused. "Going back doesn't mean staying back. Going back is�" she looked for the word� "completing something. And then returning." She paused. "If the physics allows a return."

He said: "Does it?"

She said: "Haverstock's estimate covers the going. The return is a different calculation that he's working on." She paused. "The physics that allows the going should, in theory, allow the coming back. The instability is bidirectional. But the same factors apply � the variation, the presences, the threshold." She paused. "He thinks yes. With the same level of confidence he has about the going."

He held her gaze.

He said: "So you'd go. You'd return. The time differential�"

She said: "Is the question I don't have a full answer to yet. The physics of time displacement through the interface is something Haverstock is still working out." She paused. "In the original event, going through the portal displaced me temporally as well as spatially. The return might do the same thing." She paused. "Or it might not. The original event was an unstable crisis point. What we're describing is a controlled, coordinated interaction with a dormant instability. The physics is different."

He said: "Might and might not."

She said: "Yes."

He was quiet for a long time.

She watched him hold the weight of this and she didn't try to make it smaller or easier than it was, because it wasn't small or easy and he deserved the full version.

He said: "When would you know? Whether you're going."

She said: "When the physics is sufficiently understood and the presences confirm they can coordinate and the threshold is reachable. That's what the decade estimate means � that's when those conditions align." She paused. "I'm not deciding now. I don't have enough to decide now." She held his gaze. "But I'm asking the question. Out loud. For the first time." She paused. "I wanted you to know I was asking."

He held her gaze.

He said: "Thank you for telling me."

She said: "It's the kind of thing you tell someone."

He said: "Yes. It is."

They held each other's gaze in the specific quiet of the room.

Then he said: "The decade. Whatever we build in the decade matters. Regardless of what you decide."

She said: "Yes."

He said: "Then we build."

She said: "Yes."

He reached across and took her hand.

She looked at his hand holding hers and thought about the decade and what it was going to contain and what she was going to know at the end of it that she didn't know now and what would be the same.

She thought: forward.

She thought: both directions.

She held his hand and let the question exist and didn't make it resolve before it was ready and the evening came in around them and it was, despite the weight of the question, still good.

Still very good.

~~~

She told the Constant that she was asking.

Not the full conversation. Just the acknowledgment. Through the channel at its ambient level, not the full contact, just the awareness directed toward the Constant's presence and the question she was asking it to hold with her.

The Constant's response was the quality of something that had been waiting for exactly this and was now doing what it did, which was the long patient work of preparation.

She told Witness through Eris, who was now on Manticore and who had become part of the Vigil's research structure in the specific way of someone whose accompanying presence added a dimension that the purely human researchers didn't have.

Witness's response, through Eris: it has been aware. It has been factoring it into the interface coordination work.

She held this.

She thought: they knew before I did.

She thought: of course they did.

She thought about what it meant that the presences had been factoring this into their work before she'd consciously asked the question, and she thought about the Keeper's knowing about Witness before Barnett and about the specific quality of very old things that were very patient having a longer view than she had.

She was going to spend the rest of her life being gently startled by this.

She thought: that's fine. That's the shape of it.

She sent a long message to Haverstock that was equal parts appreciation for his response and additional questions that his response had generated, because Haverstock's responses always generated additional questions and he was the kind of person who found this energizing rather than exhausting.

He wrote back within a day with the specific enthusiasm of someone who had been waiting for the additional questions.

They began the work of understanding the physics properly.
