Chapter 54 (45 of 54 in this book)

The three to five years arrived as three years, which was the faster end of the estimate and which Haverstock attributed to two things: the interface variation accelerating slightly beyond his projected rate in the second year, and the presences' coordinated capacity proving, in practice, to be greater than his theoretical estimate had allowed for.

She was thirty when he sent the letter that said: the threshold is reachable. Not in three months and not tomorrow. In approximately six months, during a window that the variation and the presences' alignment will produce. The window will last between seventy-two and ninety-six hours based on current models. After the window closes, the next comparable alignment is approximately eleven years away.

She read the letter sitting at her desk in the Manticore capital quarters with the city outside the window doing its morning things and the Keeper very present and Vigil through the bond somewhere in Sphinx and the first Clinkscales document in its case on the desk where it had been for three years.

She read it twice.

Then she put it down and looked at the window for a long time.

She was thirty.

Haverstock had said thirty to thirty-two. He'd been accurate.

She thought about the three years. What they had been and what they had contained. She thought about Yeva, who had been in the operational lead for two and a half of those years and who had done with it what she'd known Yeva would do. She thought about the Vigil, which was now something she could look at from the outside and see as a thing that existed rather than a thing she was continuously producing by existing.

She thought about the garden in Sphinx, which was in its fourth season of establishment and which had become what established gardens became, which was itself, fully, without apology.

She thought about Scotty.

She put the letter in her pocket and went to find him.

He was in the kitchen of the capital quarters, which was where he often was in the mornings, and he looked at her face when she came in and he knew before she said anything.

She sat across from him.

She said: "Haverstock's letter."

He said: "Tell me."

She told him.

Six months. Seventy-two to ninety-six hours. Eleven years to the next window.

He held this.

He said: "Six months."

She said: "Yes."

He said: "That's the decision timeline."

She said: "Yes."

He was quiet for a long moment. Not the quiet of someone processing alone. The quiet of someone who had been holding this possibility for three years and was now holding the specific version of it that was real rather than theoretical.

She held the quiet with him.

He said: "What are you thinking?"

She said: "That I need to finish thinking. That six months is enough time to finish thinking if I use it." She held his gaze. "And that I want to spend some of those six months not thinking about it at all. Just in the life. Before the decision."

He held her gaze.

He said: "How much time not thinking about it?"

She said: "Most of it."

He said: "Okay."

She said: "And then the last month thinking about it properly."

He said: "All right."

She held his gaze.

She said: "I'm going to ask you something and I want the honest answer."

He said: "You always get the honest answer."

She said: "What do you want me to do?"

He was quiet.

He said: "I want you to come back. Whatever you decide about going." He held her gaze. "If you go I want you to come back. That's what I want." He paused. "What I think you should do is�" he stopped. "That's your question. Not mine."

She held his gaze.

She said: "I know it's my question. I'm asking what you want. Those are different questions."

He held her gaze for a long time.

He said: "I want you to be who you are. All the way." He paused. "The person I've been building this with has the door as part of her. The not-asking was costing her something. The asking has been different." He paused. "I want you to be the version that has closed what needs closing. Even if that means going." He held her gaze. "And then I want you to come back and be here with me."

She held this.

She said: "That's the most honest thing you've said."

He said: "I've been practicing for three years."

She held his gaze.

She said: "I love you."

She hadn't said it in those words before. There had been other words and other ways and the whole of what they'd built together said it continuously in a language that didn't require those specific words. But the specific words had their own weight and the moment warranted the weight.

He held her gaze.

He said: "I know." He paused. "I love you too."

She held this.

She let it be fully present, the specific simple weight of it.

She said: "Six months."

He said: "Six months."

She said: "We build."

He said: "We build."

She reached across and took his hand.

~~~

The six months were the fullest she'd known.

Not because she was performing fullness or trying to store it against a possible absence. She was incapable of that kind of strategic emotion and she knew it and didn't try. The fullness was because she was present in a way she recognized as the deepest version of herself being present, the Vigil and the question and the life and the people all held simultaneously, and the simultaneous holding produced something she didn't have a precise word for.

Presence, as a practice rather than a performance.

She was present for the Vigil's work in the way she'd always been present for it, fully and without the management that divided attention. She was present for the candidates in the network, the ones she met directly and the ones she knew through the reports and the ones the bond's ambient quality brought to her attention through channels she couldn't entirely articulate.

She was present for Yeva, who was leading the operational track with a capability that was exceeding even what Buffy had known was coming, the ceiling still not visible, the thing Yeva was becoming still in the process of becoming.

She sat with Carson in the third month and talked for four hours about the archive and what it needed to be and what she wanted the document she was writing to become after she'd finished it and after that after.

Carson listened and then said: "You're writing it to be passed on."

She said: "Everything I've built is to be passed on."

He said: "Yes. But the document is specifically�" he paused. "It's a Watcher's journal."

She held this.

She said: "I always thought I'd have hated being a Watcher."

He said: "You'd have been a terrible Watcher in the Council sense." He held her gaze. "You're an excellent Watcher in the sense of someone who watches over the lineage and keeps the record and passes the knowledge forward."

She looked at him.

She said: "That's what the Vigil is."

He said: "Yes." He held her gaze. "It's what you always were. You just had to build the version that wasn't terrible."

She held this for a long time.

Then she said: "Write me into the archive."

He held her gaze.

She said: "The complete version. Not the operational record and not the research record. The whole one. Everything in the document and everything that's not in the document yet and whatever I tell you in the next six months that needs to be in it." She held his gaze. "When this is finished I want the archive to have the full picture of what happened in this generation."

He said: "I've been keeping that record since Hades."

She said: "I know. I want you to finish it."

He held her gaze.

He said: "All right."

She said: "Thank you."

He said: "Don't�"

She said: "I know. Thank you anyway."

He almost smiled.

She almost smiled back.

~~~

She talked to Honor in the fourth month.

Not because she hadn't been talking to Honor continuously throughout the six months. Because the specific conversation that needed to happen hadn't happened yet and the fourth month was when it needed to happen.

Honor came to the capital quarters on a Thursday evening with Nimitz and they sat in the way they sat, and before Buffy said anything Nimitz came to her shoulder and did the thing, the brief touch of his true-hand, the communication of what he communicated.

She scratched between his ears.

He stayed.

She said: "I want to talk about what comes after."

Honor said: "After you go."

She said: "After I go or after I decide not to. Either way." She held Honor's gaze. "The Vigil needs a different kind of leadership than I've been giving it for the past year. Yeva has the operational side. You have the diplomatic and political side." She paused. "The long-work side, the interface research and the presences coordination and the thread that runs from the Constant through everything�" she paused. "That needs someone."

Honor held her gaze.

She said: "You're not describing that role for me."

She said: "No. You're doing what you're built for in the diplomatic work." She paused. "I'm describing Eris."

Honor held her gaze.

She said: "Eris and Witness."

She said: "Yes. The interface work is Haverstock's domain in the physics sense, but the long-work coordination�the relationship with the presences, the thread that runs through the record, the thing that connects the Constant's eleven hundred years of work to what the Vigil is doing now�that's Eris's domain. She's been in it for four years. She understands it in a way nobody else does." She paused. "Except me. And I'm not going to be the one holding that thread indefinitely."

Honor was quiet for a moment.

She said: "You've been thinking about succession."

She said: "I've been building toward succession since the beginning. I just wasn't calling it that." She held Honor's gaze. "The Vigil was always supposed to outlast me. I've been trying to build it that way." She paused. "The succession is just the formalization of something that's been the structure all along."

Honor held her gaze.

She said: "And if you come back?"

She said: "If I come back I come back into whatever the Vigil has become without me. Not to take it back. To be part of it in the way that's right then." She paused. "The same way I'm part of it now but not the same role." She held Honor's gaze. "The Vigil is bigger than any one role. That was always the design."

Honor was quiet for a long time.

Nimitz on her shoulder was very still.

She said: "You're not afraid."

She said: "I'm afraid of several things." She held Honor's gaze. "The time displacement. Whether the return physics holds. What I'll find on Old Earth." She paused. "Whether I'll make the right decision." She paused. "I'm afraid of the right things. I'm not afraid of the wrong things."

Honor looked at her.

She said: "What are the wrong things?"

She said: "Whether the Vigil can survive without me. Whether the people here will be all right." She paused. "I know the answers to those. I built the answers in." She held Honor's gaze. "You and Yeva and Eris and Carson and Fritz and Allison and Scotty. The presences. The network." She paused. "The wrong fears are fears about things I've already addressed. I know they're addressed. So I'm not afraid of them."

Honor held her gaze for a long moment.

She said: "When did you become wise?"

She said: "I'm not wise. I've just had time."

Honor said: "You've had less than four years."

She said: "I've had thirty years plus four." She held Honor's gaze. "The thirty didn't count for most things when I arrived. They count for this."

Honor was quiet.

Then she said: "Come back."

She said: "That's the plan."

Honor said: "I know it's the plan." She held her gaze. "I'm not saying it as a request. I'm saying it because�" she stopped. She paused. "I came very close to not surviving Hades. And you were there, and the person you've become is partly because of what happened there and partly because of who you already were." She paused. "I want that person to come back."

She held Honor's gaze.

She said: "I will."

She said it with the same quality she'd heard Scotty use, the directness that wasn't bravado and wasn't performance. The directness of someone who meant the thing they were saying.

Honor held her gaze.

She said: "All right."

Nimitz made the sound.

She thought: yes. That's it exactly.
