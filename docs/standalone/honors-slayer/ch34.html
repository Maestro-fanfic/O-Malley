The something she'd been building toward with Scotty happened three days after his arrival, which was not planned but which had the quality of things that happened when the conditions were right rather than when a decision was made.

They were on the terrace. Late, after dinner, which had been a smaller dinner than the first one, just the two of them because Allison had gone to bed early for Allison reasons that were probably accurate and possibly diplomatic, and Honor was doing something she'd described as correspondence with people she needed to correspond with and which Buffy suspected involved more of the war's changing situation than correspondence usually implied.

The city was in its nighttime configuration. Beowulf at night had a quality that was different from Manticore's nighttime and Grayson's nighttime and was its own thing, and she was adding it to the catalog of things that were their own things rather than comparisons to things she'd lost.

Scotty had been telling her about the Talbott Cluster, the full version now that he had context and time to organize it, and she'd been listening in the complete way of someone who was interested in both the information and the person telling it.

He finished and they were quiet for a moment with the city below them.

Then he said: "Buffy."

She looked at him.

He looked at her.

He said: "I've been thinking about what comes after the current situation. The next posting. The way things are developing with the war, the next several months are going to look different. There are going to be choices about where I go and what I do that I haven't had before." He paused. "I've been thinking about those choices with you as a variable."

"A variable," she said.

"A significant variable," he said. "The most significant one, currently." He held her gaze. "Is that � do you want to be that?"

She thought about the letters. The four months of letters that had built a structure she couldn't have named at the beginning and could name now. She thought about him in the docking facility saying hi with his face doing the thing it did. She thought about Allison saying you're the one from the letters and what that confirmed.

"Yes," she said. "I want to be that."

He breathed out.

She looked at him and he looked at her and the terrace held the Beowulf night around them and neither of them said anything for a moment because the moment was doing what it needed to do without additional input.

Then she said: "Autumn."

He looked at her.

"You said autumn in Sphinx," she said. "The spectacular version. You said I should see it before the winter."

"I did," he said.

"Is it still on offer?"

He held her gaze. Something settled in his expression, the same settling she'd felt in herself when the Constant's communication had organized what she'd known into what she understood. "Yes," he said. "Whenever you can get there."

"The work here is going to take another few weeks," she said. "Maybe a month. And then Manticore, to report in properly. And then�" she paused.

"And then Sphinx," he said.

"And then Sphinx," she agreed.

The city did its things. The stars were the wrong stars and they were hers and he was here, on the terrace, and she was here, and this was the after.

She thought about Joyce Summers who had asked her, once, when she was fourteen and the asking was hypothetical, what she wanted her life to look like.

She hadn't known then.

She thought she knew now.

Not the details. The shape.

This. The work and the people and the something worth fighting for. The wrong stars that were hers. The accumulated weight of a life being built in a place she hadn't planned to be and couldn't imagine not being now.

This was the shape.

She looked at it and it held.

The final week on Beowulf had the quality of things that were ending and things that were beginning simultaneously, the specific texture of a transition rather than a stop.

Haverstock's interface research had reached the point of sufficiency for the next phase, which was bringing it back to Manticore and integrating it with the institutional framework and the lineage network that was growing. He was coming to Manticore for the next phase. He seemed, she thought, genuinely happy about this, the specific happiness of someone whose work had found its context.

Allison's biological research had reached the point of the first major paper, which she'd shown Buffy in draft and which Buffy had reviewed and approved with specific redactions and which was going to be, Allison said, the most significant publication in her specialty in the past several decades.

She'd said: I'll believe you.

Allison had said: you should.

The Constant was quiet in the specific way of something that had communicated what it needed to communicate and was returning to its patient background position. She'd made one more contact, brief, not to receive new information but to confirm what she'd received and to establish that the channel was open and available when it was needed.

The Constant had confirmed both things with the quality of something that had been waiting a very long time for exactly this and was now, in whatever the Constant's equivalent of relief was, able to return to its patient watching from a different position.

She thought about that on the last night. Sitting on the terrace again, alone this time, with the city and the wrong stars and the Keeper's presence and the open channel that was quieter now but present.

She thought about the long shape of what the Constant had been doing for eleven hundred years and about the long shape of what the lineage had been doing for two thousand years and about her specifically, the specific person at the specific intersection, the seventeenth-now-eighteenth-year-old girl from Sunnydale who had been the most recent in a long line of called ones and who had ended up here, in this century, at this place, at this particular moment when the various long threads were converging.

She didn't believe in accidents.

She'd stopped believing in accidents around the time she'd stopped being surprised by the specific way that the universe placed the right people in proximity to each other at the right moments, not through design in the sense of a plan that had been made and was being executed, but through the accumulated probability of things that wanted to be in contact eventually finding each other.

She'd found the people she was supposed to find.

She was doing the work she was supposed to do.

The displacement and the trauma and the terrible months and the recovery and all of it had been real and costly and she wasn't going to pretend otherwise. But the shape of what it had aimed her at was visible now and the shape was right.

The Girl ran through the ancient dark at the edge of her awareness, present in the way she was always present, the ancient lineage doing what it did, which was simply continuing. Forward through time as it always had. Patient and specific and uninterested in the distinction between what was expected and what was not, which was the oldest thing about it, that it had never required expectation to continue.

She let herself be that for a moment. Just the continuation. The oldest part of herself, the part that was in the lineage and the channel and the open connection to the interface, just present in the flow of it.

Then she was also the girl on the terrace. The one who was eighteen and had a bag to pack and a ship to catch in the morning and a life that was large and specific and hers.

Both of those things at once.

She looked at the city.

She looked at the stars.

She thought: I'm ready for what comes next.

She thought it with the full weight of what that meant, not the bravado of someone who was trying to convince herself but the grounded knowledge of someone who had done the preparation and was now at the point where the preparation gave way to the doing.

I'm ready.

She got up.

She had a bag to pack.
