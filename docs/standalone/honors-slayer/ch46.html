Chapter 46 (37 of 54 in this book)

The expanded mapping array, when Haverstock presented his full results, produced a picture that required several hours of presentation and several more hours of processing before she could hold it.

The room for the presentation was the Vigil's largest working space, which had been the original residence room and had expanded with the Vigil's expansion and which now had the quality of a place that serious things happened in regularly, the accumulated texture of work.

Everyone relevant was there. Fritz and Allison and Prieto and Carson and Honor and Scotty and Castellan and Yeva, who had become the most consistent presence in the Vigil's daily work besides Buffy herself.

Haverstock presented for two hours.

The mapping array, expanded across the systems it could now reach, was showing a picture of the dimensional interface that was unprecedented in its resolution and scope. The patterns she'd learned to recognize, the Constant's signature and Witness's signature and the two others that Witness had introduced her to since the Barnett meeting, were visible in the map as distinct stable presences at the interface, each with its own character and its own location.

Five, confirmed. More that the array hadn't yet reached, suggested by the communication patterns between the known five.

Not all of them were working through channels the way the Constant had worked through Yuen Sharra. Not all of them were working alongside a human the way Witness worked alongside Eris. Each had its own approach, consistent with what Eris had said about Witness's description of itself: more mobile, different method, same direction.

What Haverstock had found in addition to the presence signatures was something that took longer to explain.

The interface itself was not static.

She'd understood it as a fixed boundary, a stable dimensional edge that the presences inhabited and worked through. What the expanded mapping was showing was that the interface had its own activity, independent of the presences, a slow and large-scale movement that was happening across the full reach of the array.

Not threatening movement. Not the kind of activity that the thing that made the Hellmouth had produced in the geological record she'd found in the Constant's knowledge. Something different.

She said: "What kind of movement?"

Haverstock said: "The closest analogy I have is tidal. Something is causing a periodic variation in the interface's boundary position across a very large area." He paused. "The period is long enough that human observation wouldn't have detected it without the specific instruments we're now running." He paused. "And the direction of the variation is�interesting."

He put the map on the display.

The variation was toward human space.

Not in the threatening sense of something pressing in. In the sense of something that was moving toward, the way weather moved, the way ocean currents moved, large and slow and present regardless of the intentions of anything it encountered.

She sat with this.

She said: "Is it natural?"

Haverstock said: "I don't know yet. We don't have a baseline long enough to know whether this is part of the interface's natural dynamic or something else." He paused. "The presences know about it. I asked Witness through Eris." He paused. "Witness says it's not natural. It's being caused."

The room was quiet in a specific way.

She said: "By the thing that made the Hellmouth."

Haverstock said: "That's what Witness says. It doesn't call it that. The name it uses I can't render in phonetics. But yes. The cause is the long-patient thing that the record describes."

She held this.

The geological timescale thing was doing something to the interface. Not the activation she'd been reading as the eventual threat in the record, not the main event. Something preparatory. The way you prepared ground before you built something in it.

She thought about the record and the timeline and what several centuries meant in the timescale she was working with.

She said: "How long has this variation been happening?"

Witness, through Eris who was present by communicator, said approximately forty years.

She thought about the vampire network and the Eirene Constantia's forty-year established route and about what established something in a system forty years ago and what they'd been doing since.

She said: "The network. The vampires. The infrastructure they've been building."

Honor, who had been quiet for the presentation, said: "The variation in the interface makes certain things easier."

She looked at her.

Honor said: "If the interface is moving toward human space, if the boundary is becoming thinner in some meaningful way, then the things that work through the interface�" she paused� "would find it easier to establish and maintain operations."

"Yes," Buffy said. "That's it." She looked at the map. "The network isn't incidental. It's preparation. The forty years of it are forty years of establishing infrastructure that will be more effective as the interface variation progresses."

She looked at the map.

She thought about geological patience and about forty years and about what several centuries looked like as a preparation timeline when you had the kind of patience the long-patient thing had.

She said: "How long until the variation reaches the level where the interface is thin enough to matter?"

Haverstock and Witness, working through Eris, conferred via the communicator display in a way that was entirely strange and entirely practical, the physicist and the presence at the interface doing math together through a human intermediary.

The answer took seven minutes.

Haverstock said: "At the current rate? Approximately ninety years."

Ninety years.

Not several centuries. Not the comfortable long distance of the record's timeline.

Ninety years.

The room processed this.

She held it.

Ninety years was still outside the lifetimes of most of the people currently in the room. But it was in the lifetimes of the candidates she was training. In the childhood of people alive right now. A thing that was going to happen within the span of known and predictable futures rather than the abstract long distant future of the record.

She said: "The timeline has changed."

Nobody in the room said anything that wasn't true.

Honor said: "What do we do?"

She held her gaze for a moment.

She said: "The same things we were doing. Faster."

~~~

The month she'd asked for became two weeks because the timeline changed what was urgent, and the urgent things needed her specifically rather than the delegation she'd been developing.

She worked without stopping for two weeks.

Not the way she'd worked before she'd learned to pace herself, the burning kind of work that consumed everything in range. The better version, the sustainable version, the work that used what was available without exceeding it, that trusted the people around her to carry what they could carry.

She worked and the Vigil worked with her.

The operational pace of the network search increased. The contact rate for lineage candidates went from the careful measured pace of the first two years to something faster, the expansion of the search into Haven's systems moving ahead of the diplomatic timeline on the grounds that the candidates were there regardless of whether the diplomacy had caught up.

Tolman's network on the Havenite side began to function in coordination with Usher's network on the Manticoran side in the specific way of two professional operations that had been working toward the same things in parallel and were now exchanging what they'd developed independently.

The results were significant.

The Havenite network had found things that Usher hadn't found. The Manticoran network had found things the Havenite network hadn't found. The combination produced a picture of the distributed vampire infrastructure across human space that was considerably more complete than either side had assembled alone.

She and Honor and Usher spent a full day with the combined picture.

She came out of that day with a list of seventeen active nodes across explored space.

Seventeen.

She was two and a half years into the Vigil's operational work. She'd addressed seven nodes. There were seventeen more.

She held the list.

She thought about the ninety years.

She thought about the pace of what was possible with the Vigil's current operational capacity versus the pace of what was necessary given the timeline.

She thought about Yeva.

She went to find Yeva.

Yeva was in the training space with two of the newer candidates, the specific focused quality of someone who had become good enough at something to teach it without losing their own edge in the teaching. She looked up when Buffy came in and something in her expression changed, the read she'd developed on Buffy's quality when she arrived somewhere.

She said to the candidates: "Take a break."

They took a break.

She looked at Buffy.

Buffy said: "I need to tell you something."

Yeva said: "Tell me."

She told her.

The timeline. The seventeen nodes. The operational pace required versus the operational capacity current. The math of what she had and what the math needed.

Yeva listened.

When Buffy finished Yeva was quiet for a moment.

She said: "You're going to need more of me."

Buffy held her gaze. "Yes."

She said: "Define more."

Buffy said: "Operational lead. Not supporting. Leading. With your own team, with Castellan's support structure, working nodes that I assign based on assessment of your capability and the node's character." She paused. "Not the highest-complexity nodes yet. The ones that are in the tier below. While I continue to address the highest-complexity ones."

Yeva held her gaze.

She said: "You said yet."

"I said yet," Buffy confirmed. "Your ceiling is not visible to me from where you currently are. I'm not putting a ceiling on this conversation."

Yeva was quiet for a longer moment.

She said: "Two years ago you sat across from me in my family's apartment and told me you weren't going to ask me to do anything with what I am."

"Yes," Buffy said.

"And now you're asking."

"Yes," Buffy said. "The situation has changed. The timeline has changed." She held Yeva's gaze. "I'm asking. Not requiring. Not obligating. Asking. You can say no. You can say yes with conditions. You can say yes." She paused. "I need your answer to be yours."

Yeva looked at her for a long moment with the quality she had when she was thinking about something completely, taking it in at every level simultaneously.

She said: "Can I have a day?"

"Yes," Buffy said.

"Then I'll have an answer tomorrow."

"Okay," Buffy said.

She left the training space and went back to her own work and the day continued around her with its pace and its requirements and its specific texture of being in the middle of something that was always moving.

Vigil, she felt through the bond, was present in the way it was present when she was working hard, the specific accompanying quality of something that was alongside rather than assisting, the companionship of a consciousness that didn't do the work but was there while the work was done.

She thought about Nimitz and Honor, about what Honor had described as the thing the bond provided, which was the knowledge of being fully seen and not alone in the seeing.

She thought: yes. That's it exactly.

She worked until eight in the evening and then she went home and Scotty was there and dinner was there and the Sphinx autumn was coming in two weeks for the meeting with his parents and all of it was the shape of the life.

She ate and she told him about the timeline and the list and Yeva and he listened and when she finished he said: "Ninety years."

"Yes."

"Not comfortable."

"No," she said. "But workable. If we do the work."

He held her gaze. "You'll do the work."

"We'll do the work," she said. "All of us."

He held her gaze.

He said: "I've been thinking about my next posting."

She looked at him.

He said: "The Vigil's operational work is going to expand significantly. The pace you're describing is going to require coordination and logistics and support capacity that the current structure doesn't fully have." He paused. "I've been thinking that what I can contribute in that direction is more useful than another ship deployment right now." He paused. "If you'd use it."

She held his gaze.

She thought about what he was offering, which was specific and generous and which she needed and which she'd been aware of needing without knowing how to ask for.

She said: "You'd need to talk to the navy."

"I've been talking to the navy for a month," he said. "The framework exists. I wanted to know if you wanted it before I made the request formal."

She looked at him.

She thought about two and a half years and the Vigil and the work and the people in it and him specifically, the person who saw the whole of it and thought alongside her.

She said: "Yes. I want it."

He said: "Then I'll make it formal."

She looked at the dinner and the table and the city outside the window and the life that was here and being built and continuing to build.

She said: "Thank you."

He said: "I'm not going anywhere."

She looked at him.

She thought: he keeps saying that and it keeps being true and I keep being glad of it.

She thought: that's the shape of it. That's what the after looks like.

She ate her dinner.

Tomorrow there was Yeva's answer and the expanded network coordination and the operational planning for the accelerated timeline and all the work that the work was.

Tonight there was this.

She held both and they held.

~~~

Yeva's answer came in the morning, not by letter or communicator but in person, Yeva appearing at her office door at seven with the specific quality of someone who had done the thinking and arrived at the other side of it.

She said: "Yes. With one condition."

Buffy said: "Tell me the condition."

Yeva said: "The newer candidates. The ones I've been working with. I want to keep that work. Not instead of the operational role. Alongside it." She held Buffy's gaze. "I don't want to become someone who only operates. I want to be someone who also builds what comes after."

Buffy held her gaze for a moment.

She thought about herself at seventeen, before the Council, before Merrick, before any of it, and she thought about what she'd needed and hadn't had.

She thought about what she'd said to Yeva two years ago: I'm not going to ask you to do anything with what you are.

She thought about the condition Yeva was setting and what it said about who Yeva was becoming.

She said: "That's not a condition. That's required." She held Yeva's gaze. "The Vigil builds and operates simultaneously. Anyone who does one and not the other is doing half the work." She paused. "I should have been clearer about that when I asked."

Yeva looked at her.

She said: "Okay. Yes."

Buffy said: "Yes."

They held each other's gaze for a moment in the specific way of something being settled between two people who both understood what was being settled.

Then Yeva said: "When do we start?"

Buffy said: "Now."

They started.
