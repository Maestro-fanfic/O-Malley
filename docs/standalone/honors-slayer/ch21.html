Chapter 21 (12 of 54 in this book)

By the end of the first week she'd found seven.

Seven was not a colony. Seven was a founding population, established and reproducing at a controlled rate, which meant something was managing the reproduction. Vampires left to their own inclinations tended toward exponential growth that inevitably triggered its own collapse through either discovery and elimination or resource depletion, and the ones who survived were the ones who understood carrying capacity and practiced it.

Something here understood carrying capacity.

That was the concerning thing.

She told Fritz after the seventh and she told the Queen through Chen after the seventh and she told no one else, which felt like withholding in a way that was uncomfortable but was also correct. The wrong kind of official response to this would make everything worse.

Fritz received the updated picture with the systematic thoroughness he brought to everything and said, "Management implies leadership. Or at least organization."

"Yes."

"You haven't found the leadership yet."

"No. Everything I've found has been drones, essentially. Following an established pattern. Feeding within defined parameters." She looked at her map. "Whatever set the parameters is older and smarter than anything I've seen so far."

"How do you find it?"

"Follow the drones. They're reporting to something." She paused. "And look for the anomalies. Something this organized is going to have an operation beneath the operation. Some mechanism for sustaining itself that isn't just hunting. Something that explains how it got here."

Fritz was quiet for a moment. "How it got here is the part I keep returning to."

"Me too." She looked at the map. "Vampires don't have interstellar travel capability. They're a supernatural infection of an existing organism. They go where the organism goes." She paused. "Which means either something in the past two thousand years of human expansion brought them here deliberately, or they were carried here accidentally by someone who was already infected and didn't know it."

"Or who knew and didn't care," Fritz said.

She thought about that. "That's a category too," she said. "Yes."

The information access that Chen had arranged proved to be more useful than she'd expected, which was itself useful information about the Queen's intention to treat the offered resources as real rather than nominal. The three-year death record expanded to ten years when she asked for it and expanded again to thirty when she pushed, and across thirty years the pattern was cleaner and older and more structured than the short view had suggested.

Forty years.

The founding population was approximately forty years old. Small and careful in the early years, the death record showing the specific signature of a predator learning its environment and establishing sustainable practice. The current activity level was elevated relative to the historical baseline, which said something had changed in the past year. Something that had increased the population's appetite or reduced its caution.

She sat with this information for a long time.

She was still sitting with it on a Tuesday afternoon when she had a visitor.

The visitor was not announced in the way that visitors to her quarters were usually announced, which was through the building's intercom system. The visitor knocked on the door directly, which meant they were inside the building already, which meant either they lived in the building or someone had let them in.

She opened the door.

The woman on the other side was tall, older than Buffy but younger than middle age, with the kind of face that looked like it had spent a significant amount of time making very careful decisions and had the specific set of lines around the eyes that came from that. She was wearing civilian clothes in the way of someone who was always actually in uniform regardless of what they were wearing.

She had no treecats and no insignia and no identification that was visible.

She looked at Buffy with dark eyes that were doing the assessment thing that Buffy had come to recognize as characteristic of a specific type of professional.

"Lady Buffy," she said. "My name is Usher. I work for the Queen."

"Chen didn't tell me you were coming."

"No. Chen doesn't know I'm coming." She held Buffy's gaze. "May I come in?"

Buffy looked at her. The Slayer sense was not triggering in any of the ways it triggered for actual threat, which didn't mean threat was absent but meant that the threat, if present, was human and contained and not currently active. She stepped back from the door.

Usher came in and looked at the room with the brief comprehensive scan of someone who inventoried spaces by habit and came to the map on the table.

"You've been busy," she said.

"Yes." Buffy let the door close. "Who do you work for specifically?"

"Her Majesty." She looked at the map. "The part of Her Majesty that doesn't go through Lieutenant Chen."

"Intelligence."

"A specific kind." She looked at Buffy. "We've known about the warehouse district situation for approximately two years. We've been monitoring it."

Buffy looked at her. "You've been monitoring it."

"Yes."

"Three deaths in the past month. Twelve over eighteen months. Forty years of what looks like sustained predatory activity in the city of Landing." She kept her voice even. "And you've been monitoring."

"Yes." Usher's voice was equally even, carrying no defensiveness. "Because we don't have a mechanism for addressing it. We know what it is. We don't have the capability to do anything about it." She paused. "Until you arrived."

"How long have you known what it is?"

"Twenty years." She pulled out a chair at Buffy's table and sat in it with the ease of someone comfortable making herself at home in ambiguous circumstances. "There's a historical tradition. Not the Clinkscales tradition, which is theological. A separate line, more operational in character. People who knew about this category of entity and who maintained the knowledge practically rather than liturgically."

"A Watchers' Council," Buffy said.

Usher looked at her. "Nothing so formal. More of a�" she paused� "lineage of awareness. Passed within certain families and certain institutions. What the Church of Humanity Unchained knows as mythology, we know as operational reality." She paused. "With the significant limitation that knowing about something and having the capacity to address it are different problems."

"The Slayer," Buffy said.

"The Slayer," Usher said. "Yes. When you arrived, we took an interest."

Buffy looked at her. "You've been watching me."

"Yes. With Her Majesty's knowledge. Not with the intent to interfere. To understand." She held Buffy's gaze. "The monitoring of the warehouse district is ours. The deaths are real and they trouble us. We've been trying to prevent them without the capability to prevent them and that's a specific kind of failing that�" she stopped. "We've been waiting for something that could actually address this."

Buffy thought about this for a moment. She thought about what Usher represented, which was an institutional awareness of what she was that was neither the Council's model nor the Clinkscales model but a third thing, practical and unsentimental and constrained by capability.

"What do you want from me?" she said.

"What we want," Usher said, "and what we're asking are different things. What we want is for the warehouse district situation to be resolved. What we're asking is whether you'll share information with us while you resolve it, and whether you'll allow us to support you in ways that don't interfere with the operational approach you've already identified."

"What kind of support?"

"Information. We have forty years of records on this population. More detail than the city's medical archive." She paused. "And we have a theory about the leadership question that you're probably already forming yourself."

Buffy looked at her map. "Tell me the theory."

"There's a Solarian merchant vessel that has operated an irregular route through Manticore for the past forty-two years," Usher said. "The Eirene Constantia. Registered out of the Solarian League, ostensibly a luxury goods carrier. Her port visits correlate with the activity pattern in the warehouse district." She paused. "We believe the ship's master is the organizing intelligence you're looking for."

Buffy looked at her.

"A vampire in command of an interstellar merchant vessel," she said.

"Yes."

She thought about what that meant. The scale of it. Not a vampire who had stumbled into the space age through being carried by an infected human. A vampire who had adapted to the space age, who had built an operational infrastructure around the requirement to feed, who was moving through the star systems of the explored galaxy on a regular route with a sustainable practice and what amounted to a franchise operation in at least one port city.

Forty years. At minimum.

"How many systems?" she said.

Usher's expression told her she'd asked the right question. "We don't know. The Eirene Constantia's route takes her through eleven systems on a roughly four-month cycle. We have data on two of them that suggests similar patterns to Landing." She paused. "The others we haven't been able to assess."

"But likely."

"We think it likely."

Buffy looked at her map and the map expanded in her mind to something much larger, eleven star systems connected by a merchant route and at each port city a managed predatory population that fed at a controlled rate and generated a death record that looked like unexplained medical events to people who didn't know what they were looking for.

"When is the Eirene Constantia next in port?" she said.

"She's due in fourteen days."

Fourteen days.

She had fourteen days to understand the local population well enough to address the master when they arrived, which meant she needed to stop mapping and start acting, which meant the approach shifted now from surveillance to operation.

"Okay," she said. "Tell me everything you have."

~~~

Usher's files were meticulous.

The kind of meticulous that came from twenty years of systematic documentation by people who knew what they were looking at and had developed rigorous methods for capturing it. She spent three days going through them alongside her own accumulated information and the picture that emerged was clearer than anything she'd had before.

The Landing population was six. She'd accounted for five. The sixth was the oldest of the local drones, a century-old presence in the city who had been here before the Eirene Constantia's established route and who was therefore either the seed of the local population or a coincidental resident that the ship's master had absorbed into their operation.

The oldest one was going to be the hardest. Older vampires were more capable, more strategic, less likely to be caught in the circumstances that had caught the younger ones. She was going to have to think carefully about the approach.

She thought carefully about it.

On the fourth day she went back out and found the sixth, who was in the warehouse district in the way that an established resident occupied their territory rather than the way a hunter occupied it, more settled, less urgent. It saw her coming because it was old enough to have learned to sense what she was in return, the specific activation of the supernatural awareness that older vampires developed as a survival mechanism.

It ran.

She ran faster.

The chase went through three city blocks and over two rooftops and into a second warehouse that was not in her map because she hadn't found it yet and was clearly a long-established refuge, the kind of space that a careful predator maintained as an emergency option, with secondary exits that she noted even as she went through the primary one.

She caught it.

The fight was the longest she'd had since Hades. Not because she was outmatched but because it was old and strategic and conserved its energy for the moments when expenditure was likely to produce an outcome rather than spending on fights it couldn't win. She respected the intelligence behind that even while she was dealing with it, the specific respect of a professional for another professional's competence applied to a completely different purpose.

It went to dust eventually.

She stood in the warehouse and looked at what remained of the established refuge. A sleeping space. Some containers of supplies. A communication device of a type she didn't recognize.

She picked up the communication device.

She brought it to Usher.

Usher looked at it for a long moment and then looked at Buffy with something that might have been satisfaction and might have been relief. "This is Solarian manufacture," she said. "Commercial encryption with some nonstandard modifications." She paused. "We can work with this."

"I thought you might," Buffy said.

~~~

The communication device yielded, over the next four days, the shape of something she'd suspected and hadn't wanted to confirm.

The Eirene Constantia was not the only ship.

The network was larger. How much larger, the device's contents didn't fully reveal, but the traffic implied regular communication with at minimum three other vessels on routes through different parts of explored space. The master of the Eirene Constantia was not the top of the organization. They were a node.

There was a top.

She sat with this in Usher's office, which was in a building that she'd been to twice now and whose location she'd been asked not to share, which she hadn't, and looked at the map of explored space that Usher had pulled up and thought about what you did when the problem was larger than the immediate operational scope.

"You knew this was a possibility," she said.

"We suspected," Usher said. "The scale of a sustained operation across multiple star systems over multiple decades implied resources and organization that exceeded a single ship's capacity." She paused. "But suspicion isn't knowledge."

"Now it's knowledge."

"A preliminary version of knowledge." Usher looked at the map. "The full scope is still unclear."

Buffy looked at the map. Explored space was enormous. She'd learned this over the past months, built the sense of it through reading and briefings and the accumulated process of understanding a civilization that had been doing something she couldn't have imagined from Sunnydale. Eleven hundred years of interstellar expansion, hundreds of inhabited systems, billions of people spread across a volume of space that made the Hellmouth look like a pothole.

"There are Slayers," she said.

Usher looked at her.

"The lineage," she said. "The Clinkscales records. Carson showed me�there's a tradition that the Slayer is one, always one, but the mythology isn't necessarily accurate about the mechanics." She paused. "I'm not one. I'm the most recent, but the line didn't end with me. Someone else was called when Kendra was called. Someone else was called when Kendra died." She stopped. "That's two thousand years of called Slayers. In a species that has spread across a thousand star systems."

Usher was very still.

"They don't know what they are," Buffy said. "Almost certainly. The institutional knowledge is gone. The Council is gone. There's no infrastructure connecting them to the tradition." She looked at the map. "But the Slayer sense doesn't depend on institutional knowledge. It's operational whether you know it or not." She paused. "There are girls out there who dream of ancient women and who are faster than they should be and stronger than they should be and who have no idea why."

She thought about fifteen-year-old herself, before Merrick, before the library, before any of it. She thought about what it had been like to have the power without the context and how much harder everything had been because of the missing context.

"We need to find them," she said.

Usher was quiet for a moment. "That's a significant operational undertaking."

"I know." She looked at the map. "Everything significant is." She paused. "But the reason matters. If there's a network operating across explored space, the vampire population in Landing is not an anomaly. It's a data point. And right now the Slayer lineage is scattered across a thousand systems with no connection to each other and no knowledge of what they are." She paused. "That's the strategic problem. The warehouse district is the tactical one."

"You're thinking beyond the tactical," Usher said.

"I always think beyond the tactical," Buffy said. "I just have to deal with the tactical first."

She looked at the communication device. She looked at the map. She thought about fourteen days until the Eirene Constantia arrived in port, and the node she was going to have to address when it did, and the larger network behind the node that was going to require more than she currently had.

She thought about the deal she'd made with the Queen.

Resources, the Queen had said. Support. And honesty about what she needed.

"I need to talk to Her Majesty," she said.

"I'll arrange it," Usher said.

~~~

The Queen received her in the same informal way she had at the waterfront, in a sitting room off the official receiving chambers that had the character of a space actually used rather than a space prepared for use. Ariel was on the back of her chair. Tea was on the table.

Buffy sat down and told her everything.

The Queen listened in the way she'd listened at the waterfront, with the complete attention of someone who was receiving information rather than managing a presentation. She asked three questions, all of them the right questions, and when Buffy finished she was quiet for a long moment.

"Fourteen days," she said.

"Until the Eirene Constantia arrives. Yes."

"And you want to address the ship's master."

"I want to address whoever is on that ship. If the master is the organizing intelligence for the Landing population, then yes, the master. If the master is human and the actual target is something aboard that hasn't shown itself yet�" she paused� "then whatever it is."

"Without triggering the larger network."

"Ideally. If the network realizes its Landing node has been addressed, it will reorganize and we'll lose the intelligence value of what we've found." She paused. "I want to take the node intact, not destroy it."

The Queen's eyebrows moved slightly, which was her version of a significant reaction. "Intact."

"The information it has is more valuable than its elimination in isolation." She paused. "I want to know the scope. I want to know the other nodes. I want to know the top." She met the Queen's eyes. "I've been doing this for two years and I've never had a vampire network this organized or this large. Understanding it fully is worth more than the immediate cleanup."

"What do you need?"

"Support for the boarding. Naval. People who know ship boarding procedure and who won't panic at what they find." She paused. "Scotty's not here."

"No." The Queen looked at her steadily. "I have people. If I can brief them on the situation."

"They'll need to be briefed. Yes." She thought about what she'd just said and what she was asking, which was for the Queen to tell a group of naval personnel that vampires existed and were running an interstellar operation and that the Slayer was going to be leading a boarding action against one of their ships. She thought about how that briefing was going to go. "It's going to be a difficult briefing."

"I've given difficult briefings," the Queen said. "What else?"

"Usher's resources. Full access to what they've found."

"Done."

"And I need someone to start a search," she said. "For girls between fourteen and nineteen who present with the specific physical profile. Across Manticore first and then wherever we can reach." She paused. "I know that's a long-term project. I know it's not the immediate priority. But if the network is as large as I think it is and the Slayer lineage is as scattered as I know it is, those two facts are related and eventually they become one problem."

The Queen held her gaze for a long moment. "You're thinking about building something."

"I'm thinking about what should exist and doesn't." She paused. "The Council kept the tradition alive for a thousand years. Imperfectly. Badly in some ways. But it kept the knowledge connected to the people who needed it." She paused. "Something needs to do that again. Something better than the Council was." She met the Queen's eyes. "I'm not the person to build it alone."

"No," the Queen said. "But you're the person who understands what it needs to be."

Buffy looked at her. "Is that a yes?"

"It's a yes to thinking about it," the Queen said. "Which is the beginning of all the yes that comes after." She poured tea for both of them and handed a cup across. "Let's talk about the boarding first."

Buffy took the cup. "Yes," she said. "Let's."
