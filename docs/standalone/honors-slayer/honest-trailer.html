Honest Trailer

Bonus Chapter: The Honest Trailer

A black screen. Stars emerged one at a time: unfamiliar, indifferent, and two thousand years too late.

Then the voice arrived—the one normally reserved for wars, legends, and sequels that inherited an impossible amount of unfinished business.

“In a universe where humanity has conquered the stars, perfected interstellar war, and developed several thousand new ways for governments to ruin a perfectly survivable week…”

The stars tilted. A prison planet filled the screen.

“…one seventeen-year-old girl will discover that being ripped out of Sunnydale and dropped into the Honorverse is somehow not the worst thing that happens to her.”

Buffy opened her eyes on Hades: injured, displaced, connected to something older than language, and surrounded by people who had already used up their allotted lifetime supply of desperate escapes.

“Meet Buffy Summers.”

Quick cuts: Buffy healing too fast. Buffy eating enough for a platoon. Buffy giving Honor blood. Buffy staring up at stars that were not hers.

“She’s the Slayer. She’s a temporal refugee. And according to the medical staff, she’s also where all the calories went.”

Honor Harrington watched her with the grave patience of a woman whose résumé already included naval command, political catastrophe, assassination attempts, and adopting traumatized strays with terrifying combat reflexes.

Nimitz bleeked.

“Correct,” said the narrator. “Another one.”

Watch as a teenage supernatural weapon meets a military culture that understands duty, sacrifice, and turning personal damage into an administrative structure.

“It’s Buffy the Vampire Slayer…”

Buffy drove something monstrous into the ground.

“…with orbital mechanics.”

A warship fired.

“And Honor Harrington…”

Honor made a decision that would alter several governments.

“…with vampires.”

A room full of officers went very still.

From Hades to Manticore, from Grayson to Beowulf, follow Buffy as she searches for the answer to the oldest Slayer question:

What am I when nobody is giving me something to kill?

The answer, regrettably, requires decades of institutional design.

“Because defeating a monster is easy.”

Buffy hit a monster.

“Defeating the system that keeps producing monsters requires committees.”

Buffy sat at a table with Haverstock, Prieto, Allison, Tomas, and Carson.

She looked as if she would prefer the monster.

“So thrill as the Chosen One becomes the Founding One—building the Vigil, assembling the Keeper’s Archive, tracing a lineage written through blood and memory, and discovering that ancient cosmic intelligences are perfectly happy to communicate in profound emotional concepts instead of usable sentences.”

The Keeper touched the edge of her awareness: recognition, patience, witness, continuity.

Buffy waited.

More qualities followed.

“Great. A mystical interface with no search bar.”

But this is not just a story about the Keeper, the Constant, the Seal, or the slow excavation of a hidden supernatural history among the stars.

It is the story of a girl once treated as a disposable weapon learning how to build something that will outlive the emergency.

The music softened.

Buffy stood beneath an alien sky and grieved the constellations she remembered. Honor sat beside her without trying to solve it. Nimitz offered the quiet certainty of presence. Carson became family by the ancient and highly technical process of continuing to show up.

The narrator cleared his throat.

“We will not be making fun of that part.”

A beat.

“We have standards.”

The music immediately became dramatic again.

“Instead, prepare for—”

The screen began counting.

One prison-planet escape.

Several governments that desperately need adult supervision.

Two primordial intelligences with very different approaches to personal space.

One sentient species of empathic murder-cats.

Nimitz showed his teeth.

“Affectionate empathic murder-cats.”

An entire archive assembled because nobody in human history thought to leave the Chosen One a decent operations manual.

And enough food to make a naval quartermaster whisper, ‘Dear God.’”

Starring:

Buffy Summers—as Lady Buffy of Sunnydale, because if the universe insists on giving her titles, she is at least going to make them nervous.

Honor Harrington—as the only person in the room whose life is complicated enough to make Buffy feel comparatively normal.

Nimitz—as Emotional Support Apex Predator.

Carson Clinkscales—as Found Family, Grayson Edition.

Allison Harrington—as Science, Curiosity, and Several Questions Buffy Was Hoping Nobody Would Ask.

The Keeper—as An Entire Cosmology Delivered Through Vibes.

The Constant—as Proof That Finding One Ancient Mystery Only Unlocks the Next Research Tree.

And the Vigil—as the revolutionary proposition that Slayers should receive backup before the funeral.

The screen filled with alternate titles:

Buffy the Vampire Slayer: Deep Space Nine Millennia Later.

The Long Patrol.

Lady Buffy and the Extremely Complicated Filing System.

The Chosen One Builds Human Resources.

The narrator paused.

“No.”

The alternatives vanished.

“Some titles carry more than one person’s work. Keep this one.”

The title appeared against the stars.

# HONOR’S SLAYER

Continued with love, grief, and the stubborn refusal to let a good journey end unfinished.

The screen faded—then returned on Sphinx, fifty years after Hades.

Buffy, sixty-seven, worked in her garden. The Archive was growing. The Vigil was doing the work. Somewhere in the canopy, a treecat waited without demanding that she be ready before she was ready.

“In most stories,” the narrator said quietly, “the hero saves the world and gets an ending.”

Buffy pressed another seedling into the soil.

“This one lets her have an after.”

She looked toward the trees.

Still here.

Forward.
