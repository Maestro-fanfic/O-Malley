Chapter 59

Corrin didn't move in, exactly — she took a small room at the college, close enough to walk Dara to and from her lessons most days — but she became a fixture at the household's kitchen table with a speed that surprised everyone except, apparently, Willow, who claimed she'd seen it coming from the moment Corrin had accepted a cup of tea without flinching at how it was offered.

"That's a very small thing to build a prediction on," Xander said, watching Corrin and Dara hunched together over Meren's old journal at the far end of the table, heads bent close, tracing some diagram with matching careful concentration.

"It's not that small," Willow said. "People who've spent years alone by choice usually flinch at kindness the first dozen times it's offered, out of habit, before they let themselves believe it's not a trap. She didn't flinch once. That tells you something about how badly she wanted to stop being alone, whether she'd have admitted it herself or not."

"When'd you get so good at reading people?"

"I've had a lot of practice," Willow said, simply, "living in a house that keeps adopting complicated strangers who all secretly want the exact same thing underneath whatever complicated thing they think they want on the surface."

"Which is?"

"Somewhere to belong that doesn't ask them to earn it first," Willow said, and didn't look up from what she was doing, and Xander thought, not for the first time, that Willow understood this whole strange, sprawling, thoroughly adopted household better than any of them, including possibly herself.

Across the table, Corrin looked up from the journal, catching Dara mid-explanation of some technique, and something crossed her face — not quite surprise, something gentler than that, the quiet astonishment of watching a piece of someone she'd lost live on, clear and unmistakable, in someone she'd only just met.

"She does that," Corrin said, quiet, mostly to herself, though Xander was close enough to catch it. "Explains things exactly the way Meren used to. Same rhythm to it. I didn't expect that to survive a teaching, passed down secondhand like that. I thought that kind of thing died with a person."

"Some things don't, I guess," Xander said.

"No," Corrin agreed, watching Dara a moment longer before turning back to the journal, something settled and quiet in her expression now. "Apparently they don't."
