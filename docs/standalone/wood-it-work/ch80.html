Chapter 80

Ishara came through alone first — "trust is built one small risk at a time, and I would rather be the one taking it" — and stood blinking in the hub's cool dim light for a long moment, taking in the couch, the shelves, the whole strange domestic clutter of a household that had clearly never once considered how odd it might look to someone stepping in from open desert.

"It's smaller than I expected," she said finally.

"Everyone says that," Xander said. "About the shop, about the college, about basically everything we've got going on. I think it's a theme."

"It suits you," Ishara said, and there was no insult in it, just plain observation. "Grand things invite grand enemies. Small things get overlooked. I begin to suspect that's not an accident."

"It's not," Elan said, settling into the chair across from her with a fresh pot of tea Willow had already started brewing on pure instinct. "Now. Terms. What does fair, jointly-overseen water access actually look like, on your side of things? I don't want to guess and get it wrong."

Ishara talked for the better part of an hour, and the household listened — really listened, the way Corrin had once said this house was good at, letting someone talk until they'd said the whole of it instead of only the convenient part. The deep-sand clans didn't want ownership of the well, she explained; ownership implied a right to withhold, and withholding water from anyone in true need was, among her people, close to the gravest violation there was. What they wanted was a rotation — clear seasons, clear shares, an agreed method for settling disputes before they turned into raids, and someone trusted by every clan to hold the actual mechanism of the door steady in the middle of it all, since a source like this, left in any one party's hands alone, would eventually become a weapon whether anyone intended it that way or not.

"That someone doesn't have to be us," Elan said. "If your own people would rather hold it themselves—"

"We would not," Ishara said, flat. "No clan trusts another clan with something this valuable. Old grievances run deep in the sand. But we might all trust a household of strange outsiders with no stake in our old feuds and, apparently, an inexhaustible generosity toward anyone who simply asks nicely." A dry, knowing look. "You gave away a fortune in aluminum for carrots, I'm told. Word of that reaches farther than you'd think."

"That story keeps getting bigger every time somebody tells it," Xander said.

"Let it," Ishara said. "A reputation for foolish generosity is, in the desert, a very useful thing to have. Nobody suspects the fool of a hidden agenda."

"I'm not sure whether to be offended," Xander said.

"Don't be. I mean it as the highest compliment my people have to give." Ishara set down her tea, considering the room once more. "We will draft the terms properly, with Tomas and with your Elan, over the coming weeks. But I wanted this first meeting to happen in your space, on your terms, so you would understand we come as partners and not as a threat you need to manage."

"Message received," Elan said. "Loud and clear."
