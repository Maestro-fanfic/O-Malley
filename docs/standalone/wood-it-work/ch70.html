Chapter 70

Alberton visited on a Thursday with an announcement he'd clearly been sitting on for maximum dramatic effect, judging by the way he made everyone gather in the kitchen before he'd say a word of it.

"I've decided," he said, "to name the college's new lecture hall."

"Please," Xander said, already bracing, "please don't say a vegetable."

"I'm naming it after the engine block," Alberton said, with the serene dignity of a man announcing something he considered entirely reasonable. "Not literally. Obviously. But the spirit of it. The Hall of Precise and Repeatable Wonders. I've had the sign commissioned already."

The kitchen went very quiet.

"You named a lecture hall," Jesse said slowly, "after an engine block."

"After the principle of the engine block," Alberton corrected, wounded. "The idea that a wonder doesn't stop being a wonder just because you can make it the same way twice. I think about that concept more than perhaps any other single idea I've encountered since arriving here, and I wanted it commemorated properly."

"That's actually kind of beautiful," Amy said, surprising everyone, herself included.

"Thank you," Alberton said, brightening considerably. "I thought so too. Corrin thought it was pretentious."

"Corrin was right," Corrin called from the other room, not looking up from Meren's journal.

"Corrin is entitled to her opinion," Alberton said, "which is wrong, but which she's entitled to hold regardless." He pulled a rolled parchment from his bag with a flourish, spreading it across the table to reveal an actual architectural rendering, complete with a small engraved plaque design at the entrance. "I even had them work in a little relief carving. Of the engine block. Subtle. Tasteful."

"It is not subtle," Willow said, examining it. "It's a giant stone engine block over the door of a magic college."

"It's tasteful and subtle relative to what I originally proposed," Alberton said, "which involved considerably more gold leaf."

"What was the original proposal?"

"An enormous gold engine block, suspended, rotating slowly, visible from three districts away."

The whole table stared at him.

"I was talked out of it," Alberton said, with the air of a man who still, privately, mourned the loss. "Kestrian said it would trigger a diplomatic incident. Something about implying we did, in fact, have a secret gold engine operation, which would undermine two years of very carefully maintained cover story."

"Kestrian saved you from yourself," Xander said.

"Kestrian saves me from myself weekly," Alberton said, entirely without embarrassment. "It's become something of a formal arrangement. I believe it's in the college's founding charter at this point, informally."

"It is not in the founding charter," came Kestrian's voice from the hub doorway, arriving mid-conversation with the long-suffering patience of a man who'd clearly had this exact argument before. "Though I have, on three separate occasions, considered proposing it be added as an actual clause."

"See?" Alberton said, delighted. "Informal tradition. Practically law."

Kestrian looked at the ceiling, said nothing, and accepted the mug of tea Willow handed him with the weary gratitude of a man who had genuinely, at this point, come to consider unofficial keeper-of-Alberton's-worse-ideas a core part of his diplomatic portfolio, whether the crown had ever formally assigned it to him or not.
