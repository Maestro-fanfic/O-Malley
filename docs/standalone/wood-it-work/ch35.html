Chapter 35

A month later

The regular updates turned into a rhythm of their own — Alberton visiting the palace once every few weeks, carefully vague, always truthful, always careful — and somewhere in that rhythm, a second idea began to take root in him, one he raised almost shyly, over dinner, like he expected to be laughed at for it.

"I've been thinking," he said, "about a school."

"A school for what?" Amy asked.

"For this. All of it. Enchantment, properly taught, with materials nobody's had access to before, methods refined by working with metal this pure." He turned his water glass slowly between his hands, not quite meeting anyone's eyes. "I've spent years watching promising students fail simply because they couldn't afford proper materials, or because nobody bothered teaching them the fundamentals properly before throwing them at the hard problems. I think I could build something better. Something that actually gives ordinary people a real chance at this work, not just the wealthy few who can already afford good tutors."

"That sounds amazing," Willow said, and meant it.

"It would require leaving," Alberton said, quieter now. "Setting up somewhere real, somewhere permanent, not just borrowing a corner of your workshop forever. I don't say that to make anyone feel obligated to keep me. I say it because I think it's time, and I wanted you to hear it from me before it became a rumor Kestrian had to explain."

The table was quiet for a moment.

"We're gonna miss you," Xander said, plain and simple, and Alberton's careful composure cracked, just slightly, at the honesty of it.

"I'm not leaving you," he said. "I'm leaving the workbench. There's a considerable difference, and I intend to make full use of that door of yours to make sure everyone remembers it."
