Chapter 67

The following month, a letter came through the hub door that wasn't from Kestrian or the college or anyone official at all — a plain, unstamped envelope that simply appeared on the workbench one morning, addressed, in careful, unfamiliar handwriting, to Dara, care of the household with the door.

Dara turned it over twice before opening it, an old wariness flickering across her face that hadn't shown up in months, the particular caution of someone who'd learned the hard way that letters didn't always bring good news.

Inside was a single page, brief and plainly written.

I heard, through people who still talk to people who talk to me, that Meren's student had resurfaced, safe, and had found Corrin, and that the two of you were teaching again. I knew Meren too, once, a long time before Corrin did, back when we were all young enough to think the work was simple. I don't expect you to know my name. I'm not sure I have much to offer beyond an old woman's memories of a friend I lost touch with before you were born. But if you ever want another voice who remembers her, before all of us who did are gone — I'm not hard to find, if someone knows where to look. No hurry. Some doors are worth leaving open rather than rushing through. — R.

Dara read it twice, then set it down on the workbench, quiet for a long moment.

"Another one," Willow said, coming to read over her shoulder. "Another person who knew her."

"Looks like it," Dara said, and there was something different in her voice than there'd been the first time a letter like this had upended her whole understanding of who Meren was — steadier this time, more curious than afraid. "I don't recognize the name. Just an initial."

"You going to look into it?"

Dara considered the letter a moment longer, then folded it carefully and tucked it into the same pocket where she'd once kept Meren's warding cord, close and safe and not urgent.

"Eventually," she said. "Not today. Not even this month, probably. But eventually, yeah." She looked up, something easy and unhurried in her expression that would have been unrecognizable on the terrified, soaking-wet girl who'd first stumbled through that door months ago. "I used to think every piece of Meren's life I didn't know was a hole I had to fill immediately, before it swallowed me. I don't feel that way anymore. Turns out some doors really are worth leaving open, instead of rushing through the second they appear."

"That's a very grown-up thing to say," Willow said, warm.

"I've had good teachers," Dara said, and smiled at the sound of it, the phrase that had traveled from Xander to Elan to Alberton to Kestrian to, apparently, now her — a small, worn, well-loved piece of this household's shared language, passed hand to hand the same easy way everything else worth keeping seemed to get passed around here.

She tucked the letter away for another day, and went back to work, and the shop settled back into its ordinary spring rhythm around her, one more door left open on purpose, patient, unhurried, waiting for whenever she was ready to walk through it — which was, everyone in that household had slowly learned, very often the best way for a good story to keep on being told.
