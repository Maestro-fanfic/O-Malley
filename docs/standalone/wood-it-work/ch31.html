Chapter 31

Two weeks later

The ring finished on a Tuesday, and Alberton insisted on a small, entirely unnecessary ceremony to mark the occasion, which mostly consisted of everyone standing around the workbench while he held it up to the light with both hands like it might dissolve if he breathed on it wrong.

"It's beautiful," Willow said, and meant it. The band caught the light in three distinct colors depending on the angle, evidence, Alberton explained, of the layered bindings working exactly as intended rather than fighting each other for dominance.

"Try it," Jesse said. "Come on. Cast something."

Alberton slid it on, closed his eyes, murmured something under his breath, and a small, warm light bloomed in his open palm, steady and unhurried, the kind of cantrip that would have taken real concentration to hold a minute ago and now sat there like it cost him nothing at all.

"It works," he said, and there was something almost boyish in the way he said it, all his careful scholarly composure cracking for just a moment into pure, uncomplicated delight. "It actually works."

"Course it works," Xander said. "You're good at your job."

"I am good at my job," Alberton agreed, still staring at the light in his hand like he'd never seen anything like it, even though he'd built it himself, "but good has never once, in my entire career, meant fast, and this took a fraction of the time it should have, because your metal doesn't fight me the way ordinary metal does. Do you understand what this means? If I can teach even a handful of other craftsmen to work with aluminum this pure, we could change what magical items even are. Cheaper. Faster. Available to people who could never have afforded them before."

"That sounds like a big deal," Amy said.

"It is a very big deal," Alberton said, sobering slightly. "Which is also, I suspect, going to be a problem, because big deals tend to attract attention, and I don't think either of our worlds is quite ready for the kind of attention this could bring."
