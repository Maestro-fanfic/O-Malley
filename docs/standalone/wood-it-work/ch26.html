Chapter 26

The workshop was bigger than it had any right to be, which Xander had stopped questioning around the time he'd found out closets could lead to spice shops.

Alberton set the engine block down on a workbench like it was made of eggshells and immediately started rearranging three other projects to give it more room. "It needs its own table," he muttered. "A pedestal, perhaps."

"It's aluminum, not the Ark of the Covenant," Jesse said.

"To you," Alberton said.

Willow wandered along the shelves, trailing a finger over jars of things Xander had learned not to ask about. "You said tattoos would take longer to figure out than rings," she said. "Why?"

"Rings I understand," Alberton said, not looking up from the block. "Enchant an object, bind the effect, done. Tattoos aren't objects. They're skin. Skin moves, skin heals, skin argues with the magic if you don't get the binding exactly right." He frowned. "I had a teacher who lost a finger that way. The tattoo decided the finger wasn't skin enough to keep."

"Good to know," Brack said. "Add that to the list of things we're not telling the boys before they agree to it."

"They're going to find out eventually," Elan said.

"Eventually," Brack agreed, "is a fine word. It means not today."

Xander leaned against the workbench and watched Alberton run his thumb along the machined edge of the engine block again, the way a man might touch a photograph of someone he missed. "You okay there?"

"I keep thinking about how you made this," Alberton said. "Not the metal. The evenness of it. Every groove the same depth as the last." He shook his head like he was trying to clear water out of his ears. "We carve by hand and call it fine work if two craftsmen can't tell whose hand did which half."

"We've got machines that do the carving," Xander said. "People just tell the machines what to carve."

"Machines that carve," Alberton repeated slowly, "on command."

"Don't," Willow said.

"I wasn't going to ask," Alberton said, in the tone of a man who was absolutely going to ask, later, when he thought no one was listening.

Amy poked her head out of the hub doorway. "The movie's over. Sharon wants to know if we're doing dinner here or if we're all walking back before Xander's uncle notices his engine block walked off."

"He noticed weeks ago," Xander said. "He just doesn't believe it's gone until he actually goes looking for it, and he never goes looking for it."

"That's not reassuring," Amy said.

"It's accurate," Xander said. "Different thing."

Alberton finally tore himself away from the block long enough to look at the room full of children discussing theft of a stranger's property like it was a chore rotation. "Does anyone else find it odd that the three of you talk about wonders and thefts and gifting away kingdoms' worth of goods with the same tone you'd use to plan a picnic?"

"We've had a while to get used to it," Xander said.

"You're ten," Alberton said.

"Ten's relative," Jesse said. "Ask us again in a decade."

"I intend to," Alberton said, and went back to his notebook, already three lines into a new list before he'd finished sitting down.

Elan caught Xander's eye across the room and tipped her head toward the door, a small motion, the kind that meant walk with me rather than follow me. He fell into step beside her without being asked twice.

"You gave him a fortune today," she said once they were out of easy earshot. "You do that a great deal, from what I've seen. Willow isn't wrong that you overpay."

"We're not exactly hurting for aluminum," Xander said.

"That's not what I asked."

He glanced at her. She wasn't smiling the way she usually did when she teased him; she had the same look Willow got right before she asked a question she already suspected the answer to.

"Where I'm from," Xander said carefully, "people who have more than they need and don't share it end up alone with it. That's not a fate I'm interested in."

"That's a very old answer for someone who's supposed to be new to all this," Elan said.

"I've had good teachers," Xander said, and left it there, and after a moment, so did she.

Not if. When."

"And?"

"And he thinks you should be worried about that a great deal sooner than you currently are."

"I worry about it," Xander said. "I just don't let it slow anything down."

"That's not the same as worrying."

"It's the only kind I've got time for," he said, and there was nothing performed about it this time, no tidy line waiting to land — just the plain shape of the fact, said the way a person says something they've already made peace with. "We'll deal with it when it's an actual problem instead of a maybe."

Elan didn't answer right away. Somewhere behind them Alberton's voice rose in delighted disbelief over something Jesse had apparently just offered him, and Brack's low laugh followed a beat later.

"For what it's worth," she said finally, "I don't think it'll be as sudden as Brack fears. Kings move slowly when they're not sure yet what they're looking at."

"And when they are sure?"

"Then you'll have wanted every pound of goodwill you ever gave away," Elan said. "So keep going. I'll tell you when to worry properly."

"Deal," Xander said.

"Also," she added, already turning back toward the workshop, "if Alberton names anything after a vegetable, I want it on record that I objected."

"Noted," Xander said. "Loudly and on the record."

They walked back in together, and neither of them mentioned it again that day.

Dinner ended up being pizza, because Sharon pointed out that nobody had actually cooked anything and Alberton had made it three notebooks deep into cantrip theory without noticing the sun had gone down.

"You people eat like this regularly?" Alberton asked, holding a slice at an angle that suggested he suspected it of being a trap.

"Regularly is generous," Willow said. "Xander eats like this. The rest of us eat like this when Xander's cooking isn't an option."

"Hurtful," Xander said, not looking up from his own slice, "but fair."

"What is it, precisely?" Alberton asked, turning the slice another quarter turn.

"Bread, cheese, tomato sauce, whatever you want on top," Jesse said. "Ours has pepperoni, olives, and Xander's contribution."

"Which is?"

"More cheese," Xander said.

"There is such a thing as too much cheese," Amy said.

"There is a rumor of such a thing," Xander corrected. "I've never actually witnessed it."

Alberton finally bit into the slice, chewed once, twice, and then went very still in the specific way that meant a man was recalculating his entire relationship with food. "This," he said slowly, "is going to be a problem."

"Welcome to pizza," Jesse said, saluting him with his own slice.

"No, I mean it will genuinely be a problem," Alberton said. "I have responsibilities. A college to found. Apprentices to train. I cannot in good conscience spend the rest of my days sitting in this room eating flatbread with cheese."

"Sure you can," Sharon said. "You just did the math wrong. Add it to the college. Founder's privilege."

Alberton considered that with the seriousness of a man weighing a genuine theological argument, and took another bite.

Brack leaned over to Elan. "We are going to lose him."

"We lost him around the aluminum," Elan said. "This is just him noticing."

By the time the last box was empty, Alberton had fallen asleep sitting upright in his chair with a half-finished diagram of a Ring of Cantrips still open on the table in front of him, one hand resting on the notebook like he was afraid it might wander off without him.

"Should we wake him?" Amy asked.

"No," Willow said, already pulling a blanket out of the chest she kept in the corner for exactly this kind of situation. "He'll be embarrassed and rush off before he's actually rested, and then he'll make a mistake tomorrow because he's tired, and then he'll blame the mistake on us instead of himself."

"That's oddly specific," Brack said.

"He's not the first mage to fall asleep at that table," Willow said, draping the blanket over Alberton's shoulders with the same brisk competence she used for everything else. "He won't be the last."

Xander caught Jesse's eye across the room, and neither of them said anything, because they'd both learned a long time ago that some things about Willow's hub were easier to accept than to ask about.
