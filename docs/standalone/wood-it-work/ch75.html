Chapter 75

The second flower bed came in ahead of the first, which nobody had predicted and which Amy took as a personal vindication of her seed-spacing argument with Jesse, a victory she milked for considerably longer than the actual stakes of the disagreement warranted.

"I was right," she announced, for what had to be the fifth time that week, standing over a row of thriving marigolds while Jesse's original bed limped along a few feet behind.

"You got lucky," Jesse said.

"I got right. There's a difference."

"Is there, though."

"Yes, Jesse, there is, and I will be reminding you of it regularly for the rest of the summer."

She did, in fact, remind him regularly, and Jesse bore it with the long-suffering patience of a kid who'd learned, over two years of exactly this kind of household, that some losses were better absorbed with grace than fought to the bitter, dignity-losing end.

It was during one of these gardening arguments, midweek, that Alberton arrived unannounced through the hub door, notebook in hand, looking less like a man with an emergency and more like a man who'd simply missed everyone and used the pretext of "checking on the pollinator project" to justify the visit.

"How's the lecture hall?" Xander asked, not looking up from the truck he was working on.

"Finished," Alberton said, with real pride. "The relief carving turned out better than I hoped. Kestrian still thinks it's excessive. I maintain it's exactly the right amount of excessive."

"There's no such thing as the right amount of excessive," Corrin called from the garden, where she'd taken to spending her afternoons off, ostensibly helping Amy, mostly just enjoying the sun and the quiet in a way that still visibly surprised her every time she let herself fully relax into it.

"There is, and I've found it," Alberton said, settling onto the porch steps with the easy familiarity of a man who no longer needed an excuse to simply be present in this household's ordinary hours. "How's the garden faring against Jesse's considerable incompetence?"

"I heard that," Jesse said.

"You were meant to," Alberton said, echoing Kestrian's old line with visible delight at getting to use it himself for once, and the whole yard dissolved into the easy, half-affectionate laughter that had become, over two years, this household's most reliable weather.
