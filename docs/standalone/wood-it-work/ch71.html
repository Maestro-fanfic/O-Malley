Chapter 71

It was Sharon who first noticed that the household had, at some point, quietly accumulated an entire filing system of running jokes with no clear origin, no formal rules, and an alarming tendency to keep growing.

"We have a bit," she announced one evening, holding up a notebook she'd apparently started dedicating to the cause, "called 'I've had good teachers,' with — Xander, seven documented uses. Elan, four. Dara, two. Alberton, one, deployed with what I can only describe as reckless enthusiasm."

"That's not a bit," Xander said. "That's just a sentence people say sometimes."

"It's a bit," Sharon said, unmoved. "It has a documented history. It has spread across two households and a college. That's a bit."

"What else is in there?" Amy asked, delighted, leaning over Sharon's shoulder.

"'Don't thank me, thank the household,'" Sharon read, "currently at three uses, all delivered by people who'd clearly picked it up from each other without realizing it. 'That's not entirely reassuring,' four uses, exclusively in response to something Brack or Kestrian has just said. And a newer one — 'you don't have to.' 'I know. I'm doing it anyway.' Two full exchanges so far, both from Xander, both immediately copied by somebody within the week."

"Who copied it?" Xander asked.

"Alberton," Sharon said. "To Dara. About the workshop."

"That's not copying, that's just — a nice thing people say to each other sometimes."

"It's a bit," Sharon said again, patient, the way you'd explain something to a child who was very sweet but very slow on a particular point. "This whole household runs on, like, four recycled sentences and everyone's too fond of each other to notice they're doing it."

"I refuse to be tracked," Jesse said.

"You're in here twice," Sharon said. "Both under 'that happened,' both delivered after something genuinely wild, both completely deadpan."

"That's just a normal thing to say after something wild happens."

"It's a bit, Jesse."

Jesse opened his mouth to argue further, considered the sheer weight of documented evidence now stacked against him, and closed it again, defeated — a kid who knew a losing battle once Sharon had brought actual data to it.

"Fine," he said. "It's a bit. I stand by every single deployment of it, though. Historically accurate usage, every time."

"Nobody's disputing the historical accuracy," Sharon said, already turning the page to start a new entry. "I'm just saying somebody should be keeping track, and apparently that somebody is me now, because none of the rest of you seem to have noticed you've built an entire shared language out of, like, gentle affection and repetition."

"That's kind of a nice way to put it, actually," Willow said, considering.

"I have my moments," Sharon said, and went back to her notebook, and the household, entirely unbothered by having its own running jokes catalogued back at it with academic rigor, went right back to producing several more of them before the evening was out.
