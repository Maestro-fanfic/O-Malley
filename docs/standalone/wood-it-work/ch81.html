Chapter 81

The treaty took six weeks to draft properly, three trips back and forth through the trunk-door, and one truly memorable afternoon when Jesse, left briefly unsupervised near the negotiating table, tried to explain the concept of a keychain to a delegation of deep-sand elders and somehow ended up promising them a shipment of "little metal water drops, for luck," which turned out, against all odds, to become one of the most popular trade goods the household ever produced.

"You cannot just promise sacred water-luck charms to a foreign delegation without checking with anyone first," Willow said, for what had to be the fourth time that week.

"I didn't promise sacred anything," Jesse said. "I promised keychains. They're the ones who decided it was sacred."

"That's worse, somehow."

"It's good business," Jesse said, entirely unrepentant. "You should be thanking me."

By the time the terms were finally settled — a rotation calendar, a dispute process, Corrin quietly volunteered to help maintain the door's stability given her considerable experience with exactly this kind of delicate, high-stakes hub-work — Tomas's little settlement had grown from a lonely outpost into something closer to a proper waystation, deep-sand traders passing through regularly now, trading water-rights goodwill for goods from a dozen different worlds the household had quietly stitched together, one open door at a time, over years nobody had quite noticed adding up.

"You built a trade network," Alberton said, delighted, when he finally heard the full scope of it. "An actual, functioning, multi-world trade network. Do you have any idea how many scholars would kill to properly document this?"

"Nobody's documenting anything," Elan said, firm. "Quietly is the whole point."

"Quietly, then," Alberton agreed, though the gleam in his eye suggested he intended to write it all down eventually, very carefully, very privately, for an audience of exactly no one but himself.

Ishara visited twice more that season, once to finalize the last details, once simply because, as she put it, "I find I enjoy your tea, and your particular flavor of foolishness, more than I expected to." She brought a gift the second time — a small, sun-bleached carving, a creature Xander didn't immediately recognize until Willow, delighted, pointed out the shape of it against the light.

"It's a mouse," Willow said. "Like the one on your second moon."

"You noticed that too," Ishara said, pleased. "Most outsiders never look up long enough to see it."

"We read a book," Jesse said, straight-faced, and Ishara laughed — a real laugh, warm and surprised out of her, the same way Then'tal's had been, the same way this whole household seemed to have a gift for coaxing out of people who hadn't expected to laugh that day.
