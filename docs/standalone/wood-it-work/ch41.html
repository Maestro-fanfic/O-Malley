Chapter 41

The quiet lasted eleven days, which Willow noted, dryly, was a new household record, before the hub door hummed to life on a Wednesday evening and deposited, not a herald, not a scout, but a girl about their own age, soaking wet, breathing hard, radiating the frantic energy of someone who'd started running before she'd decided where.

"Sorry," the girl said, immediately, both hands up in the exact gesture Kestrian had used, which suggested it was less a personal habit and more something people over there learned early.

She was younger than Elan, older than Amy, soaked through from collar to boots, and clutching a battered leather satchel to her chest with both arms like someone might try to take it from her at any moment. A row of fine metal tools peeked out from the satchel's open flap — chisels, a small hammer, instruments Xander didn't recognize but whose careful, well-used condition he understood immediately, the same way he understood his own tools.

"Whoa, okay," Jesse said, hands up too, mirroring her without quite meaning to. "Nobody's mad. You're just — really wet, and really fast, and also really not who we usually get through that door."

"Where am I?" the girl asked, eyes darting around the hub, taking in the couch, the shelves, the whole impossible domestic clutter of it with the particular wide, disbelieving stare of someone whose whole understanding of the world had just been rearranged in the last five minutes.

"Somewhere safe," Willow said, gentle, already moving toward her slow and non-threatening, the same careful approach she used with any frightened creature. "What's your name?"

"Dara," the girl said, and then, in a rush, like she'd been holding it in since the moment she'd arrived, "I didn't steal anything. Please. Whatever this door is, whoever it belongs to, I didn't steal anything, I just needed somewhere to run, and it opened, and I didn't have anywhere else—"

"Nobody thinks you stole anything," Willow said, though her eyes flicked once, briefly, to the fine tools in the satchel, the kind of question anyone might reasonably have.

Dara's hands were shaking, and Willow, without a word, draped a blanket over her shoulders, the same brisk, practiced kindness she'd shown Alberton months ago. "I didn't steal anything. The tools are mine. My master gave them to me before she died, they're the only thing I have left of her, and I just — I panicked. I ran. I didn't even think about where the running was taking me until I was already through."

"How'd the door open for you?" Willow asked, checking the controls again, brow furrowed. "It's warded. It shouldn't just open for anybody who happens to be standing near it."

Dara reached into her coat, hands still trembling, and produced a small, worn, leather cord with three tiny beads strung on it. "My master gave me this too. She said it was keyed to a friend of hers, somewhere far off, someone she trusted enough to send me to if things ever went badly enough. I didn't know what it meant until the door just — opened. On its own. When I was close enough and scared enough, I suppose."

Willow examined the cord carefully, tracing the small, worn warding etched into each bead, and her expression shifted, slow and startled. "This is old work. Really old. Whoever made this knew exactly what they were doing." She looked up. "Who was your master? The hedge witch?"

"Meren," Dara said. "Everyone just called her Meren. She never talked much about where she came from, before the village. Just that she used to know people who did impossible things with doors."

The kitchen went very quiet.

"Meren," Elan repeated slowly, something complicated moving across her face. "That's — I know that name. Or I knew it, once, a long time ago, in a life I don't talk about much."

"You knew her?" Dara asked, hope and disbelief tangled together in her voice.

"I knew of her," Elan said carefully. "Which in her case was usually as much as anyone ever got." She looked at Xander, and there was something new in the look, something that hadn't been there a moment ago — not alarm, exactly, more the look of someone recalculating a situation in real time. "If I'm remembering correctly, and I hope I'm not, because it complicates things considerably — Meren wasn't just a hedge witch who happened to know a friend with a hub door. She was one of the very few people who ever helped build one."
