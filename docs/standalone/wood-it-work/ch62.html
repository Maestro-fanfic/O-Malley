Chapter 62

Preparing for a king turned out to involve considerably less pageantry and considerably more argument than Xander had expected.

"We are not," Elan said, for what had to be the fourth time that evening, "walking in there pretending none of this happened. That's the single fastest way to lose his trust permanently. He already knows enough to ask the question. If we try to hide the rest, he'll assume we're hiding something worse than the truth actually is, and act accordingly."

"Nobody's saying hide it," Alberton countered, pacing the length of the kitchen with the restless energy of a man who'd clearly been rehearsing arguments in his head for days. "I'm saying there's a difference between honesty and volunteering every detail of a fifteen-year-old near catastrophe to a king who's never even met Corrin, in a room full of his advisors, with no context for any of it."

"He'll have context," Elan said. "He'll have whatever context Kestrian's already given him, filtered through however many advisors got their hands on the report first, which means half-truths, guesswork, and probably at least one advisor who's already decided this is dangerous and is looking for reasons to prove it. The only way to control that narrative is to get ahead of it with the actual truth, told plainly, before somebody else's version of it fills the gap."

Corrin, who'd been quiet through most of the argument, finally spoke. "She's right," she said, and both Elan and Alberton turned, slightly surprised. "I spent fifteen years believing that hiding was the safest choice. It kept me alive, I suppose, but it also kept me alone, and it left exactly this kind of half-story sitting out there for someone else to eventually stumble across and misread." She looked at Dara. "I don't want to make that mistake again. Not with something this important. If the king wants the truth, I'd rather give it to him myself, in my own words, than have him piece together a worse version from rumor and old fear."

"You'd testify," Xander said. "In front of the king. About all of it."

"I would," Corrin said, steadier than Xander expected. "I'm tired of being a rumor other people get to define. I'd rather be a person he actually meets."

The room was quiet for a moment, taking that in.

"Then that's the plan," Elan said, finally, some of the tension in her shoulders easing now that the argument had resolved itself in the direction she'd been pushing. "Full honesty about the history — Meren, Corrin, the scaling theory, why it was abandoned, why it matters that it stays abandoned. Careful, measured honesty about the present — what Dara's rediscovered, what it means for the college, what it doesn't mean, specifically, for anyone hoping to weaponize it." She looked at Xander. "And you, and this household, presented exactly as what you actually are. Not more mysterious than the truth. Not less remarkable, either. Just true."

"That's a tall order for one audience," Jesse said.

"It's a tall order for anyone," Elan agreed. "But it's mine and Corrin's and Kestrian's tall order to carry in that room, not yours. Your job is a lot simpler."

"What's my job?" Xander asked.

"Sit still, be yourself, and let the grown-ups do the talking parts that matter," Elan said. "You're good at the second one. Work on the first."

"Rude," Xander said, without much heat, and went to go find his good shirt, because even a ten-year-old knew you didn't show up to meet a king in the one with the grease stain.
