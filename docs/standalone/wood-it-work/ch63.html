Chapter 63

The night before the audience, Xander couldn't sleep, and found Willow awake too, sitting on the porch steps with a mug of something steaming despite the cold, looking out at a street gone quiet and dark under a sky thick with more stars than the city ever properly showed.

"Nervous?" she asked, as he sat down beside her.

"A little," he admitted. "Not about the talking part. I've talked my way through worse than a king's advisors. More about — I don't know. What happens after. If it goes well, what does 'well' even look like, long-term? If it goes badly, what does badly cost us?"

"I don't think anyone knows that yet," Willow said. "I don't think anyone can know it, until we're standing in that room and finding out in real time." She sipped her drink, thoughtful. "For what it's worth, I'm not scared of the audience itself. I've watched this household walk into harder rooms than a king's court and come out the other side mostly intact. I trust us for that part."

"What are you scared of, then?"

Willow was quiet for a moment, considering the question honestly instead of brushing past it. "I think I'm scared of what happens once the door's fully open," she said finally. "Right now, this is still small. Contained. A handful of people who know the whole truth, a household that's learned to hold a lot of complicated things carefully because it's had practice. Once a king knows, officially, formally, with advisors and records and whatever comes after that — it stops being small. It becomes a thing kingdoms track. A thing that gets written down in places we don't control, referenced by people we'll never meet, shaping decisions years from now that none of us will have any say in." She looked over at him. "I like small. I like knowing everyone who knows. I don't know how to be scared of something that specific and also know there's nothing we can actually do to stop it from happening, other than walk in there tomorrow and do our best to shape what it becomes instead of just bracing for whatever it turns into without us."

Xander considered that, the cold settling around them, the stars indifferent and steady overhead. "That's a good thing to be scared of, I think," he said finally. "Better than being scared of the wrong thing."

"Is that supposed to be comforting?"

"A little," Xander said. "Mostly it's just true. You're not scared because you think we'll mess up tomorrow. You're scared because tomorrow's the last day any of this gets to stay ours alone. That's a real thing to grieve a little, even while we're doing it on purpose, for good reasons."

Willow was quiet a moment, then leaned her head against his shoulder, unexpected, familiar in a way that had become easy between them over years of exactly this kind of late, quiet honesty. "When did you get good at that?" she asked. "Naming the actual thing instead of just the comfortable version of it."

"I've had good teachers," Xander said, and felt her huff a small laugh against his shoulder at the phrase worn smooth from months of use across this whole sprawling family.

They sat together a while longer, not talking, just watching the stars and the quiet street and the last ordinary night before everything got, in whatever shape it ended up taking, a little less small.
