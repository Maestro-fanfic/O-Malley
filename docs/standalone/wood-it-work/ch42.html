Chapter 42

"Okay," Jesse said, into the silence that followed, "I feel like that sentence needs about four more sentences after it before I understand how big a problem we just adopted."

"It's not necessarily a problem," Elan said, though she didn't sound entirely convinced of that herself. "It might just be a coincidence with unusually good timing. Meren could have been one voice among several who helped with old hub-door construction, decades ago, and simply never mentioned it because it never came up. Not every old story is a dangerous one."

"But you don't actually believe that," Willow said.

"I don't disbelieve it either," Elan said. "I believe we don't have enough to know yet, and I'd rather sit with not-knowing than guess wrong in either direction." She looked at Dara, who'd gone very still and very small on the couch, blanket still around her shoulders, clearly bracing for the household to decide she was more trouble than she was worth. "Whatever Meren was or wasn't, that's not your fault, and it's not a reason for anyone in this room to look at you differently. You're a scared kid who just lost the only home she had. That's the part that matters tonight."

Dara's shoulders came down half an inch, the first real relaxation since she'd stumbled through the door.

"Can she stay?" Amy asked, into the quiet, the question everyone else had clearly been circling without quite asking it outright.

"Of course she can stay," Xander said, like it wasn't even a question worth debating.

"Welcome to the extremely long list of people we've adopted without entirely meaning to," Jesse told Dara, raising his glass of lemonade in something like a toast. "You're in good company. Ask the wizard. Oh, wait, he's not here. Ask literally anyone else, then."

Dara laughed again, steadier this time, and took the offered glass Amy handed her, and for the first time since she'd stumbled soaking wet out of a door she hadn't known existed an hour before, something in her shoulders came all the way down.
