Chapter 54

Elan left three days later, traveling light, dressed like a merchant's daughter with a small pack and a story about visiting distant cousins that she'd built the same careful way she built every cover story — mostly true, arranged to mislead.

"How long?" Xander asked, helping her check the pack one last time before she stepped through the hub door toward the nearest safe crossing point into the northern territories.

"A week, if the village is easy to read. Two, if it isn't. Longer, if I find something worth staying for." She looked at him, steady. "Don't let Dara spiral in the meantime. She'll want to. Give her something to do with her hands instead."

"That's oddly specific advice."

"It's advice I've needed myself, more than once," Elan said, and didn't elaborate, and Xander didn't push, because some things about Elan's own history were still doors she hadn't opened yet, and he'd learned better than to knock on them uninvited.

The week that followed was, true to Elan's prediction, hard on Dara in exactly the way Elan had anticipated. She threw herself into work with a kind of fierce, unrelenting focus — long afternoons at the college with Corrin close by, refining diagrams, cross-checking Meren's remembered techniques against whatever fragments Alberton's archives could offer, filling notebook after notebook with careful, cramped handwriting until Corrin finally had to gently insist she take an actual afternoon off before she wore herself down to nothing.

"I don't know how to wait well," Dara admitted, on one of the evenings she came through to the shop instead, sitting on the workbench with her knees pulled up, watching Xander finish a brake job on an old sedan. "Meren used to say waiting was its own kind of work, if you did it right. I never really understood what she meant until now."

"What do you think she meant?" Xander asked, not looking up from the brake line, giving her the specific kind of half-attention that made it easier to talk, the way working alongside someone was always easier than facing them directly.

"I think she meant you can wait badly, all clenched up and miserable, making the waiting worse by fighting it," Dara said slowly, working it out as she spoke. "Or you can wait like it's a job. Like there's an actual task in it — staying steady, staying ready, not letting the not-knowing hollow you out before there's even anything to know. I think that's what she meant by doing it right."

"That sounds like a hard skill."

"It is. I'm not good at it yet." She picked at a loose thread on her sleeve. "I keep imagining every version of how this goes. Elan finds her, and she's exactly who I hoped. Elan finds her, and she's a stranger who doesn't remember Meren fondly at all. Elan finds nothing, and the not-knowing just — stays, forever, the same shape it's always been, and I have to make peace with that instead." She looked up. "I don't know which version I'm actually braced for. I think that's the part that's hardest. Not knowing what to brace for."

Xander set down his wrench, wiped his hands, and sat on the workbench beside her, not saying anything for a moment, just letting the quiet sit between them the way quiet sometimes needed to sit before words were any good.

"For what it's worth," he said eventually, "whichever version it turns out to be, you're not finding out alone. That's not nothing."

"No," Dara agreed, something in her shoulders easing slightly. "It's really not."

They sat together a while longer, the garage quiet around them, the ordinary sounds of a Saturday afternoon drifting in from outside — a lawnmower somewhere down the block, a dog barking twice and then losing interest — the ordinary noise of a world that had no idea how much was hanging, right now, on one woman's careful questions in a village eleven years gone cold — and neither of them said anything else, because for once, there wasn't anything else that needed saying.
