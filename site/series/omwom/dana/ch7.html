Chapter 7 of 16

Eyes Only

"Harry Tasker" (POV):

Traffic filled the avenue in both directions, the ordinary daytime crawl of downtown, pale government buildings rising up behind the trees on either side. Harry rode it out in the passenger seat same as always, still in the dark suit he wore for a job that officially didn't exist, until Helen's building came up on the right.

"Pull over here."

Gib glanced across at him, surprised. "What's up?"

Harry was already reaching for the door. "I screwed up with Helen last night." The car had barely stopped rolling before he was out of it. "Gonna see if she's free for lunch. Smooth things over."

Gib blinked at the empty passenger seat. "So, you just want me to hang?"

"Yeah. Just hang." Harry didn't slow down to say it.

"Okay, buddy." Gib settled back, resigned. "I'll just be hanging."

Inside, Helen's office was nothing like his own, gray partitions and stacked folders and the low hum of people who actually did the jobs they said they did. Harry came down the aisle relaxed, already halfway into the version of this lunch where he apologized and she rolled her eyes and forgave him before the check came.

Behind the front desk, Allison had a call on hold. She swiveled toward Helen's cubicle, delighted with herself. "Helen. It's your mystery man. Line two."

Helen's head came up fast. "Simon?" Then, softer, half to herself: "Oh, my God."

Harry stopped walking.

He didn't announce himself. He drifted sideways instead, behind the nearest file shelving, out of Helen's line of sight before he'd fully decided to hide at all.

Helen picked up the line. "Hello? Simon?" A pause, listening, her eyes flicking around the office to see who might be close enough to hear her half of it. "Yes, I can talk." Another pause. "You mean right now?" Then, quick, decided: "Yes. I can meet you."

Harry stood very still.

"Okay," Helen said, and there was no mistaking the delight in it. "I can't wait." A beat. "Bye."

She hung up and turned straight to Allison. "Can you cover for me? An hour?"

"Just an hour?" Allison didn't bother hiding the grin. "Tell that stud to take his time."

"Shut up." Helen was already gathering her things, flustered and lit up at once. "I should never have told you about him."

She came down the aisle fast, close enough that Harry had to press himself back against the shelving to keep from being seen, and passed within three feet of him without ever turning her head.

"Have a good time," Allison called after her.

"Get a little for me while you're at it."

"Shut up," Helen said again, laughing, and then she was gone.

Harry didn't move for a long moment. He'd walked in carrying an apology and a lunch reservation. What he walked out with instead was mystery man, and Simon, and I can't wait, and that stud, five fragments of somebody else's conversation stitched together into a story he'd never actually heard the other half of.

He came out of the building like a man who'd been hit and hadn't noticed the punch land yet, gaze somewhere past the middle distance, one hand pressed to his own stomach.

He walked straight into the street without seeing the bus.

The horn was already sounding when Gib grabbed him, hauled him back onto the curb hard enough to stagger them both, the bus close enough behind them that its own driver had time to lean out and add an opinion on the way past.

Gib got him upright against the car and studied his face. "What's going on, man. You sick or something? You look like you got gut-kicked."

Harry opened his mouth. "It's Helen."

Gib waited.

"It's Helen."

"It has something to do with Helen, I'm guessing."

"Helen's having an affair."

Gib absorbed it, and for one horrible second his whole face did something almost like relief, like a man recognizing a room he'd been in before. "Welcome to the club, man."

"It can't be. Not Helen."

"Nobody thinks it can happen to them the first time, buddy." Gib was talking faster now, the words outrunning whatever was actually behind them. "Same exact thing happened to me with wife number two, remember? Had no idea anything was going on. I come home one day and the house is completely empty. And I mean completely empty. She even took the ice-cube trays out of the freezer. What kind of a sick bitch takes the ice-cube trays out of the freezer?"

Harry wasn't hearing any of it. "Not Helen."

Gib tried again, softer this time, a hand landing on Harry's shoulder like he was talking somebody off a ledge. "Hey, Harry. Hey, listen. Helen still loves you. You know, she just wants to bang this guy for a while, you know? It's nothing serious. You'll get used to it."

Harry had him by the shirt and the tie before either of them seemed to register it happening, Gib's back against the car, Harry's face six inches from his.

"Stop. Cheering. Me up."

Gib went very still, and when he answered this time the joking was gone out of his voice entirely. "What'd you expect, Harry? Helen's a flesh-and-blood woman, and you're never there. It was just a matter of time."

Harry's hands opened. He let him go.

Gib straightened his collar, his tie, didn't say anything else for a second while Harry turned and stared out at the traffic like it might have an answer he could use. The street kept moving around both of them, ordinary and indifferent, while Harry stood still with the whole ordinary cost of a life he'd spent fifteen years hiding from the one person he'd built it to protect.

"You know what?" Gib recovered first, steering him gently toward the passenger door like a man walking home someone freshly concussed. "I say we concentrate on work, buddy. That's what I do every time my life turns to dog shit. I concentrate on work, and that gets me by. All right? It's gonna be great. We're gonna catch some terrorists. We're gonna beat the crap out of them. You're gonna feel a hell of a lot better."

He got Harry folded into the seat, watched his head on the frame, shut the door, came around and got behind the wheel himself.

Harry sat rigid the whole way, staring straight ahead, two fingers pressed near his own mouth like he was holding something in.

Gib glanced over, found enough of his usual self to offer one last word on the subject. "Women." A beat. "Can't live with 'em." Another. "Can't kill 'em."

The car pulled away from the curb, out into traffic, the daytime street sliding out of frame behind them.

By the time the Tasker house came back into view, it was night again, and nobody standing in it had any idea what Harry Tasker had spent his afternoon deciding was true.
