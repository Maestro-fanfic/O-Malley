The Call

The satellite phone rang at an hour that meant trouble before he'd even answered it, and Xander fumbled it awake in the dark of his rented room, half-braced for bad news about the compound, about one of the girls, about anything except the voice that actually greeted him.

"Hey," Faith said, and something in the single syllable was wrong, worn thin in a way he'd never once heard from her, not even at the worst of the war.

"Faith?" He sat up fully, all the sleep gone out of him at once. "What's wrong?"

"Nothing. Everything. I don't know." A long, ragged breath crackled down the line. "I left Robin. Tonight. Didn't really think it through, just, grabbed what I could carry and walked."

Give her room, Coulson said, quiet, immediate. Don't fill the silence. Let her get to it at her own pace.

Xander held the phone, held the silence, waited.

"You're not gonna ask what happened?"

"Figured you'd tell me when you were ready. Not gonna push."

Something that might have been a laugh, small and broken, came down the line. "Yeah. That's kind of why I called you and not, I don't know, literally anyone else in this whole operation."

"How'd you find out? Whatever it was."

"Overheard him. Stupid, right? Straight out of a bad movie." She let out a breath that wasn't quite a laugh. "He was on the phone in his study, door not quite shut, and I was just gonna ask if he wanted tea, and instead I heard him laughing with somebody about how far he'd come, using words like 'asset' and 'PR value' and 'the wild one, finally settled,' like he was describing a horse he'd broken instead of a person he'd been sleeping next to for a year."

Another breath, steadier this time. "I found out he's been working an angle. Whole time. Politicking with the Council behind my back, telling people he's the one who tamed the wild Slayer, like I'm some kind of trophy he landed instead of an actual person he's supposed to, I don't know, actually give a damn about."

"Faith..."

"Let me finish. I need to actually say this out loud once, all the way through, or I'm gonna choke on it." Her voice hardened, old defenses locking back into place even through the hurt. "I confronted him. Tonight. Told him exactly what I'd heard, word for word, gave him the chance to tell me it was a lie. He didn't. Got angry instead. Real angry. Raised his hand like he was gonna..." She stopped, and Xander felt something cold and furious settle in his own chest, waiting. "He didn't. I want to be clear, he didn't actually hit me. But he raised it, and I saw exactly what he was capable of in that one second, and that was enough. That was more than enough."

"Man," a new voice said, one nobody in the gallery had heard from before, brash and half a beat too eager, "that's a lot of pain in a very pretty package. Come to think of it, that package looks very fa..."

The voice stopped itself. Dead, mid-word, the easy leer draining out of it in real time as whatever had been about to follow that sentence apparently caught up with the man saying it, some private recognition landing a half-second too late to stop the first half of the thought but plenty fast enough to strangle the second half in its crib.

"...Never mind," the same voice said, considerably smaller now. "Forget I said anything. That's, huh. That's somebody's kid. That's actually somebody's kid, isn't it. Christ."

"GIBSON." Fury's voice, flat, immediate, though there was something almost merciful in the timing of it, cutting in right as the man clearly needed something else to focus on. "Not tonight. Not this scene. Sit down."

"Sitting down. Already sitting down. Extremely sat."

Nobody asked who Gibson was, or what he'd almost finished saying, or whose kid he meant. This narrator suspects there will be time for all three later. Tonight was not it.

Xander, Coulson said, low, urgent. Stay steady for her. Whatever you're feeling right now, she needs steady, not fury, not right this second.

Xander breathed through it, banked the fury down where it could wait its turn, though it didn't go anywhere, just sat there, patient, a debt he fully intended to collect on someday if the opportunity ever presented itself. "You did the right thing. Leaving. Right then, no packing, no waiting around to see if he'd apologize his way back into your good graces."

"Didn't feel like a choice, honestly. Felt like my legs just decided for me before my brain caught up." A shaky breath. "I keep replaying it. The look on his face when I said his name out loud, before I even told him what I'd heard. Like he already knew, already had the excuse half-built. That's the part that gets me, Xander. He had a speech ready. Like he'd been waiting for the day I'd catch him, rehearsing it."

"That's its own kind of answer, isn't it. Man doesn't build a defense speech for something he's not already guilty of."

"Yeah. Figured that out somewhere around minute two of him giving it to me."

"Yeah. I know that logically. Doesn't stop it from hurting like hell." Her voice cracked properly this time, the wall finally giving. "I thought he was different, Xander. Thought I'd actually found somebody who saw me as, I don't know, a person, not an asset, not a story he could tell about himself. Guess I was wrong about that too."

"You weren't wrong to want it. That's not the same as being wrong about him." Xander felt his way through the words carefully, wanting to get this exactly right. "Some people are good at looking like the real thing right up until it costs them something to actually be it. That's on him, Faith. Every bit of that's on him. Not on you for believing somebody who was good at lying."

Silence, for a long moment, just her breathing, steadier now, something in it slowly settling.

"Why'd you call me and not Buffy? Or Willow?"

"Because you're the only one who's never once needed anything from me." Faith said it plain, no defenses left to hide behind. "Everybody else, there's history, there's old stuff, there's them needing me to be a certain version of okay so they don't have to worry. You've just always kind of, taken me as I come. Never asked for anything back. Figured you were the one person who'd actually let me fall apart without needing me to apologize for it after."

"Man," Gibson's voice said, quiet, none of the earlier leer left anywhere in it, "I'm starting to feel all gooey inside."

"Shut up, you." But there wasn't any heat in it, just Fury, sounding almost fond of the man he'd told to sit down twenty minutes ago.

Something warm and complicated settled in Xander's chest, hearing that, a feeling he didn't have a name ready for yet and didn't try to force one onto.

"You can fall apart as long as you need to," he said, quiet, meaning it entirely. "I'm not going anywhere. Literally can't, actually, I'm on a different continent, but you know what I mean."

That got a real laugh out of her, small but genuine, the first crack of actual light through the whole conversation. "Yeah. I know what you mean."

"Where are you now? Physically, I mean. You said you walked."

"Bus station. Cleveland's a big enough city, there's always a bus station open somewhere." A pause, and when she spoke again some of the rawness had receded, replaced by something more like her usual dry, deflecting humor, worn thin but present. "Don't worry, I'm not actually sleeping on a bench like some tragic movie montage. Got a room. Just needed somewhere quiet to make this call without an audience."

"What happens now? With the house. The rebuild."

"Guess I finish what I started, professionally, and figure out the personal stuff on my own time. Not gonna let him chase me off a project I put actual sweat into." Something fierce came back into her voice, familiar, steadying. "Might take some time off after, though. Head out somewhere nobody's expecting me to be anybody's anything for a while."

"That sounds smart, actually."

"Don't sound so surprised."

"I'm not surprised. I'm impressed. Different thing." He managed a small smile she couldn't see but might have heard anyway, in his voice. "You're allowed to take care of yourself, Faith. Not just everybody else. Been meaning to tell you that for a while, actually, long before tonight gave me the opening."

"Yeah," Fury's voice said, quiet, nothing dry left in it at all. "The girl's got the right stuff."

She was quiet a moment, and when she spoke again her voice had gone soft in a way he'd rarely heard from her. "When'd you get so good at this? The saying-the-right-thing stuff. Feels like it snuck up on you sometime in the last year."

"Had a lot of practice. Bunch of scared girls, one very patient roommate, and a whole continent's worth of learning how to actually listen instead of just waiting for my turn to talk." He shrugged, though there was no one there to see it. "Turns out it's a skill like anything else. You just gotta actually work at it."

They talked another hour after that, the specifics of Robin's betrayal giving way slowly to smaller things, the girls Xander was working with, the half-finished renovation on the Cleveland house's east wing, a story about Faith accidentally starting a minor kitchen fire that had nothing to do with anything and made them both laugh anyway, an old friendship finding its footing again in the dark, easier with every passing minute, and by the time Xander finally set the phone down, the sky outside his window had started, faintly, toward gray.

That was Faith, Coulson said, quiet, something thoughtful in the observation. Really Faith. Not the armor she usually wears for everyone else.

Yeah. Xander lay back down, sleep nowhere close to reach now, turning the whole conversation over in his head. Guess we've both had a year of figuring out who actually sees us instead of the story other people tell about us.

I think that might be more significant than you're letting yourself notice right now.

Yeah, well. Xander closed his eyes, exhaustion finally catching up despite everything. Ask me again sometime when I've had actual sleep. Tonight I'm just glad she called somebody who'd actually pick up.
