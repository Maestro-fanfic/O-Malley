The Legal Part (Such As It Is)

Die Hard 2 and John McClane belong to 20th Century Studios, by way of Gordon Company and Silver Pictures, based on characters originated by Roderick Thorp and carried forward by Walter Wager, and yes, that's currently a Disney property too, same as half the industry these days. Worm and everyone in Brockton Bay belong to Wildbow, who wrote several million words of it into existence on his own steam and owes no studio anything for the privilege. One side of this crossover answers to a mouse. The other answers to a guy on the internet with an extremely punishing update schedule. They have never met, and this story is the closest they're going to get.

No infringement is intended and no profit is being made. If anyone from Fox, Disney, or Wildbow's own inbox happens to be reading this: it was Loki's idea again. He gets around.

This is a fan work, offered freely, for love of both properties, an homage to Ack's I, Panacea and Security! and their passenger-seat premise, per the series-wide note above, and to Rob O'Make O'Malley, who does not, as far as anyone can prove, actually exist, and who would very much like it kept that way.

The Restless Clause

O'Make Way, O'Malley! is my own sandbox, not Ack's; the debt to I, Panacea and Security! is one of inspiration and premise, not continuation, and this story is not set in Ack's continuity or written with his characters.

That said, per Rule 9a: I, Maestro, as the creator and sandbox owner of O'Make Way, O'Malley!, give explicit, fully open permission for any author to write additional stories set in this sandbox, the ROB O'Make O'Malley passenger-seat premise, its core mechanics, and the anthology format described in the series bible. No further conditions apply, beyond the standard disclaimer and credit requirements already required of you, and beyond whatever additional terms may be imposed at the sole discretion of Mr. O'Malley, who cannot currently be reached for comment and has historically been unreliable about explaining himself even when he could be.

The Second Opinion Clause

Mr. O'Malley, despite considerable and largely unearned confidence in his own recruiting instincts, would welcome a critical eye on his work. Beta readers, canon nitpickers, and anyone willing to point out that a chokepoint isn't actually a chokepoint are warmly invited to reach out before a chapter posts, not after. He finds retroactive correction far less charming than most reviewers seem to assume he would.

Reviews and thoughts are genuinely welcome. Flames will be filed, calmly, under Things That Do Not Rattle a Man Who Has Already Walked Across Broken Glass Barefoot, and answered with all the urgency the building's air traffic control tower generally reserves for a plane that's still got fuel to spare.
