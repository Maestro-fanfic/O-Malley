Chapter 21 of 29

Wrong Runway

Taylor stayed beneath the banquet table until Danny and the injured man disappeared behind the stage.

She tracked them with the moth in Danny's collar. Two gunmen escorted him into a treatment room attached to MedHall's first-floor clinic. Anders remained in the atrium, still among the hostages, one hand resting on the councilwoman's shoulder while the sound-board operator negotiated with Armsmaster in a machine voice.

"Our conditions are simple," the operator said. "No aircraft within two miles. No parahuman personnel inside the outer drive. At midnight, three vehicles with full fuel tanks will be delivered to the loading entrance. Drivers will exit and walk away."

"You have placed explosives against structural supports," Armsmaster replied. "No vehicle approaches until our engineers receive the trigger design and placement."

Every head inside the atrium turned toward the columns.

The charges had been hidden behind decorative panels. Armsmaster had just told the hostages they existed.

Panic moved through the room without anyone standing. People leaned away from the columns, pressed closer together, whispered names into a silence where no phones remained to call them.

The movement reached the guards before it became a rush. One man stepped into the center aisle and raised his weapon. Another dragged a banquet chair against the main doors, not because the chair could stop anyone, but because scraping metal across marble made every head turn toward him.

"Stay seated."

A woman near the north column began to cry. Her husband put both arms around her and drew her down until his forehead rested against the back of her neck. Two rows away, a city planner Taylor recognized from one of Danny's union meetings stared at the decorative panel hiding the charge and silently measured the distance to the nearest door. He saw Fenja there. He looked at the main entrance and saw Menja. He stopped measuring.

Near the stage, Anders raised his voice just enough to carry.

"Everyone remain calm. The Protectorate knows the building. They will not provoke a detonation."

It was exactly what a respected civic leader should have said. It also told his people that the newly exposed charges could remain where they were.

Taylor felt the effect move through the hostages. Shoulders lowered. Whispering thinned. The room accepted comfort from the man who had put explosives beside them.

In the clinic behind the stage, Danny and the second hostage lowered the injured donor onto an examination table. The room had been designed for demonstrations rather than emergency care. Its cabinets held sealed dressings, sample packages, and diagnostic equipment no one had expected to use. The escorting gunman swept a tray of brochures to the floor with his forearm.

"Keep him alive."

"I drive a forklift," Danny said. "If you've got a doctor, this is the point where you bring one in."

The gunman pointed through the glass partition at a gray-haired woman kneeling in the first hostage row. "She said she was an anesthesiologist. You get four minutes."

He left Danny with the other hostage and locked the treatment-room door from outside.

Taylor's moth stayed beneath Danny's collar. His pulse beat hard enough to move its feet.

He wanted proof the caller was real, McClane said. Armsmaster just gave it to him over an open channel.

The sound-board operator looked toward Anders.

Anders bowed his head as if comforting the councilwoman. His fingers tapped once against her shoulder.

"One vehicle," the operator said. "Armored. No tracking equipment. You have forty-five minutes."

Taylor slipped from beneath the table while every guard watched the frightened hostages. She returned through the concealed service door and moved toward the western stairs.

"Tower," Miss Militia said through the radio. "Confirm you are mobile."

Taylor keyed once.

"Confirm the caller's claim that Anders is directing the hostage takers."

Taylor keyed twice.

No.

The answer made her stop.

You said he is.

He is. She asked whether you can confirm it. Right now you've got hand signals and a bug nobody else can hear through. That's knowing, not proving.

Taylor hated the distinction because McClane was right.

Miss Militia waited, then said, "Understood. We have Anders visible on the hostage feed. He appears restrained and under guard. Armsmaster assesses the emergency caller may have mistaken his efforts to control panic for command activity."

There it is, McClane said. Wrong picture, taken by a camera the bad guy pointed for them.

The hostage feed was not merely a picture. It was a composition.

The camera showed Anders seated beneath the MedHall banner with a frightened councilwoman tucked against one side of him. Alabaster stood behind them with a weapon taken from a security guard, his white hair hidden beneath a black knit cap and his face angled away. The injured donor's empty place remained visible in the foreground. Every element said captive, guard, casualty.

It did not show the sound-board operator watching Anders's hands. It did not show the charge ten feet left of the frame. It did not show the clinic door where Danny was being held apart from everyone else.

Taylor sent a beetle over the camera housing. A red tally light glowed on the front. Two cables ran from the back, one toward the building's media cabinet and another down through a hole drilled roughly into the stage.

The second cable had not belonged to MedHall.

Prepared feed, she thought.

Prepared story, McClane corrected. The picture's just how he's selling it.

Outside the east windows, moths clustered beneath the floodlights gave Taylor fragments of the response Anders was selling it to. Police cars sealed the road at both ends. An ambulance crew had unfolded two empty stretchers beside a brick planter and now waited without patients. PRT troopers formed teams behind armored vans while officers pulled on helmets, checked seals, checked one another, and continued not entering.

Armsmaster stood within a loose circle of equipment cases beneath a portable canopy. A technician held a transparent building plan against a lit board. From Taylor's insects it was only a bright rectangle and moving shadows. Miss Militia stood apart from the others, facing MedHall instead of the plan.

The distance put them beyond Taylor's reliable detail. That was almost worse. There were dozens of trained people outside, all moving with purpose, and the man inside still controlled what they believed their purpose was.

"Tower, we need actionable observations. Number and identity of capes. Weapon positions. Explosive locations. Do not speculate on command."

Taylor keyed once.

She wanted to throw the radio through the wall.

Instead she pressed her thumbnail against the edge of its casing until the pain narrowed her attention.

She's not dismissing you, McClane said. She's asking for something she can carry into an argument.

I gave them Anders.

You gave them your conclusion. Give her the work.

Taylor looked through the moth in Danny's collar. The anesthesiologist had been brought into the clinic and was cleaning the donor's wound while the guard watched through the glass. Danny stood at the counter opening dressings before she asked for them. He kept glancing at the door, then at the ceiling, as if he knew Taylor might be in either direction.

He could not see the fly under the upper cabinet. He had no way to know she was watching him.

He still positioned himself between the doctor and the gunman.

Instead she returned to the storage room, unfolded her paper map, and compared it to the building through her swarm.

Hookwolf remained at the main stairs. Cricket patrolled the mezzanine. Alabaster covered the service corridor. Fenja and Menja had reduced their size enough to fit beneath the atrium ceiling and now guarded the east and main doors. Eleven armed personnel remained visible. At least four more moved outside her reliable range near the loading dock and parking bridge.

The charges sat against six supports, not three. Armsmaster had identified only the ones visible through the hostage camera.

Taylor clicked once and waited.

"Tower. Speak for ten seconds after the next public transmission. Begin with cape count."

Armsmaster's voice filled the enemy channel. "We have received your vehicle request. Before delivery, release ten hostages and provide a view of the injured."

Taylor pressed transmit while his words still echoed from radios throughout MedHall.

"Five capes. Hookwolf at main stairs, Cricket mezzanine, Alabaster west service, Fenja east doors, Menja main. Fifteen armed minimum. Six charges, west and north supports not visible on camera. Anders commands by hand signals."

Nine seconds.

She released the button.

The transmission felt much longer from inside it. On the third second Alabaster turned his head. On the fifth, Cricket stopped in the middle of a step. At seven, the sound-board operator lifted his free hand and began counting positions from the direction of the interference. At nine, a guard at the western doors looked directly toward the wall behind which Taylor crouched.

The sound-board operator looked down at his radio.

Every armed person in the building heard Armsmaster. Whether any had heard the whisper underneath depended on volume, distance, and attention.

Cricket turned toward the west service corridor.

She had heard.

Move, McClane said.

Taylor snatched up the inventory sheet and the pencil. Leaving either behind would tell them exactly where she had been and what she knew. The pencil rolled against her palm as she reached the door. She nearly dropped it, caught it against her skirt, and lost two seconds doing so.

The first guard entered the far end of the corridor before she reached the kitchen.

"West storage clear?"

"Checking now."

"She can't have crossed the atrium."

Cricket's answer arrived as three measured taps against the mezzanine rail, transmitted through the building more clearly to Taylor's insects than any voice. Men split at the next junction. One pair came through the service corridor behind her. Another descended toward receiving. A third moved to seal the parking bridge.

They were not chasing a teenage girl anymore. They were clearing sectors.

Cricket did not chase her body. She chased the channel. Guards began checking storage rooms from both ends of the service floor, one pair opening doors while another watched the corridor intersections. Taylor moved through the kitchen, passed beneath a prep counter, and climbed into the dumbwaiter shaft her bugs had found an hour earlier.

The kitchen was not empty. The fourteen catering workers had been moved out of the cold room and seated along the far wall with their hands locked behind their heads. A guard stood near the ranges, covering them and the two service doors at once.

Taylor came through the pantry on his blind side.

A dishwasher saw her first. His eyes widened. Taylor put one finger against her lips.

The guard noticed the change in his face and turned.

Taylor pulled open the nearest warming cabinet. A tower of clean sheet pans slid out, struck the tile one after another, and filled the kitchen with metal crashes. She went low while the guard brought his weapon around. The catering workers folded toward the floor.

"Stay down!"

He shouted it at everyone. Taylor crawled behind the prep island, sent flies into his eyes, and felt him stagger into the hanging rack. Ladles, strainers, and whisks swung above him. She reached the dumbwaiter, opened its narrow door, and got one knee inside.

The dishwasher stood.

For one second Taylor thought he meant to follow. Instead he caught the warming-cabinet door and shoved the remaining pans into the aisle behind her.

"There!" he shouted, pointing toward the opposite pantry.

The guard fired at the empty doorway.

Taylor pulled herself into the shaft before he could discover the lie.

The car sat one floor above. The shaft below dropped to receiving. Taylor braced her shoes against one wall and her back against the other, suspended in darkness while a guard opened the kitchen door.

"Radio traffic came from this section."

"Could be reflected signal."

"Cricket says it wasn't."

The man checked beneath the counters. Taylor felt his flashlight through roaches on the floor. Another opened the dumbwaiter door below her.

Light climbed the shaft and stopped inches beneath her shoes.

Taylor held herself above it until her legs shook.

The shaft had been built for trays, not people. Grease filmed the guide rail beside her cheek. The wall pressed cold through the back of her dress. She had one shoe wedged against a seam in the metal lining and the other braced on the narrow lip of the service opening. Every breath shifted her weight. Every shift made the cable above her tick against its pulley.

Below, the flashlight beam moved away from her feet and returned.

"Nothing."

"Check the car."

"It's upstairs."

"Then she went down the shaft."

The lower guard leaned inside. Taylor felt his hand close around the guide rail below her.

The radio at her waist clicked against the wall.

Both men went quiet.

The lower man raised his light. Its edge found the sole of Taylor's shoe.

"Got her."

Up, McClane said.

The dumbwaiter car above her was held by a steel cable. Taylor reached for the insects inside its mechanism, packed them into the door interlock, and forced the latch to release. The car dropped six inches before the brake caught.

The noise came from above.

The guards looked up.

Taylor pushed away from both walls. She slid down the shaft, struck the lower man's shoulders feet-first, and drove both of them through the open receiving door. He hit the floor beneath her. His flashlight spun away.

Her right ankle folded on impact. Pain flashed white along her shin. She rolled with it because McClane shouted the instruction before she knew she needed it, and the lower guard's hand closed on empty air where her shoulder had been.

The second guard lunged through the kitchen opening. Taylor rolled behind a stack of flour sacks as two suppressed shots punched into the dumbwaiter wall.

Flour burst into the air.

One round split a sack at shoulder height. The other struck the steel frame and threw a spark into the cloud. Taylor flinched from the flash and waited for fire that did not come. Her bugs became useless inside the flour. Wings clogged. Antennae lost the air. For three steps she was reduced to her own eyes in a white room.

The cloud hid the whole receiving room. Taylor sent insects low across the floor, found the gunman's legs, and moved opposite him. He fired toward a rolling cart she shoved with one hand. The bullets chased the cart. Taylor went through the loading-prep door behind him.

Her bad ankle struck the threshold. She bit down on the sound, grabbed the crash bar, and pulled the door shut behind her. A bullet punched through above her hand. Another hit the closer mechanism and showered her hair with plaster and metal filings.

The door no longer latched.

She seized a linen cart parked in the passage and drove it sideways beneath the handle. The guard hit the other side. The cart rolled six inches. Taylor put her back against it until a second crash bar opened farther down the hall and boots entered from ahead.

Left, McClane said.

That's a closet.

Then find out what kind.

Taylor went left. The narrow door admitted her into a janitorial room crowded with chemical drums, replacement bulbs, and a floor sink. No second exit. She closed herself inside and sent bugs into the wall.

Pipes rose behind the sink. Above them, an access panel opened into the cavity beneath the main staircase.

The screws had been painted over.

Taylor put the trauma shears under one edge and levered until the first screw tore through plaster. The corridor door opened behind her as she pulled the panel far enough to fit one shoulder through.

A hand caught the back of her dress.

Taylor twisted, leaving fabric in the guard's fist, and crawled headfirst among the pipes. Her injured ankle struck the frame. He grabbed for it. She kicked blindly and felt her heel connect with fingers.

Then she was through.

The wall cavity narrowed around her ribs. Dust filled her mouth. She dragged herself beneath the stairs while the guard tore at the opening behind her, reached a maintenance hatch on the far side, and fell through it into the western fire passage.

She did not stop until two fire doors separated them.

"Tower," Miss Militia said. "Threat map received. Break contact if pursued."

Taylor keyed once while running.

"Armsmaster is preparing a roof reconnaissance. No entry authorization. Repeat, no entry authorization."

Through a spider sheltering beneath the roof access, Taylor felt a vibration above.

Metal touched gravel.

Weight settled beside the skylight.

Someone was already on the roof.

No entry authorization, Taylor thought.

Doesn't mean no entry, McClane said. Means somebody wants a closer look and thinks calling it reconnaissance changes what happens if they're spotted.

Cricket left the mezzanine and climbed the north stairs.

Hookwolf moved away from the atrium.

Anders tapped two fingers against the floor among the hostages.

The sound-board operator raised his radio. "Roof team in position. Hold until both targets enter the stair enclosure."

Taylor pressed transmit.

"Miss Militia, they know. Abort the roof. They know."

The words went out openly. Every radio heard them.

For the first time that night, Max Anders looked toward no camera, no hostage, and no member of his crew.

He looked directly at the nearest ceiling speaker, as if Taylor's voice had given him a face to meet.

Then the roof access exploded.
